Orange lay across Juexing’s body, weeping quietly. She had never been this heartbroken. She had longed for him to step forward the way Shang Xuan always did—to save her at the moment of greatest danger—yet she had never wanted that longing to become reality in so cruel a form.

The silent morning was broken only by her soft, broken sobs. Shang Xuan stood outside the observation window for a long time before he finally opened the door, walked to her side, and offered a tissue.

Orange’s eyes were swollen. She had not left the bedside since the previous night.

“Sometimes,” Shang Xuan said, looking at the still, closed-eyed figure on the bed, “I really wish he would just stay like this.”

Orange sat up straight but did not answer. Her gaze remained fixed on Juexing, as if the slightest lapse in attention would allow him to slip away.

“I don’t know who told him what, or where, but—”

She cut him off.

“Both of you are important in my life. But only one of you can be a friend, and only one can be more. What I feel for you can only be friendship.”

“Is it worth it?” Shang Xuan’s voice carried quiet indignation on her behalf. “He’s always hot and cold with you. Why put yourself through that?”

“He’s trying to change for me. That’s what I see. He started eating the food I cook. He started telling me which people I shouldn’t get too close to…” Her voice broke. Short, sharp sobs followed.

Shang Xuan rested a hand on her shoulder.

“Five years ago Juexing and I both met Li Mei. It felt like fate. No matter how hard I tried, I could never change the fact that she fell in love with him. He treated her the same way he treats you now—distant, half-indifferent. Every time she was in danger, I was the first one at her side. I never understood why his weight in her heart was heavier than mine.”

“Love isn’t measured by who saves whom more often,” Orange said. “It’s a feeling that can’t be explained. Once it finds its place, it’s almost impossible to move.”

“If I had given up even once back then, maybe I wouldn’t still be in this much pain.”

“But you didn’t. You kept going without looking back. That’s your principle, isn’t it?”

Shang Xuan fell silent. The hand on her shoulder withdrew of its own accord. She was right. Principle allowed the person he loved to love someone else. From the outside, all one should wish for was the other’s happiness. Why insist on being the one who provided it?

“You saved me many times,” Orange continued. “After you confessed, I redefined the places you both held in my heart. Only then did I realize the direction of my heart had never changed. Maybe you think I’m foolish. Maybe you think it’s unfair to you. But that is my choice. Even if I end up covered in wounds, I won’t blame heaven or anyone else.”

“I won’t give up,” Shang Xuan said. “The love I couldn’t receive from Li Mei, I’ll take back from you. I intend to compete with Juexing fairly. Maybe one day your view will change. I’ll wait.”

He turned and left the room. Orange watched him go, then lowered her head and held Juexing’s hand. She felt the temperature in his palm beginning to fall.

---

Yu Xun finished organizing her report, archived the confidential materials, left the office, bought a bouquet of violets at a flower shop, and drove to the hospital. She wanted to check on Juexing’s condition.

Orange had just hung up a call from the college when she turned and saw Yu Xun.

“You’re here.”

Yu Xun nodded and followed her inside. The doctors had just completed another examination; there was still no significant improvement. They left the room temporarily to discuss other options.

Orange accepted the violets, removed the wilted lilies from the vase, and arranged the new flowers on the table.

“He still hasn’t woken up?” Yu Xun asked.

Orange nodded and pulled over a stool for her.

“Is something wrong?” Yu Xun asked again.

“Yes. Could I trouble you to watch him for a while? I need to go to the college for something.” Orange was deeply reluctant to leave, but the dean had insisted the matter was important and required a face-to-face discussion.

“Of course. I’ll wait here until you get back. But you should eat something before you go, or your body will collapse.”

Orange picked up her bag, glanced once more at the peaceful, unconscious figure on the bed, and finally closed the door.

Alone with him, Yu Xun studied the man who looked almost like a corpse. She took out a small notebook filled with random notes and fragments—the essential scraps a seasoned reporter always kept.

“Ever since I started covering these paranormal cases, I’ve discovered your strengths,” she said quietly. “You’re like a bridge connecting the living and the souls. To understand your past more deeply I searched for a great deal of material on you. Eventually I found it—the story of you, Shang Xuan, and Li Mei…”

She opened the notebook and read a short passage aloud, then closed it again.

“You and Shang Xuan are both cursebreakers, yet you belong to two different worlds. Perhaps Li Mei never liked Shang Xuan because he was too practical. Why she preferred the more introverted you is a question I’ll keep exploring. That wound in your heart—has it healed yet? Who…”

She turned her head. Someone was standing outside the door.

Jiaye greeted her and walked in.

“You’re here too?”

“I came to visit the protagonist of the story. If he kicks the bucket I can update the article right away.”

“Listen to you, getting witty. Sounds like all of you are hoping he just stays like this. How heartbreaking.” Jiaye pulled a theatrical face of grief.

“Enough. Stop joking around. He’s helped you plenty of times.”

“Look at that—now you’re putting all the responsibility on me. Let me tell you something: as his brother, if I don’t nod, he isn’t allowed to die!”

The words had barely left his mouth when the monitors began to scream. Juexing’s vital signs plummeted. His heart rate, previously steady at eighty, dropped in a straight line.

“You jinx! Get the doctors!” Yu Xun shoved past him and ran into the corridor. Medical staff poured into the room and began emergency resuscitation.

The curtains were drawn. Jiaye and Yu Xun could only wait outside in rising panic, with no idea what had triggered the sudden collapse.

---

Orange stepped out of the taxi and went straight to the dean’s office. The dean gestured for her to sit and began discussing a temporary reassignment.

“What? You want me to go to the vocational high school as a counselor?” Orange stared at the smiling dean with exhausted eyes.

“Yes. In terms of both qualifications and character, Teacher Qingmu is the ideal choice. And I recall you hold a doctorate in psychology—more than capable of handling the counselor role.”

“But right now I…”

“I know. It’s because of Teacher Luo’s situation. The college has already raised a donation for his medical expenses. Here.” The dean took a thick envelope from the drawer—stacks of hundred-yuan notes totaling well over ten thousand.

Orange made no move to accept it.

“This is a voluntary collection from everyone. Take it without worry. The transfer to the vocational school is rather sudden, so I can only give you ten days. Next Saturday I will accompany you there and help with the arrangements.”

The dean’s tone left little room for refusal.

“Don’t worry. As soon as we recruit a suitable counselor, I’ll bring you back. In addition to continuing your teaching in the tourism major, I’ve also reserved the position of vice-president of the student union for you. Quite the incentive, isn’t it?”

Orange accepted the envelope. In the corridor outside, several colleagues gathered around her with quiet words of concern for Juexing. Their care eased the tightness in her chest a little.

She reached the school gate and was about to hail a taxi when Jiaye’s call came through. The moment she heard the words, the envelope slipped from her fingers. Hundred-yuan notes scattered across the pavement.

She did not stop to pick them up. Bag clutched tight, she ran toward the hospital.

Jiaye had told her Juexing might not make it.

---

By the time she arrived, the operating-room lights had been dark for only a short while. The surgeon emerged looking haggard. The white sheet covering Juexing’s face and the people standing around the bed told her everything with brutal clarity.

Orange walked forward in a daze. Her hands trembled as she pulled the sheet back. The bloodless face beneath it could never return to what it had been.

“No… don’t…”

She sank down, gripping his nearly icy hand. Suddenly she felt a pulse—one beat, two, three. It was no illusion.

“His finger moved!” Yu Xun’s sharp eyes caught the tiny motion. Jiaye spun around and shouted for the doctors. The moment the monitors were reconnected, a miracle unfolded. Every vital sign climbed in a steep geometric curve. Bodily functions stabilized and began to recover. The attending physicians stared in open astonishment. None of them had ever seen anything like it.

Although the turnaround was dramatic, Juexing still did not wake. Jiaye and Yu Xun eventually left. Orange remained to care for him. She was certain he had sensed her presence and chosen not to leave.

Shang Xuan returned the envelope she had dropped, bought her a bowl of hot porridge and a piece of cake, then departed. If she wanted to be present on the day Juexing opened his eyes, she had to keep herself alive. She lifted the bowl and began to eat.

Shang Xuan stood outside the room for a moment longer, watching through the glass. Then he smiled faintly and left the hospital. He had someone to find—an old woman who pushed a cart.

### Character Notes

**Qingmu Chengzi (Orange)**  
Her confession to Shang Xuan is quiet, clear, and final: friendship only. The ten-day deadline imposed by the college adds external pressure to the private vigil. When she returns and Juexing’s vitals surge, the narrative strongly implies that her presence itself is the anchor keeping him in the world of the living.

**Shang Xuan**  
Openly declares he will continue to compete for Orange’s heart, framing it as unfinished business from the tragedy with Li Mei five years earlier. His final action—leaving to seek Granny Luo Shen—suggests he is already looking for another way to influence the outcome.

**Luo Juexing**  
Remains unconscious, yet twice responds to the emotional field around him: first by crashing when Jiaye jokingly “forbids” him to die, then by surging back the moment Orange returns and touches him. The cyan light of the previous chapter has subsided, but the bond remains.

**Yu Xun**  
Continues her dual role as chronicler and observer. Her private monologue over the unconscious Juexing reveals she has been researching the Li Mei incident in depth, positioning her as a potential keeper of the larger story.

**Huang Jiaye**  
Provides the blunt, brotherly presence that both lightens and endangers the moment. His careless remark nearly costs Juexing his life, yet he is also the one who summons help and later delivers the critical phone call to Orange.

**The Ten Days**  
The chapter title and the dean’s deadline create a clear temporal frame. Whatever recovery or further crisis awaits Juexing, it must unfold within that narrow window.
