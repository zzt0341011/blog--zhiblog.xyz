“Is this the end?” Shang Xuan asked, puzzled.

“I think it’s only the beginning.” Juexing swept his flashlight across the bottomless abyss that yawned before them like a vast, dried-up well.

“Do we go down?” Shang Xuan asked.

“I need to say this first,” Juexing replied, voice firm. “One of us has to make it out alive.”

“That would be you, of course.” Shang Xuan answered without hesitation.

“You know this isn’t a joke.” Juexing glanced at him.

“I know. But do you know there’s still someone waiting for you up there? If it were me, I would never leave Orange behind.” Shang Xuan smiled—the kind of smile that said he had already accepted whatever consequences might follow.

“All right. With those words, both of us will make it out.” Juexing extended his hand.

The old Shang Xuan would never have taken it. Yet this time he did. The moment their palms met, a violent gust of wind surged from behind and hurled them both into the void.

Orange had already waited in the vehicle for a full hour. Her patience was gone. She grabbed the horse-slaying saber from the car and headed straight for the exhibition hall. By the agreed time Jiaye should have arrived—yet something had gone wrong on the road.

Jiaye’s Mercedes reached a large gas station nearby. An empty tanker sat to one side. He and Yuxun had no choice but to pump gasoline back into it. The work was brutal, and they also had to watch over Meixue, whose emotions were fraying. Just when things could not get worse, several children who had been hiding at the station suddenly ran out. The sudden movement summoned the demonic vines that now laced the entire city. The moment any human activity stirred, the vines erupted from the earth and began to kill.

To save the children Jiaye dropped the pump and seized a nearby watermelon knife, fighting the thrashing vines until he was covered in wounds. He managed to pull the children free. Yuxun watched from the side, heart pounding; she could not leave the half-filled tanker.

Through sheer effort they finally filled the tanker to about eighty percent. For the children’s safety they had to leave at once. Before departing, Jiaye left a can of gasoline in the station office, lit it, and drove away. The towering flames forced the vines back; they could only wither and die in the blaze as the tanker roared into the distance.

Yuxun clung tightly to Jiaye. She was terrified that their wedding would be delayed—or never happen at all.

“Don’t worry. We’ll go back and get married. Juexing and the others too. As long as we believe and keep fighting, even the biggest problems will break.”

Yuxun said nothing. Blessings or forgetting, tears or separation—everything felt terrifying.

The Metropolitan Police had underestimated the vines’ power and were forced to expand the protective perimeter by another kilometer. From the highest defense tower the city looked like a pagoda completely overrun by vines.

“The Soul-Binding Tower?” the police chief murmured, as if he recognized the old story.

Juexing and Shang Xuan continued to fall. Neither knew how long the freefall lasted, but their joined hands never loosened. Orange, jolted and determined, finally reached the edge of the abyss and shouted down. The sound caught Juexing’s attention.

“Damn—that’s Orange’s voice.” He lunged and caught a vine. Both men jerked to a stop, dangling in mid-air.

“Juexing, can you hear me?” Orange called again. Only silence answered from the darkness.

Anxiety rose. Once more Luo Juexing had left her behind. Anger flared. She was about to look for a way down when a vine struck her from behind and shoved her over the edge.

A horse-slaying saber flashed into Shang Xuan’s view. He twisted left just in time to avoid being struck. Juexing, one hand locked on the vine, caught Orange’s wrist with the other. The three of them dropped another short distance before the vines held.

“For heaven’s sake, I told you to wait for Jiaye!” Juexing scolded.

“You’re one to talk. It’s been over an hour. He was late, so I came down myself.” Orange pouted.

Shang Xuan shouted from above, “You two okay?”

“We’re fine. You?” Juexing called back. No answer came. When he looked up, Shang Xuan was gone.

“Shang Xuan! Shang Xuan!” The air above them felt heavier; breathing grew labored.

Orange felt the oxygen thinning too. “What do we do now?”

“It’s all right. We’ll climb down a little farther and see.” She twisted and seized another vine. The two of them began a slow descent, unaware that the closer they came to the truth, the closer they came to the end.

Shang Xuan had been seized by vines and dragged into a side passage halfway down. He struggled to shift his grip so he could use the axe. The moment he swung and severed the vines, his head struck stone. He felt the solid rock wall.

Two hours later Jiaye’s tanker smashed through the fifth defensive line and finally stopped in front of the exhibition hall. Apart from the military truck, no one was in sight. They were a full hour late.

Jiaye clenched his fists and stared at the lighter resting on the dashboard. Juexing had told him: if they had not appeared after one hour, pour the gasoline into the tunnel and burn everything to ash.

Yuxun saw the struggle on his face. Perhaps their delay had created this exact crisis. Without hesitation she snatched the lighter, hoisted the fuel hose, and started toward the hall. The children helped drag the hose. Only Jiaye remained in the cab.

“What are you hesitating for?” Meixue demanded, eyes full of despair.

“I can’t do it. If Juexing and Orange are still inside, I’ll be burning them alive. I could never forgive myself…” His eyes reddened; his hands shook. He smashed a fist through the windshield. Blood welled on the back of his hand.

“I’ve been married to DouDou for less than a week and this horror happens. I know I’ve lost control, but compared with the whole city we’re nothing, aren’t we?”

Jiaye exhaled, watched Yuxun open the valve, then struck his own chest three times. Gritting his teeth he climbed out, took the lighter from her, and said, “Get the kids in the car. The moment I light it we leave.”

Yuxun nodded, ran back, and took the driver’s seat, hands already on the wheel, ready to reverse.

Jiaye walked heavily to the tunnel mouth and stared at the gasoline pouring in. It might be the entrance that ended the catastrophe—or the entrance to death. The choice of whether to light it no longer existed.

“Don’t light it!” Little Bell slapped the lighter from his hand and shoved him down.

Jiaye scrambled up and shouted to Yuxun, “Get her out of here—now!”

Yuxun jumped out. Little Bell bit Jiaye hard; the sharp pain did not make him let go. Yuxun slapped the girl across the face.

“You’re not the only one who cares about Shang Xuan. Juexing and Orange are inside too. Do you think we want to do this?”

Little Bell threw her arms around Yuxun and sobbed. She had finally confessed to Shang Xuan less than half a month ago—and already they might be separated by life and death.

Yuxun guided her into the car. Jiaye, ignoring the pain in his hand, picked up the lighter that had fallen into the gasoline. It refused to spark.

“Damn it, light!” He flicked it again and again. Nothing. The vines, catching the scent of gasoline, began crawling toward the tunnel mouth.

Gasoline poured like seawater, drenching Orange and Juexing below.

“Gasoline!” Juexing cried.

Orange smiled faintly. “It’ll be over soon. We may not get out.”

Juexing smiled too. He took her hand and walked to the root of the great vine, meeting its arrogant gaze.

“You’re finished. Disappear from this earth.”

Sensing the threat, the vine erupted in fury. Wrist-thick tendrils lashed out and bound them both. Even so, their clasped hands refused to open.

The coils around their necks tightened, as if every breath of oxygen were being sucked from the air. Juexing tried to raise the axe to cut the vine around Orange, but halfway up his strength failed. The axe slipped from his fingers and sank into the rising gasoline.

“I can’t die…” Shang Xuan opened his eyes. He was lying in a stone chamber—more precisely, inside a tower.

He struggled upright. The vines were gone. It was simply a tower, ten stories high. Every level was carved with images of plants: ancient man-eating flowers and strange specimens even he did not recognize. At the very top bloomed a black six-petaled flower identical to the one in the old painting.

On the highest floor lay a book. Shang Xuan opened it. A flash of green light erupted. The Soul-Binding Tower vanished. He found himself standing once more in the tunnel, face to face with Juexing and Orange, the book that recorded the tower’s secrets still in his hands.

### Character Notes

**Luo Juexing**  
Still the anchor in crisis. Even while falling and later drowning in gasoline, he refuses to release the hands that matter. His final words to the vine are pure defiance.

**Shang Xuan**  
Dragged into a side passage and into the legendary Soul-Binding Tower itself. The book he brings back may hold the key to ending the vine catastrophe—or opening something far worse.

**Qingmu Chengzi (Orange)**  
No longer willing to wait. Her decision to descend alone nearly costs everyone their lives, yet her presence also forces the group to confront the abyss together.

**Huang Jiaye**  
Torn between duty to the city and loyalty to his friends. The delayed tanker and the order to burn the tunnel force him into the most painful choice of the crisis so far.

**Yuxun & Meixue**  
The personal cost of the disaster is written on their faces—postponed weddings, fragile new marriages, and the knowledge that any of them could die before dawn.

**Little Bell**  
Her desperate attempt to stop the fire reveals how deeply Shang Xuan’s fate has already become intertwined with the group’s survival.

**The Demonic Vines & the Soul-Binding Tower**  
The vines are no longer random growth; they respond to the gasoline as both threat and opportunity. The tower that briefly claimed Shang Xuan appears to be the ancient heart of the phenomenon.
