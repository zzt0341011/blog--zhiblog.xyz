It was an ordinary handcart. It was a figure that seemed to be everywhere. In front of the swaying shadow, a pair of fire tongs moved with practiced ease, tossing a plastic bottle into the cart.

Shang Xuan stood behind Granny Luo Shen, watching every movement.

“You’re here?” she said without turning. She had sensed him long ago.

“Why did Juexing start trying to change?” Shang Xuan asked.

“That’s not a question you should be asking me.” She continued searching through the trash bin. She never let anything of value escape.

Shang Xuan exhaled. “Is it because of Orange?”

“Sighing isn’t your style. You’ve competed with Juexing for so long—still unwilling to shake hands and make peace?”

“I can’t. I can’t forget what he did to Li Mei. He’s the one who killed her…” Tears gathered at the corners of his eyes. Every time the name left his lips, the same self-loathing rose: he should never have pushed the woman he loved toward someone else.

“Is that so?” Granny Luo Shen’s voice sharpened in the quiet night. “Do you truly believe Juexing killed Li Mei?”

Shang Xuan’s heart clenched. He stumbled back several steps, clutching his chest, breathing hard. When he opened his palm, the memory flooded back without mercy: the gu he had planted in Li Mei’s consciousness… the final moment…

“It was me. I killed Li Mei…” He clawed at his own hair. Tears fell freely, unstoppable.

Granny Luo Shen stopped working and turned to look at him. He had collapsed against the flowerbed railing, face twisted in pain and conflict.

“Do you have any idea how much pain Juexing had to endure, how much courage it took for him to make that choice? Even now he still believes it was his fault—because he trusted you too much as a friend.”

She delivered the truth like a blade, then pushed her cart away into the darkness.

Little Bell came skipping down the path, humming a tune. She happened to see Shang Xuan slumped against the railing.

“What’s wrong with you?” She crouched down, concern clear in her young voice.

Shang Xuan lifted his gaze to the sky. The deep blue-black overhead was calm and peaceful. He wondered whether a certain someone had woken yet.

A small pair of hands covered the back of his. The warmth spread through his entire body. He lowered his head and looked at the girl beside him. Her clear eyes still held the last traces of childhood.

He managed a smile. “I’m fine. It’s late. You should go home.” He stood and started walking toward his own place.

Little Bell rose as well and watched his figure grow distant. Suddenly she ran after him.

“What is it?”

“Teacher Qingmu is coming to our school soon. You’ll come too, right?”

Shang Xuan answered with a quiet “Mm,” ruffled her hair, and continued on.

“I’ll be waiting for you to show up!” she called after him, then skipped home full of bright anticipation.

Orange remained at Juexing’s side every day—washing his hair, bathing him, changing his clothes—like a private nurse who never left her post. Even though he showed no sign of awareness, as long as his heart still beat she refused to give up.

Yu Xun made only minor adjustments to the “Ghost Train” report. Once it was published it caused another sensation. The mystique of the cursebreaker was reawakened; covers of newspapers and magazines featured the story once more. Following the instructions in Juexing’s letter, Jiaye renovated the central control room and platform of the station and built a proper grave for Yang Xueqing and her father on that land, so they could continue watching over the place that had always been theirs.

As a result Jiaye’s reputation soared. Officers began requesting transfers to his unit. The newly formed “Paranormal” team under his name was officially established and given its own office space within the police force—though it also attracted more than a few hostile stares.

After work Yu Xun brought a newspaper to Orange. The headline made her chest tighten with a quiet ache.

“A lot of people are praying that Juexing will wake up,” Yu Xun said with a smile. “This world can’t do without him.”

“Thank everyone for me.” Orange did not know what else to say. The ten days had passed in a blink. She would have to leave the city—and him—for a while.

“Have you fixed the date?”

“Tomorrow afternoon’s train.” Her eyes never left Juexing’s face. She wished he would open them now, even if only to say goodbye.

“Don’t worry. Go do your work properly. I’ll look after him for you. The moment he wakes, I’ll call you.”

Orange looked up at the woman standing beside her and could only nod.

“Thank you for taking care of him. I’ll come back to see him as soon as I have leave. I hope by then he’ll be able to talk to me…” She rested her head against Yu Xun’s shoulder. Tears soaked through the fabric.

Yu Xun patted her shoulder. “Juexing knows there’s someone treating him like this. He’ll get better.”

“I should go pack. I’m leaving him in your hands.” Orange bent down, pressed a soft kiss to Juexing’s forehead, picked up her bag, and walked out to the waiting car. She did not know when she would see him again.

Yu Xun pulled a stool close to the bed, picked up the newspaper, and began reading the article aloud in a low voice. As if he could hear, a single tear slipped from the corner of Juexing’s eye.

---

Saturday afternoon was overcast. The dean drove Orange to the vocational high school—an affiliated high school of Jingzhi City University. Many of its graduates could advance directly into the university if they performed well enough.

Orange had made no preparations. The recent days of worry over Juexing had left her noticeably thinner and drained of energy.

“It’ll be fine. Teacher Luo is a lucky man. He might even come visit you in a few days.” The dean led her into the office. The moment Orange stepped across the threshold a wave of dizziness hit her; she had to grip the doorframe to keep from falling. The teachers already present looked at her pale face with curiosity. She managed a polite smile and greeting, which eased the atmosphere a little. After brief introductions and a few instructions, the dean drove away.

A female teacher surnamed Chen escorted Orange to the staff dormitory building and suggested she rest first; work would begin on Monday.

The room was clean, as if someone had known she was coming and prepared it in advance. Teacher Chen hesitated, then handed over a small slip of paper and said in a low voice:

“I don’t think you need to know too much about the strange things in this school. Just remember—after eleven at night, no matter who knocks, do not open the door.”

Her evasive tone and the way her eyes kept scanning the corridor made Orange uneasy.

Once the door closed, Orange removed her coat and collapsed onto the bed. Exhaustion she had held at bay for more than ten days finally claimed her. Some nights she had woken in cold sweats, terrified she would never see Juexing again.

Deep into the night she sat on the edge of the bed looking out at the campus. Scattered figures hurried past under the lights. She wanted to call Juexing, yet feared the phone would ring unanswered.

“Are you all right?” she whispered. “Do you know I pray for you every day? Please wake up soon.”

She closed her eyes, crossed her fingers, and bowed her head. The fragile wish slipped out the window, rose through the clouds, and drifted quietly into Juexing’s mind.

In the hospital room, his eyes slowly opened. He stared at the silent ceiling above him.

Yu Xun stepped in from the corridor and froze in delight.

“You’re awake!”

Juexing shifted his gaze from the ceiling to her smiling face. She looked like a friend he had known for a long time.

“How long was I out?”

“A little over half a month. But it doesn’t matter—you’re awake now!”

“Were you the one looking after me the whole time?”

A flicker of selfishness crossed Yu Xun’s mind. From his expression it seemed he remembered very little. She nodded.

“Yes. I washed your hair, bathed you, changed your clothes, fed you…”

Juexing reached out with a cold hand and covered hers.

“Thank you.”

Yu Xun lowered her eyes, unsure how to answer. She had told a partial lie, yet her heart felt unexpectedly light. Perhaps he would forget there had ever been someone else waiting for him.

“It’s late. Rest properly. Tomorrow I’ll take you out for a walk.” She drew the blanket up over him and sat down at the desk. Juexing continued staring at the ceiling. The past was fragmented: a train, a station, crowds of panicked people. That was all.

---

In the staff dormitory, Orange lay under the covers staring at her own ceiling. If Juexing never woke, would she have to keep guarding an incomplete dream? Shang Xuan’s words circled in her mind again and again, trying to overturn her original resolve.

*What exactly do you like about him? The cold act? The aloofness? The way he ignores you?*

She truly could not list the reasons. By contrast, Shang Xuan’s honesty and care were exactly what so many devoted women longed for—yet she had shut that door.

She closed her eyes and exhaled slowly.

“No matter what anyone says, I still want to hear him reject me with his own mouth. Only then will I give up for good.”

Heavy eyelids won. Exhaustion pulled her into sleep. In the dream everything was black except for a thin line of light leaking from a doorway. She walked toward it with light steps. The moment she pushed the door open, a stream of smiling students poured out. Floating musical notes and laughter wove into a bright melody. She smiled back and told herself: face every person and every event with a smile. As long as there is a smile, there is strength to keep living.

That faint upward curve of her lips traveled through the clouds and struck someone’s heart—just as a soft creak of a door opening sounded in the real world.

Orange’s dormitory door swung open. She did not notice. She was far too tired, and she truly needed one full night of rest.

### Character Notes

**Shang Xuan**  
Forced by Granny Luo Shen to confront the truth he has denied for five years: the gu that destroyed Li Mei was his own doing. The revelation shatters the foundation of his hatred toward Juexing and leaves him raw. Little Bell’s innocent mention of Orange’s arrival at the school plants a new thread.

**Qingmu Chengzi (Orange)**  
Her ten-day window closes. The kiss on Juexing’s forehead and the quiet prayer in the new dormitory mark both devotion and the beginning of physical separation. The warning about never opening the door after 11 p.m. introduces the next layer of mystery at the vocational high school.

**Luo Juexing**  
Awakens with fragmented memory. The first face he sees and thanks is Yu Xun’s—setting up immediate emotional complications. The tear that fell while she read the newspaper earlier suggests he was not entirely unreachable.

**Yu Xun**  
Chooses, in a moment of opportunity, to let Juexing believe she was his sole caregiver. The lie is soft, almost gentle, yet it carries the potential to rewrite the emotional map between the three of them.

**Granny Luo Shen**  
Once again the sharp-tongued catalyst of truth. Her brief appearance dismantles Shang Xuan’s long-held narrative and forces the story toward a more honest reckoning with the past.

**Teacher Chen’s Warning**  
“After eleven at night, no matter who knocks, do not open the door.” The line plants the seed of the next supernatural threat tied to the vocational high school.
