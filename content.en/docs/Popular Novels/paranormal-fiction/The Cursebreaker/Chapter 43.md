Orange could never have imagined that something so profound had once happened between them. Something similar must have occurred again later; otherwise a high-school girl could not have become so obsessively fixed.

“He was locked up for assaulting a minor,” Little Bell continued. “Because of insufficient evidence he was released soon after. I thought we would never cross paths again in this lifetime. Yet not only did he not blame me or seek any revenge—he became even more concerned about me and taught me self-defense… Once I asked him why. He only said, ‘It was fate.’”

“And after that?”

“Later he disappeared from my sight. Now I know he went into the deep mountains with Juexing to train and vowed to become a cursebreaker. The next time we met I was in my first year of high school. He had dyed half his hair blond; his presence was no weaker than before. But just when I had waited so long and finally found him again, Sister Li Mei appeared…”

The moon had already claimed the best vantage point in the sky and quietly watched their conversation. The simple exchange carried a quiet ache. Whether it was the vast firmament, the solitary moon, or the men and women walking the streets—if any of them paused for even a moment and listened, they would have been moved to tears by this genuine, bitter past.

Speaking the memory that had been carved deep into her heart brought Little Bell some relief. Still, she asked Orange to keep the secret. She wanted to tell Shang Xuan herself one day, even if he refused to accept it.

“Don’t worry. I’ll keep it for you. It’s getting late—go take a shower. Sleep here tonight and go home tomorrow.” Orange smiled.

Little Bell nodded, took the towel and pajamas Orange fetched from the bedroom wardrobe, and went into the bathroom.

Juexing and Jiaye ordered two late-night meals, switched seats, and began to eat. The current calm was what worried them most. Every time Shang Xuan left the house he still sent Juexing a short message—yet an entire day had passed with no word.

“Don’t worry. I’ve arranged round-the-clock surveillance. The moment anything moves we’ll get a call. Relax.” Jiaye patted his chest, seeing the heavy look on Juexing’s face.

Juexing set down his chopsticks, closed the takeaway box, and leaned back against the sofa, staring at the ceiling as if some answer were written there.

After the confrontation during the day, Shang Xuan’s vigilance had risen sharply. He devoted himself completely to protecting Wanting. During this period Yu Xun had buried herself in the company, turning over every file connected to Lu Zhengyao’s inheritance case from three years earlier. At last she found a suspicious point. When she looked up at the clock it was already past eleven at night. Jiaye, unwilling to disturb her work, had only sent messages rather than calling. The final message told her he would stay at Juexing’s place until work the next morning and reminded her to go home early and stay safe.

She smiled to herself, stretched her stiff limbs, gathered her bag, coat and files, and left the office.

Little Bell spent the entire night watching Orange’s every move. Only after confirming the full plan did she quietly return to her room to sleep. The moment she lay down she noticed the chandelier above the bed. Glass beads of every color hung from it—red like strawberries, yellow like oranges, white like milk candy, purple like grapes, blue like… like the eyes Shang Xuan fixed on her. He seemed to be telling her not to involve herself in this mess.

“Don’t worry. I’ll help you through this crisis, because you are mine.” A strange smile curved Little Bell’s lips. After the bedside lamp went out, the smile transformed into a cold, sharp light that leapt out the window and vanished into the vast night sky…

At dawn the next morning, while Orange was still asleep, Little Bell slipped into her room and took the phone from the table. She quickly pressed a few numbers, typed several messages, and sent them all to the same number. After receiving confirmation that the other party agreed to meet, she deleted the records and returned the phone to its place. Then she went back to her own room, picked up her phone, and dialed an unfamiliar number. The moment the call connected she covered the receiver lightly and spoke in a disguised voice:

“At ten o’clock this morning Lu Wanting will appear on the walkway bridge at Jiugong Mountain carrying a tulip. You should know what to do.”

A startled voice answered, “Who are you? Why should I believe you?”

“The police have already intervened. You have no choice but to believe. Why not take the gamble?”

Silence on the other end. Little Bell guessed the man was already uneasy. She continued, “I don’t care about the tangle between you and Lu Wanting. She simply latched onto my man, so I have no choice…”

“I understand. I’ll send people. You’d better hope she shows up!” The line went dead with a sharp click. The man stood by the phone in sweat-soaked pajamas, sleep completely driven away. Could heaven itself be helping him?

Shang Xuan left the house at nine. Wanting did not tell him about the messages she had received—partly because she did not want him to worry, partly because she feared he would stop her. Besides, Orange was Shang Xuan’s good friend; there was no reason she would harm her. So while promising Shang Xuan she would notify him the moment she went out, she waited for him to leave.

Following the instructions in the messages, Wanting first slipped away unnoticed, bought a single tulip at a flower shop, took a taxi to the seaside park, and then transferred to a bus bound for Jiugong Mountain.

Orange woke at 9:15. She quickly got up to prepare breakfast for Little Bell. Before starting she peeked into the other room; the girl still appeared to be sleeping.

She closed the door and went into the kitchen. Little Bell opened her eyes, rose, changed clothes, and came out smiling. “What’s for breakfast?”

“I made three-flavor macaroni the way we do it at home.”

“Is it authentic Japanese style?”

“More or less. If you ever get the chance to visit Japan, you should try it.” Orange stirred the eggs while she spoke.

Fifteen minutes later two bowls of colorful macaroni were set on the table. Little Bell stared at the bright toppings, mouth watering.

“How is it?”

With noodles still in her mouth she answered, “It already looked delicious. I didn’t expect it to taste even better than it looked!” She gave a thumbs-up.

“You keep eating. We’re out of soy sauce. I’ll run to the supermarket and also see what you’d like for lunch.”

“I want fried rice—the kind with lots of beans!”

“All right. Stay home and don’t go anywhere. I’ll be back soon.” Orange picked up her bag and a shopping bag, then changed into canvas shoes by the door.

“Teacher Orange… I feel that Teacher Luo is going to be very happy in the future.”

Orange smiled and nodded, opened the door, and left.

Waiting was hard. Wanting checked the time; it was just past ten. She sat on a wooden bench beside the walkway bridge, looking left and right, but there was no sign of Orange. A single tulip lay quietly beside her. She still did not understand why Orange had asked her to bring one. Unless…

A little boy ran onto the bridge from the road, ice-cream cone in hand, licking it as he went. Watching him, Wanting remembered how she used to pester her father for ice cream when she was small. Perhaps he was running too fast, or trying to dodge the crowd; the boy suddenly tripped and fell. Wanting rose at once, helped him up, and brushed the dust from his clothes. The palm of his left hand was scraped and bleeding.

He paid no attention to the injury; he only stared at the fallen ice cream with a pout. Wanting wiped his hand with a tissue and smiled. “Shall Sister buy you another ice cream?”

The boy’s tears turned to a smile at once; he nodded hard. Wanting took his hand and led him toward the McDonald’s dessert shop across the street.

Orange came out of the supermarket carrying two large bags and crossed the street toward home. As she passed a bus stop the plastic bag was sliced by a broken signpost. She did not notice and kept walking.

Even good-quality plastic could not withstand a sharp edge, especially when filled with items of different sizes. At last the bag split open. Several oranges and a head of cabbage rolled onto the ground. Orange had no choice but to set the remaining bags on a long bench of the walkway bridge and turn back to pick up the fallen things.

Yu Xun stepped out of a restaurant and saw Orange collecting the scattered groceries from a distance. She waited for the green light, intending to go over and say hello.

Just then Orange finished rearranging the bags and discovered she had accidentally pressed one of them onto a tulip. The delicate petals were crushed flat, all of their original grace gone. She carefully lifted the flower and was examining it when a black Delica van pulled up. Three men jumped out and strode straight toward her.

Before Orange could react, a white cloth was pressed over her mouth and nose. She struggled only briefly before losing consciousness.

The speed was so great that people nearby noticed nothing. The scene, however, was not perfect. Yu Xun saw every detail clearly. She immediately flagged a taxi and told the driver to follow the van. She had intended to call Jiaye or Juexing at once, but when she took out her phone she hesitated. At that exact moment she saw Wanting approaching with the little boy. Her thoughts turned; she suddenly understood what had happened. Changing her plan, she dialed Shang Xuan instead.

### Character Notes

**Little Bell**  
Completes the painful confession of her past with Shang Xuan, then executes a calculated plan: she uses Orange’s phone to lure Wanting, tips off Lu Shengnan’s side, and remains behind with a calm face while the trap is sprung.

**Qingmu Chengzi (Orange)**  
Unaware of the deception, she leaves only to buy soy sauce and becomes the unintended victim of the kidnapping arranged for Wanting.

**Lu Wanting (Wan Ting)**  
Follows the false messages out of trust for Orange, brings the requested tulip, and is drawn away from the meeting point by a child’s fall—narrowly escaping the fate prepared for her.

**Yu Xun**  
Witnesses the abduction in real time, recognizes the mistaken identity when she sees Wanting still free, and deliberately calls Shang Xuan rather than the police, keeping the information inside the circle that can act fastest.

**Shang Xuan**  
Still unaware that the woman he is protecting has become the bait, and that the wrong person has already been taken in her place.
