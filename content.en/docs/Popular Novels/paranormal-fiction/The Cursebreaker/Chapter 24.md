Orange stood in her soaked clothes, turning to leave, when someone held out a towel. The familiar flying-kiss gesture followed at once.

“Dry off first. Go back to the dorm and take a hot shower.”

Shang Xuan stepped behind her and began carefully wiping her long hair and clothes with practiced gentleness. Once again, in the moment she needed help most, he had appeared.

Orange took the towel from his hands and led him toward her dormitory. The instant they reached the door, the red birthmark on Shang Xuan’s arm flared. She unlocked the door and gestured for him to enter.

“What’s wrong?” she asked when he did not immediately follow.

“You live here?” Shang Xuan stepped inside and scanned the room out of long habit. A faint but unmistakable resentment clung to the space.

“Is there a problem?” Orange poured him a glass of water, then gathered clean clothes and disappeared into the bathroom.

Shang Xuan stood in the center of the room holding the glass. The rippling surface of the water slowly reflected a human silhouette. He jerked his head upward. A long strand of hair was descending from the ceiling, accompanied by soft, rhythmic creaking sounds…

---

Yu Xun had become a regular presence in Juexing’s apartment. Jiaye found himself trapped in a tangle of contradictions. Should he tell Orange the truth? Yu Xun’s attitude did not seem like a joke, and he still intended to pursue her. If he ruined everything with a careless word, he would lose both the girl and the friendship.

Yet how could he stand by and watch the woman he liked grow so intimate with his closest friend?

He decided to call Yu Xun out and demand a clear explanation. At the very least he needed to know what she was planning. He was certain Juexing needed Orange; emotional entanglement with Yu Xun was impossible.

When the call came, Juexing was cooking. Yu Xun told him she would step out for a short while, then left with her coat.

Jiaye arrived at the meeting place half an hour early, growing more impatient by the minute to untangle the awkward situation.

“I’m here. What is it?” Yu Xun’s expression was openly dismissive.

“What exactly are you playing at?”

“Playing? Can’t you see for yourself?”

“You’re not… with Juexing…” He could not finish the sentence. Her impatience left him with no solid ground.

“I’ve suddenly realized you and I are completely incompatible. We never even officially held hands, so let’s just forget it.”

She stood at once. Jiaye moved to block her path.

“That’s not right. Your change of heart is a little too fast, isn’t it? And I haven’t done anything to wrong you.”

Yu Xun looked at his innocent expression. A flicker of reluctance crossed her eyes, but she still said, “I’m telling you formally: the person I choose to like now is Luo Juexing. I’m sorry.”

She walked past him, leaving him standing alone and stunned. His body went slack; his heart felt close to shattering. He sank onto the chair, drinking a beverage he had not ordered, staring blankly at the girl across the table.

“Heartbreak is common. Doesn’t Detective Huang know that a woman’s heart is as deep as the sea?”

Jiaye looked up at the speaker and exclaimed, “What does a brat like you know?”

“I’m not little anymore. I’ll be a university student after graduation this year. And unlike you, I will like only one person with my whole heart.”

The speaker was Little Bell. Her tone suggested she had already seen through every current of the situation. She waved a waiter over.

“He’ll pay.” Then she picked up the box of fries on the table and left in high spirits.

Before Jiaye could scold her she had already vanished. He sat back down and stared at the glass in front of him. He still could not believe Yu Xun would treat him this way. There had to be a reason. Since she refused to explain, he would find a breakthrough through Juexing. As long as he could make Juexing remember Orange, everything would become manageable. With that thought he dialed Juexing’s number and arranged to meet him that evening at the swing ground in the seaside park.

---

Every evening after dinner most people strolled through the seaside park. The swing ground was not large—only six swings—but it remained the children’s favorite place. The seats rose and fell, declaring the pure joy of childhood.

Everyone has to grow up, yet the swings themselves remain like children who never age, changing friends generation after generation.

Juexing arrived a little early. He stood beside one swing, watching a child on another. The girl’s face glowed with innocent satisfaction as she smiled through every rise and fall. Life was the same—valleys and peaks. Truly resilient people faced every person and every event with a smile.

He could not remember from whom he had learned so profound a truth. His thoughts seemed stuck in yesterday, stuck inside the hospital. He tried hard to recall the girl named Orange. Where had she gone?

Jiaye approached from behind carrying two cans of beer and cleared his throat to pull Juexing out of his daze.

“Didn’t expect you to be early.” He handed one can over.

Juexing nodded in thanks but did not open it at once.

A sharp crack. White foam rushed from Jiaye’s can. He tilted his head back, drank a third of it, and looked at Juexing through the faint haze of alcohol.

Juexing roughly understood why he had been called out. In truth he also wanted a proper conversation with Jiaye.

“Yuxun isn’t your girlfriend.”

“I know she isn’t.” Juexing’s eyes stayed fixed on the unopened can in his hand.

“Do you really not remember Orange—the one who stayed by your side without leaving for more than ten nights?”

Juexing remained silent. Sometimes he truly did not know what he was doing. Granny Luo Shen had told him clearly that if he only kept running away instead of fighting for what mattered, he was no man at all. Yet after waking this time he retained not a single impression of Orange—not even the desire to learn where she was now.

Jiaye refused to let him wear that expression. He upended the remaining beer over Juexing’s head, crushed the empty can, and hurled it to the ground.

“Wake up. Even if you don’t plan to be with her, at least reject her properly. Watching you like this… it really hurts.”

His eyes had grown wet without his noticing.

Silence slowly consumed the time between them. Jiaye could think of nothing more to say. He checked his phone, offered no goodbye, and simply turned and left.

Beer still clung to Juexing’s hair and face. The bitterness of the liquid no longer matched the bitterness inside him. Yu Xun had anticipated that Jiaye would make this move; she had simply not expected such a result.

Juexing sat on the long stone bench against the railing, staring out at the hazy, glittering surface of the night sea. Yu Xun appeared and held out a tissue. He did not take it. His gaze remained fixed on the water.

She sat down beside him, arranging her long skirt, and started to rest her head against his shoulder. Before she could, he stood and walked to the railing, resting both hands on the cold metal.

Yu Xun stepped behind him and wrapped her arms around his waist, whispering close to his ear, “I know I shouldn’t have hidden things from you. But I don’t think either of us has done anything wrong. Don’t you agree?”

A camera flash fired from the shadows. Someone grinned with open satisfaction, as though already plotting a larger scheme. Who was the hidden hand behind the lens?

Juexing removed her arms from around him and turned to face her.

“Is what you’re doing fair to Jiaye?”

“There was never anything real between Jiaye and me. It was only his one-sided wish. I know clearly who I like.” Her expression was soft, affectionate, without a single visible flaw.

“But… Tangerine…” The name slipped out. He was the only person who had ever insisted on calling her that.

“Did you ever like her? If you truly did, why have you neither asked nor cared for more than a month?”

Silence had always been Juexing’s most natural weapon. When words failed him he fell back on it—and silence often gave others room to advance.

Yu Xun took his hand and gently traced the scars on the back of it, head lowered.

“Maybe you and Orange once shared beautiful memories. But all of that is in the past. I believe I can give you many beautiful memories as well. Can’t I?”

Juexing looked into her tear-bright eyes. Why did his heart remain so calm when he looked at her? If the person standing before him were Orange, would the rhythm of his pulse be different?

He freed his hand.

“It’s late. Go home and rest. I still have class tomorrow. Good night.”

He forced a small smile onto his face, turned, and walked away with both hands in his pockets, slowly leaving her field of vision.

Yu Xun stood alone, momentarily at a loss. She could no longer predict what stranger things might still occur. Yet one resolve remained firm: she would not let Juexing slip from her grasp. Holding onto him felt like holding a rope that kept climbing higher.

---

The elevator doors opened. Juexing stepped out into a deep, empty corridor. A window at the far end stood open; night wind wandered and played through the quiet space.

He unlocked his door, closed it behind him, walked into the bedroom, and switched on the light. He stared at the bed. Nestled among the covers was a smiling, sleepy face, uneven bangs framing features that were quietly, piercingly lovely.

He leaned against the doorframe and watched for a long moment. When he finally approached the bed he brushed against the nightstand. A small notebook fell to the floor.

He picked it up and opened the first page. Diary entries written in both Chinese and Japanese recorded every small moment of contact between them.

*Today is Thursday. I ran into him on the street. The first time I saw him, so many questions rose in my heart. Suddenly I wanted to understand the man in front of me. I asked my colleagues; they said this is what people call love at first sight. I think they might be right…*  
A small smiling face was drawn beside the entry, followed by the Japanese word for “do your best.”

*Today was the first time I cooked for him. I thought it would bring us closer. Instead I was kicked out of the house. I was so angry. But then I thought—I can’t just give up. Love has to withstand tests. I refuse to believe he won’t eventually fall at my feet. Later my colleagues told me “fall at my feet” is actually a negative phrase. I’ve decided never to use it again!*  
Another smiling face. Beneath it a longer passage that made Juexing’s heart seize the moment he read it. He had not expected that, besides Li Mei, there truly existed someone in this world who treated him with such unreserved kindness.

He closed the notebook, curled into the corner of the room, and looked out at the star-scattered night sky.

“Where did you go? Are you all right… Tangerine…”

### Character Notes

**Luo Juexing**  
The diary becomes the first genuine crack in the wall of amnesia. Reading Orange’s private, hopeful, stubbornly affectionate entries awakens an emotional response that Yu Xun’s presence has not been able to produce. The whispered name “Tangerine” is the clearest sign yet that the lost memories are beginning to surface.

**Shang Xuan**  
Once again appears at the exact moment Orange needs practical help. The red birthmark’s reaction and the descending hair inside her dormitory confirm that the residual resentment tied to Lin Qiaohe is already active in the room where Orange now sleeps.

**Yu Xun**  
Completes her severance from Jiaye with clinical finality and continues to tighten her hold on the amnesiac Juexing. The secret photograph taken during their embrace at the park suggests she is not the only one maneuvering in the shadows.

**Huang Jiaye**  
His frustration peaks in the beer-pouring scene—an act of desperate, almost brotherly violence meant to force Juexing back to himself. Walking away without goodbye shows how close he is to emotional exhaustion.

**Orange’s Diary**  
A quiet, powerful narrative device. The entries reveal her first-sight attraction, her determination after rejection, and the depth of care she offered while Juexing was unconscious. It is the emotional counterweight to Yu Xun’s calculated presence.

**The Swing Ground**  
Serves as both literal setting and metaphor. Children rise and fall without fear; adults carry the weight of memory and choice. Juexing’s moment of watching the swinging child foreshadows the choice he will soon have to make.
