Somehow the news of the two students’ bizarre deaths spread through the school and the city like wildfire. The most popular explanation was, of course, a curse. Almost overnight, the long-forgotten title of “cursebreaker” rose again in people’s minds.

Years earlier, countless frauds had used that same title to swindle the superstitious. Even the genuine practitioners had been tarred with the same brush of deceit. In the end Luo Juexing had been forced to abandon the calling and choose an ordinary life.

Orange sat beside him in the van, watching his profile. So this was the wound he carried—the belief that he could save people from the unnatural, only to be branded a fraud. The injustice of it made her chest tighten. Without thinking she shifted a little closer. The moment she began to lean toward him, Juexing opened the door and stepped out. Orange nearly bumped her head against the window.

“You all right?” he asked, turning back.

She smoothed her hair with forced calm and followed him out. “We’re here already?”

“This should be Yu Xiaohai’s house.” He double-checked the number on the gate.

“Once we gather all three of them… should we take them with us? I’m worried something will happen if we leave any behind.”

“That’s fine. We’ll need their answers anyway. But be prepared—I can’t guarantee what might happen on the road.” He raised a hand and knocked.

After a long conversation with the parents—helped by Huang Jiaye’s official presence and the extraordinary nature of the case—they finally agreed to let their son go. Yu Xiaohai was the class study officer, a high-achieving boy with wide interests. Outwardly he remained steady, yet the fear underneath was impossible to hide. This was not a movie. It was real.

“One more to go—Du Xiaoxiao,” Juexing said.

“Don’t worry. Your teachers and the police will help lift the curse,” Orange tried to reassure him.

Xiaohai only nodded in silence. At this point he had no real choice.

The van had been driving for about half an hour when Xiaohai suddenly spoke.

“Xiaoxiao… is from Dumei Village.”

“Dumei Village?” the three adults echoed at once.

Xiaohai’s body began to tremble like a leaf in the wind. Orange placed a steadying hand on his shoulder. “It’s all right. You’re safe.”

When he had calmed a little he continued, “During the cultural festival it was her idea to visit her village. She said there would be a deity festival… lots of food and fun things…”

Juexing’s expression sharpened. “Does Xiaoxiao usually live at home or in the dormitory?”

“Same as us—she boards at school. She only goes home on weekends.”

“Then her family is in Dumei Village.”

Xiaohai nodded. At that moment his eyes fell on the blurred photograph Juexing had taken from his pocket.

“That’s… that’s the photo…” He squeezed his eyes shut in terror. Juexing quickly put it away. A sudden image flashed through his mind.

*Could it be…?*

“Jiaye—change course. We’re going to Dumei Village now.”

“Driving mountain roads at night isn’t exactly safe,” Jiaye muttered.

“Time is short. We also have to keep Chen Cheng safe in the city.”

Chen Cheng, paralyzed by fear, had secretly taken sleeping pills in an attempt to escape the disaster through unconsciousness. He now lay in a hospital bed under the joint watch of doctors and police, unable to join them.

To Juexing the Medusa statue in the photograph pointed unmistakably to Dumei Village. He had been there once, years ago. He had seen the shrine and the stone image of the snake-haired goddess. The five students had visited the village and only afterward begun to die. The answer had to lie in Dumei Village.

The van left the paved road for a rough gravel track, jolting heavily. Recent rains had left the mountain path in poor condition; even after several clear days the ride remained punishing.

“Drive carefully!” Juexing called.

At that exact moment something struck the right rear tire. The van lurched violently and sank into a shallow mud pit.

“We’re stuck.” Jiaye pressed the accelerator, but the vehicle only rocked side to side, unable to move forward.

The situation was both frustrating and frightening. Juexing suspected the rear wheel had dropped into a hole.

“Stay in the van. I’ll take a look.”

Orange caught his wrist, eyes full of worry. He shook his head. “Stay with Xiaohai. I’ll be fine.”

He closed the door carefully and walked toward the rear with a flashlight. The beam revealed one wheel buried deep in mud. While he was still deciding how to free it, small stones suddenly rose from the ground all around the van and began pelting the bodywork with sharp *clacks*. Orange instinctively pushed Xiaohai down and ducked. In his panic Jiaye floored the accelerator without shifting out of gear. The engine coughed twice and died.

Mud and grit sprayed upward, catching Juexing’s pant legs before he could fully dodge.

“Sorry—really sorry!” Jiaye scrambled out, apologizing.

Juexing looked at the trapped wheel, then at the frightened faces inside. “We’ll have to stop here for the night. We leave at first light.”

Once they stopped moving, the mountain fell silent again. Orange stayed beside Xiaohai until he finally drifted into uneasy sleep, then climbed out and walked toward the small fire Juexing had built.

Flames leapt against the deep night sky, offering a fragile circle of warmth. Sparks rose and vanished like brief, vulnerable lives.

Orange pulled her coat tighter, sat on a low stone, and rested her chin in her hands, quietly watching the man across the fire.

Jiaye remained in the van, keeping an eye on Xiaohai—and taking the chance to rest.

“What kind of person are you, really?” Orange asked suddenly. She kept her voice low, yet in the empty night it carried clearly.

Juexing lifted his head a fraction but did not answer.

“I met someone who showed me something I never thought I would believe. Curiosity made me want to understand him. Then I met another person who not only saved me but also told me about the profession of cursebreaker.”

Juexing’s gaze held quiet skepticism. Since she already knew, further explanation seemed pointless. He stayed silent.

“This is a complicated world, full of things that shouldn’t exist. Your presence proves that…”

“What exactly are you trying to say?” he asked at last.

“I thought you’d gone mute. I just want to understand you—your life, your work, the joys and sorrows you keep locked away.”

“Are all psychologists this tedious?”

“How is that tedious? Are you planning to spend the rest of your life hiding because of what happened with Miyazaki Li Mei?”

The name landed like a slap. Juexing’s cheek seemed to burn. He turned his eyes to the fire and said nothing.

“Trying to look cool? You’re not even interesting when you’re angry.”

“Li Mei was a good girl,” he said quietly. “She sacrificed herself so I could finish what needed to be done…” He did not know why he was telling her this. Perhaps the words had been locked inside too long. Or perhaps, as Little Bell had said, Orange truly resembled her.

Orange sensed the opening and pressed carefully. “Were the two of you the kind of couple who loved each other desperately?”

“No. We were simply… partners who looked forward to a better life together.”

“Liar. Didn’t you want to marry her?”

“Marriage was something for much later. Now there is no chance.” A cold, brief laugh escaped him.

“If you could go back, would you make the same choice?”

“Yes. I couldn’t put my own feelings above the entire town.” He closed his eyes. A single clear tear slipped down.

Orange’s heart ached. She should not have forced the memory open.

“I’m sorry.”

Juexing shook his head, stood, took off his jacket, and draped it over her shoulders from behind. “Don’t catch cold.”

She caught his wrist. “No matter what happens, I believe in you. And I’ll keep supporting you.”

He stood beside her and gave a small smile, looking up as a meteor streaked across the sky. Orange pressed her palms together and closed her eyes to make a wish.

“Old people say a wish made the moment you see a shooting star is the most powerful,” she said, opening her eyes with bright hope.

“What did you wish for?”

“I’m not telling. Otherwise it won’t come true.” She stuck out her tongue.

“As if I wanted to know.” He gave a dismissive snort and started back toward his stone.

Orange jumped up and followed. “You clearly want to know. Pretending to be cool is just hypocrisy!”

“Which eye of yours saw that I want to know?”

“Both eyes. And every star in the sky can testify.”

“Should we count the monkeys in the zoo while we’re at it?”

They traded lines until both of them were laughing—the first natural, unguarded laughter either had shown in days.

“Orange—there are eyes floating behind you!” Juexing pointed at several drifting points of light.

She spun around too fast, stumbled, and fell against him. He caught her arms and playfully pushed her toward the lights. She yelped, “That’s terrifying! Why would you put me in front—!”

“You’re a goddess. Those little fireflies should be scared of *you*.”

“Fireflies?” She looked again. They really were only fireflies, dancing in the dark—nothing like eyes.

She wrenched free and stamped hard on his foot. Juexing hissed in pain. “Women really are the most venomous—”

While he bent to rub his toes she pinched his ear. “That’s what you get for bullying me! Punishment: turn into a pig’s head!” Her laughter rang out, bright and familiar—so much like Li Mei’s that it almost hurt.

“Still hurt?” she asked a moment later, sitting beside him again.

Juexing only sighed and returned to silence. Orange yawned, rested her head lightly against his arm, and drifted into sleep.

### Character Notes

**Luo Juexing**  
For the first time he speaks the name Miyazaki Li Mei aloud and admits the choice that cost her life. The resemblance Orange bears to her unsettles him deeply. Under the mountain night he allows a rare moment of warmth—and a single tear.

**Qingmu Chengzi (Orange)**  
Her curiosity shifts into genuine care. By naming Li Mei she forces open a door Juexing had kept sealed. The quiet hours by the fire mark the beginning of real trust between them.

**Yu Xiaohai**  
The sole remaining student who can still speak of the cultural-festival trip. His terror is honest, yet he provides the crucial link: Dumei Village and Du Xiaoxiao’s invitation.

**Huang Jiaye**  
Practical as ever—driving, protecting, and quietly giving the others space. His presence keeps the operation officially sanctioned.

**Miyazaki Li Mei**  
A name from the past. She sacrificed herself so Juexing could protect an entire town. Her memory still shapes every decision he makes, and Orange’s resemblance to her is no longer easy to ignore.

**Dumei Village**  
The origin point of the Medusa photograph and the students’ fatal outing. The mountain road itself already resists their approach.
