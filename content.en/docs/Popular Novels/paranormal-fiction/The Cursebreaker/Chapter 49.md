Several hours later, when the laughter and applause had finally cooled, Yu Xun quietly pulled Orange aside and took five ticket-like slips from her bag.

“What are these?” Orange asked.

“Free admission tickets to the Shibuya Art Gallery in Japan. I thought we could all go play first, then come back and hold the wedding together. What do you think?”

“Both couples getting married at the same time?”

“Yes. Once the holiday is over, we come back and get married together.”

Orange nodded, then stole another glance at the ring on her finger—the very thing she had longed for day and night, now resting solidly before her eyes.

“The departure is in three days. We’ll go to the airport together. I originally wanted to invite Shang Xuan as well, but I couldn’t find him, so it’ll just be the four of us.”

“We really should relax properly. After such a long stretch of strange journeys, our exhausted nerves need a chance to unwind.” Orange smiled.

“When the time comes you’ll have to play host—food and lodging are on you!”

“Of course. Leave it to me. I’ll show you how to play Shibuya properly!” The two women exchanged a smile that looked exactly like that of deep sisterly affection.

Jiaye had drunk a little too much and was now thoroughly drunk. Juexing supported him into the car; in that condition he certainly could not drive. Yu Xun waved to them and drove away from the Star City Grand Hotel.

After watching their car disappear, Juexing turned around only to receive a deep, heartfelt hug from Orange. The two of them held each other tightly under the moonlight while scattered groups of people occasionally cast envious and congratulatory glances their way.

“Thank you, Luo Juexing.” Orange’s emotions still had not settled from beginning to end.

Juexing patted her back and said with quiet meaning, “This is a gift from the Matchmaker God. I will cherish it well.”

They walked home hand in hand, hearts full of joy. Behind them two long shadows stretched out, slowly merging into a single graceful silhouette that flickered and stirred a sudden gust of wind. The wind lifted a poster; as it fell, a close-up of an oil painting could be seen—a tower radiating an intoxicating magic, a black six-petaled flower blooming at its peak, growing larger and darker until it swallowed the entire tower and vanished into the boundless night…

Three days later the four of them arrived at the airport on time. Yu Xun took their passports and went to the counter to complete the formalities while Juexing, Orange and Jiaye sat in the waiting hall. She finished everything quickly and returned in high spirits to sit beside Jiaye.

“I’ve never even been abroad before!” Juexing sighed.

“Lucky you—going abroad and meeting the future in-laws at the same time. Two birds with one stone.” Jiaye teased.

“True. But I wonder whether Orange’s parents speak Chinese?” Yu Xun said.

“They speak a little. I’m happy to interpret, though. Some simple Japanese you should be able to understand.”

“Once we get to Japan we really have to walk around everywhere—especially to see what the beautiful girls look like!” Jiaye’s mouth curved with a slightly mischievous expression.

Yu Xun pinched him and laughed. “Men are always up to no good. Orange, keep a close eye on him.”

“Jiaye and I are not on the same path. I’m very self-aware.” Juexing hurried to defend himself.

“You little traitor!” Jiaye glared at him.

The announcement system began broadcasting: the flight from the capital to Shibuya, Japan, would depart in ten minutes. Passengers were requested to prepare for boarding.

Each of them picked up their luggage, presented their tickets at the gate, and boarded the plane bound for Shibuya one after another.

The sensation of weaving through layers of cloud was genuinely pleasant. That lofty, commanding feeling was not something ordinary people experienced every day. The four of them sat facing one another—some reading newspapers, some listening to music, some chatting. They had no intention of wasting the short forty-minute journey through the sky.

Orange felt a little sleepy, so she put down the newspaper, rested her head lightly on Juexing’s shoulder, and closed her eyes for a while. Juexing smiled at her and continued listening to a tender love song.

She did not know how long the plane had been flying, nor how long she had slept—only that she had a brief dream that felt like an omen and jolted her awake.

“What’s wrong?” Juexing asked.

“Where are we?”

“About ten minutes from the airport. Did you have a nightmare?”

“Maybe I’m just nervous. It’s nothing.” Orange let out a breath and took his right hand in both of hers. Juexing could feel the slightly frantic beat of her heart and the coldness of her skin.

He smiled. “It’s fine. I’m not even nervous. What’s there to be afraid of? Meeting the parents isn’t something shameful.”

Orange smiled and nodded. Five minutes later the four of them left the plane, climbed into a taxi, and headed first toward Orange’s home in Shibuya.

She asked the driver to pass through several of the busier streets so she could introduce the important sights one by one. Shibuya is one of Tokyo’s special wards. Because of its extreme prosperity it is listed, together with Shinjuku, as one of the “streets that never sleep for twenty-four hours.”

Besides the forest of high-rises, famous department stores and fashion boutiques were everywhere. Dining, coffee shops, amusement facilities—all of it lifted the entire atmosphere of Shibuya to a higher level: intricate, colorful, dazzling.

The taxi stopped in front of a two-story house. In the courtyard a little girl was playing; a middle-aged man sat in a lounge chair beside her, responding to the child while reading a newspaper.

Orange paid the fare, led the three of them out of the car, and pushed open the wooden gate twined with ivy. The little girl looked at them with wide, surprised eyes.

“Onee-chan! Papa, Onee-chan is back!” she called in Japanese, not even bothering to brush the sand from her hands before running to Orange.

Orange answered “Tadaima!” Only then did the middle-aged man rise from the lounge chair, put down his newspaper, and break into a broad smile. He warmly guided them into the house.

Orange’s mother—later they learned her name was Mizusawa Kaori—came out of the bedroom. The moment she saw the three Chinese friends her daughter had brought, she was so delighted she could hardly close her mouth. Before Juexing and the others had even finished introducing themselves she had already plunged into the kitchen.

Juexing could only introduce himself in rather simple Japanese. Yu Xun, a journalist well-versed in English, Japanese and Korean, naturally chatted with them with ease. Jiaye was left staring blankly at the rapid exchange. Orange’s little sister, watching Jiaye’s comical expression, latched onto him firmly and occasionally made faces at him. The two of them bickering made everyone else laugh until they nearly fell over.

“What’s your name?” Jiaye asked, unwilling to remain silent.

“I’m Qingmu Hyoki!” she answered in Chinese, though the pronunciation was not perfectly standard.

Orange smiled and patted Hyoki’s head. “Go see what Mom is preparing.”

Hyoki nodded, ran over to Jiaye, pinched his nose, then dashed into the kitchen. Jiaye stuck his tongue out at her; when she looked back she quickly covered her eyes with both hands. Mizusawa Kaori came out carrying freshly cut fruit and said with a smile, “You’ve all had a long journey. Have some fruit first. I’ll prepare a proper meal for everyone in a little while.”

Orange took the fruit and translated, “Mom says to eat some fruit first, then we’ll have lunch together.”

Juexing and Jiaye nodded politely in unison and said “Arigatou gozaimasu” in Japanese. Mizusawa Kaori and Orange’s father, Qingmu Rimu, exchanged a smile.

While lunch was being prepared, Orange stayed in the kitchen to help her mother. Yu Xun and Juexing followed Qingmu Rimu to the back garden to look at the vegetables and fruit the family grew themselves. Jiaye remained in the front yard building sand sculptures with Hyoki. Three locations, three different scenes—together they formed a complete picture of a happy family.

Mizusawa Kaori glanced out the window at Juexing and asked, “How long have you known him?”

Orange’s hands were in the water, carefully washing coriander. A faint blush rose in her shallow dimples. After a moment she answered, “A little over half a year…”

“Only half a year and he already bought you such an expensive ring?”

Only then did Orange remember she had forgotten to put away the dazzling diamond ring.

“When a child grows up and finds a good place to belong, a mother is of course happy. And these friends of yours all look very fine—one a journalist, one a police officer, one a teacher.”

“So Mom agrees that I should keep seeing Juexing?”

“A woman’s life, no matter how hard she works, eventually leads to marriage. As long as you yourself feel it’s right and good, your father and I have no reason to oppose it.” Mizusawa Kaori smiled.

Orange nodded and glanced out the window at Juexing. He was squatting beside her father examining sweet potatoes, his focused expression looking exactly like a member of an inspection team.

“He’s very good to me. We’ve been through emotional low points together. I didn’t give up, and neither did he…”

Mizusawa Kaori set down the knife, turned, and looked at Orange with earnest seriousness. “Shall I test him a little this evening?”

“Test him?” Orange was startled.

“Heh. Do you really understand him? Once Mom takes the field I’ll know whether he’s a reliable man.” She spoke with full confidence.

Orange’s expression was helpless, yet she still nodded in silent agreement. Mizusawa Kaori laughed. “No need to be so tense. My daughter is so capable—she needs an even more capable man to keep her in check.”

Orange smiled. “Hmph. He still isn’t qualified to compete with me!”

“Look at you—already acting like the head of the household before you’re even married. Truly domineering!” her mother teased.

The conversation slipped into whispered plotting centered on lunch. Soon the clock struck twelve. Seven people sat cross-legged around a large square table. The array of Japanese dishes made Jiaye and Juexing’s mouths water. On their first trip abroad they were already eating food more satisfying than any restaurant Japanese cuisine.

### Character Notes

**Qingmu Chengzi (Orange)**  
Returns home to Shibuya with her fiancé and friends. She acts as host and interpreter while quietly receiving her mother’s approval—and the promise of a parental “test” for Juexing.

**Luo Juexing**  
Experiences his first trip abroad and his first meeting with Orange’s family. His focused, respectful manner already leaves a good impression on her parents.

**Huang Jiaye & Meng Yu Xun**  
Join the journey as the other engaged couple. Jiaye provides comic relief with the little sister; Yu Xun handles language and logistics with professional ease.

**Qingmu Rimu & Mizusawa Kaori**  
Orange’s parents. Warm, welcoming, and quietly assessing the young man their daughter has brought home.

**Qingmu Hyoki**  
Orange’s energetic little sister. Instantly latches onto Jiaye, pinches noses, and answers in imperfect Chinese, becoming the source of much laughter.
