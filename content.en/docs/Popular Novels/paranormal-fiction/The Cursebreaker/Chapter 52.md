The midday wedding banquet would probably remain the most unforgettable of Dou Dou and Miyuki’s lives. Forced by circumstances, Dou Dou—who had never been a strong drinker—could only grit his teeth and down cup after cup, because as a man he could not allow the teetotal Miyuki to go against her own wishes.

Men often feel the need to show strength; perhaps that is how latent potential is sometimes awakened. In real life, however, it is still safer to keep both feet on the ground and not blindly chase the unreachable extremes of films or novels.

Completely drunk, Dou Dou was helped into the wedding car by Miyuki. The driver set off toward their new home. After the couple left, the other guests gradually departed as well. That day a total of thirty million yen in charity funds had been raised. Even Juexing and Orange jointly purchased an oil painting worth one hundred thousand yen, intending to give it to Orange’s father as a birthday present—Qingmu Rimu’s birthday was only half a month away. Thus they performed a good deed and obtained a gift at the same time. Yu Xun and Jiaye were less interested in oil paintings, so they simply donated ten thousand yen.

Because both Jiaye and Juexing had been drinking, they decided not to continue shopping and returned home directly. Mizusawa Kaori, knowing they would be back around this time, had prepared a large chilled watermelon. Little Hyoki was already waiting impatiently beside it, staring without blinking and imagining the bright-red flesh that was about to be revealed.

After an afternoon of lively activity everyone needed a moment of quiet. Jiaye was somewhat tired and returned to the guest room for a short rest. Juexing and Orange took the newly purchased painting to a specialty shop for framing, so they arrived home a little later than the others.

The art gallery’s exhibition would last three days. The most eye-catching pagoda painting continued to draw the largest number of viewers. Yet if one looked carefully, the pagoda had already risen another level. Would the entire tower stand fully upright on the horizon by nightfall without anyone noticing?

Orange examined the still-packaged painting inside the house. Without warning a picture forced itself into her thoughts—a picture that immediately recalled the pagoda oil painting.

She asked Juexing, who was reading a magazine nearby, “Did Dou Dou mention the name of that painting?”

“I think it’s called the Soul-Binding Pagoda…”

“Oh…” she answered thoughtfully.

Juexing put down the magazine, puzzled by the sudden question.

“It’s nothing. I just feel there’s something wrong with that Soul-Binding Pagoda.” Even she could not fully explain the subjective judgment she had formed about the painting.

“Now that you mention it, I recall a few details as well.”

“What details? Did you see something too?” She turned to face him.

“Yes. I saw the pagoda actually rise one more level…”  

The moment the words left his mouth Orange’s heart skipped. So she had not been seeing things after all.

“I just remembered the dream I had on the plane.”

“You mean the dream about the oil painting?”

“Yes. I was a little tired, then I found myself wandering alone on a road. Next I saw the art gallery holding an exhibition of oil paintings. I didn’t want to go in, but my feet refused to obey and I pushed into the crowd. After I looked at one particular painting the entire hall plunged into darkness. Then I heard screams from many different throats… and I woke up.” Even now the memory of that nightmare still left her uneasy.

Juexing did not believe it was merely a nightmare. It was very likely a premonition. Therefore he decided to return to the art gallery that night and examine the strange painting once more.

He gave Orange a simple reason. Although she found it somewhat forced, she still agreed to let him go out alone for a while, because he promised not to stay long.

He took a taxi, handed the driver a slip of paper written in Japanese, and the driver nodded and headed toward the gallery.

Nighttime Shibuya was indeed livelier than the capital. How could it not be, given its prosperity—an artwork one never grew tired of looking at, standing proudly on this land. The closer they came to the gallery, the thinner the crowds became. Perhaps the majority of people remained quite ordinary: as long as material needs were satisfied, there was little necessity to cultivate artistic cells.

The taxi stopped. Juexing stepped out and glanced at the scattered people leaving the gallery, most of them carrying a trace of satisfaction.

He checked the time—already eight o’clock. At this hour the gallery should not be too crowded. With that thought he made a more comprehensive survey of the building’s exterior. As he looked, a strange illusion gradually took hold of him: under the night sky the structure itself seemed to have become a vivid, living pagoda.

He shook his head, closed his eyes to cool his thoughts for a moment, and when he opened them again everything had returned to normal.

He walked slowly, step by careful step, into the gallery. No one remained inside viewing the paintings. By rights the security staff should already be preparing to close, yet the vast hall contained not a single human figure. The fact made Juexing suspicious.

The Soul-Binding Pagoda painting that had stood in the center was gone; only the empty easel remained. He assumed the work was too valuable and had been put away early.

He circled the main hall once. Just as he was about to abandon the idea of examining the painting again, the sound of a door opening came from a corridor behind him. In the silent air the noise carried an irresistible pull. He turned toward the somewhat dark corridor. A faint light leaked from the crack of one of the doors—by its quality it should be the beam of a flashlight.

“Could there be a thief?” he muttered to himself and moved quietly toward the room. When he drew close he looked up and saw three characters clearly written on the door: Collection Room.

The door was slightly ajar. Through the crack he peered inside. A figure holding a flashlight stood in front of an oil painting, eyes circling the content of the canvas.

From bottom to top Juexing’s gaze settled on the half-blond hair of the person. No wonder the back had felt so familiar—it was Shang Xuan.

A small surge of excitement made him careless; his hand brushed the door. The soft *bang* immediately drew Shang Xuan’s attention.

Juexing quickly closed the door, then called out softly. Only then did the figure in the darkness switch the flashlight on again.

“What are you doing here?” Juexing asked.

Shang Xuan looked the same as ever, yet the tight furrow of his brow added a further layer of mystery.

He glanced at Juexing, the corner of his mouth lifting slightly, then returned his attention to the painting. Following the beam of the flashlight, Juexing realized the work Shang Xuan was examining was none other than the Soul-Binding Pagoda.

“You came because of the painting as well?”

“Have you noticed anything wrong with it?” Shang Xuan asked while continuing his careful observation.

Juexing rested his chin in his hand and studied the painting once more. When his gaze reached the top of the tower, a black speck that looked like a flower bud caught his attention. He then recounted the levels of the Soul-Binding Pagoda. There were three more than before. If he had not miscounted, the tower should have ten levels in total; it had already risen to the ninth.

Shang Xuan noticed the concentration of his gaze. “You saw this painting this morning, didn’t you? There must be something different.”

Juexing stretched out a hand and touched the black speck at the summit. Before contact it had been still; the moment his finger brushed it the speck seemed to move, growing six slender points—more precisely, a flower bud about to open, putting forth six petals.

“It looks like a flower,” Shang Xuan said.

“Yes. This morning there was no flower at the top, and only half the tower stood above the ground. Now it has become nine levels.” Juexing stated the anomalies aloud.

“Doesn’t it resemble some kind of countdown?” Shang Xuan seemed to have grasped something, yet could not express it clearly for the moment.

“Countdown?” Juexing murmured. Both fell silent. At the exact instant the surrounding quiet returned, a scream rang out behind them. They turned simultaneously. The door had somehow opened.

“Did you hear a scream?” Shang Xuan asked, wanting to confirm he was not hearing things.

“Yes. I heard it too.”

They exchanged a glance and walked out of the collection room on silent feet. The corridor outside remained empty of people. Even stranger, the visitors beyond the gallery entrance seemed completely unaware of any abnormality inside.

While they were still puzzled, a continuous *sha-sha* sound once more drew their attention. Almost at the same moment they looked toward the dark end of the corridor. A black shadow flicked like a rope.

“What is that?” Juexing asked.

Shang Xuan shone the flashlight. On the wall at the far end of the corridor a faint greenish trace remained, wave-shaped, disappearing around the corner.

“Let’s take a look,” Shang Xuan proposed.

One behind the other, both highly alert, they approached the wall. The pale-green mark was interspersed with tiny red spots. Instinct told them the red was blood.

Juexing touched the green trace with a fingertip. Instantly a fluorescence unique to plants flashed across his skin, and when he sniffed he confirmed the scent of vegetation.

“Look at the floor!” Shang Xuan directed the flashlight downward. Irregular pools of blood spread toward a door not far away. The threshold of that door had been chiselled into an opening; above the damaged section—the lower half of the door itself—was covered in cracks, as though the slightest touch would cause it to shatter.

Both men were stunned by the scene. Information tumbled through their minds, yet not a single clue matched.

Shang Xuan was about to step forward when Juexing instinctively pulled him back. With a sharp *bang* the door was smashed open by a half-visible human figure. A man covered in blood struggled to crawl outward, continuously crying “Help me… help me…”

They stared in a daze at his dying struggles. Before either could react, something behind the man yanked him back inside. Only the fading cries for help continued to echo in the corridor. Juexing and Shang Xuan ran to the doorway. Beyond it lay a tunnel of unfathomable depth. Where had the tunnel come from, and where did it lead?
