That entire noon Shang Xuan and Wan Ting stayed home. After lunch she told him everything that had happened to her, from beginning to end. From that moment on Shang Xuan felt an even deeper tenderness toward the woman in front of him. He had already considered abandoning his own identity just to stay with her for the rest of their lives. He had also considered going to Juexing to confess and ask him not to interfere. What he could not yet imagine was that someone else would never allow them such an easy ending.

“Claustrophobia?” Juexing stared at the white-bearded Dr. Li in surprise.

“Simply put, it is an anxiety disorder triggered by enclosed spaces—especially elevators, train carriages, or airplane cabins,” the doctor explained.

“So her death could have been caused by the claustrophobia itself?”

“It is possible. The old case still has no identified killer.”

Several hypotheses circled in Juexing’s mind, yet the one that convinced him most was the same: behind the inheritance lay another secret, and that hidden truth was very likely the real cause of her death.

“You are a friend of Granny Luo Shen, aren’t you?” Dr. Li asked.

“Yes. She sent me to ask you a few questions. Thank you for telling me.” Juexing bowed slightly and was about to leave when the doctor called after him.

“Everything is karma. You should help your friend. Do not let him remain lost in delusion.”

Juexing nodded, smiled, and left the hospital. The sky was blue, yet a dull gray lingered beneath the surface. Perhaps her appearance had already altered the color of the heavens. To restore it to what it should be, the curse would have to be broken.

An anonymous letter was dropped into the mailbox of a duplex apartment. The dusty green box had not received a letter in three years. For reasons the owner could not explain, she took the letter out without thinking.

*Click, click, click.* The sound of high heels. A woman in office attire and black pumps, chewing gum, walked into her own front door.

She hung up her bag and sank into the sofa. The cushions were soft; the moment she sat down a wave of comfort washed over her. Anyone watching would have seen how thoroughly she enjoyed it.

She turned the envelope over and over. Aside from the four characters “Must Read” written on the front, there was no other mark.

She gave a cold laugh. Just as she was about to tear open the flap she stopped, as if suddenly worried. After a moment’s hesitation she opened it anyway and pulled out the photograph.

It was simple: a man and a woman kissing. Pictures like this could be grabbed anywhere on the street. She was about to toss it into the trash under the label of “prank” when the woman’s earring caught her attention.

She stared at the earring for a long time. A pair of pink crystal butterflies. The camera was a little distant, yet she knew those earrings better than anything—she had looked at them for nearly three full years. How could she possibly forget?

“Impossible…” She glanced at the date printed on the photo. It was from this very month. When her eyes moved to the woman’s profile, she suddenly flung the photograph to the floor. The face on the paper slowly turned, and the right eye fixed her with a strange, piercing stare.

“Hallucination. It has to be a hallucination!” She closed her eyes for five full seconds. The moment she opened them the telephone on the table rang.

She looked at the photograph still lying on the floor. It looked exactly as it had before. Steadying herself, she answered the call.

“Darling Zhuzhu, I forgot my keys. Come open the door for me.” It was her husband’s voice. Usually that meant he was still in the car and not yet at the entrance.

She quickly picked up the photograph. After thinking it over she decided not to throw it away. She still could not make sense of what she had seen; better to wait until he returned and discuss what to do.

The doorbell rang twice. She opened the door. He gave her the habitual hug, stepped inside, closed the door, changed into slippers, and only then followed her into the living room.

“Shengnan, I need to talk to you…” Zhuzhu said.

Lu Shengnan glanced sideways at her and gradually sensed an abnormal atmosphere filling the house.

“What is it?”

Zhuzhu handed him the photograph with trembling hands. Shengnan glanced at it and laughed. “It’s not April Fool’s Day. Who’s playing tricks?”

The words were barely out of his mouth when the woman in the photograph turned her face again—this time almost looking straight at both of them with both eyes.

“Ah!” Shengnan flung the photograph away. Both of them stumbled backward several steps.

“What the hell is going on?” Shengnan swallowed hard.

“When I got off work just now I found this letter. The woman in the photo is…” Zhuzhu could not finish the sentence. Her heart pounded so hard it hurt.

“Impossible. Right now she can only be lying in the public cemetery. She can’t…” Beads of sweat slid down Shengnan’s forehead. He shivered mid-sentence. Both of them stared in the direction of the photograph. A sudden gust of wind rose, lifted the picture gently, and carried it higher and farther until it disappeared completely from their sight.

“I’m calling Shubin. He has to come over. Whether she’s still alive or not, we need to get to the bottom of this.” Shengnan’s hands shook as he dialed the number of Tian Shubin, the lawyer who had once represented Lu Zhengyao.

Zhuzhu rested a hand on his shoulder and said softly, “I’ll go cut some fruit.”

In the same city four different groups of people, each carrying their own intentions, were circling the same question. Only one pair continued to hold to their private agreement even as the crisis around them threatened to explode.

As the sun set, Orange walked toward Juexing’s apartment carrying a basket of fruit, her heart light. Passing the seaside park she noticed someone sitting on the swing set—a familiar back with half-blond hair. It was Shang Xuan.

“Hey, in such a good mood you’re out here swinging?” she said, sneaking up behind him and giving him a light pat.

“Oh, it’s you. Looking for Juexing?” He glanced at the fruit in her hands.

“Yes. Here—this guava is for you. I bought it at the supermarket when I got off the train. Very fresh.” She held out a large, green guava.

The scene was witnessed by Wan Ting, who was returning with milk tea she had just bought.

“A friend of yours?” Wan Ting appeared beside them without a sound, startling Orange.

Orange recovered after a second, bowed slightly, and smiled. “Hello.”

“Let me introduce you. This is my good friend, Qingmu Chengzi. And this is my girlfriend, Wanting.” Shang Xuan rested a hand on Wan Ting’s shoulder. In the last light of the setting sun the two of them looked especially sweet.

Orange’s smile grew brighter. “Congratulations. I told you there are plenty of other girls in the world. See? This is much better.” She handed another guava to Wan Ting.

“Nice to meet you. Come over to our place sometime.” Wan Ting accepted the fruit with a smile and shook Orange’s hand.

The instant their palms touched, a wave of icy cold shot through Orange’s entire body. It felt exactly like shaking hands with a sculpture carved from ice.

Shang Xuan smiled and drew Orange a few steps aside. “I’m leaving Juexing in your care. As for my own affairs, I hope he won’t interfere.”

Orange looked at his serious expression, a little lost, but still nodded. “Don’t worry. Just remember to invite us to the wedding banquet.”

She waved to Wan Ting. “I won’t interrupt your time together. I’m heading off. Come over and play when you’re free.”

Wan Ting waved back. The two of them watched Orange leave the swing area of the seaside park. Before she was completely out of sight Orange stole one more glance at Wan Ting. The crystal butterfly earring on her earlobe seemed almost alive, radiating a quiet, intoxicating magic.

Orange unlocked the door to Juexing’s apartment, hoping to surprise him. The moment she entered the living room she found him sprawled awkwardly across the sofa, fast asleep in a completely undignified posture. Papers were scattered across his body, the table, and the floor. Fortunately the windows were closed; otherwise a single breeze would have made the mess even worse.

“Honestly… if you’re tired, go sleep in the bedroom!” she muttered. She set the fruit on the dining table and began picking up the scattered pages one by one. By chance her eyes fell on an A4 sheet with the three characters “Lu Wanting” written on it. She picked it up and examined it.

Juexing sneezed, turned his head, and saw her standing there. “Huh? What are you doing here? It’s not Sunday.”

“Look at this mess. Go sleep in the bedroom. I’ll tidy up for you.” She tried to help him up, but he waved her off, shook himself fully awake, and joined her in gathering the documents.

The atmosphere remained slightly awkward. Orange chewed an apple while watching the silent Juexing. He could sense she had something she wanted to say.

“You ran into them, didn’t you?” he asked.

“Yes. Seeing him like that makes me happy. Why? Are you unhappy?”

“I don’t know whether I should be happy. If the investigation turns out badly, what’s there to be happy about?” His low voice seemed to pull the night closer. In the blink of an eye the setting sun had vanished, replaced by a half-round moon.

“What exactly is going on? That girl Wanting… the first moment I saw her I felt something was wrong.” After everything she had experienced, Orange trusted her intuition more and more.

“Behind this story is an inheritance dispute. We may need Jiaye’s help.” Juexing had spent the entire afternoon analyzing the materials and was beginning to see a clearer thread. Yet every hypothesis still needed to be verified one by one; otherwise everything would remain empty talk.
