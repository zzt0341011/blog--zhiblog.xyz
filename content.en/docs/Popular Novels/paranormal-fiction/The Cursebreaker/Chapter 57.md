That year in France, at the height of the industrial boom, a story was recorded around the Soul-Binding Tower—a legend of nature’s punishment upon mankind.

It was a rich and peaceful village. Scattered houses dotted the landscape like stars, loosely yet firmly encircling a single tower. The tower had already stood there when the villagers first arrived. No one knew who had built it or how old it was. They only knew it was a mysterious pagoda that protected the village with fair weather and good harvests.

The villagers treated the tower with deep care. Every month someone was assigned to repair and maintain it. Later, as the village prospered, the government decided to invest heavily in industry. They bought the entire tract of land at great cost. The villagers moved away. Construction crews arrived. Even the seemingly ordinary tower was demolished according to the plan. Factories and concrete soon replaced the natural landscape.

A painter who had long dreamed of seeing the tower arrived too late. Where the pagoda once stood he found only a construction site piled with cement.

Strangely, on the very night he decided to abandon the project, he dreamed of the tower. When he woke he ran outside and looked toward the old site. The tower had reappeared—only half of it, the other half sunk into the earth. He immediately took paper and brush and sketched the half-tower under a full moon. By the time he finished, the moon in the painting had become a black six-petaled flower.

After the factories were completed, they appeared to engage in ordinary industry. In truth a secret laboratory had been built underground to research biological and chemical weapons. Massive amounts of toxic waste were dumped deeper into the earth, seeping until they collected directly beneath the site of the old tower. There a terrifying plant began to sprout.

One year later, just before dawn, a green light flashed across the factory. Every person inside vanished. Their bodies were never found. The developers, sensing something wrong, abandoned the site and fled with the remaining funds. The painter returned home and locked away the painting of the tower. From then on strange events continued. People disappeared without reason. A city of tens of thousands was struck by an unprecedented catastrophe. Only the painter’s family of three survived.

After the painter’s death the oil painting was buried with him. Over time people gradually forgot the disaster, as if it had never happened. They never knew that the vanished innocents lay buried beneath the tower, their white bones stacked as punishment for mankind’s destruction of the natural world.

Juexing and Orange were rapidly losing consciousness. Shang Xuan raised his axe to free them, but an invisible hand seized his wrist. A voice, fragmented and distant, spoke:

“One of you must remain.”

“I will stay. Let them go.” Shang Xuan swung the axe, cutting the vines one by one. Juexing and Orange fell to the ground, still unconscious.

Shang Xuan took a lighter from his pocket.

“Whatever punishment you seek, you have no right to treat human lives as nothing.”

“When you people hung sheep’s heads while selling dog meat, you should have expected retribution. I merely moved a few fingers.”

“Even if you punish everyone, history is already fixed. There is no second chance. Why stain yourself with so much blood?”

“You want to negotiate with me? When they buried radioactive waste in my bones, who came to save me?”

“I am not negotiating. I only want to tell you this: we are not as fragile as you think.”

Flame bloomed in Shang Xuan’s hand. The wavering light immediately agitated the gasoline vapor around them. Just as he prepared to drop the flame, several vines shot up from the ground, binding his hands and feet. The lighter slipped from his fingers, extinguished, and fell into the gasoline with a soft splash.

“Heh. Naive child. Let me show you what true punishment looks like.”

Juexing regained consciousness. His hand was still locked with Orange’s. He quickly lifted her into his arms and saw Shang Xuan bound by the vines.

“I’m coming to save you!” He set Orange down, picked up the axe, and was about to strike when Shang Xuan shouted:

“Go! If you stay, all of you will die here.”

“I can’t leave you!”

“Get Orange out first. We’ll talk later.” Shang Xuan urged him, knowing Orange could not last much longer in the foul air.

Above ground, Jiaye hurled the useless lighter to the ground and crouched, clutching his hair, nearly driven mad by the broken device. Vines had gathered thickly at the tunnel mouth, staring at him with predatory intent. Yuxun sensed the danger and jumped from the car, but another figure moved faster, dragging Jiaye back.

A single spark glowed on the tip of a cigarette. She took a slow drag; the red ember brightened.

As the vines lunged she flicked the cigarette. The spinning red spark landed at the tunnel mouth, rode the flowing gasoline downstream, and within moments the flames roared out of control, igniting everything the gasoline had touched. Jiaye and Yuxun leapt clear of the exhibition hall. Towering fire filled their vision like a vast crimson screen.

The flames poured downward without mercy, turning everything to ash. Shang Xuan felt the rising heat. Juexing looked up and saw the entrance completely sealed by fire. He had no choice but to climb back the way they had come.

“I never expected you would choose to die for those ignorant people.”

Shang Xuan closed his eyes. The vines wrapping him tightened into a cocoon. Juexing, still holding Orange, pressed them both into a recessed rock wall and shielded her with his own body as the firestorm swept past. The gasoline below ignited completely. The arrogant green vine was slowly reduced to flying ash.

A long, sky-rending cry painted an indelible mark across the gray heavens. That fire burned away every secret, every unspoken grievance.

In DouDou’s arms the painting of the tower suddenly caught flame. Fire curled across the paper and swallowed the entire pagoda—including the black six-petaled flower at its peak.

Jiaye, Yuxun, and Little Bell stared in the same direction. The fire burned for four full hours, from daylight into evening. When it finally died, the world returned to normal, as if the demonic green plant had never existed. Only the exhibition hall remained a blackened ruin, still releasing heat.

“Report! An unexplained fire has completely destroyed the monster!” a guard shouted excitedly.

The police chief raised his binoculars. The tower was gone, and with it the killing vines. He wiped tears from the corners of his eyes. A deep sense of relief settled over him. He wanted to thank the people who had fought the demon—though they might no longer be alive—and to walk to the site and properly mourn the innocent souls.

Jiaye and the others continued to wait. They refused to leave. They believed Juexing, Orange, and Shang Xuan were still alive.

“Stop waiting. What must leave will leave. Spare yourselves a little more sorrow.”

The speaker was Granny Luoshen, pushing a small cart. A large red envelope fell from her pocket. Everyone saw the bright characters written on it:

“Granny Yu congratulates Juexing and Orange on their wedding!”

Perhaps it would remain a blessing that could never be delivered. The moment Granny Luoshen bent to pick up the envelope, two figures emerged from the burned exhibition hall. At the height of their grief, Juexing appeared, supporting Orange.

“Where is Shang Xuan?!” Little Bell rushed forward and seized Juexing’s arm so tightly that her nails dug into his skin.

“I’m sorry. I couldn’t save him…”

“Why? Why didn’t you save him? He was trying to save both of you! How could you be so heartless—” She shoved him away. The force sent both Juexing and Orange to the ground.

Orange coughed and finally regained full consciousness. Juexing, however, remained face-down on the earth and slowly closed his eyes.

“Let all the misfortune fall on me alone…”

“Why? Why didn’t you save him…”

“It was a punishment—the deepest confrontation between nature and mankind…”

“Is it over? No… The fire only destroyed its body. Its will is branded into this land and will keep watching forever.”

Half a month later everything returned to normal. The streets were lively again, as if the disaster had never happened—only a brief, passing illusion.

Today was Qingmu Liwu’s birthday. Gathered around the cake were Orange, Jiaye, Yuxun, Granny Luoshen, Shuize Xin, and Bingxi. Laughter filled the room as they sang. The moment the candles were blown out, Orange began to cry. One more person should have been present at this birthday. Because of a single careless moment, that person would never wake again.

“Don’t cry. Today is your father’s birthday.” Yuxun patted her shoulder. She could no longer hold back her own tears.

After that unforgettable birthday, Orange bid farewell to her parents and younger sister. She prepared to follow Yuxun back to the capital and continue the beautiful task they had left unfinished. Though the task was no longer perfect, it still needed to be made real.

### Character Notes

**Shang Xuan**  
Chose to remain. His final words and actions turn him from enigmatic outsider into the one who paid the price for everyone else’s survival. The book he carried and the choice he made will echo long after the fire dies.

**Luo Juexing**  
Survives the inferno by shielding Orange with his own body, yet collapses the moment they reach safety. The cost of the victory is written on his face and in the silence that follows.

**Qingmu Chengzi (Orange)**  
Walks out of the flames only to watch Juexing fall. The birthday that should have been whole becomes the moment her tears finally break free.

**Huang Jiaye & Yuxun**  
Wait through the four-hour blaze, refusing to abandon hope. Their relief at seeing two figures emerge is immediately shattered by the absence of the third.

**Little Bell**  
Her grief is raw and unfiltered. The confession she made less than a month earlier now has no one left to receive it.

**The Soul-Binding Tower**  
Its true history is revealed at last: a monument of protection twisted by industrial greed into a vessel of vengeance. The fire destroys its body, but the final voice warns that its will remains embedded in the land.
