When the gallery closed for the night, two security guards made their routine inspection of the exhibition halls and painting rooms. Apart from finding the door of the collection room slightly ajar, they discovered no other abnormalities. After writing the four characters “Everything normal” in the logbook, they locked the main entrance and left one after the other.

On the wall at the far end of the corridor the greenish trace had grown even more distinct. Even from outside the gallery Juexing and Shang Xuan could clearly feel its vitality. At the same time an image was transmitted to them: a strange plant, waving its graceful branches, coiling around some object—an oil painting, for example.

“They didn’t notice the blood?” Juexing wondered aloud.

“I find this entire matter extremely strange. We need to find an opportunity to enter that tunnel and take a look.” Shang Xuan said.

“Agreed. I’ll investigate the origin of the painting thoroughly. It seems this time we will have to fight side by side.”

Shang Xuan slipped the flashlight into his pocket, gave a cold laugh, and patted Juexing on the shoulder. “It won’t be that simple…”

A familiar, silvery voice sounded beside Juexing. The lively figure had changed so much that he almost failed to recognize her. Shang Xuan rested a hand on Little Bell’s shoulder and walked forward. She did not forget to greet Juexing; he nodded in return, then turned and headed toward Orange’s house.

When he passed the gallery once more it seemed to have become a vast plant kingdom under the night sky, radiating a quiet, otherworldly green. Within that green the indispensable element was cluster after cluster of small red flowers—six-petaled blossoms the color of blood…

Orange sat in the courtyard, glancing repeatedly at the clock in her hand. Juexing had been “strolling” outside for nearly three full hours; she could not help growing worried.

Just as she was about to call and ask what was happening, Juexing pushed open the ivy-covered gate. Orange sprang to her feet; the clock slipped from her fingers and fell onto the sand.

He walked over, picked it up, and said, “Sorry. Something came up, so I returned late.”

Orange said nothing. Instead she threw her arms around him. The embrace was especially warm, especially tight—so tight that Juexing found it a little hard to breathe.

“No matter how late it is, I will wait for you to come back…” Tears slid from the corners of her eyes, as though recounting the deepest fears and reluctance in her heart.

Juexing patted her back and smiled. “Don’t worry. No matter what happens, I will never leave you behind.”

They lingered under the moonlight for ten full minutes before releasing each other and turning into the house.

The next morning Jiaye and Yu Xun left early for outdoor exercise and to bring back breakfast. Juexing finished adjusting his clothes and prepared to go out. Orange stood in the doorway as though she had something to say.

“What’s wrong?” he asked.

“I don’t object to you going out, but could you please stay away from the art gallery…” Her words carried a deeper meaning that only increased Juexing’s interest in the mysterious painting.

“How did you know I intended to go to the gallery?”

“I don’t know how to explain what happened last night, but I truly ‘saw’ that scene.” She lowered her gaze; her drifting eyes circled across him in a state that was difficult to put into words.

Juexing stepped forward and drew the slightly frightened Orange into his arms. “Perhaps it is already destined that on this journey we must pass through trials before we can achieve the proper outcome. I have no reason to flee.”

“If you insist on doing so, then please let me stay by your side—whether in life or death, whether we stay or leave.”

His hand gently stroked her long hair. Never before had he felt so reluctant to part from her.

“We’ll go together and take a look. I couldn’t sleep well last night either. Something major is probably about to happen.”

The two of them left the house together. Mizusawa Kaori stood by the window watching their departing figures; a shadow of melancholy immediately settled on her face. Perhaps she felt that Orange’s return this time had been an unwise choice. Yet disaster has no sympathy; it only brings people one “surprise” and “accident” after another.

Several police cars with flashing blue-and-red lights were parked in front of a residential house. According to unreliable statements from the neighbors, the male head of the household had vanished into thin air inside his own home at midnight. The following morning, when the wife noticed something wrong, she immediately called the police.

Officers arrived at the scene at once and reviewed the local surveillance footage. The missing man had indeed entered the house and, between that moment and midnight, had not left the premises by so much as a single step. Yet nowhere inside the entire dwelling could any hiding place for him be found. Was this truly a case of “vanishing from the mortal world”?

Such an explanation was, of course, extremely absurd. A particularly careful female officer noticed something unusual about the toilet in the bathroom. She carefully lifted the lid of the water tank behind the bowl and looked inside—then recoiled several steps in terror, dropping the lid so that it shattered on the floor. The inspector who hurried over at the sound, prepared to reprimand her for negligence, was himself turned white by the human head floating in the tank.

A flurry of camera flashes followed. The inspector ordered two subordinates to examine the rest of the bathroom. They soon discovered an opening beneath the water tank. When they shone a flashlight into it they found green, branch-like tendrils tightly wrapped around the victim’s body. The tendrils appeared to possess life of their own and were still writhing—an especially nauseating sight.

“See if you can get the body out,” the inspector said, then stepped back to the bathroom doorway to give his subordinates room to work.

The two officers studied the water tank carefully. They could not understand how a full-grown living man had managed to force himself into a pipe too narrow even for an arm.

“Why don’t we just smash it open and drag the whole thing out?” one suggested.

The other fetched a large hammer. He had just raised it to carry out the “humane destruction” when a jet of bloody water sprayed from the tank and struck him full in the face with a wet *pu*.

He spat, wiped the liquid away, and raised the hammer again. Another spray of blood. The sequence repeated three times until he was thoroughly enraged. He handed the hammer to his companion and said, “You do it.” The second officer took the hammer timidly. Before he could even lift it, a green tendril grew out of the mouth of the floating head. In full view of both men the tendril yanked the head downward, leaving only swirling red liquid behind…

“Inspector—something’s wrong!” The two officers, still recovering from their fright, shouted at once. The heavy hammer fell to the floor with a loud *clang*, punching a hole in the tiles.

“What are you doing?” The inspector shook his head and walked to the tank. The head had disappeared.

“Where is the head?” he roared.

“It… it was… dragged down…” one officer stammered, his broken voice making his terror obvious.

“Useless idiots, all of you—move!” The inspector decided to handle the matter himself and give these juniors a lesson. Gripping the hammer with both hands he gave a loud shout and smashed the toilet to pieces. Green liquid sprayed outward, covering him from head to toe. A small red flower clung to his face as though it had grown directly from the skin and could not be scraped away.

A sense of panic rose in him. He dropped the hammer and clawed at the flower with both hands, yet the more he struggled the more firmly it adhered. In the end it fused completely with his flesh, stamped onto his face like a plague god.

Soon his face alternated between heat and cold, as if something were flowing beneath the skin. A green line threaded across his vision, coiling around his entire head like a snake.

“Inspector?” The two officers behind him noticed something wrong. The moment they touched his body it felt like touching a pillar of ice; they instantly withdrew their hands.

The inspector stood rooted like a wooden post, completely motionless. After a moment of silence one officer forced himself to step in front of the man. He should not have looked. The instant he did he collapsed onto the floor, eyes wide with terror, and screamed “Mom!” before scrambling out of the bathroom on all fours.

The inspector’s face had turned a dull blue-green. From his mouth came intermittent *ge-ge* sounds. Something resembling a vine seemed to have grown inside his body; it gradually broke free of the constraints of flesh and emerged. Before long it had bound the inspector as tightly as a twisted rope. He had no time to ask why before the strange plant dragged him into the small opening beneath the shattered toilet and vanished from everyone’s sight…

In a short time an inexplicable panic spread across the whole of Shibuya. One disappearance after another, one piece of tragic news following hard upon the last, like a vast net settling over the lively city. Every clue pointed toward the same strange green vine-like plant. Exactly what was that sacred existence, and where had it come from?

When Juexing and Orange received the news they did not go to the gallery. Instead they changed course and headed for the latest crime scene. The house was already enveloped in an otherworldly green. Several police cars were parked outside; they appeared to have arrived only a short while earlier, yet inside the house there was no movement at all. The surrounding area was unnaturally quiet, as though a great migration had just taken place.

“Stay behind me. If anything goes wrong, run out first.” Juexing tightly held Orange’s hand and advanced step by careful step toward the house.

He gently pushed the door open. The interior was in complete chaos. Floor, walls, and ceiling were covered with green vine-like plants. A few drops of liquid that looked like blood dripped from the ceiling onto Orange’s shoulder. She held her breath and slowly raised her head. The body of a police officer hung suspended beneath the ceiling, still occasionally twitching inside the coils that bound him. The sight made Orange seize the arm of Juexing, who was still moving forward.

“How can this be happening?” Even Juexing felt no confidence. The dense aura of death filling the house was like the presence of malevolent spirits—so oppressive that breathing itself became difficult.

With a sharp *pa* the officer hanging from the ceiling fell. He stretched out a blood-soaked hand, pointed toward the doorway, and with his last breath managed one final sentence:

“Quick… run…”

### Character Notes

**Luo Juexing**  
Returns late after the gallery encounter, comforts Orange, and the next morning chooses to face the spreading plant force head-on rather than avoid it. At the crime scene he moves carefully, protecting Orange while confronting the living vines.

**Qingmu Chengzi (Orange)**  
Waits anxiously for Juexing’s return, then insists on accompanying him to the crime scene despite her fear. She is the first to notice the hanging officer.

**Shang Xuan & Little Bell**  
Briefly appear after the gallery closes. Shang Xuan warns that the situation will not be simple; Little Bell’s presence signals that the circle of those drawn into the event continues to expand.

**Mizusawa Kaori**  
Watches her daughter and Juexing leave with growing foreboding, sensing that Orange’s return may have been a costly decision.

**The Green Vine Force**  
Originating from the Soul-Binding Pagoda, it now spreads through plumbing and hidden tunnels, claiming victims from locked rooms and leaving behind living green tendrils mixed with blood and six-petaled red flowers.
