Juexing spent the morning visiting places near the train station, gathering what information he could. He planned to sort through it properly once he got home. He had just taken out his key when the door opened from the inside. Orange’s voice floated from the kitchen:

“You’re back!”

He stepped in, closed the door, and walked to the kitchen doorway. Orange stood at the stove, tasting soup from a ladle. The rich aroma told him it was fish-head soup.

“I went to the market with a few colleagues at noon. We ran into some really fresh silver carp, so we bought one. Head for soup, body steamed. Almost ready.” Her eyes narrowed into happy crescents.

Juexing set his materials on the desk in the bedroom, took a bottle of cold water from the refrigerator, and drank. The chill ran from his throat all the way to the soles of his feet. He loved that sensation.

“Thanks for the hard work,” he called toward the kitchen, then dialed Jiaye.

“Yeah, I found a few leads. I’ll come by later,” Jiaye said, flipping quickly through papers.

“Come over for lunch. We can discuss strategy while we eat.”

Jiaye’s hands stopped moving. Silence filled the line long enough for Juexing to feel a flicker of unease.

“Hey, you okay?”

“I’m fine. I’m on my way right now. Don’t start without me—I can already smell the silver carp…” Jiaye hung up in a rush, stuffed every document into a folder, grabbed his jacket, and headed straight for the parking lot.

Orange carried the steaming, fragrant fish-head soup to the table and glanced at Juexing. He was still holding his phone, frozen.

“You all right?”

“Fine. I invited Jiaye for lunch.”

“Great. I was worried there’d be too much food. With that bottomless stomach joining us, nothing will be left overnight.” She smiled and returned to the kitchen.

Fifteen minutes later Jiaye appeared at the door. Orange opened it. He stepped inside and greeted her with exaggerated respect:

“Hello, sister-in-law!”

Both Juexing and Orange stared at him in identical confusion. Jiaye clutched his chest in mock terror.

“Even your expressions match. You’re perfect for each other!”

Orange laughed and gave a small bow as she closed the door. Juexing walked over and clapped a heavy hand on Jiaye’s shoulder.

“If you don’t speak, no one will treat you like a mute…”

Jiaye immediately wailed, “Sister-in-law, help! Your future husband is using violence!”

Orange appeared with the rice cooker. “All right, enough. Time to eat.”

Throughout the meal Jiaye took a bite, then stared at Orange, then made soft appreciative noises. Juexing nudged him.

“Why do you keep staring at Orange while you eat?”

“I’m thinking about something. If Orange really plans to marry a Chinese guy, I wouldn’t mind marrying a Japanese girl myself!” He covered his mouth and snickered.

Orange smiled. “I think Chinese men are fine. They have a different kind of boldness from Japanese men.”

“Listen to that—‘boldness’! Classic!” Jiaye gave a thumbs-up.

Juexing sighed, ladled a bowl of soup for Jiaye, and said, “Less talking, more eating. Eat the fish brain—like nourishes like.”

“Tch. You’re clearly hiding something. I’m telling you, don’t fail to appreciate the good fortune right in front of you.” The remark landed squarely on Juexing’s soft spot.

Juexing’s gaze shifted to Orange. She was looking at him at the same time.

After a moment he said quietly, “Orange is a really good girl. Everyone likes her…”

Orange lowered her head and smiled to herself, then said with mock seriousness, “I told you—my name is Orange, not tangerine!”

“Orange, tangerine, same thing to me.” Juexing laughed and filled a bowl for her as well. The meal stretched past two hours. Only after they were full did Jiaye spread his documents across the living-room table. The two men sat down to work out a plan.

Orange finished the remaining chores and stood silently in a corner of the living room, clearly waiting to speak. Juexing noticed.

“Don’t just stand there. If something’s on your mind, say it.”

She came over and sat beside him.

“It’s about my classmate Song Xi. After he heard about the ghost train he’s been terrified. He hopes you can help him.”

Juexing covered the papers with his hand and looked at her for a long moment.

“Jiaye and I will handle this together. And that Song Xi—stay away from him from now on.”

Orange bit her lip. A quiet sweetness rose in her chest. She rarely saw him show such clear concern for her. She nodded.

“I know. I’ll listen to you.”

“You’ve been busy all morning. Go rest in the guest room.”

“Can I sleep in your room instead?”

“Sure, I don’t think that’s a difficult problem!” Jiaye answered before Juexing could speak, already pointing at a line of text in the documents.

Orange flashed Jiaye a “yes” gesture and walked straight into Juexing’s bedroom.

The new express-train project involved six key figures. Investor Fan Shijie had already died on the train. Qian Weiqing had died, almost by accident, in the elevator. The question now was whose name would be next on the list.

“Before you and Yu Xun left,” Juexing asked suddenly, “did you see a figure on the rear window of the ambulance?”

“More or less. It was only a second, and every photo Yu Xun took came out completely blurred.”

Juexing flipped rapidly through the files, the pages rustling with urgency.

“Notify the police immediately. Gather the remaining four and protect them. Hurry.”

Jiaye had just taken out his phone when a call came in from a colleague. The moment he answered, the anxious voice on the other end said:

“Chief—Zhao Dezhu has been hospitalized…”

Juexing watched Jiaye’s expression darken.

“Bad news. We missed a step. Zhao Dezhu has a mild heart condition. The scare was enough to put him in the hospital.”

“No time to waste. Get to the hospital now. We have to protect him before she strikes.” Juexing was already pulling on his coat.

“I’ll have the others gathered and placed under protection right away.”

“That may not be necessary. Once Zhao Dezhu is in the hospital, the other three will panic. They’re probably already there.” Juexing’s instinct was clear.

They climbed into the car. To save time Jiaye switched on the emergency lights and the siren.

Another hospital scene. One might have thought the pattern was growing tiresome—yet this time everything felt different. When Juexing stepped out of the car he sensed none of the girl’s resentment. Nurses and patients moved through the corridors in ordinary calm. Nothing looked wrong.

“Second-floor nursing ward,” Jiaye said, leading the way upstairs.

Zhao Dezhu lay quietly on the bed wearing an oxygen mask. Beside him stood Song Xi, Tang Gufeng, and two nurses.

“How is he?” Jiaye asked the cardiologist.

“Stable. It was mostly severe fright. Half a month of rest and he’ll be fine.”

Juexing stood inside the ward and examined every corner. He found no trace that the girl had been there. Song Xi and Tang Gufeng kept their faces carefully composed, but their eyes held a clear, restless fear—the fear that the curse might fall on them next.

Song Xi noticed Juexing and drew him into the corridor.

“I know I was blinded by money at the time and did something unforgivable to Orange. But please—I’m begging you—save me. I’m my father’s only son…” His voice rose and fell theatrically; tears even appeared. He looked for all the world like a man who had suffered a great injustice.

Juexing gave a cold smile.

“I’m working with the police to find leads. But there are a few things I want to discuss with you alone.”

“I’ll tell you everything I know, as long as it’s useful.”

“Do you know a girl named Yang Xueqing?”

“Yang Xueqing? Never heard of her.”

“I’ve read the planning documents for the express line. There was one family that refused to sell their land, correct?”

“Ah… now that you mention it, there was a father and daughter who absolutely wouldn’t sell. In the end the government issued a compulsory order and they had no choice.”

Song Xi’s words were slippery, carefully incomplete.

“You’re lying. You people operated behind the scenes so the government would issue that order. Her father was driven to hang himself in his own home. The daughter went to the government twice demanding answers. Both times she was beaten by men you arranged—and worse.” Juexing stopped himself. The yellowed newspaper report he had found still made his blood burn.

“But she disappeared, didn’t she?” Song Xi seemed to grasp the implication at last.

“She disappeared. And I want to know where she is even more than you do.”

“So… you’re not going to help us?”

Juexing said nothing. He walked past Song Xi and back into the ward. The trip had yielded no immediate protection, only confirmation of a darker truth. He spoke quietly with Jiaye for a moment. Two officers were assigned to guard the ward around the clock and not to leave without direct orders.

Juexing made the situation plain: this was a revenge curse. Resentment born of deep, personal hatred ran far stronger and harder to break than ordinary lingering spirits. One misstep and everything would be lost. The priority now was clear.

Find the missing girl—Yang Xueqing.

### Character Notes

**Luo Juexing**  
The domestic warmth of the lunch scene stands in sharp contrast to the cold clarity he shows at the hospital. His refusal to help Song Xi and his direct confrontation over Yang Xueqing reveal that his sense of justice has hardened. Protecting Orange and stopping the curse now outweigh any obligation to the project’s remaining survivors.

**Huang Jiaye**  
Still the practical investigator, but increasingly willing to follow Juexing’s lead. The playful “sister-in-law” teasing at lunch humanizes him even as the case grows darker.

**Qingmu Chengzi (Orange)**  
Creates a brief pocket of ordinary happiness with the fish-head soup and quiet conversation. Her request on behalf of Song Xi and her immediate acceptance of Juexing’s warning show both lingering kindness and growing trust in him.

**Song Xi**  
Desperate, self-pitying, and still lying by omission. His attempt to frame himself as a victim collapses under Juexing’s knowledge of the land seizure and the violence done to Yang Xueqing’s family.

**Yang Xueqing**  
The missing girl at the heart of the revenge. Her father’s suicide after forced land acquisition, followed by her own beatings and disappearance, provides the emotional and moral core of the curse now stalking the six men of the express-train project.

**Zhao Dezhu**  
The third name on the list, currently hospitalized by fright rather than direct supernatural attack. His mild heart condition made him the easiest target for psychological pressure.
