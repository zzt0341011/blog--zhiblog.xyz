The soul-exchange ritual continued—a psychological contest between the living and the dead. Juexing sought to melt her hatred with the most sincere prayer he could offer.

Driven by the heart, Qiaohe turned the written symbols in the center of the box and seemed to see the past once more.

Outside the mirrors, opposite Juexing, stood a girl covered in wounds. She still wore the blood-stained white dress and stood barefoot.

“Every heart has a window of sin,” Juexing said. “Once it is opened, every ugly form is laid bare. They bullied you out of jealousy. You, seeing them continue to bully others, remembered your own helplessness and opened that window yourself. Evil took the reins and made you do what you should never have done. It was not your fault—it was the demon inside whispering. Everyone still remembers you. They remember the last piece of goodness they shared with you. Whether in the past, the present, or the future, your original heart never changed. You do not remain because of hatred. You remain because of the longing for forgiveness.”

He walked to her side, turned her gently, and made her face the mirror on the right.

Inside that glass was a face full of youth and innocence—beautiful, without a single flaw, the kind of face that could make anyone pause and stare.

“This is the real you. Do you see? You have never changed…”

Qiaohe stepped closer to the mirror and looked at her own reflection. Tears fell. In that moment she seemed to understand everything.

He thought the ritual would end smoothly. The killing intent in her eyes was already fading. Then, without warning, the red threads that bound the mirrors were snapped by an unseen force. It was as if she had been shoved back into a bottomless abyss. Qiaohe spun around and glared at Juexing, voice venomous:

“I believe you—but those who refuse to repent still need me to settle the final account…”

Juexing calculated rapidly. The situation had shifted. The instant he moved to stop her, both mirrors shattered at once. A blast of powerful resentment hurled him out of the room and down from the second floor. The explosion drew the attention of the security guards. He lay on the ground. Although he had fallen from the second story, Qiaohe had not intended to kill him; he was able to push himself up. At that moment his phone began to ring.

From breakfast through lunch that Sunday became the happiest stretch of time in Zilan’s life. Orange’s smile and Zilan’s own smile rose and fell together on the swings of the seaside park. To anyone watching, they looked like the two happiest people in the world.

“Teacher, will you marry Star-ge?” Zilan asked.

“Hmm, what do you think?” Orange took a bite of her ice-cream cone.

“You will. Every time you mention him your whole face lights up.”

“When Zilan grows up you’ll meet a boy who treats you very, very well. Then the two of you will live together, smile together, and face every hardship together.”

“Really? I can be like you?” Zilan’s eyes were wide with doubt.

Orange pulled her into an embrace.

“Of course. Study hard, get into a good university, and then fall in love openly and wholeheartedly.”

Zilan bit into her cone. The sweetness that filled her was greater than any ice cream; it sank straight into her heart.

“Thank you, Teacher.” Her eyes suddenly reddened—tears of pure joy.

“As long as you remember me, I’ll be your friend for life. Remember: you’re not alone. You have me, you have Star-ge, and you have many people waiting to meet you and care for you.” Orange’s own chest ached at the girl’s tears.

“But…” Zilan’s thoughts drifted back to the people at school.

“They won’t bully you anymore. You can even become friends. Give yourself confidence, and believe they can change one day.”

“If… one day I do something wrong, will Teacher Orange forgive me?”

A bad premonition instantly rose in Orange’s mind. She did not understand the full weight of the question.

The moment Zilan sat up, two coarse sacks dropped over both their heads…

“We’re heading up the mountain. They’re moving fast, and it looks like they already discovered our stakeout.” Jiaye spoke into the phone while gripping the steering wheel.

Juexing sat in a taxi, regret flooding him. He had calculated everything except this desperate move. Now nothing could be taken back.

“Driver, please go faster—” He urged again. At the intersection an accident had already blocked the road; traffic police had sealed it. Juexing glanced at his watch, paid, jumped out, and began to run. He did not dare imagine what those children might do to Orange and Zilan.

Inside the bouncing van neither Orange nor Zilan struggled; they had been drugged. Across from them sat Shuangfei and the others. Xiao Qing remained terrified the entire way, led by the nose by Shuangfei. She had never agreed with the plan, but the thought of ending up like Wang Mei kept her silent and afraid.

“Calm down. She struck first. We’re only defending ourselves.” Shuangfei tried to soothe her.

Xiao Qing only kept crying, which began to irritate Shuangfei.

Chen Dong said, “We’ll burn everything. I’ve already found a homeless man. Even if the police come, they won’t be able to pin it on us. Don’t worry.”

Shuangfei reassured herself: “It was only an accident.”

“But what about Teacher Orange?” Xiao Qing asked timidly.

“She’s unrelated. We’ll just dump her somewhere later.”

“No. When she got in the van she called your name. She already knows it’s us. If she wakes up she’s a living witness. We’re finished.”

“But she’s our teacher… and she…” Shuangfei hesitated.

“No choice. She protected that girl. Burn them both and it ends cleanly.” Chen Dong clenched his jaw.

The van stopped. The driver told Chen Dong the road ahead was too narrow for the vehicle. They carried the two unconscious girls the rest of the way to the summit. Not far from the school rose Jiugong Peak; on it stood a gloomy abandoned house. Rumor said students who came to the mountain for private meetings had seen strange shadows pass through the empty house and vanish. From that time on the place had been called the “Gate to Heaven.”

“Do we really have to do this?” Xiao Qing was still frightened.

“Take Xiao Qing back to the van. Leave the rest to us.” Chen Dong spoke to Shuangfei, then he and the driver opened the gasoline cans they had brought and began soaking the empty house.

Shuangfei and Xiao Qing did not stay in the van. Shuangfei was too uneasy; they hid a short distance away and watched.

The sharp reek of gasoline filled the house. The smell choked Orange awake. When she opened her eyes everything was black. She rolled over and her foot struck something.

Her hands and feet were bound; she could not break free. Yet she remembered Zilan was with her.

“Help! Help—” She kept shouting, coughing. In the distance a single spark flared like a falling meteor and vanished. Then the flames rose.

In the firelight she saw Zilan lying motionless beside her.

“Zilan…” Orange struggled to sit up. Her wrists were already raw and bleeding. The only thought in her mind was how to get the girl out of the sea of fire.

The blaze grew. The towering flames looked like a clawed beast determined to swallow the empty house inch by inch.

Smoke woke Zilan. The moment she opened her eyes the leaping tongues of fire terrified her. Orange soothed her:

“It’s all right. Teacher is almost free of the ropes. Just a little longer.”

With every ounce of strength Orange finally frayed the rope around her wrists enough to break it. Ignoring the still-bound ankles, she dragged herself to Zilan and began untying her.

A burning beam from the roof crashed down. Orange shoved Zilan aside; the wood slammed into the floor and sprayed sparks, missing them both.

Zilan dared not run; fire was everywhere. One wrong step and they would be consumed.

Orange freed her own ankles, then pulled Zilan into her arms. There was no way out.

More beams, weakened by the flames, began to fall. Orange could only shield the girl and dodge left and right.

Outside, Chen Dong and the driver watched the roaring fire with satisfaction and nodded. They failed to notice what was happening behind them.

Xiao Qing suddenly stopped crying. The sudden stillness made Shuangfei uneasy. She turned quickly. Xiao Qing was staring at her with eyes that no longer belonged to her.

Shuangfei stumbled and fell. She pointed, voice breaking:

“You… you…”

“Heh. Someone had almost persuaded me. I was ready to decide—then you destroyed it.” Xiao Qing’s voice was soft as she stepped closer.

Shuangfei scrambled backward.

“You’re not Xiao Qing. You should be inside—”

“You wanted me burned in that fire too. Unfortunately the cursebreaker pulled me out of Zilan’s body. Now I’m inside Xiao Qing. I want to watch how you die.”

A violent wind blurred Shuangfei’s vision. The air that struck her face and body felt as sharp as blades. Blood appeared on her hands; her face felt as though it had been carved by knives. The pain was unbearable.

Her scream drew Chen Dong’s attention. He spun and ran toward the sound—only to see a figure covered in blood rushing at him. The sight froze him in place.

“Save me, A-Dong—” Shuangfei wailed. Chen Dong stared. The person lunging toward him was not Shuangfei at all, but…

Xiao Qing’s hands locked around his throat. He kicked hard into her stomach, sending her flying; she collapsed and did not move.

Chen Dong had barely caught his breath when Shuangfei stood beside him again, head hanging like a ghost.

“Are you all right?” He reached out to steady her.

She slowly raised her head. Beneath the tangled hair was a face that no longer resembled a face—raw, peeled, horrific. Chen Dong sucked in a sharp breath and shoved her away. His foot caught her skirt. The two of them tumbled, one after the other, down the slope…

Two figures almost simultaneously crashed into the burning house. One on the left, one on the right—each shielding a person as they burst back out of the flames.

### Character Notes

**Lin Qiaohe**  
The ritual brings her to the brink of release. For a moment she sees her unblemished self and weeps. The sudden snapping of the threads and her final decision to “settle the account” show how fragile the path to forgiveness remains when the living continue to choose violence.

**Luo Juexing**  
Survives the blast from the shattered mirrors and immediately races toward the mountain. The chapter ends with him (and another) diving into the fire—continuing the pattern of physical sacrifice that has defined his role since the train arc.

**Qingmu Chengzi (Orange)**  
The quiet morning on the swings is the last peaceful moment before the kidnapping. Her first clear thought upon waking in the smoke is not her own survival but Zilan’s. The determination to free and protect the girl even while still bound underscores the depth of the bond she has formed.

**Mo Zilan**  
Experiences genuine happiness and the first real promise of a future, only to be dragged back into the cycle of violence she has always endured. Her question—“Will you forgive me if I do something wrong?”—hangs unanswered over the rest of the chapter.

**Lu Shuangfei, Zhao Xiaoqing & Chen Dong**  
Their plan to burn the evidence (and the witnesses) collapses into chaos once the residual spirit switches hosts. The disfigurement of Shuangfei and the fatal tumble into the fire close the circle of the bullying that began two years earlier.

**The Empty House on Jiugong Peak**  
A place already marked by rumor as a “Gate to Heaven.” In this chapter it becomes the stage for both attempted murder by fire and the residual spirit’s final, furious judgment.
