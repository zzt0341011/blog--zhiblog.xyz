Summer vacation finally lifted its curtain. With the college entrance exams over, the blazing heat of summer arrived in full force. Streets and alleys brimmed with a cool energy—sundaes, ice cream, and four-fruit soup instantly became the hottest-selling items.

Crowds gathered around the fountains in the parks. Soaring water columns and laughing people created scene after scene of varied, lively scenery. Some sat on stone benches watching the children splash; others raised cameras to capture the most natural and unpretentious moments. Among them was the well-known reporter Meng Yu Xun.

During this period one man began rushing about, appearing in jewelry shops and accessory stores one after another. In search of the most precious gift he finally had no choice but to seek out a certain Teacher Luo.

The moment summer break began, university teachers naturally found themselves with free time. Juexing, however, refused to stay idle. He had even begun studying oil painting. For someone with no natural talent, painting was pure torment, yet in order to fill the empty hours—apart from preparing experimental materials for the next semester, eating, sleeping, and fooling around with Orange—he spent the remaining large stretches of time in front of the canvas.

*Bang, bang!*

“Open up! Open the door!”

Jiaye could not even be bothered to press the doorbell; he simply shouted outside.

Juexing, completely absorbed in his painting, jerked his hand. The nearly finished work was ruined in an instant by Jiaye’s sudden racket.

He threw down the brush, stormed to the door, and blocked the entrance with his body. “What do you want?”

Jiaye used every ounce of strength to shove Juexing back inside, then, as if it were the most natural thing in the world, took a can of ice-cold beer from the refrigerator, took a long, satisfying swallow, wiped his mouth, and said, “Big news!”

Juexing looked at Jiaye’s smug face, sat down unhappily in front of the easel, and continued examining his ruined painting.

“You’re seriously ill. Planning to become an artist?” Jiaye walked over, glanced at the canvas, and sprayed the mouthful of beer all over the painting.

He laughed until he held his stomach. “What the hell is that? King Ghidorah?”

The painting was already destroyed; Juexing no longer cared how Jiaye trampled or commented on it.

“Finished laughing? Then get out.” He glanced at the baseball bat leaning against the wall.

“Don’t look at me like that. This time I really have something important.” Jiaye immediately jumped aside because Juexing had already gripped the bat.

“I think you’re just here to cause trouble.”

“I’m going to marry Yu Xun. But I don’t know how to choose a wedding ring, so after thinking it over I came to you.”

“Wedding ring?” Juexing muttered.

“Yeah. You’re free anyway. Let’s go take a look together.” Jiaye jerked his thumb toward the door.

Juexing raised the bat. Jiaye instantly threw up his hands in a defensive pose. A moment later Juexing lowered the weapon, checked his wallet, and said, “Fine. Let’s go look at wedding rings.”

Jiaye exhaled in relief, hurriedly opened the door, and made a polite “after you” gesture.

It was the first time the two grown men had ever stepped into a jewelry store. Faced with the dazzling array of ornaments they were truly “dazzled by the myriad blossoms.” Five colors, five lights—picking one or two superior pieces from among them was no easy task.

“Are the two gentlemen looking at rings?” A tall, curvaceous saleswoman approached.

Jiaye looked her over from shoes to hair before answering, “Yes. Any suggestions?”

“If it’s wedding rings, we have a dedicated counter with many styles. You’ll know the moment you see them.” She guided the pair to a larger glass case. The diamond rings inside were indeed more eye-catching than the rest.

Juexing rested his chin in his hand and compared them one by one with focused attention. Each possessed its own unique style; choosing even one or two was genuinely difficult.

The saleswoman handed Jiaye two boxes. One contained a two-carat diamond set in platinum, the cutting precise, every facet revealing the stone’s full brilliance. Jiaye held the ring, swallowed, nudged Juexing, and asked, “What do you think?”

Juexing still rested his chin in his hand, shook his head, and continued searching for the one in his mind.

“This piece comes from South Africa—you know, the country famous for diamonds. It’s the second signature item in our store, second only to the five-carat.”

Juexing scanned the entire counter and finally settled on a one-carat gold diamond ring. In both design and presence it was more pleasing to the eye than the others. He asked the saleswoman to take it out.

She praised him, “Excellent eye, sir. This ring arrived only three days ago. There were three in total; this is the last one. If you purchase it we will also include a white-gold pendant chain—very nice.”

“What’s its name?” Juexing asked, holding the ring.

“Sunset Love.”

“Like the lingering glow of the setting sun, slowly circling, moving the heart.” Juexing examined it carefully.

The saleswoman watched his absorbed expression and quietly smiled. Beside him Jiaye reached out to take a look, but Juexing closed the box at once, quickly produced his wallet, and said, “I’ll take it. How much?”

“Thirteen thousand eight hundred, plus the complimentary white-gold chain.” She took his bank card and swiped it smoothly.

Jiaye shouted, “Damn! You said you were coming to help me look, and then you buy one yourself. No loyalty at all!”

After signing the receipt Juexing took the bag containing the ring and chain. “All right. I already knew you bought yours days ago. Stop muttering.” He gave a toss of his head and walked out of the shop.

“Hey, how did you know I already bought one? I didn’t leave any openings, did I?”

“The opening was obvious. You showed zero impulse to buy, which proves you already had one.”

“Heh—don’t say that. Look, we actually bought the same design.” Jiaye pulled out the ring he had purchased three days earlier. Apart from being platinum instead of gold, it was almost identical to Juexing’s.

“So you bought it three days ago, and it’s from the same series.”

“Of course. Sometimes our taste is surprisingly similar…”

“Stop right there. That only applies to choosing rings. Everything else is off the table.”

“What’s that supposed to mean? You think you’re so great just because you know a Japanese girl? My woman is a returnee from overseas!”

“Look at you. Let’s find somewhere to drink. There’s something I suddenly want to discuss with you.” Juexing turned into a casual bar; Jiaye followed at once.

They talked until afternoon. Before they parted ways they had discussed matters of marriage. Exactly how far the discussion went could be judged from the triumphant air each of them carried.

Orange stayed home packing. She planned to book plane tickets in a few days and fly to Japan with Juexing, spending the rest of the summer there before returning.

She smiled at herself in the mirror. That morning while shopping she had secretly stepped on the electronic scale by the supermarket entrance. Sure enough, the saying that a woman bathing in happiness gains weight had come true. Fortunately Orange was the type who could eat as much as she liked without showing it—no wonder Juexing, secretly watching from the side, felt his heart bloom with delight.

She turned sideways to show off her figure and caught sight of Juexing standing nearby in the mirror. She spun around and stared at him.

“How did you get in without making a sound?”

“You were too absorbed. I rang the doorbell for a long time, then simply used the spare key.”

Orange turned a full circle in front of him; the flying hem of her skirt rose like dancing butterflies, as if a fairy had descended to earth.

“Mm. Very beautiful. And your figure is excellent…”

Orange felt a warm sweetness. She walked over and said softly, “I didn’t cook tonight. Let’s eat out together.”

“I was thinking the same thing. And I have something to give you.” Juexing smiled until his eyes narrowed to slits.

“So you came prepared.”

“Of course. And it will be a super surprise!” He raised his eyebrows.

“All right. I’ll get my bag. Let’s go!” Orange skipped into the bedroom, retrieved her bag, linked arms with Juexing, and left the house together.

Sometimes it no longer mattered whether something was coincidence or carefully arranged. What mattered was that a grand scene was about to unfold. When the lights of the city first came on, Jiaye, dressed in a suit and tie, gleaming leather shoes on his feet, walked into the hotel lobby with Yu Xun on his arm in an evening gown. They had taken only a few steps when warm applause broke out all around them. He had chosen this very night to become engaged to Yu Xun—at the most luxurious and imposing hotel in the entire city, the Star City Grand Hotel.

Juexing and Orange were already seated at a VIP table, watching the smiling pair.

“Oh—so they’re getting engaged. How happy they look!” Orange glanced at Juexing. He was clapping for Jiaye and did not notice the complicated look in her eyes.

“Jealous?” he suddenly asked.

“How could I not be?” she pouted.

The lights suddenly went out. A special spotlight fell upon the VIP table. Juexing gazed at Orange with deep emotion. The people around them held their breath and watched the two of them with equal tenderness.

It was the first time Orange had been looked at by Juexing in quite that way; she felt a little unaccustomed. She was about to shift her gaze when his eyes told her not to.

After a brief pause he slowly took a black box from the inner pocket of his jacket. When he opened it a flash of golden light passed before Orange’s eyes and then vanished. A diamond ring now rested in front of her.

“I am proposing to you—tonight.” He pushed the box toward her. She could hardly believe it was real.

In the surrounding silence Orange’s already racing heart lost all rhythm beneath the chorus of voices calling “Say yes! Say yes!”

Juexing took the ring out and slid it onto the ring finger of her left hand. Orange stared at the diamond; a flush of red immediately colored her dimples. She was so moved she could not utter a single word.

### Character Notes

**Luo Juexing**  
Uses the pretext of helping Jiaye choose a ring to buy one of his own—“Sunset Love.” That same evening, in the middle of his friend’s engagement party, he publicly proposes to Orange.

**Qingmu Chengzi (Orange)**  
Prepares for the Japan trip, enjoys the quiet happiness of daily life with Juexing, and is completely unprepared for the spotlight and the ring that appears in front of her.

**Huang Jiaye**  
Decides to marry Yu Xun, already purchased a matching ring three days earlier, and stages a grand engagement party at the Star City Grand Hotel—unknowingly providing the perfect stage for Juexing’s proposal.

**Meng Yu Xun**  
Appears at the engagement celebration in an evening gown, the center of Jiaye’s public declaration.
