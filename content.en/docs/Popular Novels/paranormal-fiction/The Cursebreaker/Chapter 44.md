After seeing the little boy off, Wanting walked back to the bench. Orange was still nowhere in sight, yet the tulip that should have remained on the seat now lay on the ground, crushed and ugly, as if many feet had stepped on it. As she bent down she noticed an orange under the bench, and beside it a bracelet. She was certain someone had been here—more than one person.

When she crouched to pick up the bracelet, a familiar figure entered her line of sight. She could feel the faint reproach in his eyes.

“I’m sorry. I didn’t tell you beforehand.” Wanting clutched the bracelet and kept her head lowered, not daring to look at his face.

“This is a critical time. You know perfectly well your cousin is looking for you!” Shang Xuan let the words slip in his anxiety.

“Cousin?” Wanting stared at him with wide, confused eyes.

“Anyway, from now on, if you go out you must tell me. Let me know where you are and where you’re going. I don’t want anything to happen to you.” He quickly changed direction.

“All right. I understand. It won’t happen again.” She pressed her lips together.

“What were you doing here?”

“Orange asked me to come. She said she needed to talk about something. I waited from ten o’clock until now and never saw her. I was just about to call when you arrived.”

“Orange?” Shang Xuan’s voice rose in alarm.

“Look—is this her bracelet?” Wanting opened her palm. A silver-white chain with a red cord twisted around it. The cord had been tied by Juexing himself; the combination was unique.

“This is bad.” Shang Xuan raked a hand through his hair, clearly frantic.

“You’re that worried about her?”

“Of course I’m worried. I—” He glanced at Wanting. The question carried an ambiguous edge; he nearly walked into the trap.

“She’s my friend, and she may be in danger. Of course I’m worried.” He softened his tone.

“Should we call the police and let them handle it?”

“No. I’ll take you home first, then go look for her. You stay inside and don’t go anywhere.” He held her arms firmly.

The van jolted along the road toward a remote area on the outskirts. Once it left the normal operating range of the taxi, Yu Xun had no choice but to get out and continue on foot. Fortunately the vehicle did not travel much farther before stopping beside a village house. The surroundings were deserted; even if the police came looking it would take them time.

As a precaution she still sent Jiaye a short message. The moment he read it he leapt to his feet, face drained of color.

“What’s wrong?” Juexing saw the terrible expression.

Jiaye thrust the phone at him. Juexing did not even stop for a jacket; he tore out the door. Jiaye locked up and chased him into the elevator.

“Brother, she didn’t give an address. Where are we supposed to look?”

Juexing slammed a fist against the elevator door. “If anything happens to Orange, those bastards can wait for prison.”

“The evidence Yu Xun is gathering will be useful. Once Wanting steps forward, Lu Shengnan and the rest are finished.”

Juexing could think of nothing else. He cared only about Orange’s safety. The elevator sank floor by floor and finally reached the lobby.

Yu Xun sent another hurried message with a rough location. Jiaye and Juexing climbed into the car and drove toward the place she had indicated.

Orange, blindfolded, gagged, and bound hand and foot, was carried into the village house. The building was large, built in the style of a traditional courtyard. Most of these old structures had been abandoned, waiting only for the demolition crews.

Yu Xun slipped inside with careful steps and found the room where Orange was being held. Two men who looked like underworld muscle stood outside, staring straight ahead without blinking.

She pushed open the door of the adjacent room and discovered a small connecting door between the two spaces.

Outside the sun was bright; inside the room was nearly black. Orange could clearly feel dozens of eyes fixed on her. Footsteps of leather shoes approached from outside. The door opened.

The black cloth covering her eyes was ripped away. In the weak light she saw the faces around her—fierce and ugly.

A still-working incandescent bulb was switched on. By its glow the man looked at Orange and froze.

“What the hell? Who are you?”

The moment she heard the words she knew they had grabbed the wrong person. She made muffled sounds, trying to indicate that he should remove the tape over her mouth.

“Boss, isn’t it her?” one of the underlings asked.

“I would recognize my cousin’s face even if it turned to ash. How could you be so careless and grab the wrong woman?” he scolded.

“Then what do we do?”

“What do you think we do?”

“Finish her and be done with it.”

“You want to go to prison so badly you have to add murder?” He was clearly uneasy. Looking at the panic in Orange’s eyes, he finally tore the tape from her mouth.

Orange drew a breath. “Who are you people? Why did you bring me here? The cousin you mentioned—is she Lu Wanting?”

His heart gave a jolt. So she already knew something of the inside story.

“Speak. What else do you know?” he demanded.

“I don’t know anything. Only that she is living well now. Why do you have to destroy that?”

“Destroy? She was already a dead woman. Instead of staying quietly underground she comes up to make trouble. Haven’t you noticed the financial crisis has started?” He gave a cold laugh, already planning to extract what he needed and then silence her permanently.

“So it’s exactly as she said. Three years ago you people did something unspeakable to the Lu family, and she has come back for revenge.” Orange spoke deliberately. She had noticed a figure standing not far away—someone who had once been a rival in love and might now be an ally.

Yu Xun understood the signal at once. She took out the small DV camera she carried and began recording. This conversation could become the strongest evidence they possessed.

“Shut up. This is Lu family business. What business does an outsider have sticking her nose in?” He sounded nervous.

“A friend told me that both the inheritance case three years ago and Wanting’s later accidental death have suspicious points. I figure the odds are high that you and your partners arranged it.” Orange continued to provoke him.

“Enough. You know too much. Don’t blame me. Make it quick!” His mind had been thrown into disorder; any more talk and he would start contradicting himself. He gestured for his men to deal with her.

“You’re just going to kill me like this? One Wanting already weighs on your conscience. Add another dead soul and you may never have a peaceful day again.” Orange spoke as if she had already set life and death aside—or perhaps she had guessed he would not finish her so easily.

He did stop, murmuring, “Retribution… it really is retribution…”

“I’m an atheist. I never believed in ghosts or gods. But after meeting a cursebreaker I learned that a certain kind of being really does exist alongside us.” Orange switched tactics, hoping to draw out the truth.

“Cursebreaker?” The word seemed familiar to him.

“What I mean is, if Wanting really is a ghost, then only a cursebreaker can resolve the crisis. With luck it will simply vanish like smoke.”

“Hmph. Trying to trick me into talking. I underestimated you.” He turned back, walked up to her, and narrowed his eyes. Under the dim yellow light a malicious glint appeared.

“Looking closely… you’re actually quite attractive.”

“What do you intend to do?” A flicker of fear entered Orange’s voice.

He glanced at the men around them. “I wonder whether you can handle this many.” The words had barely left his mouth when a low murmur of anticipation rose.

Yu Xun sensed the atmosphere turning dangerous. In her tension she accidentally kicked a broken piece of wood that was still attached to a half-collapsed table. The noise was sharp and startling in the silence.

“Who’s there?” One of the men spotted Yu Xun behind the door.

She immediately pocketed the DV, kicked the door open with full force, spun, and drove a sharp kick into the first man who rushed her, sending him to the floor.

The noise drew the attention of everyone else, including Tian Shubin who was just approaching.

Several large men surrounded Yu Xun. They took fighting stances but did not advance. She struck first—a straight punch, then used the momentum to spin and kick another man flying. None of them had expected a woman who looked so slender to possess such skill.

In a short time the men were scattered and defeated. Only Lu Shengnan remained, trembling in a corner, barely daring to breathe. Yu Xun seized the chance to untie the ropes binding Orange. Outside the village house Jiaye’s loud voice shouted several times. A pair of black-gloved hands dragged Lu Shengnan away. In the blink of an eye the entire group vanished.

Yu Xun supported Orange to the doorway. Juexing burst in, wasted no time on greetings, and pulled her tightly into his arms.

Yu Xun stepped back beside Jiaye and watched them.

“I’m all right. Don’t worry…” Orange patted Juexing’s back.

“Let’s go back and make some ginseng tea to settle the nerves,” Jiaye suggested.

Tian Shubin sat beside Lu Shengnan, watching the man’s hands continue to shake, and knew the situation might already have been exposed.

“What do we do now?” Shengnan asked.

“The police have entered the case. But we still have one chance—depending on whether you have the nerve.”

“I have no ideas left. Zhuzhu is about to give birth. I don’t care if I go to prison, but I can’t drag her and the child down with me.” His voice was pleading.

“I told you not to act rashly. Did you let anything slip just now?”

“No. She almost tricked me, but I didn’t say anything.” Shengnan wiped the sweat from his forehead.

“I’ve already found out that Wanting is living with a man named Shang Xuan. If we grab him, she will come quietly. Then we make sure there are no living witnesses left. They won’t be able to touch us.” Tian gave a cold smile.

Lu Shengnan said nothing more. The situation had already slipped beyond control. Could it really be, as the woman had said—a curse, a revenge?

**Yu Xun**  
Follows the van on foot, infiltrates the village house, records the confession, and single-handedly fights multiple attackers to free Orange.

**Luo Juexing**  
Races to the scene the moment he learns Orange is in danger. The moment he sees her safe he can think of nothing else.

**Shang Xuan & Wan Ting**  
Find the crushed tulip and Orange’s bracelet. Shang Xuan realizes the trap was meant for Wanting and immediately prioritizes getting her home safely.

**Lu Shengnan & Tian Shubin**  
Realize they seized the wrong woman. With the police now involved they shift strategy: capture Shang Xuan to force Wanting’s compliance and eliminate witnesses.
