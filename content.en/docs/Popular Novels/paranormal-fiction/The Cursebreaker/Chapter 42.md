Tian Shubin hurried into Lu Shengnan’s office, briefcase under his arm. Shengnan closed the door, drew the curtains, and the two men began to talk.

“Well?” Shengnan asked.

“We’ve checked thoroughly. In appearance it is indeed Lu Wanting. I also went to the cemetery. Her remains are still there, untouched, no sign of having been moved.”

“You’re certain?”

“Names can coincide. Faces can resemble each other. But one symptom cannot be dismissed as coincidence.” Tian stroked his chin.

“What symptom?”

“Two days ago a fire alarm went off at the Tiantianhao Supermarket where she works. She was supposedly tidying the third-floor storeroom when someone locked the door from the outside. She collapsed inside and was later rescued by a man.”

The description immediately brought back Wanting’s confirmed diagnosis of claustrophobia.

Tian watched Shengnan’s face remain locked in unease. After a pause he continued, “Whether it is really her or an elaborate prank, we have to act decisively. If the events of three years ago are dug up, both of us will go to prison.”

Shengnan looked up at the lawyer’s grim expression. His mind was in turmoil; he could form no clear plan.

“But what if it really is just coincidence? If we overreact we might expose ourselves instead.”

“So what do you suggest? Pretend we saw nothing and keep living carefree lives?”

“Let me think…” Shengnan waved a hand and sank into his office chair.

“Fine. I’ll go back for now. Contact me when you decide. But I’ll say this first: if the truth comes out, I can claim I was only an accessory. You will take the full weight.” Tian deliberately applied pressure. He was beginning to feel that Wanting’s reappearance somehow confirmed the old saying about karmic retribution.

In the crowded streets, several highly alert men moved back and forth—clearly tracking or watching someone. The scene shifted to a Chinese restaurant where Shang Xuan and Wanting sat laughing over lunch, seemingly oblivious to the changed atmosphere outside.

In an unguarded moment Shang Xuan noticed two men at a nearby table staring in their direction. He immediately looked away and smiled. “How is it? Tasty?”

“Yes. I feel happier every day… and I think I’ve gained a little weight.” Wanting’s smile was radiant, bright eyes and white teeth like a fairy’s.

Exactly as Juexing had expected, the moment he finished work he began searching for their trail. Sure enough, he found many suspicious figures around them. He sent a short message to Shang Xuan. Shang Xuan checked his phone, then glanced toward the window. Juexing was standing outside. Several men smoking cigarettes circled the restaurant entrance as if waiting for them.

Shang Xuan replied, then leaned close and whispered a few words into Wanting’s ear. She looked at him in confusion. He smiled, stood, and went to the counter to pay.

Jiaye strode boldly toward the restaurant with several colleagues. Juexing gave him a small nod; Jiaye answered with an “OK” gesture and walked straight inside.

“Miss Lu?” Jiaye asked courteously.

Wanting nodded.

“We are police officers. We need to ask you a few questions about the night you were trapped. Please come with us.” He showed his credentials. After Shang Xuan gave consent, Wanting followed them out.

At the doorway two of the smoking men blocked their path. “What do you think you’re doing?”

Jiaye held up his ID. “Police business. Find somewhere else to cool off.”

The men had no choice but to step aside and slink away.

In a side alley Jiaye met Shang Xuan.

“Why police?” Wanting asked.

“The rest is up to you two. But I’ll say this quietly: every move you make is being watched by people arranged by Tian Shubin. Be careful in everything.” Jiaye clasped his fist in a brief salute and left with his colleagues.

Shang Xuan smiled. “Let’s talk about it when we get home, all right?”

Wanting looked unhappy, yet she followed him toward the apartment. She was beginning to sense that he was keeping something from her.

Juexing shadowed them until they reached the apartment safely, then turned for home.

He had walked only a few steps when he saw Little Bell standing across the street, her expression fierce enough to tear him apart. Orange stood beside her.

“She insisted on coming. I couldn’t stop her, so…” Orange apologized.

“Why are you helping them instead of me?” Little Bell demanded.

“I am helping you. But first the immediate crisis has to be resolved.”

“That’s their family business. It has nothing to do with me. You should hand Wanting over to Lu Shengnan. Then Shang Xuan will finally give up.”

Juexing looked at the gritted teeth and the pain in her face; his resolve softened. Orange took his hand, silently warning him not to lose his footing.

“Forget it. Pretend I never said anything…” Little Bell read the helplessness in his expression, turned, and walked away. Orange said, “I’ll stay with her. You go ahead.”

Her steady gaze eased something in Juexing’s chest. “Be careful. Call me if anything happens.” He watched them leave.

Orange followed Little Bell as she wandered the streets without purpose. She neither shopped to vent her anger nor spoke a word. Silence like that was the most dangerous. On impulse Orange caught her arm. “Why don’t we go to my place? I’ll make salad for you.”

Little Bell turned. The lost look in her eyes made Orange’s heart ache. She smoothed the girl’s long hair over her shoulder and smiled. “It’s only a matter of time. If you truly like Shang Xuan, he will understand one day.”

Little Bell studied Orange’s sincere face, hesitated, then nodded. They changed direction and walked toward Orange’s apartment.

When Wanting and Shang Xuan reached her home she pulled him aside before they could even sit. “Something’s happening, isn’t it? I’ve already noticed people watching us…”

Shang Xuan asked her to sit first so he could organize his thoughts. He stammered for a long time without revealing a single concrete fact. To Wanting his expression looked as if catastrophe were about to fall; every trace of helplessness and inner conflict was written openly across his face.

“Never mind. I won’t force you. Just… please don’t leave me.”

He sat beside her, drew her into his arms, and said gently, “I won’t. This will be over soon. We’ll stay together.”

The temporary safety allowed everyone to breathe—especially Shang Xuan.

The real play was still being prepared and could erupt at any moment. Juexing and Jiaye sat facing each other, watching the busy street beyond the window, neither daring to relax. Juexing toyed with a miniature violin; Jiaye turned a realistic toy C6 pistol in his hands. Together they looked like a mismatched Holmes and Watson—two extreme eccentrics.

Little Bell had never tasted a salad so delicious. Seeing the first real smile return to her face eased Orange’s heart. While the girl ate, Orange quietly sent Juexing a short message reporting the situation. The small movement did not escape Little Bell’s notice. At once a new plan formed in her mind.

“Do you know why I like Shang Xuan?” Little Bell wiped the last traces of dressing from the corner of her mouth.

“I imagine the two of you went through something serious together.”

Little Bell looked out the window and began to speak of the one memory she could never forget.

It was the second year of junior high. A heavy rain was falling. Her father’s car had broken down and he could not come to pick her up. She opened an umbrella and decided to walk home from the city school to the suburbs—ninety minutes at the fastest. She had almost never walked the route before, so this time she wanted to try.

Wind and rain lashed the streets. Pedestrians hurried past in small groups. The night deepened the melancholy of the scenery. She kept her head down, pushing forward through the storm, and accidentally bumped into a drunk man. The nightmare began…

She no longer remembered whether there were three or four of them. They were rough, emboldened by alcohol, and dragged her into a dark side alley. She was terrified and cried for help, but the passers-by either watched coldly or pretended not to see. No one offered a hand. A single pear blossom was left to suffer.

Her cries only excited them further. They tore at her thin, rain-soaked clothes until they were in rags. Beneath the tangled hair a pair of helpless, lost eyes stared at the ground as the rainwater carried everything away. She stopped struggling, her gaze empty, ready to accept the worst—when a long shadow stretched toward her and restored a spark of hope.

At that time he still had one strange blue eye that glowed quietly in the darkness. His hair was an ordinary crew cut; the half-blond style had not yet appeared. He shouted for them to stop, but his presence alone could not overawe them. They charged. His movements were sharp and precise. In the rain he fought several men at once and put them all down with ease. In her eyes he was a timely, uncomplicated hero.

They fled in panic. Only then did she curl up and sob freely. Unluckily, some busybody had already called the police. Just as Shang Xuan crouched down beside her, two uniformed officers appeared…

“It was him! He attacked me!” Little Bell had shouted the words in front of everyone.

He never expected such a turn. There was no one present who could prove his innocence. She herself never imagined she would say such a thing—betraying the very person who had saved her, for the sake of her own fear and shame.

“He didn’t say a single word. He just went with the police. At that moment I thought, if we ever meet again he will slap me hard across the face. Or if one day I meet bad people again and he happens to be there, he will simply watch coldly like everyone else…”

By the time she reached this point, tears were falling in an uncontrolled flood.

Her jealousy drove the storeroom incident. Now the deeper wound is revealed: a traumatic assault in junior high, a rescue by Shang Xuan, and a false accusation born of panic that has shaped her obsession ever since.

Remains the steady presence who draws Little Bell away from confrontation and listens without judgment as the painful past finally surfaces.
