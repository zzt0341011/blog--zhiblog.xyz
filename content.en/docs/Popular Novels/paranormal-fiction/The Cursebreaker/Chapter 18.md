A man in a Zhongshan suit appeared at the main entrance of the hospital. Something dark flickered between his brows; the corner of his mouth twitched at irregular intervals. He stood for a moment looking up at the second-floor window of Zhao Dezhu’s ward, then began climbing the stairs one measured step at a time.

It was noon, yet the sky hung heavy and gray. A nurse had just finished giving Zhao his lunch and recording his vitals before leaving the room. Of the two officers assigned to guard him, one was still on his way back from the restroom; the other sat rigidly on the wooden chair opposite the door, ignoring the repeated protests of his empty stomach.

The man in the Zhongshan suit reached the ward and placed a hand on the doorknob with mechanical precision. The remaining officer immediately blocked him.

“Sir, without Chief Huang’s permission you can’t go in.”

The man turned, produced an official ID, and said evenly, “I’m only here to visit an old friend. I won’t stay long.”

The officer examined the credentials carefully. They identified the visitor as Director Duan Ming of the municipal government. After verifying the photo he stepped aside.

The moment the door opened, a cold draft slipped into the corridor. The officer shivered and his stomach growled again.

“Director Duan just went in—keep a good watch,” he called to his returning partner, then clutched his belly and hurried away. The second officer, still picking his teeth, sat down on the bench with a satisfied belch.

Inside the ward everything was quiet. Zhao Dezhu lay still. Duan Ming stood still.

The atmosphere thickened. The curtains were drawn slowly, shutting out every trace of daylight. The room sank into gloom.

A pair of piercing eyes locked onto the man on the bed. She could already picture the expression he would wear after the struggle—terror, shock, and a final, helpless wonder.

*Zhao Dezhu was faking illness. Do you believe it?*

She gave a dry, humorless laugh. No one else believed it. She did. And while everyone else accepted the performance, she had slipped quietly to his side.

Perhaps the cold finally became unbearable. Zhao sat up abruptly and stared at the figure standing beside the bed.

“What are you doing here? The heat’s on us. Aren’t you afraid of dying?” he whispered.

Duan Ming did not answer. He only returned a strange, unblinking stare that made Zhao’s scalp prickle.

“You…” The word stuck in Zhao’s throat. He reached up to clear whatever was blocking his airway, but no matter how he tried, something remained clamped around his neck.

A girl’s soft laughter echoed through the room. Outside, the officer on duty was too busy chatting with a newly added contact on QQ to notice anything wrong.

Zhao could not make a sound. Breathing heavily, he swung his legs off the bed and tried to reach the emergency button. Duan Ming would not allow it. With a single pointed finger he forced Zhao’s raised hand to reverse direction and slap himself hard across the face. Stars exploded behind Zhao’s eyes.

He collapsed onto the edge of the mattress, pointing at Duan Ming in mute accusation.

“Do you recognize me? Of course you don’t. I am the high-school girl who went to the government to demand the return of our land—and was raped by you. My name is Yang Xueqing.”

Each word landed like a blade.

Zhao seized an ashtray from the chair and hurled it at the window with all his strength. Duan Ming’s hand snapped out, caught it cleanly, and smashed it back. White-hot pain bloomed across Zhao’s forehead. When he wiped his face, his palm came away red.

He scrambled backward until his shoulders hit the wall. There was nowhere left to go.

“Go on, run. Look at you—so terrified of dying. If you were that afraid, why did you do so many evil things? You deserve what’s coming.”

Zhao waved his hands frantically, shaking his head, begging without a voice.

“Spare you? No one is coming to save you. This is the moment you pay the price.”

Duan Ming laughed once—a short, cold sound—and lunged. In the space of a heartbeat a clean arc of motion carried the body through the open second-floor window. A heavy, wet impact followed. Director Duan Ming of the municipal government lay in a spreading pool of blood on the pavement below.

The officers heard the screams from the street and rushed downstairs. When they finally returned to Zhao Dezhu’s ward, they found him hanging from the ceiling fan by his own necktie.

Two sharp cracks sounded from inside Juexing’s coat pocket. Jiaye heard them clearly.

“What was that?”

Juexing drew out a paper packet. Inside lay four wooden sticks, each the thickness of a chopstick and about four centimeters long. Two of them were cleanly broken.

“We missed a step.” His expression had gone rigid.

“What do you mean?”

“These four sticks represent Song Xi and the other three. The ones marked Zhao Dezhu and Duan Ming have both snapped. They’re dead.”

“Impossible. It’s broad daylight, and I left two officers there.”

Juexing closed his eyes for a moment.

“Duan Ming never visited the hospital last night. She must have possessed him after we left, used his body to reach Zhao, and then…”

“Damn it—” Jiaye drove his fist into a utility pole hard enough to make the wood shudder.

“I told you. A revenge curse is the most dangerous kind. If we’re not careful, we’ll be dragged in as well.”

“Then why keep going?”

“Because you’re my friend. And because I’m a cursebreaker. This is the task fate gave me. I have no choice but to finish it.”

Jiaye clapped him on the shoulder.

“We don’t quit until the very end.”

Juexing nodded. “Tonight we have to find her and talk. At the midpoint station. Get the last two men there somehow. I have something else to take care of first.”

He checked his watch and they parted ways. Jiaye received a call from headquarters and drove straight to the hospital to deal with the two bodies.

Bad news travels faster than good. Word of the hanging and the fatal fall reached Song Xi almost at once. Already frayed, he became nearly frantic. Tang Gufeng remained calmer. Suddenly he suggested:

“Maybe our only option is to ‘invite’ Teacher Qingmu here.”

Song Xi slapped his thigh. “Why didn’t I think of that? As long as we have Orange, Luo Juexing and Huang Jiaye will do anything to keep us alive.”

Tang Gufeng only nodded; he did not openly agree.

“No time to waste. I’ll send people to Orange’s place right now.” His hands shook as he dialed.

---

Minutes ticked past. Night fell.

The sky looked ready to rain; only scattered pedestrians remained on the streets. No thunder rolled on the horizon—only a heavy, suffocating pressure in the air.

In a large living room one man stood and one sat, both watching the grandfather clock. Every swing of the pendulum felt like a heartbeat that refused to settle into a normal rhythm.

Orange stepped out of the school gates. The last few nights spent at Juexing’s apartment had freed her from the nightmares. For a moment she considered simply moving in—yet every friend insisted a girl should keep some reserve.

She looked up at the moonless sky and greeted the slate-blue expanse in soft Japanese.

A black Mercedes slid to a stop beside her. Before she could react, a white cloth pressed over her nose. A sharp chemical smell flooded her senses. Her vision darkened. Hands guided her into the back seat.

By the time Shang Xuan reached the spot, the car was already gone. He noted the model and plate, recognized Song Xi’s handiwork, flagged a taxi, and took a different route toward Song Xi’s mansion.

As expected, every light in the mansion blazed. Yellow talismans papered the walls inside and out. The successive deaths had shaken Song Xi badly; the story was no longer just front-page news—it was feeding the city’s growing folklore of the uncanny.

Shang Xuan circled the property once. At least twenty men stood guard, following the advice of some superstitious Taoist who believed concentrated yang energy could block yin forces. In reality such theatrics no longer mattered. Real hauntings did not obey stage directions.

Two men carried the unconscious Orange into the main hall and laid her on a sofa. Song Xi lunged toward her like a drowning man seizing a floating plank. He clutched her hand tightly.

“It’s all right now. Seeing you makes me feel safe. I refuse to believe Luo Juexing will abandon you.”

A Taoist in full robes appeared behind him and tapped Song Xi’s shoulder with a peach-wood sword.

“Mr. Song, I cast a bamboo-slip divination for you. Tonight carries great misfortune. If you do not open an altar at once and perform the rites—”

“Get lost. Another fraud after money. Throw him out!” With Orange in his possession Song Xi no longer needed counterfeit priests.

“Police are here…” Tang Gufeng had noticed the flashing lights outside.

“Let them come. I’m not leaving this house, and neither should you if you want to stay alive.” Song Xi kept his grip on Orange’s hand, studying her sleeping face. She looked like a ripe peach—pale skin flushed with soft color. He wanted to take a bite.

Shang Xuan snapped his fingers once. An idea locked into place. He needed only one prop to walk straight into the mansion.

Three police cars stopped at the gate. Several officers climbed out. One of them looked slightly off—the corner of his mouth twitched at irregular intervals.

Shang Xuan, already disguised as one of the officers, fell into step with the group. He did not notice that the man walking ahead of him was equally out of place.

---

“Same method as before.”

Juexing stood alone at the midpoint station. Joss paper drifted down from the darkening sky in ever greater numbers. Because of the “incidents” on train 5120, service had been suspended. The empty platform held only the silent tracks and a single fire basin, lonely under the falling spirit money.

### Character Notes

**Yang Xueqing (the High-School Girl)**  
Her identity is finally spoken aloud inside the hospital room. The rape by Zhao Dezhu, the forced land seizure, and her father’s suicide form the unhealed core of the revenge curse. She now moves freely between bodies, using possession as her primary weapon.

**Luo Juexing**  
The snapping of the two wooden sticks confirms the deaths in real time. His decision to confront the ghost directly at the midpoint station that night shows he has accepted both the danger and the necessity of negotiation rather than pure exorcism.

**Song Xi**  
Panicked and increasingly unhinged. Kidnapping Orange is his last desperate attempt to force protection from Juexing and Jiaye. His lingering predatory gaze at the unconscious girl underscores how little he has changed.

**Shang Xuan**  
Once again positions himself as the wild card. Disguising himself as a police officer to enter the mansion places him inside the final confrontation before anyone else realizes he is there.

**Duan Ming & Zhao Dezhu**  
The third and fourth names on the list of six. Their deaths—one by forced suicide, one by possessed self-defenestration—prove the curse can strike in daylight under police guard when possession is involved.

**The Wooden Sticks**  
A simple but effective tracking charm. Each stick is linked to one of the remaining targets; their physical breaking signals death at a distance.
