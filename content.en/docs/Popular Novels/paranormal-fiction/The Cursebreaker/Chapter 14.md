Train 5120 had been in service for only a week and was already receiving glowing reviews. Reporters swarmed it, desperate for a story, yet nothing noteworthy surfaced—until one of Yu Xun’s colleagues captured a single strange photograph. Only then did the whispers begin.

“Is it real? Did you actually see it?” Yu Xun slammed both hands onto the man’s desk, furious that he had acted without permission.

“This is the only copy. With that attitude, I’m not sure I feel like showing you.” He closed his laptop and crossed his legs, shutting her out completely.

Yu Xun’s expression cooled into a thin smile.

“Fine. Then you can wait for the evil spirit to attach itself to you. Don’t say I didn’t warn you.”

She turned to leave. The man suddenly remembered the Medusa footage Yu Xun had risked her life to obtain. A chill ran through him.

“Wait.”

She paused.

“If this really is a curse,” she said, lowering her voice until it sounded almost sinister in the quiet office, “I know how to break it.”

He hesitated, then bargained. “I can give you the photo… but I should get something out of it. I went to a lot of trouble.”

Yu Xun’s intimidating aura made him falter. She cut him off.

“If this turns into a major story, you’ll receive a very generous tip fee. Deal?”

He opened a drawer, took out a folder stored inside a box, and carefully extracted the photograph. The moment he looked at it, the color drained from his face.

“Hey… are you all right?”

He swallowed hard and handed the print to her in silence.

Yu Xun examined the image. The carriage was completely empty, yet the train was clearly racing at high speed. There was no figure of any kind.

“Are you playing games with me?” She flung the photo back at him.

His body began to shake uncontrollably. When he glanced under the desk, a pair of deathly pale hands shot out and locked around his legs. He screamed, toppled from the chair, and curled into a ball behind it.

Yu Xun was not frightened. She had faced the Medusa curse herself and found this far less impressive. She peered under the desk. Apart from a camera that had fallen to the floor, there was nothing.

Smirking, she picked up the camera, quietly removed the film, and offered a few empty words of comfort.

“It’s only a hallucination. I told you I’d help.”

She dropped the camera on the desk and walked out without looking back.

With the film in hand she went straight to the darkroom. If the print was flawed, the negative might still hold the original image. Wearing gloves, she held the strip up to the light and examined every frame. One negative looked wrong. She set to work developing it.

Tens of minutes later she lifted the print from the chemical bath with tweezers. An empty train hurtled through the frame. In the center of a carriage stood a blurred figure—long black hair, a high-school uniform.

Yu Xun brought the photo closer. The blur seemed larger. She blinked. The figure had turned. It was now facing her.

She dropped the print. Her body trembled. She backed against the door, yet still could not look away. The darkroom lights flickered. The photograph on the floor began to thrash. The silhouette swelled until it filled the entire frame, then burst outward into a single bloodshot, hollow gray eye.

“Ah—!”

Orange jolted awake from the nightmare and sat upright, switching on the bedside lamp. The apartment was silent. The clock on the table read exactly 3:00 a.m. Ever since she had ridden that train, the same dream had woken her at this hour for two nights in a row.

She got up, went to the kitchen, and poured a glass of water, hoping to calm herself. A sudden wind slammed against the windows. Startled, she dropped the glass. It shattered. As she crouched to gather the pieces, a sharp creak of an opening door cut through the quiet.

She listened intently. Silence returned. Holding her breath, she threw the fragments into the trash. The moment she prepared to leave the kitchen, the soft sound of bare feet on wooden flooring drifted from the living room.

Orange froze behind the kitchen door. The footsteps continued to pace, refusing to leave. She barely dared to breathe. Slowly she peered out. Moonlight through the swaying curtains illuminated a pair of pale, slender calves in the center of the room. Her eyes widened in terror. She clamped a hand over her mouth and sank down.

The legs turned and began walking toward the kitchen. Orange retreated deeper inside, trembling.

Footsteps sounded in the hallway—her landlady. The knock and the woman’s voice yanked Orange back to reality. When she looked again, the living room was empty.

She scrambled up and rushed to the door.

“Mrs. Xu, I—”

The instant her hand closed around the knob, a wave of cold struck her heart. What she held was not the doorknob. It was a pale, emaciated hand.

Orange screamed and fell backward. The wind howled harder. The air filled with the scent of death. She searched the darkness wildly, but the room remained empty of any visible presence.

She staggered into the bedroom, seized her phone, and dialed Juexing.

The ringtone woke him. He almost ignored it—until he saw the same unknown number that had called him before. Sleep vanished. He answered at once.

“Juexing, help me—it’s Orange—she’s in my apartment—”

That was all he heard. The line filled with the rushing roar of a train, followed by a girl’s soft laughter that grew louder and louder until the call cut off with a sharp click.

“Orange—”

Juexing knew something was badly wrong. He threw on clothes and raced for the elevator. In the lobby he nearly collided with Granny Luo Shen, who had just returned. She pulled a newspaper from her bag and tossed it to him.

“Read the headline.”

Juexing unfolded it. The front page screamed:

**GHOSTLY GIRL APPEARS ON NEW TRAIN**

He crushed the paper in his fist, flagged a taxi on the street, and headed straight for Orange’s building. The moment she opened the door she collapsed into his arms. Whatever had happened, it had clearly terrified her.

“It’s all right now.”

He heated a cup of milk and handed it to her. She curled into the corner of the sofa, clutching the warm cup, eyes fixed on the kitchen.

Juexing glanced toward the kitchen, then opened the newspaper and scanned the article. It mentioned only that someone had taken a paranormal photograph; no deeper clues were given.

“Did you see her too?” Orange asked suddenly.

“Yes.”

“I’ve woken up from the same nightmare two nights in a row. And it’s getting more real… as if she’s right beside me.” She kept her head down, glancing around the room as though the girl might still be hiding in a corner.

Juexing set the paper on the table and sat beside her.

“It’s okay. Maybe she’s trying to tell us something. If this really is a curse, I’ll do everything I can to end it. I have to protect the person who might change my fate.”

Orange turned to look at him. Tears slipped down her cheeks. She rested her head lightly against his chest and wept.

“Hey—that’s hot!”

Only then did she notice she had spilled the milk all over him.

“I’m sorry… did it burn you?” She set the cup down and grabbed tissues to wipe his shirt. Suddenly Juexing caught both of her hands.

“I’m the one who’s sorry. I was wrong before. Can we start over?”

Orange pulled her hands free and moved to the other sofa, head lowered, saying nothing.

Juexing brushed at the remaining milk stains.

“It’s fine. I should go.”

“Don’t. Stay with me. I don’t know if she’ll come back.”

“Forget it. You’re clearly unwilling. I’ll leave.”

He stood. Orange jumped up and caught his arm, refusing to let him go.

“Will you forgive me?” he asked.

Instead of answering she stepped behind him and wrapped her arms around his waist. Juexing smiled faintly.

“Everything will pass. No matter how terrifying the crisis, I’ll break it.”

He stayed until she finally fell asleep. Looking at her young, exhausted face, his chest ached. Granny Luo Shen had been right. If the trouble had begun because of him, then choosing to run would only make him despise himself. He drew the blanket over Orange, closed the bedroom door, and settled onto the living-room sofa for the night.

Tomorrow he would investigate the newly opened train line. Instinct told him something was wrong with train 5120. How it connected to the high-school girl they had both seen remained unclear—but he would not jump to conclusions until he had looked deeper.

### Character Notes

**Qingmu Chengzi (Orange)**  
The nightmare has breached her waking world. Pale legs, a skeletal hand on the doorknob, and a phone call hijacked by the train itself have shattered whatever remaining skepticism she possessed. Her plea for Juexing to stay marks a quiet turning point in their relationship.

**Luo Juexing**  
The moment the call displays the number 5120, he moves without hesitation. His decision to remain overnight and his promise to protect “the person who might change my fate” show that the resolve formed under the meteor in the previous chapter is already taking root.

**Yu Xun**  
Resourceful and ruthless in pursuit of a story. She extracts the film, develops the moving image, and becomes the first person to witness the photograph’s transformation into a single bloodshot eye—evidence that the entity tied to train 5120 is growing more aggressive.

**The High-School Girl**  
No longer confined to fleeting glimpses or dreams. She now appears in developed photographs, invades private apartments at 3 a.m., and can seize telephone lines. Her presence is expanding beyond the train itself.

**Granny Luo Shen**  
Appears once more at a critical juncture, delivering the newspaper that confirms the phenomenon has entered public awareness. Her timing remains unnervingly precise.
