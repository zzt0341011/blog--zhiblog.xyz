The abundant lunch left an unforgettable impression on Juexing, Jiaye and Yu Xun. They began to question the so-called Japanese cuisine they had eaten back home.

After resting for nearly an hour, Orange changed into a cool summer outfit and transformed into a pure, youthful girl, showing Juexing and Jiaye a completely different side of herself. In the afternoon she arranged the itinerary, starting from the pedestrian street and introducing them one by one to several important places in Shibuya. Under her guidance the group began their half-day tour of Shibuya—memories that would remain especially beautiful. Of course, as a seasoned journalist, Yu Xun never forgot her digital camera; to guarantee full-day power she had even prepared two spare batteries.

The moment the sunlight softened slightly, the pedestrian street grew lively. Because summer had arrived, the streets were filled with “scenery” that refused to stop until every man’s eyes had been treated to ice-cream-level delight.

From a short distance three young women dressed in eye-catching fashion approached and called out greetings to Orange—high-school classmates.

Jiaye was instantly drawn to their exaggerated figures. Yu Xun took it in stride, raised her camera, snapped several quick shots, then pouted and looked the other way.

At that moment two handsome young men drove a Mercedes out of a side lane. The instant they spotted Yu Xun they slowed, whistling to catch her attention. Jiaye’s ears were especially sharp; he recognized the teasing sound at once and quickly turned toward Yu Xun. She was busy photographing a few cute children nearby and paid the Mercedes drivers no attention at all.

“Hey, you’re hopeless—flirting with my classmates right in front of Yu Xun!” Orange whispered.

“I was only playing along. You know there’s only Yu Xun in my heart,” Jiaye answered seriously.

“I don’t know that.” Orange waved a hand.

“Hmph. As long as Juexing knows.” Jiaye glanced at him.

“I don’t know either…” Juexing replied.

“Tch. You two really are a married couple. I give up!” Jiaye said unhappily.

Orange could not help a quiet laugh. She linked arms with Juexing and pointed to a crossroads ahead. “There’s a cold-drink shop over there with excellent business. My father reserved seats—let’s go rest for a while.”

“Wow, your dad is a real saint. He knew we’d get thirsty walking around and prepared drinks in advance.” Jiaye praised.

“Hehe. Every summer the family likes to come here every few days for authentic iced fruit juice. And I’ll tell you a secret—the strawberries used for the strawberry shaved ice are grown by my father himself.” Orange handed the reservation ticket to the waiter, who accepted it with both hands, bowed deeply, and guided them to the reserved seats. Just as Orange sat down, a young man in waiter’s uniform carrying a tray approached. The moment their eyes met he exclaimed, “It’s you…”

“Dou Dou?” Orange had never expected to see him here again.

Jiaye quickly sized him up, exchanged a glance with Yu Xun, and both nodded with the same unspoken thought: *Could this be a first love?*

Juexing made a gentlemanly “please” gesture. The young man sat down beside Orange without the slightest hesitation, as if the other three did not exist, and began talking only with her.

“Have you been working here the whole time?” Orange asked.

“Yes. Ever since you graduated high school and left Japan I’ve stayed here, because I believed you would appear again.” Dou Dou’s heart was racing; he had truly seen her once more.

Jiaye nudged Juexing with his chin, signaling that it was time to clarify identities, but Juexing did nothing of the sort—he simply drank his cold beverage in silence.

Orange and Dou Dou talked for fifteen minutes before the young man was forced to stand; the manager had already reminded him more than once that this was working hours. He left reluctantly with his tray, but not before slipping a small slip of paper into Orange’s hand.

Orange turned to look at the silent Juexing; at the same moment he was looking at her.

“It’s over. Relationship crisis!” Jiaye muttered around his straw.

“It’s a phone number. You don’t mind, do you?” Orange unfolded the paper and laid it in front of everyone. She did not want Juexing to misunderstand; the earlier scene had been easy to misread.

“He was your high-school classmate, right?” Juexing said.

“Yes. We were in the same class for two years of high school. After I went to Peking University we rarely kept in touch. I didn’t know he had been working here the whole time.” She explained calmly.

“Was he your first love?” Yu Xun asked.

“No. In high school he liked my deskmate. Because he was rather introverted, many of his messages were passed through me to her…”

“No wonder. He didn’t catch the deskmate and ended up developing feelings for you. The world is strange that way.” Jiaye shook his head.

Yu Xun patted him. “Can you stop trying to drive a wedge between Juexing and Orange? Listening to you, anyone would think you *want* them to have problems.”

“What did you say?” Orange and Juexing asked in unison.

Yu Xun quickly waved her hands. “You’ve misunderstood me. What I meant was…”

“Forget it. Right now one of them is cement and the other is water—once mixed they’re hard to separate.” Jiaye said.

“The meaning is roughly right, but please don’t use cement and water as the central metaphors.” Juexing nodded.

“You’re really not angry?” Orange looked at him with doubtful eyes.

Juexing closed his eyes, shook his head, and said, “I trust you the same way you trust me. If I doubted your feelings for me, I would never have placed something so precious on your ring finger.”

Orange smiled and rested her head on his shoulder. Dou Dou watched the scene from the side. In truth he had already guessed Juexing’s identity. From every angle they were still the most well-matched pair. Fortunately he himself had gotten what he wished for and was now with Orange’s former deskmate. This reunion was simply so he could invite Orange to their wedding.

After leaving the cold-drink shop they headed toward the street of department stores and specialty shops. After an hour of browsing they would take the bus home and arrive just in time for dinner.

Along the way the four of them talked and laughed, occasionally taking photos here, occasionally buying something there. Before long the two men demonstrated their most valuable quality: their hands gradually filled with bags of every size.

The pure pleasure of shopping is something almost everyone enjoys—especially the majority of female friends. The accompanying gentlemen, however, naturally become free porters. Apart from the occasional envy of passerby, they basically spend the entire time accompanying the bags and lose their freedom.

Fortunately both Orange and Yu Xun were considerate. They bought only a few things. The purpose of coming to Shibuya was simply to play and experience foreign atmosphere; shopping was only a small part of the itinerary.

Time passed quickly. The sun transformed into the setting sun and slowly sank behind the western hills. Orange’s group returned laden with purchases. The moment they stepped through the door they saw two additional people in the living room—one was the waiter Dou Dou they had met earlier; the other, a more conservatively dressed young woman, was most likely Orange’s former deskmate.

“Orange!” she called out in greeting.

Orange hurried over and shared a deep embrace. “Miyuki! We haven’t seen each other in almost six years.”

Miyuki nodded. “You almost forgot me, didn’t you?”

“Never. How could I forget you? Work kept me from staying in touch. I’m sorry.”

“It’s fine. I heard you were home, so I specifically waited for you to return. I wanted to tell you in person—I’m marrying Dou Dou. The wedding is in five days, during the international oil-painting exhibition at the art gallery.”

“Yes. After graduation Miyuki went into business with her father and later became involved in art. This time she’s leading a consortium group holding a charity exhibition in Japan.” Dou Dou added.

“You’re doing well—turned into a rich young lady. Dou Dou, you have to cherish and protect Miyuki properly.” Orange smiled.

They introduced themselves and chatted. After the invitations were handed over, Mizusawa Kaori invited everyone to the table and began the sacred program of dinner.

Once Dou Dou and Miyuki had been seen off, Orange took Jiaye and Yu Xun to look at the guest rooms prepared for them. Mizusawa Kaori seized the opportunity to call Juexing to the backyard, beneath a green jujube tree.

“Auntie, you wanted to speak with me?” Juexing asked politely.

“Hehe. Nothing important—just a chat.” She spoke in quite fluent Chinese.

Juexing looked at her with slight puzzlement. She smiled. “I’m also a teacher. I mainly teach Japanese and Chinese.”

“Oh—forgive my rudeness.”

“However, I can tell you are not simply a teacher.” She seemed to have perceived something unusual about him.

“I’m not sure I understand.”

“What I mean is that you should possess a more special ability, correct?”

From her eyes Juexing read her meaning. She had probably already guessed his other identity.

“To be honest, I am a cursebreaker.” He kept his voice as low as possible, yet in the quiet night it still sounded especially sharp.

“I guessed as much long ago. Because when Orange is with you, a certain power that exists in her body is weakened.”

“A certain power?” Juexing felt as if he were listening through fog.

Mizusawa Kaori rolled up the sleeve of her right arm. Near the shoulder was a blue birthmark, faded somewhat by time, yet its shape was identical to the mark on his own arm.

“I am of mixed blood. My father was Chinese, so I was born with this birthmark. People who carry such a mark either enjoy great wealth and fortune for life, or must bear a fate far more tragic than ordinary people… Fortunately my family has been peaceful. Now I only worry about Orange.” Her expression had grown rather grim.

Juexing thought to himself: *Could this be the difference between Shang Xuan and me—one walking the path of acting for heaven, the other forced to taste all the bitterness of the world?*

### Character Notes

**Luo Juexing**  
Spends a light-hearted afternoon in Shibuya, then faces a quiet, serious conversation with Orange’s mother under the jujube tree. The matching birthmark and the warning about Orange’s “power” open a new layer of the larger mystery.

**Qingmu Chengzi (Orange)**  
Guides the group through Shibuya, handles an unexpected reunion with an old classmate with calm honesty, and remains unaware of the deeper conversation taking place in the backyard.

**Mizusawa Kaori**  
Orange’s mother. Reveals her own blue birthmark, her mixed Chinese-Japanese heritage, and her growing concern that Orange may carry a heavier fate than ordinary people.

**Dou Dou & Miyuki**  
High-school classmates. Dou Dou once liked Miyuki through Orange; now the two are engaged and will marry during the art exhibition in five days.

**Huang Jiaye & Meng Yu Xun**  
Provide comic relief and observational commentary during the street scenes and the cold-drink shop encounter.
