Orange recovered quickly from the kidnapping. Shang Xuan followed Jiaye’s car until he watched Juexing and Orange walk into the apartment together; only then did he leave with peace of mind. In his palm he tightly gripped the silver-white bracelet wound with a red cord—whether out of lingering attachment or some other purpose, even he could not say.

By the time the two of them returned to Orange’s place it was already three in the afternoon. Little Bell had waited the entire noon without any sign of Orange. She worried something had happened, yet she also feared that if she left and Orange returned to an empty house the girl would worry even more. So she could only sit and wait… and wait.

The moment the door opened, Juexing saw Little Bell sitting in the living room and seemed to understand everything at once. He strode over, grabbed her by the arm before she could react, and said, “Enough. Stop interfering in Shang Xuan and Wanting’s affairs. You almost got Orange killed—do you realize that?”

Little Bell’s face was blank with innocence. She had no idea what had befallen Orange.

“It’s all right, Juexing. I came back safely. And she’s been home the whole time—she couldn’t have been the one who tipped off Lu Shengnan.” Orange pried his hand free and pulled Little Bell close.

A sudden flash of anger rose in Little Bell. She shouted at Juexing, “Yes! Everything was my plan. I wished Wanting would disappear from my sight. I wasn’t wrong—” She shook off Orange’s hand, punched Juexing hard in the stomach, gave a sharp “Hmph,” and ran out.

Orange started after her, but Juexing caught her arm. “Don’t chase her. Let her calm down alone.”

“Aren’t you worried about her?”

“She was strong enough to punch me. What is there to worry about? The urgent matter is finishing Wanting’s case.” He rubbed his stomach and sat down. Orange could only close the door and sit beside him.

“Do you have any ideas?”

“From the materials we have, three years ago Lu Shengnan probably joined forces with Tian Shubin and his present wife Zhuzhu to plot the seizure of the merchant Lu Zhengyao’s fortune—worth over a hundred million. When Lu Wanting rushed back from abroad after receiving the news of her father’s death, she sensed something was wrong. Less than a week later she was found dead of asphyxiation in an office inside her father’s building.” Juexing had pieced every document together into this single conclusion.

“Then we can file a lawsuit and reopen the case.”

“The problem is that none of these assumptions have solid evidence. It’s a case of ‘dead men tell no tales,’ so it has never been possible to close it properly.”

“If Wanting simply appears in court, won’t the truth come out?”

“Do you think a judge will allow someone who has been dead for three years to testify? I went to the cemetery and privately opened the coffin. Wanting’s body is lying inside perfectly intact. But one detail still bothers me.”

“What detail?”

“If this is truly a curse, why does Wanting seem to have no memory of Lu Shengnan at all? Put simply—she never appears to be seeking revenge. She only wants to live a quiet life.”

“In that case we could meet Lu Shengnan privately, try to settle things peacefully, free Wanting from the shadow of the past, and let her and Shang Xuan live properly.”

Juexing was silent for a moment. “That would be going against heaven. A soul cannot bind itself to a living human. It would only destroy Shang Xuan.”

“Then what? This way is impossible, that way is impossible, and the whole affair may not stay quiet much longer. Once it becomes big news the storm will rise again. What kind of pressure will Shang Xuan face…” Orange dared not imagine the incalculable pain that would follow exposure.

“It seems we can only negotiate with Lu Shengnan first—buy time, then find a way to help Shang Xuan.” For the moment he could think of nothing better.

“By the way… if Wanting really is a curse, what will you do?” Orange had not wanted to ask the question, yet it remained the most severe barrier standing between him and Shang Xuan.

Juexing looked at her. “I will do my duty. If Shang Xuan insists on remaining lost in delusion, I only hope you leave this city and choose another way to live.”

“Here we go again. Have you forgotten our agreement? No talk of separation.”

“Some things, once they appear, leave no choice. It isn’t that I don’t want to—it’s that I cannot. You understand, don’t you?”

Orange smiled, rested her head against his shoulder, and said, “If the two of you really come to blows, I stand on your side. I won’t let Shang Xuan hurt you.”

Matters had already reached this point. Wanting had nearly recovered every memory. Shang Xuan sat opposite her. The thought that the hard-won happiness might end because of this absurd situation sent sharp pain through her chest again and again.

Shang Xuan turned the situation over and over in his mind. The only course left was to confront Lu Shengnan openly, then take Wanting and leave the city—disappear without a trace. He stood, walked to her, crouched down, and took both her hands.

“After everything we’ve been through, there’s nothing left I can hide from you. Come with me. Let’s leave this place, go somewhere no one knows us, and start over.”

Wanting shook her head and pushed his hands away. “I can’t leave. This is my home. I will not go.”

“We can’t stay here. There must be some place that will take us in. We can go to Canada, to Singapore, to Thailand…”

“No. I never planned to take the property back from my cousin. I only want to keep this home.” She walked to the small shrine in the corner of the living room. Shang Xuan rose and watched as she pulled aside the red curtain that covered it. Two memorial tablets stood there—one for her father Lu Zhengyao, the other for herself.

“It can’t be… it isn’t real…” Shang Xuan wrapped his arms around her from behind. Her body began to grow cold, as if everything had returned to the moment they first met at the elevator.

He had brought her back to this house. In his eyes it was an eighth-floor apartment. In the eyes of outsiders it was merely a place someone temporarily looked after.

He remembered that he had never approached the shrine closely. In truth, at that time he had already seen what lay beneath the red curtain. The two tablets had told him plainly that she was only a wisp of residual soul—a shadow that could vanish in the blink of an eye.

“I thought I could pass for living. I thought I could truly love someone. But when all the memories returned, I finally understood—I was only using you…” Wanting’s voice was cold.

“That isn’t true. If we leave this place, none of those things will exist anymore. Don’t you want to travel with me, to be free together?”

“I do. I think about it every moment. But I cannot escape this cage. You are a cursebreaker. Doing this breaks the precepts. Aren’t you afraid of heaven’s punishment?”

“Heaven’s punishment…” Shang Xuan spoke with a careless, almost mocking expression, stepped back several paces, then overturned the table with a shout and collapsed onto the floor.

Wanting kept her back to him. Only now did she understand how hard it was to let go once one had fallen deeply into love. She was clearly nothing more than a shadow, yet she had dared to hope for Shang Xuan’s feelings and had stolen his heart.

“I’m sorry. Can you forget me after this? Whatever path we take from here, let’s have no more entanglement. All right?” She was the first to lay the cards on the table.

Shang Xuan fumbled in his pocket, took out his wallet, and looked at the photograph inside—the two of them at the carousel amusement park. How enviable their smiles had been then. How short a time it had taken to reach this point.

“Wait for me. Don’t go anywhere. I’m going to book plane tickets right now. I’ll be back soon.” He wiped the tears from the corners of his eyes and rushed out the door. Wanting could no longer hold back her emotions; she sank to the floor and began to cry…

Shang Xuan ran the entire way and burst into a ticket office, buying two tickets to Singapore for the day after tomorrow. He had not walked a single li with the tickets in hand when an unexpected stick struck him from behind. Darkness swallowed his vision and he collapsed.

Little Bell walked aimlessly along the street. People came and went, yet she could not see a single figure that moved her. Perhaps in this world no one else could ever obsess her the way Shang Xuan did, because no one else had given her memories so deep. Sometimes love was simple: it meant remaining silently behind someone and simply watching.

A ringtone sounded from her pocket. She answered like a wooden puppet. An unfamiliar voice spoke:

“Shang Xuan is in my hands. If you can make Wanting come alone, I will release him. If she exceeds the time limit, I’m sorry—you will only be able to collect his corpse.”

“Don’t hurt Shang Xuan. Tell me the time and place. I’ll go find her at once.”

“Remember—do not call the police. I only want to settle this privately. I don’t want to involve innocent people. The time is eight o’clock tonight, at the scrap-yard in the north of the city. Only you and Wanting may come. If anyone else appears…”

The line went dead. Little Bell turned and ran toward the apartment where Shang Xuan lived. It was already six in the evening. She ignored the empty protest of her stomach; she wanted only Shang Xuan’s safety.

Jiaye received a call from a colleague: Shang Xuan had left the building in a hurry and had not returned. Lu Wanting remained inside for the moment, showing no movement.

Jiaye felt the situation growing extremely difficult. He and Yu Xun finished organizing the last of the materials and drove toward Shang Xuan’s apartment. The moment the car stopped they saw Little Bell, gasping for breath, run into the building.

“Isn’t that the little girl who always trails after Shang Xuan?” Yu Xun asked.

“This is bad. Looking at her, she’s going after Wanting. We have to get up there now.” Jiaye shouted.

They exchanged a glance and ran into the apartment one after the other.

### Character Notes

**Luo Juexing**  
Confronts Little Bell for nearly getting Orange killed, then faces the impossible choice between duty as a cursebreaker and the cost to Shang Xuan’s heart. He proposes temporary negotiation with Lu Shengnan only to buy time.

**Qingmu Chengzi (Orange)**  
Defends Little Bell while still recovering from the kidnapping. She refuses any talk of separation and declares she will stand with Juexing if the two men come into open conflict.

**Little Bell**  
Her plan has already caused Orange’s abduction. After being confronted she flees, only to receive a ransom call that places Shang Xuan’s life in her hands.

**Shang Xuan & Lu Wanting**  
Wanting recovers her full memories and the truth of her own death. Shang Xuan tries to flee the city with her; she refuses to abandon her home. Moments later he is ambushed while buying plane tickets.

**Lu Shengnan’s Side**  
Having failed to seize Wanting, they shift to the next move: capture Shang Xuan and force a private resolution at the northern scrap-yard.
