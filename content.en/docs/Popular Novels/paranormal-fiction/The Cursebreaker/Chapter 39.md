The ambulance, its red lights flashing seductively, carried Wan Ting and Shang Xuan to the hospital. Firefighters searched every corner of the supermarket and finally located the offending trash bin. It had been nothing more than a farce. After a brief conversation with the store manager they packed up and left. Once the false alarm was over, the supermarket reopened as if the entire incident had been a staged performance.

Doctors and nurses wheeled Wan Ting into the emergency room on a stretcher. Shang Xuan had no choice but to wait outside. His nerves had not yet settled. In his arms he still held the thick stack of documents he had bought from the informant.

He turned and sat on a chair in the corridor. A few sheets slipped free. He picked them up, glanced at the contents, and froze.

The emergency-room light went out. The doctor and nurses emerged looking satisfied and signaled that he could go in. Shang Xuan collected himself, folded the papers neatly, and only then stepped inside.

She opened exhausted eyes and looked at him standing beside the bed. She wanted to smile, but her stiff face would not cooperate.

“It’s all right… it’s all right…” Thick worry sat between Shang Xuan’s brows. She knew she had frightened him badly.

“I’m sorry I made you worry.” She tried to sit up, but her body felt filled with lead and refused to move.

Shang Xuan moved to her side, helped her upright, and looked down at her hands.

Wan Ting sensed something was wrong with him. She reached up and brushed his cheek. The skin was no longer cold—it was warm.

“What’s wrong? Are you feeling unwell?”

Shang Xuan’s gaze shifted to the documents in his hand. Wan Ting followed it. She stretched out both hands to take them, but he stepped back, tore the entire stack into pieces, tore the pieces again, and without mercy threw the scraps into the nearby trash bin.

He returned to the bed, pulled her tightly into his arms, and said, “No matter what has already happened, or what will happen, I will never let go of your hand…”

Wan Ting finally managed a smile. She wrapped her arms around him in return.

“Yes. I’ll stay by your side. I won’t leave.”

The scene of their embrace was witnessed by an old man with a white beard sitting in a wheelchair. He had intended to leave, but out of sympathy he knocked on the door and motioned for Shang Xuan to step outside.

Shang Xuan settled Wan Ting back against the pillows. “Sleep here tonight. Tomorrow we’ll go home.”

She nodded with a faint smile. Only then did he walk out.

“Young man, do you know what you’re doing?” the white-bearded old man asked.

“And you are…?”

“I am the doctor who treated Lu Wanting for claustrophobia three years ago. My surname is Li.” He offered nothing more.

“And what of it, Dr. Li?”

“Have you not read the reports?” Dr. Li glanced at Wan Ting lying inside the emergency room, his voice calm.

“What reports?”

“Ah… perhaps everything is simply fate. Since you deliberately pretend not to know, I will not meddle further.” The old doctor sighed, turned his wheelchair, and left.

Shang Xuan watched him go, then returned to Wan Ting’s side. Sometimes he doubted whether his own senses had gone wrong. Yet there was no reason so many people could be looking at someone who had died three years earlier.

The thick stack of documents had cost him five hundred yuan. Nearly complete, they recorded everything about the merchant Lu Zhengyao and his daughter Lu Wanting from three years ago. Yet the papers only mentioned her disappearance—they never stated that she had died.

She slept. Her delicate hand rested in his. It was a hand that invited longing, skin as pure as white jade. Shang Xuan held it and sat quietly beside the bed. The distance was real. The warmth was real. Her breathing, her heartbeat, her smile—none of it felt illusory. Why, then, did the people around them treat her as if she were already ashes?

Moonlight climbed the treetops and spilled into the room, laying soft steps across the floor beside the bed. The steps stretched long and far, matching the length of Shang Xuan’s thoughts. He wished time would stop on this night. He wanted to go nowhere and think of nothing.

He feared that when the sun rose he would have to face everything he dared not face: her background, the knots in her heart, even the news of her death…

He lowered his head, rested against her, emptied his mind, and quietly drifted into sleep.

After she was moved to a regular ward the surroundings grew even quieter. Most of the nurses and doctors had finished their shifts and left. The corridor fell silent.

Outside the ward a girl stood with one hand against the glass, watching the two people inside by the faint moonlight. Why did fate always love twists? Why was the person she liked holding another woman’s hand?

She could find no answer. Tears that had been circling in her eyes finally slipped free and turned cold. Her calm expression hardened into something sharp, as if she were already forming the next plan.

The following day Jiaye finished organizing the materials, picked up Yu Xun at her place, and the two of them went to Juexing’s house. When the doorbell rang, Juexing had just come out of the bathroom.

“These are everything you asked for. Nothing missing.” Jiaye dropped a large document bag onto the table, walked to the refrigerator, opened the fridge, and exclaimed, “Orange is going to make an excellent wife someday. Look how neatly she arranged everything.” He took out a can of orange juice and a can of Sprite.

Juexing did not answer. He began reading the files on his own, flipping back and forth, filtering out everything directly related to Wan Ting.

“Juexing, there’s something I think I should tell you.” Yu Xun interrupted him.

He looked up.

“When I was going through the report on Lu Wanting’s death by asphyxiation, this is what happened.” She had secretly brought the entire file with her.

“Blank pages?” Juexing asked, looking at the sheet she pointed to.

“Yes. I am certain detailed content used to be there. But the moment I looked back, it had become blank paper.”

Juexing concentrated. From a supernatural perspective such an event was not unusual. Yet as a cursebreaker Shang Xuan should have noticed—unless one of two possibilities was true: either he already knew and was pretending otherwise, or the woman was not Wan Ting at all, but someone who looked extremely similar.

“Hey, are you all right?” Yu Xun noticed the change in his expression.

“I’m fine. For now, please don’t interfere in this matter. I want to observe for a few days.”

“What’s there to observe?” Jiaye cut in. “It’s obvious Shang Xuan has been blinded by a beauty and is out there dating a ghost!”

Yu Xun nudged him and shook her head, signaling him not to be so absolute.

“Anyway, I’m asking you both. If there’s nothing else, I’d like to be alone for a while.”

Jiaye and Yu Xun had no choice but to leave. Once they were in the car Jiaye muttered unhappily, “Honestly. Shang Xuan has always been at odds with Juexing. Now he’s dumped this mess on him. Didn’t you see how troubled Juexing looked?”

“Even though they don’t get along, they still have six years of history. I’m worried that if this really is a curse, what is Juexing supposed to do…” Yu Xun felt uneasy about the impossible position Juexing was about to face.

“Yeah, it’s a real dilemma. What can we even do?” Jiaye glanced up at the window of Juexing’s apartment.

“I still remember something about that report. Before she died there was an inheritance dispute. Why don’t we start from there? Maybe we’ll find a clue.”

“You think her death was suspicious?” Jiaye’s professional instincts kicked in.

“Yes. I remember Wan Ting had a cousin named Lu Shengnan. According to the will, he was the heir, not her.”

“So her ‘resurrection’ has a premise. What we’re seeing now is only the opening. Bigger conspiracies will follow.” Jiaye spoke in a strange tone.

“Enough talk. Drive me to the office. We’ll split up and you can pick me up for lunch.” Yu Xun patted his shoulder.

“Yes, ma’am!” Jiaye gave a crisp salute, started the car, and headed toward her company.

Shang Xuan completed the discharge paperwork at the counter and took Wan Ting back to her apartment. She requested one day of leave from the supermarket and would not return until the day after tomorrow.

From the moment they left the hospital until they stepped through her door, Shang Xuan’s expression never changed—neither smiling nor especially melancholy. She could not know that from last night until now his mind had been locked in a fierce internal struggle.

“Xuan… did I do something that made you unhappy?” she asked softly.

Shang Xuan looked at the girl sitting beside him. He wanted so badly to voice every question bottled up inside. Even if the truth matched what the documents said, at least he would no longer be trapped in this torment.

Seeing his troubled face made Wan Ting’s own heart ache. Suddenly she remembered the scene in the hospital when he had torn the thick stack of papers to shreds.

“I know I shouldn’t have kept things from you. I was only afraid that once I told you everything, you would abandon me the same way everyone else did.” She rubbed her hands together restlessly.

Shang Xuan took both of her hands in his.

“I want to help you. I want to free you from that barrier so you can live safely and happily.”

“It’s impossible. The moment you hid your real identity from me, I already knew we could never have a future. But I just… can’t accept it…”  

Her tears fell onto the back of his hand, cold as snow.

### Character Notes

**Shang Xuan**  
Torn between the living warmth of the girl in front of him and the documentary evidence of her death three years earlier. He destroys the papers rather than let her see them, yet the internal conflict only deepens.

**Lu Wanting (Wan Ting)**  
Still fragile after the storeroom ordeal. She senses Shang Xuan’s turmoil and finally admits she has been hiding the truth out of fear of abandonment.

**Dr. Li**  
The physician who treated Wan Ting’s claustrophobia three years ago. His brief warning confirms that the woman Shang Xuan is protecting is the same person who should no longer exist.

**Luo Juexing**  
Confronted with blank archival pages and the knowledge that Shang Xuan is entangled with a possible residual entity. He chooses temporary non-interference while he observes.

**Huang Jiaye & Yu Xun**  
Deliver the remaining files and the evidence of vanishing records. They begin looking into the old inheritance dispute and the cousin Lu Shengnan as a possible lead.

**Little Bell**  
Watches the hospital reunion from outside the glass, her jealousy hardening into something colder and more determined.
