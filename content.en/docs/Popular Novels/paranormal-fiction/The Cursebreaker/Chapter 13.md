Shang Xuan stepped out of Orange’s building and immediately spotted a man carrying a suitcase near the entrance.

A cold smile touched his lips. He walked forward. In the narrowing distance between them an invisible spark seemed to leap. Juexing set the suitcase down, seized Shang Xuan by the collar, and growled:

“Enough. Leave Orange out of this.”

“Oh? So you do care.” Shang Xuan’s voice remained elegant, almost amused.

“She’s an outsider. If you still hate me for what happened five years ago, come at me directly!”

The moment the words “five years ago” left Juexing’s mouth, Shang Xuan shoved him hard.

“Why? Why did Li Mei ever love a coward like you? If it weren’t for you she would still be alive. You talk endlessly about righteousness, and look what it cost her!”

Juexing’s eyes burned red. He had never stopped blaming himself. Yet the choice had already been made; he could not sacrifice an entire town for his own private desire.

“I’ll be honest,” Shang Xuan said, each word landing like a blade. “I intend to turn Orange into the weapon that destroys you—and then I’ll tell her the whole truth.”

“Why… why does it have to be this way…” Juexing stumbled backward and collapsed onto the grass.

“Because you were never a real man. You always choose to run. And yet Heaven still dropped this burden on your shoulders. You know exactly why.” Shang Xuan’s laugh was ice.

“Whatever you want from me, I’ll give it. Just… don’t hurt Orange.” Juexing sank to both knees, head bowed.

Shang Xuan walked over and drove a vicious kick into him.

“I still don’t understand why Li Mei chose you. And I understand even less why Orange would fall for you too.”

Juexing lay on the grass, staring up at the lighted window on the fourth floor. Orange had not gone to sleep yet.

Shang Xuan crouched beside him.

“If you’re a man, face me properly one more time. Even if you lose, I’ll respect it. Better to fight your enemy with courage than to keep surrendering.”

He stepped over Juexing and walked away without looking back.

Orange pulled the curtain aside and looked down at the lawn. She saw Juexing push himself up, retrieve the suitcase, and take a few steps. Then her voice rang out:

“Is that you, Juexing?”

He stood frozen, unable to find a single word. Orange hurried closer, noticed the blood at the corner of his mouth, and gently wiped it away.

“What happened? Are you hurt?”

Juexing looked at her with eyes full of unshed tears. The suitcase slipped from his fingers. He pulled her into his arms.

Orange stiffened for a second, then smiled and wrapped her arms around his waist.

“Did you come just to see me?”

Silence stretched. At last he pushed her away.

“I came to tell you that neither I nor Shang Xuan will ever have a future with you. Give up.”

He picked up the suitcase and walked past her.

“Stop right there!”

Juexing halted but did not turn.

“Shang Xuan confessed to me. He said he likes me. I think he’s better than you… but my heart only has room for you. Every time I see you I want to talk to you, I want you to understand me, I want—”

“Enough.” Juexing cut her off. “What’s the point of all that nonsense? Don’t you have any self-awareness at all?”

“Shang Xuan was right. You’re not a man. You’re a coward. Li Mei must have been blind to choose you.” Tears slid silently down her cheeks.

Juexing spun around, eyes blazing.

“What did you just say?”

Orange stepped forward and slapped him hard across the face. Then she turned and ran back upstairs, leaving him alone and stunned on the grass.

The soft roll of wheels on grass broke the silence. A pair of fire tongs reached out and clamped onto the suitcase handle. Only then did Juexing notice the old woman.

“Oh, so you still want this? I thought it was trash.” Granny Luo Shen retracted the tongs and continued pushing her cart.

“Granny… did I do the wrong thing?” Juexing asked.

She kept walking ahead of him, glancing left and right while considering the question.

“Shang Xuan already said that much. What do you think?”

“Li Mei is a knot I’ll never untie. How am I supposed to escape it?”

“It’s been five years. Have you learned nothing? People are supposed to grow. Without regret there is no wisdom. Will you keep protecting her by running away, or will you protect her openly with your own strength?” She tossed an empty 2.5-liter cola bottle into the cart and nodded in satisfaction.

“But…”

“You’re a man. Whining isn’t your job. Find the courage to tell Orange you’re someone worth relying on. She might be the one who changes your fate.”

“The one who changes my fate…” Juexing looked up. A meteor streaked across the sky. He closed his eyes and made a silent wish. When he opened them again, Granny Luo Shen was gone.

“I won’t run anymore. No matter how hard Shang Xuan’s challenge is this time, I will not lose. I will prove to Li Mei that the choice I made back then was not wrong.” He clenched his fists. The birthmark on his arm glowed with a faint cyan light.

Not far away, Shang Xuan watched and nodded once.

“That’s more like it. My good brother.”

At the invitation of Boss Yu Xun, Juexing took train 5120 into the city and arrived at the man’s villa. Lately Yu Xun had been restless, convinced something unclean had attached itself to the house. After reading reports about Juexing he had been desperate for a visit that might bring him peace.

Juexing stood at the front gate for a full ten minutes, then circled the entire property before returning to the entrance and standing still in thought.

“Well? Is it bad?” Yu Xun asked cautiously from behind him, as if afraid of attracting misfortune.

Juexing’s brow furrowed. “Let’s go inside.”

Yu Xun’s hands trembled as he unlocked the door. The instant it opened, two or three small child-like shadows flickered past behind them. Juexing caught the movement from the corner of his eye but said nothing. Evidence first.

He stopped outside a storage room on the third-floor corridor.

“Do you often hear strange sounds without warning—bouncing balls, children playing?”

“Yes! Especially on weekends. But the moment you try to listen carefully, the sounds disappear. Sometimes late at night you hear kids laughing. It scares me half to death.”

“What’s this room used for? Does anyone go in regularly?”

“The second- and third-floor rooms are both storage. This one only gets cleaned once a month. Almost no one else enters.”

Juexing asked him to open the nearest bedroom. He walked to the outer window, leaned out, and caught sight of a small boy’s leg still dangling outside.

“You’ll hear the strange sounds very soon.” His deduction was already confirmed. This was no malevolent force.

Moments later the sounds of children playing, bouncing a ball, and clapping hands drifted in from outside. Yu Xun immediately began hiccupping from nerves. Juexing smiled.

“Let’s go see what these little rascals are up to.”

He stepped out first and stood before the storage-room door. Yu Xun shuffled forward on heavy feet. The moment they approached, the noises stopped—as if the children had sensed them.

Juexing examined the door carefully. Near the bottom of the gap he found a small piece of mirror glass. They had been using a simple periscope principle to watch for approaching adults and freeze whenever someone came near.

He raised a finger to his lips and gestured for Yu Xun to open the door.

Three children stared up in terrified confusion. One of them was Yu Xun’s own grandson.

“Grandpa…” the boy mumbled, head lowered.

Juexing ruffled their hair.

“If you’re going to hide and play, tell your family first. What if something happens?”

“Xiao Gang! Why would you hide in here?” Yu Xun had always treated the boy like a treasure. He never imagined the source of his fear would be his own grandchild.

Juexing stood.

“You don’t always have to restrict children. Because you forbade them from coming to the third floor, they found another way in. Climbing that banyan tree is far more dangerous than using the stairs.” He pointed to the large tree that leaned naturally against the outer wall.

The mystery dissolved. There was no curse—only children and a secret game. Yu Xun gathered his grandson into his arms.

“Next time you want to play up here, just tell Grandpa. I’ll open the door. No more climbing trees.”

The three children had expected a scolding. Instead they received understanding. They rushed to Juexing and chorused:

“Thank you, Uncle!”

Juexing smiled, patted their heads, and followed Yu Xun downstairs.

The older man gripped his hand tightly.

“Thank you so much. If anything had happened to Xiao Gang I could never have forgiven myself.”

“You’re overthinking it. Everything’s fine now. I should head back.”

Juexing took his leave. He had not gone far from the villa when his phone rang. The screen showed no number. He was about to reject the call when it connected on its own.

He raised the phone slowly. From the speaker came the deep rush of a train in motion, then a faint, indistinct murmuring, and finally a sharp scream. Juexing yanked the phone away. For a fraction of a second the screen displayed the number **5120**, then went black.

He shook the device. It returned to normal. The brief episode brought back the high-school girl he had seen in the seventh carriage of a train not long ago. Without further speculation he put the phone away and walked toward the station.

When he arrived, train 5120 was just pulling in. The high-school girl was nowhere to be seen.

### Character Notes

**Luo Juexing**  
The confrontation forces him to confront the unresolved guilt surrounding Li Mei’s death five years earlier. Granny Luo Shen’s words and a falling meteor push him past the threshold of avoidance. The birthmark answers with a faint cyan glow—the first visible sign that he has chosen to fight rather than flee.

**Shang Xuan**  
No longer merely an observer. He openly declares his intention to weaponize Orange against Juexing and issues a direct challenge. His final nod suggests complex, unresolved brotherhood beneath the hostility.

**Qingmu Chengzi (Orange)**  
Receives both a desperate embrace and a cruel rejection in the same night. Her slap is equal parts heartbreak and fury. She remains unaware of the larger game being played with her heart.

**Granny Luo Shen (洛神婆)**  
A street scavenger who appears at pivotal moments. Her practical wisdom cuts through Juexing’s self-pity and reframes protection as courage rather than sacrifice.

**Boss Yu Xun**  
A new client whose “haunting” proves entirely human. The case serves as a brief, almost gentle interlude—reminding both Juexing and the reader that not every strange sound is a curse.

**The Call from 5120**  
A brief, unexplained intrusion of the train’s presence. The scream and the vanishing high-school girl suggest that the residual forces tied to the railway have not finished with Juexing.
