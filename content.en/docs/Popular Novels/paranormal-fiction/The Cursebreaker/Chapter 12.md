Song Xi parked in the underground garage. Orange felt a flicker of unease. He smiled.

“Here’s the thing. The train project our company handled officially opened yesterday. I thought I’d show you around, and we can ride it to the party.”

“Oh—I saw it on the news.”

“Good. The platform is only five minutes away.”

He opened the door for her. Side by side they walked toward the platform. Lights flared ahead. A train marked **5120** glided to a stop. A sizable crowd waited; since the line opened, the trip from the capital to the city center had become much faster.

Song Xi had arranged first-class seats. With him was the designer Tang Gufeng.

“Manager Song—late by a whole minute!” Tang Gufeng extended a hand.

“Let me introduce—” Song Xi began, but Tang stepped forward at once.

“I know, I know. Hello, I’m Tang Gufeng. Looking forward to working with you.”

Orange smiled and shook his hand. The three settled into their seats. Song Xi ordered a bottle of red wine and a selection of pastries.

The train pulled out again, a long dragon sliding toward the next station. Outside the windows the wind roared; inside the carriage laughter and conversation divided the world cleanly in two.

They had talked for less than fifteen minutes when the train reached its terminus. Song Xi stood, made a quick call, then guided them off the platform and into a waiting silver Mercedes.

At the hotel Song Xi had reserved, most of the guests were already present. The moment he and his companions entered, people clustered around them. Orange, walking close beside him, naturally became the center of attention.

Shang Xuan moved through the crowd in a perfectly tailored suit. The half-blond hair was especially striking, and the way he wore formal clothes made him look every bit the elegant, handsome man.

“Look at that guy—so stylish!” A woman in a low-cut dress nudged her companion and raised her glass as she approached him.

Orange felt the press of so many strangers and stayed tightly at Song Xi’s side. It was her first time at an event this large. He turned to her.

“Red wine or brandy?”

She shook her head. “Juice is fine.”

Song Xi took her wrist lightly. “A little wine helps the atmosphere. Don’t be nervous.” She lowered her eyes and accepted the glass he offered.

A heavily made-up woman blocked Shang Xuan’s path.

“What’s your name, handsome?”

He waved her aside.

Instead of leaving she rested a hand on his shoulder and leaned close to his ear. “Interested in a private drink later?”

“Are you a wine barrel or a water barrel? All you do is drink. Careful you don’t choke.”

The insult left her livid. No one had ever spoken to her that way. She raised her glass to throw the wine in his face. Shang Xuan stopped her with a calm remark.

“That wine looks expensive. Wasting it on my face would be a shame.”

He guided her aside and walked toward the long table of food.

Orange took a tiny sip of the red wine and forced it down. Even that small amount left her dizzy. A middle-aged man with glasses approached Song Xi, drew him a few steps away, and said in a low voice:

“You’re slipping, kid. Keeping a beauty hidden away?”

Song Xi raised his glass. “About that funding—have you decided?”

“Depends on how sincere you are.” The man’s eyes lingered on Orange. Song Xi heard the implication clearly.

“I’m afraid that’s not possible…”

“Still green, aren’t you? You should learn a few things from your father.” The man looked irritated at the refusal.

Song Xi glanced at the slightly tipsy Orange, then at the “business partner” beside him, caught between choices.

The man patted his shoulder. “She’s not Chinese, is she?”

Song Xi nodded.

“One little effort and a contract worth over a hundred million is yours. She’s just a woman.” The new angle began to erode Song Xi’s resolve.

“What’s this?” Song Xi stared at the small pill the man produced.

“Drop it in her juice. Leave the rest to me.” The smile that followed was pure malice.

Song Xi held a glass of orange juice. When no one was watching he slipped the pill inside. He was still hesitating when Orange hurried over.

“Can I leave early? I don’t think I belong here.”

Song Xi steadied himself. “All right. Finish the juice first and I’ll have the driver take you home.”

Orange nodded and took the glass. She did not notice the cold sweat on his forehead, nor the bespectacled man standing a short distance away with a knife-edged smile.

“Money can turn a beast in human skin into a well-dressed gentleman.” Shang Xuan drained a glass of ice water, shivered once, and headed upstairs toward the guest rooms.

Juexing received a call from Jiaye asking him to pick up a package in the city. He was about to refuse when he remembered the trial ticket still in his drawer. Riding the new express once would do no harm; he had never taken one before.

Orange lay on the large bed in the guest room. A middle-aged man in glasses stepped out of the bathroom wrapped only in a towel, his soft body swaying unpleasantly.

“Tsk… what a prize.” His eyes crawled over her with open hunger.

He had just reached for the sash of her dress when a man’s voice cut through the room:

“Stop right there, you four-eyed animal.”

He spun around. No one was visible, yet the voice had been unmistakable.

“Hallucination,” he muttered, and reached again.

“I said stop. Can’t you understand human speech?”

“Who’s there?” Fear edged his voice. From the darkness a man in a suit with half-blond hair stepped forward.

“Who are you? How did you get in?”

“I should be asking you that, beast.” Shang Xuan hurled a drinking glass, knocking the telephone receiver from the man’s hand. In the next second he closed the distance, smashed a fist into the man’s face, sending the glasses flying, and ripped away the towel. The man curled up beside the nightstand in sudden shame.

Shang Xuan raised his phone and took a clear photograph.

“If you try anything, you’ll be on the front page by morning. I’ll make sure Reporter Meng writes a headline no one will forget.”

He kicked the man once more, lifted the unconscious Orange from the bed, and left the room.

Song Xi received the frantic call and burst in. Orange was already gone. The bespectacled man, still seething, demanded, “Who the hell was that? I won’t let him get away with this.”

“I’ll find him and settle it for you.”

The man swallowed, then changed his mind. “Forget it. I’ll count myself unlucky.”

“And the funding?”

He glared. “I don’t even know if you set this up. We’ll talk when I’m in a better mood.” He left with a contemptuous snort.

Song Xi slammed a fist into the wall. “Whoever that bastard was—when I find him I’ll make him pay.”

When Orange woke she was already seated on a train. Beside her sat Shang Xuan, still in his formal suit.

“Why are you dressed like that?”

“Doesn’t it look good? A party requires better clothes than my usual ones.”

“Your bow tie is crooked.” She reached out and straightened it. Their eyes met; in that moment the man before her seemed almost magnetic.

Shang Xuan watched her frozen hand, then gently pinched her nose. She came back to herself.

“What were you thinking?”

“Nothing… just wondering whether I was in danger again.”

“No. I happened to be in the city meeting a friend. I ran into you on the last train.” He chose not to mention what had happened upstairs; there was no need.

Orange kept her head lowered. Sitting this close to him for the first time made her heartbeat quick and uneven.

“Shy?” He took her right hand in his left.

The contact brought a flush to her cheeks. Thoughts scattered, yet she did not pull away.

Shang Xuan held her hand with both of his. They rode in silence through station after station.

At the second-to-last stop Orange turned to speak to him. A figure stood in the middle of the seventh carriage. She thought it was another hallucination. She closed her eyes, opened them again, and saw more clearly: a high-school girl with long hanging hair, wearing a school uniform, barefoot—and casting no shadow.

Orange pressed her left hand over her mouth to keep from crying out. Shang Xuan noticed her tension and followed her gaze. When he saw the girl’s raised toes he turned back.

“Orange? Are you all right?”

“Why doesn’t anyone else see her? You see her too, don’t you?”

“See what? You’re just tired.” He pretended ignorance.

“There’s a girl standing in the middle of the car!”

The moment the words left her the schoolgirl slowly lifted her head and began to raise her right hand. Orange squeezed her eyes shut and rested her head against Shang Xuan’s arm. He patted her back.

“It’s all right. Just a hallucination. We’re almost at the station. I’ll take you home.”

She did not lift her head. Shang Xuan looked again at the center of the carriage. The girl still stood with her head bowed, as though waiting for someone.

The lights inside the train flickered out for several seconds, then returned. In the sixth carriage Juexing, who had been dozing, suddenly felt a wave of residual resentment seeping from the seventh car.

He stood on instinct and walked to the glass door between the two carriages. Through it he saw the long-haired schoolgirl—and, beyond her, Orange and Shang Xuan.

### Character Notes

**Qingmu Chengzi (Orange)**  
Drugged and nearly assaulted, she is rescued without ever learning the full details. On the train the appearance of the shadowless schoolgirl fractures her already fragile sense of reality. Physical closeness with Shang Xuan adds a new layer of emotional confusion.

**Shang Xuan**  
Intervenes decisively in the hotel room, documents the crime, and removes Orange without explanation. On the train he denies seeing the ghost while clearly aware of it, continuing his pattern of selective truth.

**Luo Juexing**  
Boards the same train on a whim after remembering the trial ticket. The sudden surge of residual resentment pulls him to the connecting door, where he witnesses both the apparition and the pair in the next carriage.

**Song Xi**  
Succumbs to pressure and drugs Orange for the sake of a massive contract. When the plan collapses he is left furious and empty-handed, still unaware of who disrupted it.

**The Shadowless Schoolgirl**  
A barefoot high-school student who casts no shadow. Her appearance on train 5120 signals that a new, unresolved resentment has attached itself to the line—or to someone riding it.

**Tang Gufeng**  
Briefly introduced as Song Xi’s designer colleague; serves mainly to establish the corporate world surrounding the new express service.
