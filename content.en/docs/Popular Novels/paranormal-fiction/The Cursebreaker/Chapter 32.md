The fire swayed and climbed, painting a second skyline of flame across the summit of Jiugong Peak. People at the foot of the mountain pointed and murmured.

Inside the empty house the two figures kept dodging and fighting the blaze. As long as hope remained, they would not give up.

Orange shielded Zilan from falling beams. Sparks repeatedly kissed the fabric of her trousers; when she was not careful the sting arrived without warning. Zilan bit back tears and clung to Orange’s hand—that slender yet strong wrist that, in the girl’s eyes, was hope itself.

Smoke thickened and surged with the fire. Both of them coughed until tears ran.

Orange pointed at an office desk. “Hide under the table!”

The surface was covered by a thin marble slab—slim protection, yet the best shield against the flames. Zilan refused to leave her; she would not escape alone.

“Get under it—the roof is about to come down!” Orange’s urgent words reached Zilan in slow motion, each syllable sinking deep into her heart.

Another beam crashed down beside Orange’s feet. Ignoring the pain, she pushed Zilan beneath the desk and began searching for any other place of shelter.

A cyan light and a red light slammed into the roaring house from the same direction.

“Orange—”

A familiar voice called. Strong arms seized her, pulled her beneath a fireproof coat, and carried her out of the burning building in a single burst.

Zilan had already lost consciousness from the smoke. Her light body drifted like a maple leaf about to be taken by the wind. Someone found her under the desk and brought her out at once.

By the time night fully rose, the empty house had been reduced to ash, white smoke still curling slowly upward.

Everything seemed to have ended with the fire.

---

In the hospital Juexing sat beside Orange’s bed, holding her hand tightly. The doctors had already examined her: aside from a few scrapes she was unharmed and could leave after a day’s rest.

Looking at her exhausted sleeping face, his chest ached.

On the mountainside Jiaye recovered the bodies of Chen Dong and Shuangfei. The driver, eager to save himself, agreed to turn state’s evidence and place all responsibility on Chen Dong and the others. Fate, however, had other plans: the homeless man they had intended to frame had survived and remembered everything clearly. He too was willing to testify in court.

Because Yu Xun had followed Jiaye the entire way, she had missed the soul-exchange ritual itself. Juexing promised to give her certain details as supporting material.

Soon the “mirror curse” at the vocational high school appeared under Yu Xun’s byline, wrapped in the colors of mystery. Most readers felt deep regret for the people in the story. The article also sounded a clear warning to the school: while educating students, the most basic care must never be neglected. Through this event, everyone hoped such tragedies would not be repeated.

The curtain in the ward stirred. Juexing rose, closed the window a little, and turned to sit again—only to find Zhao Xiaoqing standing in the doorway.

“You’re all right?” he asked.

“I promise you—it ends here. I’ll return to class and become good friends with Zilan. From now on no one will ever bully her again.” She held out her right little finger, asking for a pinky swear.

“Some things are fixed,” Juexing sighed. “Even the greatest immortals cannot change them.”

“You gave them a chance. I gave them a chance too. Some people simply refused to take it. If not for me, Teacher Orange and Zilan might already…” Melancholy flickered in her eyes—an expression that did not belong to her age.

“Enough. Whether you are Xiao Qing or Qiaohe, from this day forward be a true friend to Zilan.” He extended his own little finger and locked it with hers.

Xiao Qing smiled—the first unburdened smile she had managed in a long time. She glanced back at Orange, who had already sat up and was watching them from the bed.

“I’ll go see Zilan. You stay with Teacher Orange.” She nodded a greeting to Orange and walked past Juexing.

“Qiaohe—no, Xiao Qing!” he called.

She turned. He said quietly, “This is our secret. If possible, never speak of it for the rest of your life.”

“Don’t worry. I’ll remember, Teacher Luo.” She smiled again and continued toward Zilan’s ward.

“That was Xiao Qing just now, wasn’t it?” Orange asked.

“Yes. Zilan is fine. Everyone is fine.” The words left his mouth, yet the lingering shadow between his brows did not escape her.

“Something on your mind?”

He tightened his hold on her hand.

“Tangerine… I was truly terrified the fire would swallow you. I didn’t dare imagine what the days after that would look like without you.”

“Oh? Since when does Teacher Luo say things so unlike himself?” she teased.

“Maybe I’ve started falling into your emotional whirlpool.” He smiled.

They looked at each other and laughed—like coffee with a little sugar: still bitter, yet faintly sweet.

Outside the ward Shang Xuan had been standing for some time. Seeing that Orange could speak and smile, the heavy stone in his chest finally settled. He could leave without further attachment and go to a place that belonged only to him.

After these days of repeated thought he understood that some things cannot be forced—especially love.

Perhaps in Heaven’s eyes he was only a stranger who brushed past love every time. If that was his fate, he could accept it. Yet things are rarely so absolute. Just as he prepared to continue traveling alone, a romance from another world was already beginning to unfold in silence…

---

Orange was soon discharged and returned to teaching at the vocational high school. Zilan and Xiao Qing became close friends. For a time the entire school seemed reborn, filled with fresh energy.

One week later, on a Saturday, Juexing took express train 5120 into the city. Orange had told him that Sunday would be the school’s anniversary celebration. Every class was busy with final rehearsals, preparing for this day of “revival.”

The events of a week earlier were no longer spoken of, yet they left behind deeper reflection and quiet remembrance. Forgiveness requires great courage and exacts a heavy price.

Many people regarded it all as destiny. The old Buddhist saying returned: “Cause and effect cycle without end; plant a cause, reap the fruit.”

Juexing sat in the seventh carriage. When the train stopped at the midpoint station he looked carefully. The place now felt like a warm home; the mailbox by the entrance was stuffed with letters—perhaps messages of comfort from relatives or friends.

He allowed himself a brief, relieved smile. He did not dare calculate when such events would truly end.

Fifteen minutes later the train reached the terminal. He walked toward the vocational high school. If his timing was right he would arrive at Orange’s dormitory just in time for lunch.

Standing outside her door he heard laughter and the aroma of cooking—clearly Zilan and Xiao Qing. He raised a hand to knock. The door opened a narrow twenty centimeters; Xiao Qing’s head poked out.

“Teacher went to buy soy sauce and isn’t back yet. You can’t come in.” She closed the door again.

Juexing was about to push when he turned and saw Orange standing to the side. Today she wore a striking black dress and black high heels that caught the light with quiet, deliberate allure.

He stared until his soul seemed to circle her several times; his eyes were almost ready to fall out.

Orange glanced down at herself and smiled. “Is something wrong?”

“No… nothing at all. I just didn’t realize your figure was this good…” The words tumbled out without thought.

She nodded, a little shy. “Why aren’t you going in?”

“I just arrived. I was about to knock.” He raised his hand and knocked again. Orange found the gesture odd.

After only two knocks the door opened by itself. Inside, the room was silent; the two girls seemed to have vanished.

Orange pushed the door wider and looked back at him, puzzled by the strange expression on his face.

The moment she stepped across the threshold, Zilan and Xiao Qing popped out from either side, each holding a party popper.

*Pop! Pop!*

Colored streamers exploded across the room, filling the air like a return to childhood.

Xiao Qing put on a solemn face, produced a bouquet of nine roses from behind her back, and presented them with exaggerated gentlemanly care.

“This is the surprise Teacher Luo asked us to give you!”

Juexing stood behind Orange, completely stunned by the scene.

Orange looked at the roses in Xiao Qing’s hands. Emotion rose so sharply that tears filled her eyes. In all the time she had known him, this was the first time he had ever given her roses. She turned, threw her arms around him, and kept saying “Thank you.”

Zilan made a face at Juexing; Xiao Qing gave him a thumbs-up. Watching the two girls, he suddenly understood:

Happiness is actually a very simple thing. It is when one unfortunate person meets another unfortunate person and they become close friends. In the space they share there is no suspicion, no quarrel—only smiles and sincerity.

That lunch was the happiest meal the four of them had ever shared. Afterward Xiao Qing asked Juexing to come with her to the empty school playground.

“Thank you, Teacher Luo.” She bowed deeply.

He smiled. “I accept the feeling. Seeing you both free of the old shadows makes me truly glad.”

She took both of his arms and ran with him to the swings.

“Push me,” she said, and sat down. Juexing gave the seat a gentle push. It rose and fell like a simple pendulum.

On the empty playground one sat and one stood, forming a silent picture that carried a quiet, lingering flavor—something that could be understood only by the heart. It was like starlight in a distant night sky: visible, yet forever out of reach.

### Character Notes

**Luo Juexing**  
The fire rescue and the later quiet admission of fear mark how deeply Orange has become part of his reason for continuing. The pinky promise with Xiao Qing and the request for lifelong secrecy close the supernatural ledger of the school arc while leaving a residual kindness in place of residual resentment.

**Qingmu Chengzi (Orange)**  
Survives the fire with only minor injuries. The surprise of nine roses—her first from him—brings tears of pure happiness. The chapter ends with her fully restored to the school and to a circle of people who now protect rather than harm.

**Mo Zilan & Zhao Xiaoqing**  
The former victim and the former bully (still carrying a trace of Qiaohe’s presence) become genuine friends. Their joint surprise for Orange and the peaceful swing scene on the empty playground are the clearest signs that the cycle of violence has been broken.

**Shang Xuan**  
Watches from outside the hospital long enough to confirm Orange is safe, then departs. The narrative notes that a new “romance from another world” is already beginning for him—hinting at the next thread of his story.

**The Empty House**  
Reduced to ash. Its destruction by fire, followed by the recovery of the living and the legal consequences for the perpetrators, seals the physical site of the final confrontation.

**The Nine Roses**  
A simple, staged gift that nonetheless carries real emotional weight. In a story filled with curses, mirrors, and fire, the ordinary act of giving flowers becomes the quiet symbol of a future that can now be lived rather than merely survived.
