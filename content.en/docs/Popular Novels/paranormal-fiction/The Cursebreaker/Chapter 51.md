Orange watched the two of them standing beneath the jujube tree. Smiling, she leaned on the second-floor corridor railing and gazed down, imagining that their future relationship would grow even closer than that of a true mother and son.

“From the time Orange first began to understand the world, she would occasionally sense things in advance—and the accuracy was one hundred percent. Later I wondered whether it was because of the environment, so I sent her to China. During those years of separation everything remained peaceful, and I never mentioned the matter again. Only after she returned and told me what the two of you had experienced together did I begin to worry in earnest.” Mizusawa Kaori said.

“Yes. When I first met her, there were times she could see residual entities directly. She also had strange dreams, and whatever she dreamed would later become reality.” Juexing replied.

“Because you are a cursebreaker, I am willing to tell you these things. I am not a completely superstitious person, yet this complicated world truly contains too many impossibilities. I hope you will help me keep this secret. If possible, never tell Orange for the rest of your life. And you must promise me that in the days ahead you will take good care of her.” As she spoke she tightened her grip on Juexing’s hand. He could feel a slight tremor—she seemed to fear something.

“Is there something you still haven’t said?” he asked.

“From the day Orange told me she was coming home, I had the same dream for several nights in a row. Only when the dream becomes reality will it leave my mind. I believe Orange has had the same dream; she simply hasn’t dared to tell you.”

“Is it about an oil painting?”

“The dream is rather blurred, but you have reminded me. I could never understand what was supposed to be standing on that easel. Looking at it this way, it really is a painting.”

“Auntie, I don’t know whether everything we are about to face was already foretold the moment we boarded the plane, but I can guarantee that whether it is a curse or fate, I will not let anything happen to either of you. Tonight’s conversation will remain known only to the two of us. From tomorrow onward we will not think about this matter. If it truly appears, I will face it actively.”

Mizusawa Kaori nodded, a smile returning to her face. She let out a long breath. “Perhaps after so many years this heart-trial was always going to descend. That Orange met you may truly be the way we pass through this barrier.”

“An old woman once told me that Orange might be the person who changes my fate. Conversely, I may also become the person who changes hers.” Juexing spoke with quiet certainty.

Orange had grown a little uncomfortable watching them talk for so long. She came downstairs in her slippers—*ta-ta*—and walked to the jujube tree in the backyard.

“You two keep talking. I’m a little sleepy.” Mizusawa Kaori smiled and left the courtyard.

“What were you discussing so intently? My mother has never spoken with relatives or friends for this long.” Orange stared at Juexing, afraid he might let something slip.

His thoughts raced. He smiled and answered, “Your mother was testing me. She threw out a whole series of difficult questions, but I’m no pushover—I answered every one of them fluently. She was quite satisfied.”

“Is that so? Then good. Go take a shower and sleep early.” Orange linked her arm through his and walked toward the bathroom. Neither of them noticed the look Mizusawa Kaori cast toward their backs…

Lying on the floor mattress, Orange turned on her side, rested her hand beneath her head, and watched Juexing sleeping beside her. He was facing away, completely still, as though already asleep.

Juexing felt the force of her gaze, rolled over, and answered with his eyes.

“Yu Xun said that once we return to the capital we should hold the wedding together. What do you think?” she asked softly.

Juexing said nothing. He simply nodded in the mechanical manner of a beckoning cat.

Orange gave his head a push. In a flash he caught her hand. That single action immediately invited her fists and feet; the two of them began a private wrestling match.

Soon both were tired. Orange simply rested against his stomach and quietly closed her eyes. Juexing brushed the strands of hair from her face and silently watched her as she drifted toward sleep.

“I truly hope our future will be peaceful, happy, and long-lasting…” she murmured with her eyes still closed.

Juexing smiled. “Who is as greedy as you? Wanting so many things must be exhausting.”

“You’re right. As long as our whole family is healthy and safe, that is enough.”

The night deepened. The chirping of crickets became a lullaby. While they were active, snores rose all around. Orange pillowed her head on Juexing, and both of them, almost simultaneously, entered their dreams.

Four days passed in the blink of an eye. The fifth day was Dou Dou’s wedding. The four of them each prepared a red envelope, washed and dressed, gathered for breakfast, then took a taxi together to the art gallery in the city center. Dou Dou was already arranging the venue with several gallery staff. The ceremony would begin promptly at ten in the morning; they still had two hours to prepare. The bride, Miyuki, was waiting at home for the sacred moment to arrive.

Her hair had been carefully styled by a designer; a purple jasmine hairpin was fixed behind the upswept locks. That small pin was like a full moon in the vast firmament, decorating an almost miraculous landscape. The bright-red bridal gown complemented the soft pink-white of her dimples perfectly. No wonder people said that a woman on her wedding day is the most beautiful under heaven.

She looked at herself in the mirror with satisfaction, thinking that in less than two hours she would walk with the man she loved into the sacred and solemn wedding hall. The excitement in her heart was almost impossible to suppress.

Beside her stood a young girl—an assistant left by the designer to ensure the bridal appearance remained unchanged until she left the house.

When Dou Dou saw the four of them arrive first, he hurried to seat them. Orange insisted on helping with the decorations; Juexing and the others naturally refused to stand idle. Dou Dou tried to decline, but the four were united in purpose, so he had no choice but to let them join the team.

Although each person’s aesthetic sense and approach to arrangement differed, the four of them showed surprising coordination, greatly shortening the time needed to prepare the wedding venue. Once everything was ready, Orange and Juexing took charge of stretching the caution tape so that early-arriving guests would not wander freely and create chaos that would be difficult to clean up later.

According to Dou Dou’s plan, the wedding car would bring the bride and the most precious oil painting to the gallery thirty minutes early. Fifteen minutes before the ceremony began, relatives and friends arrived one after another and waited outside the caution line for this unprecedented oil-painting-style wedding.

In the very center of the gallery stood a sandalwood easel. Upon it rested a painting still covered by red silk, yet the covering could not conceal the attraction that radiated from it. Compared with the other oil paintings hanging around the hall, this one possessed a deeper sense of mystery. Was this the centerpiece of the wedding?

When the clock struck, the master of ceremonies removed the caution tape. Guests filed in, presenting large red envelopes to the bride or groom and offering the usual blessings. After roughly twenty minutes of this processional, Dou Dou took Miyuki’s hand and walked slowly to the front of the gallery, then stood before the MC waiting for the ceremony to begin.

The MC questioned the couple with fluent ease; the applause from the floor rose in wave after wave without end.

They exchanged wedding rings and, before the entire assembly, shared a deep kiss. The guests cheered even more loudly, adding atmosphere to this beautiful moment. Next Dou Dou himself acted as guide, introducing each of the collected oil paintings one by one and beginning the charity auction. Eighty percent of the funds raised that day would be donated to poor families to help them escape poverty sooner.

The brilliant commentary and the beautiful paintings received their due under the clustered gazes of the crowd. Some people gained new knowledge; others experienced art. Today was not merely a wedding—it was also an extension of art, allowing the wealthy collectors who had come specifically for the occasion to see long-missed masterpieces.

Before lunch began, the painting that the gallery was most heavily recommending was about to reveal its true face before everyone. Dou Dou and Miyuki stood on either side, each taking two corners of the red silk, and in a motion that resembled a magic trick, pulled the cloth away.

It was an oil painting that looked extremely simple. With only a few strokes the artist had sketched half a pagoda that seemed formed by nature itself. The entire hall held its breath and stared at the half-tower. Gradually the pagoda appeared to come alive—slowly rising, climbing upward, until it broke through the earth and stood fully upright before them.

Everyone present gasped in wonder. Yet when they looked carefully again, the painting still showed only half a pagoda. The illusion had been so real that it had played a joke on the entire assembly.

“That tower moved…” In the quiet atmosphere a reckless voice spoke the words aloud, instantly causing a stir. People looked at one another, each guessing the meaning of the statement, yet every heart held the same answer: the pagoda *had* moved. Apart from that one impulsive speaker, however, no one else echoed the claim. Instead they quietly restrained their smiles and allowed the originally lively atmosphere to cool.

Orange stood beside Juexing and whispered something in his ear. He nodded and gave Dou Dou a meaningful glance. Dou Dou understood the signal, clapped his hands, and said with a smile, “This painting comes from France. It is said to have survived a great earthquake and is therefore especially precious. The most special feature of the work is this half-pagoda. Almost everyone who sees it for the first time feels that the tower is rising—an effect created by the profound skill of the master painter.”

After Dou Dou’s explanation the doubts in everyone’s hearts were resolved. He took the opportunity to announce that the wedding banquet was officially beginning. The guests gradually moved to the large restaurant across the street from the gallery and commenced their journey of hearty eating.

As they left, Orange looked back once more at the painting that was about to be covered again. She could clearly sense that the half-tower had risen one additional level. She was certain she had not miscounted…
