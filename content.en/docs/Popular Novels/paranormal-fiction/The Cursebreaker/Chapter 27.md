When Jiaye entered the restaurant he first saw Yu Xun already seated and waiting with visible anxiety. He wondered whether this would turn out to be a Hongmen banquet.

“You’re early,” he said as he sat down and ordered a glass of iced water.

Yu Xun said nothing, as if she had not noticed him at all.

Jiaye forced a small smile and stirred the ice with his straw. Her expression suggested Juexing had given some unfavorable response. Instinct told him that when Juexing arrived he would not be alone.

Juexing and Orange walked hand in hand through the streets, happier than they had ever been in public. It was as if they were deliberately showing the world how enviable they looked—yet they were also simply one more couple among many.

“Look!” Orange pointed at an old man selling candied haws.

“I haven’t had those in ages. Let’s get some.”

Before he finished speaking she was already pulling him over. They bought two bright-red sticks.

“Eat more gently—look at the mess around your mouth.” Juexing took out a tissue and wiped the sticky red sugar from the corner of her lips. She laughed, then tapped the candied haw against his nose, leaving him with a bright red tip.

“Hey! You’re teasing me!” He reached out to tickle her. She dodged left and right, the two of them weaving through the crowd and drawing more than a few glances.

Shang Xuan had just stepped out of a noodle shop when he saw them.

His heart clenched hard. Fate was cruel. If he had gone to find Orange even a little earlier, she might not be walking here now.

“Wow, what a coincidence! What are you looking at?” Little Bell appeared from nowhere and followed his gaze, but the crowd remained only a crowd; nothing noteworthy stood out.

“I remember you’re also a student at the vocational high school. Why do you keep coming back to the suburbs?” Shang Xuan asked.

“My home is in the suburbs. Besides, I have a private driver and a Toyota. One phone call and I can come back whenever I want. And I’m about to graduate—soon I’ll be a university student.” She smiled in a way that seemed to carry some unspoken feeling.

Shang Xuan smiled back. “Congratulations. Have you decided what to study?”

“I want to study design and become a great designer!”

“That’s a popular field. Work hard at it.”

“You’ll support me, right?”

“Of course. I’ll support you.”

“Do you have something you want to ask me, or something you need help with?” Little Bell always sensed when he was preoccupied.

“Yes. Let’s find somewhere to talk.” He slipped his hands into his pockets. Little Bell cleverly linked her arm through his and followed.

---

All four of them were finally present. The moment Jiaye saw Orange he understood everything. A wave of self-reproach hit him; he had misjudged Juexing.

He was about to speak when Juexing cut in first.

“There has been a real misunderstanding between us. I asked everyone here today so we could clear it up.”

Yu Xun’s face was dark. She stared at the menu in silence.

Orange also remained quiet; the atmosphere had never been good.

Jiaye forced a laugh. “Yes, yes—misunderstandings should be cleared away. We’re all good friends, after all.”

“Yuxun,” Juexing called.

She answered with a flat “Mm” without raising her head, as if to say that nothing he said would matter now.

“First, thank you for the careful care you gave me during that time. But my heart is already full of Tangerine. I won’t say more about the past. I still hope that after this we can remain good friends.”

Yu Xun lifted her head a little and looked at him.

“Since you’ve said that much, what can I do except accept it?”

“Matters of the heart are clear to everyone. Forcing a relationship never brings happiness, does it?”

“Ah—everything is clear now,” Jiaye interjected. “Shouldn’t we order? The waiter is about to fall asleep standing there.”

Orange immediately picked up the thread. “Yes, yes. Everyone’s hungry. Yuxun, you look at the menu first.”

Yu Xun glanced at Orange. There was not a trace of resentment in her eyes—only the same friendly smile as before. For a moment Yu Xun wondered whether her own capacity for tolerance had been too small.

They had just finished ordering and were about to return to the main topic when Orange’s phone rang. The screen showed a strange number. Juexing, seated beside her, recognized the pattern at once—he had received the same kind of call before the previous curse began.

“Hello…?”

“Teacher… Teacher… save me… ah—”

The phone slipped from her hand and hit the floor. Only a busy tone remained. Orange had recognized Mo Zilan’s voice.

“What’s wrong?” Juexing asked.

“I have to go back to the school. You all eat.” She grabbed her bag and left in a rush.

Juexing stood as well, went to the counter, pointed at their table, and said, “I’ll settle the bill first,” then took out his bank card.

“Tangerine, I’m coming with you.” He caught her just as she was about to hail a taxi.

She nodded. Only after he climbed in did she tell the driver to head for the vocational high school in the city.

---

Only Yu Xun and Jiaye remained. The already poor atmosphere grew colder still.

Jiaye watched her dazed expression. The rejection was a blow, yet it also gave him an opening he intended to use carefully.

“Eat something. This looks pretty good.” He nudged her elbow.

Yu Xun sighed. “Do you think I’m stupid?”

“No. You’re a top reporter—how could you be stupid?”

“I should have been harsher from the start. I shouldn’t have held back. Otherwise Orange would never have found an opening.” The words carried clear resentment.

Jiaye set down his chopsticks, moved to the seat opposite her, and said, “I don’t know what your original purpose was, but I know you didn’t want Juexing to be unhappy, and you didn’t want Orange to be unhappy either. Since they’ve ended up together, we should be happy for them. No matter who you’re with in the future, I’ll still wish you well—because I want you to be happy.”

He had never organized those sentences in his mind before. Once they left his mouth, a weight lifted from his chest.

Yu Xun slowly raised her eyes to the man in front of her. Had she truly taken the long way around only to return to the starting point?

“All right. From now on we’re still friends. Didn’t you see Orange’s expression just now? Something’s definitely happening at the vocational high school. After we finish eating we can go check it out together.” Jiaye smiled.

Yu Xun kept looking at him. From another angle, locking down Jiaye was almost the same as locking down Luo Juexing. The result would still be useful.

“You okay?” He waved a hand in front of her face.

“I’m fine. Thank you.” She rested her hand on top of his. The simple contact made Jiaye’s heart stir again.

“Eat. There’s so much good food—don’t waste it!” He flourished chopsticks and spoon with exaggerated refugee enthusiasm, drawing occasional soft laughter from her. Seeing her smile eased him considerably.

---

Traffic delayed them. Half an hour later they finally reached the school. Orange went first to her own dormitory but found no trace of Zilan. She then ran to the office and asked the teacher on duty. The teacher had not seen the girl; it was the weekend, no classes, and no one had been paying attention.

Orange left the office looking worried. Where had Zilan been when she made that call?

Her phone rang again. She answered at once. This time it was Wang Mei.

“Teacher… I’m trapped in the toilet!” Wang Mei’s voice shook, as though other people were nearby.

“Don’t be afraid. Teacher is coming. Which toilet?”

“In the—ah!”

A scream, then the line went dead. On the other end Wang Mei sat on the floor, pushing herself backward until she hit the corner. On the opposite wall hung a full-length mirror. From inside the glass a figure slowly emerged—growing larger, clearer…

Orange and Juexing searched the women’s toilets floor by floor. On the third floor they stopped outside a door marked “Out of Service.” Soft *ge-ge, ga-ga* sounds were coming from inside.

Orange pressed the handle and knocked. There was no response.

Juexing closed his eyes, sensing what was happening within. Then he stepped forward, wrenched the handle hard—*click*—and the door flew open. A powerful suction of air dragged him inside. In the far corner Orange found the unconscious Mo Zilan.

“That’s not right… the one who called just now was Wang Mei…” Realization struck. She scooped Zilan up and rushed out. Juexing remained inside. As he prepared to turn and leave, a piercing girl’s laugh rang out. He looked sideways. At some point a full-length mirror had appeared on the opposite wall. Black light radiated from its surface. Inside the glass a figure stood with bright red lips stretched in a continuous smile… smiling…

Juexing stepped closer and reached out. The mirror vanished into thin air, leaving only a black shadow burned onto the wall.

Orange carried Zilan to the office and left her in the care of the duty teacher. When she turned, Juexing was not behind her. She ran out again and nearly collided with him at the main gate.

“There’s still one more person, isn’t there?” he asked.

“Yes—Wang Mei. She said she was trapped in a toilet.”

“I found this inside.” He handed her a purple flip-style Samsung phone. “The call came from this handset. I checked the log—the timing matches.”

Orange examined it.

“This… isn’t Zilan’s phone.”

“I think Wang Mei is probably at home.”

“But… that was definitely Wang Mei’s voice. I couldn’t have misheard.”

A sense of crisis rose in Orange’s chest like a rising wind, growing stronger with every second.

### Character Notes

**Luo Juexing**  
Publicly and calmly ends the ambiguity with Yu Xun, reclaiming the name “Tangerine” in front of everyone. His immediate decision to follow Orange to the school shows that the protective instinct has fully returned. The mirror incident confirms he can still perceive and confront residual entities even after the memory loss.

**Qingmu Chengzi (Orange)**  
The emergency call overrides personal celebration. Her instinct to protect Mo Zilan and then Wang Mei places her once again at the center of an active haunting. The discovery that the phone is not Zilan’s escalates the threat from isolated bullying to deliberate, targeted deception.

**Yu Xun**  
Accepts the public rejection with outward composure, yet the private conversation with Jiaye reveals lingering resentment and a rapid recalculation of strategy. Placing her hand on his signals a possible shift in target.

**Huang Jiaye**  
Delivers an unexpectedly mature and generous speech that both comforts Yu Xun and frees himself from bitterness. His willingness to accompany her to the school keeps him inside the unfolding case.

**Mo Zilan / Lin Qiaohe**  
The sealed toilet, the creaking, the unconscious body, and the vanishing mirror with the red-lipped smile strongly indicate that the residual presence is no longer limited to passive appearances. It can now imitate voices and manipulate space.

**The Purple Phone**  
A concrete piece of evidence that the call was staged. Whoever—or whatever—used Wang Mei’s voice is actively drawing the living into the school’s unfinished business.
