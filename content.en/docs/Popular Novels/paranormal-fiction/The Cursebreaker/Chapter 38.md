She had almost convinced herself that everything before had been nothing more than a beautiful illusion. The moment she saw Shang Xuan again, she knew it was real. Even if it had to be called a dream, it was a dream that kept growing warmer and more romantic with no clear end in sight.

The heavyset supervisor stood by the counter watching Wan Ting for a long time. She had never seen the girl look so happy. That expression told her something was going on. Concerned that Wan Ting’s mood might cause her to make mistakes with the register, the woman edged around behind her and found a good vantage point to watch the screen. She found nothing to criticize, which only made her more irritated.

In a quiet moment between customers, Wan Ting slipped her phone from the drawer and sent Shang Xuan a cute smiling emoji. The smile on her own face was sweeter than honey.

“No phones while you’re on the clock,” the supervisor muttered.

Wan Ting put the phone away at once and returned to work.

Because she was on the late shift, she would not be able to leave until eleven-thirty. The remaining three hours stretched ahead of her like an ordeal. She kept glancing at the large wall clock, but time refused to move any faster or slower.

“In a hurry, are we?” The supervisor still had not left. She hovered behind Wan Ting like a shadow, as if she had been born to dislike her.

“No…” Wan Ting lowered her head and toned down her smile.

“Tell you what. If you want to leave early, go up to the third-floor storeroom at ten and tidy the shelves. Half an hour should be enough if you work fast.” The woman jerked her chin toward the upper floors. To Wan Ting it did not sound like a favor.

“It’s settled then. At ten I’ll send someone to take your place so you can’t say I’m heartless behind my back.” The supervisor decided the matter herself and walked away.

A female colleague leaned over and whispered, “Those shelves in the storeroom are a nightmare. She’s deliberately making things hard for you.”

“It’s fine. I actually wanted to leave early anyway. Maybe it really will only take half an hour.” Wan Ting smiled and checked the clock again. Half an hour until ten. If she finished by ten-thirty she might still catch Shang Xuan for a late-night meal.

That evening Shang Xuan contacted a street informant who specialized in information. The man arranged to meet him at the seaside park at eleven—cash in one hand, documents in the other.

A sharp *click* sounded as the supervisor switched on the storeroom light. The bulb was only fifteen watts and years old; it flickered every few seconds, looking exactly like the lighting used in horror films.

“Here’s the key. Lock up when you’re done. Just the two back rows of shelves. Don’t go poking around anywhere else.” She left a rusted key on a dusty little wooden table by the door and walked out.

Wan Ting looked around. The storeroom was at the very end of the third floor, far from any noise or foot traffic—only silence and a damp chill remained.

She sighed, walked to the farthest shelves, and began straightening the haphazardly stacked items: account books, personnel files, and all sorts of miscellaneous records. Some of the papers were covered in a thick layer of dust, as if no one had cleaned in a long time. She found a feather duster and carefully wiped each item. Every time she finished one, a smile returned to her face. The thought of seeing him again soon made the work feel light.

She was so absorbed that she never noticed the door being closed—softly, almost without a sound. From the outside a heavy lock clicked shut, sealing the dim yellow light inside the room.

To pass the time she hummed while she worked. Soon the back row of shelves was clean and orderly. She looked at the spotless shelves and neatly arranged files and smiled even more brightly. Moving to the second set of shelves, she bent to pick up a folder that had fallen to the floor—and realized the aisle was dark. She had not locked the door herself.

Slowly she straightened, set the folder on the shelf, and walked toward the entrance. An ice-cold door stood in front of her. Someone’s idea of a prank.

The happiness inside her began to turn into panic. Her heartbeat became irregular. She ran to the door and twisted the handle hard, but the heavy lock outside only rattled against the wood and refused to give.

“Is anyone there? Is anyone outside?” She pounded on the door and called out, but the corridor remained empty. Even the lights along the hallway began to go out one after another. Not a single cockroach scuttled past.

The last thin strip of light under the door vanished. She turned her back to the door and stared at the flickering bulb. The intervals between flashes were growing longer. Was someone playing tricks?

There was still some light, yet the constant on-and-off made her deeply uneasy—especially alone in a nearly sealed room.

She did not dare shout too loudly. She was afraid her voice might attract something strange—those things ordinary people called wandering spirits that existed in another way.

Sweat began to bead in her palms. Her breathing grew rapid and obvious in the confined space.

Her consciousness clung to a single thought: she had to get out. Suddenly she remembered her phone. She could call Shang Xuan and ask him to come to the supermarket.

She patted her pockets, then remembered the phone was in her bag. Trembling, she stood and edged along the shelves until she reached the table. She pulled out the phone and dialed his number.

The call connected, but rang for a long time without answer.

Desperate, she hung up and tried again. This time it connected, yet the other end was completely silent—no breathing, nothing.

She tried speaking. “Shang Xuan… is that you?”

Shang Xuan held the phone to his ear and answered with a quiet “mm” while counting out bills for the informant.

“Hurry—come to the supermarket—”  

Wan Ting tried to say more, but the phone died without warning and shut itself off. At the exact same moment the fifteen-watt bulb went out, plunging the storeroom into total darkness.

Shang Xuan looked at the screen. The call was still listed as connected, yet he could hear nothing. Only the name “Wan Ting” glowed on the display.

The informant held out a stack of papers. Shang Xuan stared at the phone for a long time without taking them.

“Hey, you want these or not?”

“Something’s wrong.”  

A sudden premonition hit him. He snatched the papers and sprinted toward the supermarket. In the scramble one sheet—a report on Wan Ting’s cause of death—slipped free and fell. The informant started to call after him, but Shang Xuan was already gone. The man bent, picked up the page, and tossed it into a trash bin before lighting a cigarette and walking away from the seaside park.

Some drunk later dropped a still-burning cigarette butt into that same bin. Thick smoke rose. The sensitive fire alarm began to shriek. Supermarket staff started evacuating customers.

Inside the already pitch-black storeroom, the distant blare of the alarm only made Wan Ting more terrified. She curled into the farthest corner of the wall, bracing herself once again for that extreme, painful torment.

Shadows seemed to drift around her—blue ones, red ones, spirits of the wrongfully dead, the violently dead… She buried her face in her hands and refused to let her mind invent anything. She could no longer endure being trapped like this, even if nothing was actually there.

Tears soaked the fabric of her pants. Her eyelids fluttered toward closing. A dry *ka-ka* sound escaped her throat, like the noise of a printer.

Shang Xuan ran like a mad lion, cutting through everything in his path. Twenty minutes later he stood gasping at the supermarket’s main entrance. Most of the customers had already been cleared out. Firefighters were on scene investigating.

He vaulted the police line, avoided the cameras, and charged up to the second floor. Aside from a few staff members, Wan Ting was nowhere to be seen.

The heavyset supervisor came out of an office, closed the door, and spotted him. She swayed over, rested a fleshy hand on his shoulder, and asked, “Handsome, you here to put out the fire?”

“I’m looking for someone. Have you seen Wan Ting—the cashier who was at the counter earlier?” He pushed her hand away.

“Oh, her? Haven’t seen her…” The moment she realized he was looking for Wan Ting, her expression changed. She turned and barked at the remaining staff, “Everyone downstairs for assembly! Whoever the hell started a fire in this store is going to pay.”

Shang Xuan stood at the stairwell leading to the third floor, unsure what to do next, when a female cashier edged up to him and said quietly, without looking up, “I don’t know if Wan Ting is still in the third-floor storeroom.”

“Thank you.”  

He started to run, but a security guard blocked his path.

“No one left upstairs. What do you think you’re doing?”

Shang Xuan answered with a clean shoulder throw, then sprinted for the storeroom at the far end of the third floor.

He found the door locked from the outside. If anyone had been inside, they could not have locked it themselves.

He knocked and called Wan Ting’s name several times. The room remained silent.

Stepping back, he grabbed a fire extinguisher from the wall and smashed it hard against the lock. With a sharp metallic crack the lock gave way. He yanked the door open and groped for the light switch. The bulb refused to light.

He pulled out his phone and used the weak glow to search the darkness. In the farthest corner he found Wan Ting collapsed on the floor.

He lifted her into his arms as if he had found something precious and could not bear to let go. Her faint breathing told him there was no time to waste. He had to get her to a hospital—any later and her life might be in real danger.

### Character Notes

**Lu Wanting (Wan Ting)**  
Trapped in the third-floor storeroom after being deliberately assigned the task by her supervisor. The sudden darkness, locked door, and dying phone push her into a state of extreme fear that echoes past trauma.

**Shang Xuan**  
Receives critical (and incomplete) information about Wan Ting’s background at the exact moment her emergency call reaches him. His sudden premonition and reckless race to the supermarket save her life.

**The Supervisor (Fat Woman)**  
Petty and controlling. Her decision to send Wan Ting to the isolated storeroom creates the perfect conditions for the residual force to act.

**The Informant**  
Provides documents on Wan Ting, one of which—a report on her cause of death—is discarded and later contributes to the fire alarm that complicates the rescue.

**The Storeroom**  
A confined, poorly lit space that becomes a temporary prison. The flickering light, sudden total darkness, and external lock turn ordinary isolation into something far more threatening.
