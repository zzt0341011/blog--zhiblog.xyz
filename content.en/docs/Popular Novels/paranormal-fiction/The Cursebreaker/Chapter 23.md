The next morning the doctors put Juexing through a full-hour physical examination. The conclusion was clear: apart from partial memory loss, he had recovered extremely well and could be discharged at once.

Yu Xun had been waiting outside the door since early morning. When she saw the doctor emerge smiling, she knew the outcome was good.

She entered, helped Juexing gather his belongings, then linked her arm through his and led him out of the hospital.

Warm sunlight fell across his face. He raised a hand to shield his eyes; Yu Xun immediately took an umbrella from her bag.

“Shall we have breakfast first and then go home?” she asked.

Juexing nodded. “Breakfast first.”

“What do you usually have? Congee or soy milk?”

“There’s a savory congee shop downstairs from my place. Their congee is excellent.”

“Then that’s where we’ll go.” She smiled and walked beside him.

A few steps later he stopped.

“Wait. What exactly is our relationship? Why were you at the hospital?”

Yu Xun laughed softly.

“I’m your girlfriend, of course. Meng Yuxun. Don’t you remember?”

Juexing looked at her gentle smile and, without quite realizing it, smiled back.

“Come on, don’t overthink it. From now on I’ll stay by your side.”

Jiaye finished his work, bought a box of egg tarts, and went to the hospital—only to learn that Juexing had already been discharged that morning.

“Did he go home?” He scratched the back of his head and set off for Juexing’s apartment. He never expected to witness the scene that awaited him outside a certain savory congee shop.

The box of egg tarts slipped from his fingers. Even on the noisy street the sound was clear. He started forward to confront them—then froze mid-step. What standing did he have to demand an explanation? Perhaps they were only sharing breakfast. Why was he reacting so strongly?

He had almost convinced himself it was nothing more than a meal—until her next gesture confirmed every suspicion.

Yu Xun scooped up a spoonful of mushrooms and held it to Juexing’s lips. Juexing smiled and accepted it. The intimacy of the act left little room for innocent interpretation.

Jiaye forced down the heat rising in his chest, slowed his pace, crossed the street, and stepped into the shop, stopping beside their table.

Juexing turned. “You’re here too. Join us?”

Jiaye glanced at Yu Xun, then seized Juexing by the collar and dragged him outside.

“What the hell are you doing? She’s Yuxun, not Orange!”

“I know she’s Yuxun. Who’s Orange?” Juexing looked genuinely puzzled.

Jiaye stared at him sideways. Memory loss? Impossible. The bullet hadn’t hit his head, and there was no sign of concussion.

“That’s enough. Stop it.” Yu Xun finished paying at the counter, walked out, and spoke to Jiaye in a low, level voice.

“What do you mean by that?”

“I can’t explain everything right now. Just pretend you saw nothing—and do not tell Orange that he’s awake. If you do, we’re finished as friends.”

She took Juexing’s wrist and led him toward an apartment building across the street.

Jiaye exhaled hard, raked a hand through his hair, and punched the shop door hard enough to draw stares from everyone nearby.

Sunday. No classes. Orange used the free morning to complete the handover with the counselor who was about to leave. Only one person sat in the office—a woman wearing glasses and an oddly strained expression. This, Orange assumed, must be her predecessor.

She bowed politely and stepped inside.

The woman kept her head down, sorting papers with increasing speed, as though she had something to hide. Suddenly she stopped. Her eyes slid toward a corner of the room, as if something were standing there watching them.

“Are you all right?” Orange asked.

The woman snapped back to herself, shoved an entire box of files toward Orange, wrote a long string of characters on a scrap of paper, then snatched up her bag and hurried past. At the doorway she glanced once more at the same corner. Her body gave a visible shudder before she fled.

Orange looked in that direction. Apart from a large potted plant on the floor, the corner was empty. The office felt suddenly cold and quiet. She sat down alone and began going through the materials. Before she realized it, noon had arrived.

She stretched, checked her phone—twelve o’clock—and moved the files to her own desk. As she turned to lock the door, the potted plant seemed subtly different.

She smiled at herself. Too much stress, nothing more. She returned to the dormitory for her lunch box, then headed to the cafeteria.

A few scattered students were still eating. One girl sat alone on the steps some distance from the entrance, holding her lunch box. The sight immediately raised a question in Orange’s mind: why wasn’t she eating inside?

Orange could not explain why the girl drew her attention so strongly. The way she kept her head down while eating looked quietly pitiable. Orange entered the cafeteria, collected her own meal, and had just sat down when three girls who appeared to have finished eating walked toward the steps.

A bad premonition rose in her chest. She watched without blinking.

Sunlight threw three long shadows across the girl. Her chopsticks stopped moving, but she did not look up.

The tall girl in the middle grabbed a handful of small stones and tossed them into the lunch box, then slapped the seated girl across the face.

“Eat up. You look like you’re enjoying yourself so much!”

The girl on the left seized a handful of hair, yanked it back and forth, then shoved her onto the steps.

“What’s this? Playing the quiet type? Don’t think being pretty means we won’t deal with you. If the principal hadn’t caught us last time, I’d already have carved up that face of yours.” The tall girl pinched her chin and glared down at her.

Orange snapped her lunch box shut and ran over.

“Stop it. What do you think you’re doing?”

The three girls turned, stuck out their tongues at her, and swaggered away.

The girl on the steps sat up and began picking grains of rice and bits of vegetable back into her box. Orange caught a glimpse of the bruises on her arms.

“You can’t eat that anymore. Take mine.” Orange held out her own lunch box.

The girl only stared at the little cartoon pig drawn on the lid, then stood and ran off without a word.

Orange watched her retreating back and wondered: was this one of the “strange things” the departing teacher had mentioned?

Most people on campus seemed to treat it as normal. A male teacher who had witnessed the scene approached, greeted Orange, and said casually, “She’s a new transfer student. Name’s Mo Zilan. Too introverted, so…”

“Is she bullied often? Hasn’t the school noticed and put a stop to it?”

“Well…” The man realized he had said too much, forced a polite laugh, and hurried away.

The indifference chilled Orange so thoroughly that she lost all appetite. She returned alone to the office, suddenly determined to learn more about the student named Mo Zilan.

Outside the window, a pair of lonely eyes watched her. Those eyes held both quiet joy and deep resignation. In the girl’s heart, Orange was the first person who had stood up for her—yet she already believed Orange would soon become as cold as everyone else.

Orange felt the weight of the gaze, looked up, and caught only a flicker of long hair vanishing around the corner.

She was carefully examining student files when one folder slipped to the floor. As she bent to retrieve it she saw, standing on the opposite side of the room, a pair of nearly transparent feet.

Her heartbeat surged. Instinct whispered the truth before her mind could form the words.

She jerked her head up. Apart from the potted plant, the room was empty. She rubbed her temples, opened the fallen file, and read the name printed at the top: Lin Qiaohe.

On the next page a photocopied photograph smiled out from its frame—bright, lively, the perfect image of a high-school girl in the full bloom of youth. Orange continued turning pages, her own smile slowly freezing. The file was two years old. Every other record in the office had been updated. Why did this single outdated file remain?

Her thoughts began to roar. She set the papers down and pressed her hands over her ears, trying to quiet her nerves. A sudden breeze swept through the open window, lifting her hair and flipping the pages of Lin Qiaohe’s file. On the final page a single line of red handwriting made Orange spring from her chair.

*To stop being bullied I chose the road of no return. But I will keep waiting for the day retribution arrives…*

Orange locked the file in a drawer. She needed to leave the office and rest.

On the path leading toward the classroom dormitories she saw Mo Zilan standing beside an empty swing, pushing it back and forth as though someone were sitting on the seat. Long hair hid her face. Was she truly as beautiful as the bullies claimed—beautiful enough to provoke such jealousy?

Orange abandoned the idea of returning to her room. She changed direction and walked toward the swing. Mo Zilan was so absorbed in the motion that she never noticed the three girls approaching from behind.

“Look, she’s playing on the swing with a ghost again.”

The voice belonged to one of the earlier bullies. Orange quickened her pace. The tall girl reached Mo Zilan first, seized the iron chain, and said, “I’m telling you—are you sick? Can you stop acting so weird all the time?”

Mo Zilan stood motionless, head still lowered. She no longer even considered resisting; resistance had only ever brought heavier beatings.

Another girl arrived carrying a bucket of water. Just as she raised it to pour, Orange yanked Mo Zilan aside. The foul-smelling water cascaded over Orange instead.

“Ah—!”

The three girls stared as if they had seen a ghost, dropped the bucket, and fled.

Mo Zilan looked at the soaked Orange and said nothing. Orange smiled.

“Don’t worry. After I speak with them, they won’t bully you anymore.”

Mo Zilan wanted to believe the certainty in Orange’s eyes. Yet every previous attempt at kindness had begun the same way—and ended the same way. She pulled her hand free and walked off without looking back.

### Character Notes

**Luo Juexing**  
Discharged with partial amnesia. He accepts Yu Xun’s claim that she is his girlfriend without resistance, creating an immediate and dangerous emotional triangle. His genuine confusion when Jiaye mentions Orange confirms the memory loss is real.

**Yu Xun (Meng Yuxun)**  
Actively rewrites the narrative. By claiming the role of girlfriend and threatening Jiaye into silence, she seizes the window of Juexing’s vulnerability. The intimate gesture of feeding him is both tender and calculated.

**Qingmu Chengzi (Orange)**  
Begins her new role by instinctively protecting a bullied student. The discovery of Lin Qiaohe’s two-year-old suicide note and the transparent feet in the office signal that the vocational high school carries its own residual resentment—separate from, yet thematically linked to, the earlier train arc.

**Mo Zilan**  
A quiet transfer student who has already accepted bullying as her daily reality. She pushes an empty swing as though sharing it with someone unseen, and she cannot yet believe Orange’s promise of protection.

**Lin Qiaohe**  
The previous victim. Her outdated file and the red-ink declaration of waiting for retribution establish her as the likely source of the school’s unnatural atmosphere. The bullies’ terror when the water hits Orange instead of Mo Zilan hints that Lin Qiaohe is already active.

**Huang Jiaye**  
Caught between loyalty to Orange and Yu Xun’s threat. His confrontation outside the congee shop ends in frustrated violence against a door—symbolic of how little control he currently has over the situation.
