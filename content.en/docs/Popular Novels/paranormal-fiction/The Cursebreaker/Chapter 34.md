Orange stood outside Shang Xuan’s door for more than an hour. The doorbell went unanswered; calls went straight to voicemail. He had vanished as thoroughly as if he had never existed. By 1:40 she was starving and had no choice but to find somewhere to eat first.

While waiting for the food she dialed Juexing. To her frustration the call went directly to power-off—the first time she had ever encountered that situation with him.

A quiet unease rose in her chest. She decided that after the meal she would slip over to his apartment and stage a small ambush.

---

Shang Xuan remained in Wanting’s apartment. The two of them talked as if they had known each other for years, yet whenever he asked about her family she either avoided his eyes or changed the subject. The behavior left him distinctly puzzled.

“What’s that smell?” He sniffed. A rich aroma of stewed bones had filled the living room, drifting from the kitchen.

“Oh—the big-bone soup!” Wanting suddenly remembered the pot still simmering. She stood up too quickly, tripped over the chair, and would have fallen if Shang Xuan had not caught her wrist.

The instant his skin touched hers, a wave of intense cold sank into his palm. At the same moment a stream of warmth slipped deep into her heart. A faint dimple appeared; their eyes met; both straightened at once.

“I’ll go check on it. Wait a moment.” She smiled lightly and disappeared into the kitchen.

Inside, she leaned against the cabinet, one hand pressed to her chest. She could feel her heartbeat racing and her cheeks burning.

Shang Xuan turned his palm upward. A white imprint like frost lay across the center of his hand—then vanished in a blink.

Wanting’s thoughts spun in circles, trying to filter out the chaos, so absorbed that she failed to notice Shang Xuan already standing in the kitchen doorway.

“Are you all right?”

She started, quickly turned her back, and grabbed a ladle to lift the pot lid and inspect the bones.

“I have something to take care of. If you need anything, come to apartment 1001 on the tenth floor. Goodbye.”

She wanted to say more, but when she turned he had already closed the door. He did not leave immediately; he stood in front of it, uncertain what he intended to do.

Wanting, still holding the ladle, walked to the door. Through the single panel she could sense that he had not gone.

Hopeful, she opened it. A dark silhouette filled the gap. Shang Xuan was still there, back toward her.

“I thought you’d left. Won’t you really have a bowl of soup?”

“No. Take care of yourself. I’m going.” He did not turn around. He walked straight toward the stairs. He could have taken the elevator, yet at that moment he wanted to lengthen the walk home. The reason, the reader will understand.

Wanting pressed her lips together, reluctant, and closed the door. She turned off the stove, covered the pot, and only then noticed Shang Xuan’s jacket still lying on the sofa.

She hurried over, picked it up, and ran back to the door—then stopped. She did not want to return the jacket so quickly.

---

Shang Xuan returned home. The three suitcases, the books and papers scattered across the table and floor, the clothes still strewn across the bedroom—only then did he remember the schedule he had set for himself that day.

He knocked his own forehead with a fist.

“Great. A few thousand yuan in plane tickets wasted…”

Before he finished the sentence the doorbell rang.

He opened the door. Outside stood Wanting, holding a food container.

“There’s too much soup. I can’t finish it alone. I wondered if you might help me eat a little.”

“The place is a bit messy. If you don’t mind, come in and sit.” He stepped aside, sounding faintly resigned.

She nodded and entered. After a quick scan of the entire apartment she smiled.

“You drink the soup. I’ll help you tidy up.” She set the container down and, ignoring his half-hearted protests, began organizing from the living room outward.

“Hey, I…” The rest of the sentence died in his throat. He could not bring himself to stop the girl in front of him. What exactly did her appearance mean?

---

After the lavish lunch with Jiaye and Yu Xun, Juexing returned to the university to work. His phone had been left at home and had not been charged all night; it could only lie on the nightstand in silent confusion.

Orange unlocked the door with her key. The apartment looked exactly as she remembered—clean, orderly. Men who kept house this carefully were rare these days.

She smiled, walked into the bedroom to check for laundry, and found nothing except a jacket hanging on the rack; every other garment was neatly folded inside the wardrobe.

She touched the quilt. The sheet felt a little soiled, so she took out a fresh one, changed the bedding, and started the washing machine.

In the interval she began tidying the desk. In one of the drawers she discovered a box she had never seen before. Curiosity made her open it. Inside lay a dozen letters. Each envelope carried only a single character: **晴** (Qing).

Reading someone else’s private letters was an invasion of privacy, yet the moment she saw the character “Qing” she could not help thinking of Xiao Qing. The girl had grown unusually attentive toward Juexing lately, constantly chasing Orange with questions. Although Orange had no real reason to doubt her own relationship with him, people are sometimes blind and small-minded. Suspicion drove her to take one of the letters and read it.

After finishing the first letter her head buzzed. She felt as though she had suddenly understood something—or rather, as though she had stumbled upon a truth she was never meant to know.

---

Shang Xuan did not drink the soup. He sat at the dining table the entire time, watching Wanting methodically restore order to the living room and then the bedroom. By the time she finished it was already past six in the evening.

“You’ve worked hard.” He handed her a towel.

“It’s nothing compared to the help you gave me.” She smiled.

“It’s getting late. Why don’t we go out for dinner together?”

“I’d like that. I was just about to suggest the same thing.”

“All right. You go change first. I’ll come downstairs for you in a moment.”

“Okay. I’ll take a quick shower. Come down when you’re ready.” She nodded. He walked her to the door.

Once she was gone, cold sweat gradually beaded on Shang Xuan’s forehead. The living room and bedroom that had been in complete chaos only hours earlier now stood neat and orderly under her hands.

“What’s wrong with me?” he muttered as he walked down the street.

At some point a slender, slightly cool hand had slipped into his. He turned his head. Wanting was already holding his palm.

“Where are we eating?”

“What do you feel like—Chinese or Western?”

“Western. I haven’t had steak in a long time.”

He did not release her hand. Instead he tightened his grip without thinking. Perhaps this was the turning point heaven had given him. He was not the hesitant Juexing; he was a man who dared to love and hate. He would not let such a chance slip away.

A single candle lamp stood in the center of the table. On either side of the flickering light sat two glasses of red wine. The simple arrangement, the harmonious atmosphere, the air scented with beef jus and quiet smiles—Shang Xuan and Wanting sat opposite each other by the floor-to-ceiling window, saying little while they waited for the steaks to arrive.

“I’m really happy to be able to eat with you,” Wanting said, breaking the silence.

Shang Xuan smiled faintly. “I hope there will be more chances in the future.”

The waiter arrived with the two plates. Wanting had been about to say something else, but the interruption forced the words back into her heart. She would keep them for another time.

They ate the steaks in comfortable conversation. Shang Xuan had never been so talkative. People passing their table stole glances—whether drawn by his looks, her beauty, or the elegant ease of their talk, no one could say.

Beautiful time is always short. They walked home slowly, still reluctant for the evening to end. They continued to hold hands like an intimate couple. Her hand was no longer as icy as when he had first touched it; it now carried the lingering warmth of his own.

At the foot of the apartment building he stopped her.

Wanting felt his hands close around her arms. She could sense what he was thinking. She closed her eyes lightly and tilted her face upward. Shang Xuan slowly brought his lips closer—

*Click.*

A camera captured the moment of their near-kiss.

“It’s late. Good night.” He stepped back and said goodbye.

Wanting pressed her lips together. “Thank you. I’ll remember you. I hope you’ll remember me as well…”

---

Juexing returned home after ten. The bedroom lamp was on. He called out, “I’m back,” and then saw Orange emerge.

“I knew it was you. Why did you come over?” He hung his jacket on the hook.

She did not answer. In her hand she still clutched the letter she had read.

“I’m sorry. I shouldn’t have looked at your letters. I just… don’t understand why you kept this from me.” Her head remained lowered.

Juexing glanced at the box on the desk, then at the letter in her hand, and understood at once.

He guided her to the sofa, then turned toward the kitchen. She caught his wrist and would not let him leave.

“You haven’t eaten all evening—your stomach is growling so loudly. Let me make you some congee first. We can talk after you’ve eaten, all right?” He smiled.

Orange released him, curled up, and buried her face in her arms as though she were crying. He patted her shoulder.

“It’s all right. I’ll tell you everything. But first, please trust me.”

She did not lift her head—only nodded and answered with soft, muffled sounds.

Juexing shook his head, went into the kitchen, and after some time returned with a steaming bowl of fragrant eight-treasure congee. Orange remained buried in her arms, unmoving.

The aroma finally overcame her resistance. She raised her head reluctantly and looked at him sitting beside the table, at the hot congee waiting for her. Her eyes were red; the matter clearly weighed heavily on her.

“Silly girl. Eat first. We’ll talk after. You’re not going home tonight—I intend to take proper care of you.” A faintly mischievous light flickered in his eyes. Orange averted her gaze and picked up the spoon.

### Character Notes

**Shang Xuan**  
The delayed departure becomes permanent the moment Lu Wanting appears with soup and begins quietly ordering his chaos. The cold imprint left by her wrist and the stolen photograph of their near-kiss mark the beginning of a new, still-undefined bond.

**Lu Wanting**  
Avoids every question about family, lives in a glass-door apartment, and carries a prescription from a recent hospital stay. Her decision to keep Shang Xuan’s jacket and to return with soup shows she is already choosing connection over isolation.

**Qingmu Chengzi (Orange)**  
Her discovery of the letters marked only “Qing” introduces the first serious crack of doubt into the relationship she has fought so hard to secure. The red-eyed silence while waiting for Juexing’s explanation reveals how deeply the secret has unsettled her.

**Luo Juexing**  
Returns to find both the letters and Orange’s trust temporarily broken. His request for belief before explanation, followed by the simple act of cooking congee and inviting her to stay, is an attempt to hold the relationship together while he decides how much of the past he can safely reveal.

**The Letters Marked “晴”**  
A box of correspondence addressed only with the single character for “Qing.” Their existence, and Orange’s reaction after reading one, strongly imply a hidden connection between Juexing and Zhao Xiaoqing that predates the current school arc.

**The Stolen Photograph**  
An unseen third party records Shang Xuan and Lu Wanting’s almost-kiss, introducing an external observer (and potential future complication) into the newly forming relationship.
