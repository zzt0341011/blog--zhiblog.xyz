Little Bell stood in the eighth-floor corridor. The apartment Wanting had lived in now felt cold and oppressive. Night had not yet deepened, yet a silvery glow already filled the space. She steeled herself, walked to the door, and raised a hand to knock. Before her knuckles touched the wood the door opened by itself, as if Wanting had known she was coming.

“I’ll go. Everything must have an ending. Even if you hadn’t come, I would have gone myself.” Wanting had changed into the outfit she once wore when she and Shang Xuan rode the carousel together. He had said she looked most beautiful in it.

Little Bell said nothing. A chill climbed her spine. Her feet felt filled with lead and refused to move.

“I won’t harm you, and I certainly won’t harm Shang Xuan. I only want to know why you have repeatedly stood between us.”

“Because… because I like him. Yet he likes a ghost!”

Wanting could feel the fear inside the girl. She also understood, with painful clarity, what a joke it was for a living person to fall in love with a shadow—an absurdity beyond reason.

“I once thought my feelings for Shang Xuan were only a momentary impulse. Now I see I was wrong.” Wanting turned. Under the light that spilled in from the streetlamp she cast no shadow at all—because she herself was already a shadow.

Her face was pale, as though layered with whitening powder, carrying the same chilling aura that film ghosts so often possessed.

“Eight o’clock tonight, at the scrapyard in the north of the city—correct?”

Little Bell nodded.

Wanting stepped closer. “Cherish him well. Believe that one day he will see your love and accept it.”

At the exact moment Jiaye and Yu Xun stepped out of the elevator, Wanting and Little Bell stepped into the other one. When the pair reached Wanting’s door it was locked and silent. A small sign hung on it: *Away on Business*.

Jiaye turned toward the second elevator, which had already reached the ground floor. He immediately called the colleagues still stationed below and ordered them to intercept Wanting. By the time the officers arrived in the lobby, both women had vanished. When Jiaye and Yu Xun themselves reached the ground floor the hall was empty and quiet.

“Where could they have gone?” Jiaye’s brows locked together.

“We should tell Juexing first. One more head is better than one less.” Yu Xun suggested.

Jiaye dialed, but the call went unanswered. Juexing had left his phone at Orange’s apartment; at that moment he and Orange were already racing toward the northern scrapyard. The tip had come from a mysterious old woman who pushed a cart and appeared and disappeared like a dragon’s head in the clouds.

“Why isn’t he answering?” Jiaye muttered. At that instant Yu Xun received a delayed text from Orange—half an hour late because of network trouble.

“Northern scrapyard!” she read aloud.

“Everyone, move out—northern scrapyard!” Jiaye ordered the others, then dived into the car. The drive normally took an hour, and that stretch of road was often congested. He estimated they would arrive around eight.

Throughout the journey Little Bell’s heart remained suspended. If Shang Xuan saw her bringing Wanting to save him, would he lose all reason and refuse to speak to her ever again?

Wanting watched her with a smile. Though the smile looked stiff, she was still beautiful when she smiled—a natural reaction for any man who preferred beauty.

“It’s all right. I’ve already spoken with Shang Xuan. He won’t blame you.”

Little Bell kept silent, afraid that once she opened her mouth the words would become a flood she could not stop.

The northern scrapyard was under renovation, so at night no one passed through. It was, in its own way, an excellent place for a “transaction.”

Shang Xuan’s hands were bound and he hung from an abandoned hydraulic lift. Several men carrying machetes paced around him, though the blades looked more like props than real weapons—meant only for show.

Tian Shubin and Lu Shengnan hid inside a doorless office, watching every movement outside. It was already past seven-thirty. In another half hour the target would appear. Once they dealt with her they could finally rest easy.

Yet man cannot fight heaven. Heaven’s punishment does exist; it merely takes different forms.

The moon withdrew its light completely. Even the last thin silver was taken away. The entire scrapyard sank into absolute darkness. Weeds grew wild, as if the ground itself were laced with traps. One careless step and a person could fall—into something as ordinary, and as unpleasant, as dog droppings.

Those thirty minutes felt endless to the waiting men. Time ticked forward second by second. In the distance two silhouettes appeared. The slightly taller one should be Lu Wanting; the figure beside her must be Little Bell.

“Target in sight. Everyone ready,” Tian told the man at the doorway.

Wanting walked in front, Little Bell behind. They quickly disappeared into the darkness, unaware that a net had already been spread and escape would be nearly impossible.

A bucket of cold water woke Shang Xuan. Only then did he understand he had fallen into Lu Shengnan’s hands. He struggled violently, but it was useless. Soon he saw Wanting approaching slowly, and behind her, Little Bell.

The first thought that flashed through his mind was: Little Bell actually brought Wanting here…

“I have come,” Wanting said. “Why does my cousin still not show himself?”

The words were barely finished when Lu Shengnan stepped out from the side. At the same moment a man with a machete appeared behind Little Bell; before she could react the bright blade rested against her throat.

“You—” Wanting moved to help, but Tian stopped her. “We only want you. I will not harm anyone else. But for now she must stay quiet.” He signaled, and Little Bell was also bound and hung from a second lift.

Shang Xuan struggled but could not make a sound; a cloth had been forced into his mouth. Wanting had no choice but to watch them both suspended.

“Why leave the underworld where you belonged and come up here?” Lu Shengnan sneered. “Looking for two people to take with you? These two are perfect.”

“Cousin, you seized my father’s fortune and caused my death. I never planned to harm any of you. Why can you not leave me alone?”

“Careful with your words. That fortune was left to me in your father’s will. Besides, we could have been a couple—you simply resisted and pretended to be demure. How was I to know you would lose your mind while locked in that office?” The ugliness finally poured from his mouth. Not only had he tampered with the will before Lu Zhengyao signed it; he had also desired his own cousin. A man of such crimes, once dead, would certainly be cast into the eighteenth level of hell and never reborn.

“You’re lying. My father would never have suffered a sudden stroke. It was you and this man named Tian who put poison in the food, controlled him while he wrote the will, and then—” The memories of three years earlier flooded back; sharp pain stabbed through her head. Tian seized the moment of her dizziness, pressed a prepared sewer button, and shoved her downward.

Shang Xuan watched helplessly as Wanting fell into the abyss. The ropes around his wrists were already soaked with blood. He wanted nothing more than to cut off his own hands and leap after her, yet he could do nothing.

“Enjoy the closed-in feeling once more!” Tian laughed coldly.

“No one will ever know I forged the will. No one will ever know I violated my cousin and locked her in that office until she died. No one will ever—”

Tian cut him off sharply. “Have you gone mad? There are still two people hanging here!”

“Hmph. Two fires will clean everything. Leave a suicide note full of tragic love—how moving.” In a single night Lu Shengnan had changed beyond recognition. Even he no longer knew whether he was still himself. He lit a cigarette and tossed the match onto a prepared pile of wood. With a roar the timber caught fire.

He pulled an envelope from his pocket and dropped it on the ground. “You two may rest in peace in the heavenly kingdom.”

The flames grew higher, yet Little Bell smiled. Perhaps the ending was not ideal, but to travel to the underworld together with Shang Xuan was still a kind of fortune.

Lu Shengnan looked at her smiling face and chuckled. “I never expected someone who truly sets life and death aside. Lu admires you.” He pulled the cloth from Shang Xuan’s mouth, then walked toward the waiting cars without looking back.

“Bastards! May you die without a peaceful end!” Shang Xuan’s face was twisted with grief. No matter how he shouted he could not stop the disaster. The most pitiful part was that Little Bell had been dragged into it.

“I’m sorry I dragged you into this.”

Tears filled Little Bell’s eyes. “I don’t regret it. If there is a next life, I will grow up faster and be with you.” Shang Xuan lowered his head and watched the fire slowly reach the soles of his shoes.

Suddenly an unexpected figure shot forward. Flashing police lights appeared at the same moment.

Juexing flipped through the air and landed squarely among the thugs. Orange slipped quietly behind Little Bell and began untying the ropes.

Juexing clenched his fists, eyes fixed on the gleaming blades. Moonlight returned without warning, striking the steel and casting silver flashes. In that instant he drove a rapid punch, dropping the nearest man. Several knives swung toward him at once. He spun, swept out an unexpected kick, and felled another. Jiaye, arriving a step later, blocked Lu Shengnan’s attempt to flee.

The fire was already licking at Shang Xuan’s shoe soles. Ignoring the burn, he shouted to Juexing, “Wanting is in the sewer pit—please save her! I’m begging you!”  

Hearing the heart-rending cry, Juexing’s rage overflowed. He toppled the remaining four men in rapid succession. Just as he turned to free Shang Xuan first, the bound man stopped him:

“I don’t want her to suffer claustrophobia a second time. Please—save Wanting first!”

Juexing halted. Once again he stood at an impossible crossroads. He looked at Shang Xuan’s resolute eyes above the swaying firelight and seemed to hear, from deep underground, the voice of a woman enduring torment.

After freeing Little Bell, Orange ran to Shang Xuan and began cutting his ropes. At the exact moment Juexing turned to leap into the pit, Little Bell stepped into his path and blocked the way.

### Character Notes

**Lu Wanting**  
Fully aware of her nature as a residual soul, she still chooses to face her cousin. She is pushed into the sealed pit that recreates the claustrophobic death she once suffered.

**Shang Xuan**  
Bound and suspended over rising flames, he nevertheless begs Juexing to save Wanting first, placing her suffering above his own life.

**Little Bell**  
Brings Wanting to the scrapyard as demanded, is herself bound and prepared for burning, yet smiles at the prospect of dying beside Shang Xuan. At the climax she blocks Juexing from the pit.

**Luo Juexing**  
Arrives with Orange and Jiaye in the final seconds. After clearing the thugs he is forced to choose between the man hanging in the fire and the woman trapped below.

**Lu Shengnan & Tian Shubin**  
Openly confess the poisoning, the forged will, the assault, and the sealed-room murder. They intend to erase every living witness with fire and a staged double suicide.

**Qingmu Chengzi (Orange) & Huang Jiaye**  
Orange frees Little Bell and then Shang Xuan; Jiaye prevents Lu Shengnan’s escape. Together they turn the trap into a battlefield.

*A carousel dress worn by a woman without a shadow. A pit that recreates an old death. Flames climbing toward living feet. And a single plea that forces the cursebreaker to choose whose life to save first. The scrapyard has become the stage where every hidden crime is finally spoken aloud.*
