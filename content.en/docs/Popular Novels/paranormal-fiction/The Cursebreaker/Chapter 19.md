Jiaye’s message reached Juexing with only a single cryptic line: “Same method as before.”

Juexing was already in a taxi racing toward Song Xi’s mansion. He understood at once—tonight the girl would not appear at the midpoint station.

Jiaye received a report that Song Xi refused to leave his house. Forced to turn around, he headed straight for the mansion instead. Four prominent men had died under bizarre circumstances. Headquarters had issued a special operational order demanding the case be closed as quickly as possible. In the current climate the strange deaths had become the talk of every street corner; the entire city was watching.

Rumors multiplied with every media broadcast. The missing high-school girl—Yang Xueqing—had become the center of public attention. Some quietly cheered, hoping she would deliver divine punishment to the men who had done so much evil. Others prayed that the tragedies would simply stop.

Who was right? Who could weigh such scales?

Yu Xun had prepared thoroughly for this story. A successful exclusive could make her the lead reporter overnight. She came to the mansion alone. Other less cautious journalists had already hidden nearby, hoping to capture a death close-up.

Inside the house the grandfather clock continued its cold, steady ticking.

When Jiaye arrived he found his own colleagues barred from the gate. He pushed through the crowd, held up the special order, and shouted at the closed door:

“Open up! This is an order!”

Song Xi saw the paper waving in Jiaye’s hand. Even if the Emperor of Heaven himself had arrived, he would not have opened the door.

Jiaye conferred briefly with his men. Most returned to the cars to wait; only two officers remained behind him.

The red birthmark on Shang Xuan’s right arm suddenly flared. He jerked his head toward the “colleague” standing beside him. The slight upward curve of that man’s mouth sent a cold ripple of recognition through him. He closed his eyes, concentrated, and felt his heart clench.

*It’s her.*

The doorknob turned with a soft click. The moment Jiaye’s fingers touched the metal, a jolt of electricity flung him backward. The officer behind him walked straight inside. Shang Xuan’s hand shot out and clamped onto the man’s shoulder.

Jiaye had no idea what performance he was watching. He could only stare.

Juexing leapt from the taxi and sprinted forward, arriving at the exact critical second.

“Shang Xuan—watch out!”

A strand of long hair whipped across Shang Xuan’s face, the sharp ends slicing several thin cuts into his cheek. While his vision blurred, the possessed officer drove a fist into him. Shang Xuan staggered; only Jiaye’s steadying hand kept him upright.

The door slammed shut with a force that shattered Song Xi’s lingering thoughts. The air inside the mansion changed. Everyone present could feel it.

Juexing seized the handle and twisted with all his strength. The mechanism might as well have been severed from the outside world.

“What do we do? Orange is still in there!” Jiaye had already spotted her stirring on the sofa, still confused about what was happening.

Inside, Song Xi stared at the uniformed figure who had entered without permission.

“Who are you? Did I give you permission to come in?”

Tang Gufeng studied the officer. His expression remained controlled, but sweat beaded continuously on his forehead. The thought would not leave him: *Is this the missing high-school girl?*

A soft, unmistakably feminine laugh rose from the officer’s throat. Orange recognized that laugh at once—it was the same sound that had haunted her apartment night after night, driving her to sleep at Juexing’s place. In a strange way the ghost had even helped push her closer to him.

Gratitude flickered for half a second. Then reality returned. Orange stood up.

“Stop killing people! What do you actually want?”

Song Xi immediately crouched behind her, still failing to realize that the “officer” was the girl herself.

Outside, Yu Xun seized the moment. Hidden behind a banana tree with her camcorder, she filmed everything. Other journalists abandoned their cover. Suddenly the mansion was ringed with camera lights.

“Look how many people have come to watch,” the girl said lightly. She drew the officer’s service revolver, skillfully emptied the cylinder of its six rounds, then loaded a single bullet. With a casual flick of her left hand she spun the cylinder.

“Click.”

She raised the gun.

“Who goes first? The first person has a one-in-six chance.”

Silence fell over both the house and the street. Even Juexing and the others outside stopped moving, forced to watch.

Shang Xuan opened his mouth, then closed it again. He had come only to save Orange. Everything else was secondary. Without another word he turned and ran.

“What, all of you afraid to die? Then you first.”

She pointed the muzzle at Orange and slowly squeezed the trigger.

Twenty pairs of eyes outside held their breath. The chamber was empty.

“Ah, destiny made you the lucky one, Teacher Orange.” She smiled, loaded a second bullet, spun the cylinder again.

“One-in-three chance this time. Which of you two?”

Tang Gufeng started to rise. Song Xi hissed, “She’s insane—don’t be insane with her!”

“Fine. You, then. Live or die—leave it to heaven.”

She pulled the trigger without hesitation. Tang Gufeng squeezed his eyes shut. Thirty seconds passed. His heart was still beating. The second chamber had also been empty.

“Another miracle. But the remaining one-in-two chance might not be so kind.”

She had never intended for Song Xi to live. She reloaded with practiced speed and aimed past Orange at the man cowering behind her.

“Hey—block for me! I’ll give you as much money as you want—if—”

Song Xi never finished the sentence. In the blink of an eye he had spun, placing Orange squarely between himself and the gun.

“You are not only a beast in human skin,” the girl said softly, “you are a coward who will let others die in your place. Every day you remain alive only creates more victims. Today I will act for Heaven.”

The gunshot cracked the night.

Song Xi yanked Orange in front of him as the bullet left the barrel in a spray of sparks.

A wet impact. Orange felt hot blood spatter across her face. Then a pair of strong arms slammed her to the floor.

Shang Xuan stood frozen on the second-floor landing, staring at the bloodied figure. Someone had moved even faster than he had.

Orange’s left hand pressed against the wound, fingers already slick with bright red blood. She looked up at the man who had shielded her. For the first time a profound, almost aging sensation of the heart overtook her—the entire arc from meeting to knowing to loving compressed into a single heartbeat.

“Juexing…”

It was him. At the last possible instant he had seized a fire extinguisher from outside, smashed through the floor-to-ceiling window, and thrown his body between Orange and the bullet.

The girl still held the gun, watching the scene with cold indifference. In her mind there was no room for complicated feelings. Anyone who stood between her and revenge would meet the same end.

In the stunned silence Song Xi bolted out the front door. By the time she searched for him again, he was gone.

Shang Xuan recovered, leapt forward, and tackled the possessed officer. Two more shots rang out; both missed. The girl’s spirit tore free of the body, leaving only an empty shell pinned beneath Shang Xuan.

“Find Song Xi…”  

Those were the last words Juexing managed before the pain pulled him under. Shang Xuan took the envelope Juexing pressed into his hand, then grabbed Jiaye and ran.

Song Xi fled like a condemned man, zigzagging from main streets into alleys, from elevators into stairwells. He ran until his legs refused to carry him farther. Every time he glanced back, a long-haired shadow followed at a measured pace. He swallowed hard and forced himself onward.

Jiaye called an ambulance. Orange stayed with Juexing as he was rushed to the hospital. Shang Xuan kept the envelope clenched in his fist while he and Jiaye scoured the city for Song Xi. When the moon disappeared, the resentment would reach its peak. If they had not found him by then, he would die.

“Any luck?” Jiaye leaned against a stair railing, gasping for breath. Shang Xuan tore open the envelope. Dense, unfamiliar characters covered the page—writing only another cursebreaker would fully understand.

“This is bad,” Shang Xuan said. “We need to go somewhere.”

“Where?”

---

The sound of train wheels on steel rails reached Song Xi’s ears with perfect clarity. He opened his eyes and discovered he was lying bound hand and foot across the tracks at the midpoint station of train 5120.

Yellow joss paper drifted down through the darkness like snow, whispering of the two tragedies that had already occurred here. A short distance away a dark-skinned man crouched beside a fire basin. In the firelight his rough hands held a thick stack of spirit money. Crystal tears of repentance slid down his face as he murmured:

“Stop the killing… please stop…”

Soft footsteps approached.

A high-school girl in uniform, long hair over her shoulders, walked onto the platform with a bright, almost innocent smile. She stopped at the edge, looked down at the man tied to the rails, and simply watched him.

“Please—let me go! I’ll build you a tomb! I’ll come every Qingming Festival to pay respects—your father too—”  

The words tumbled out in a desperate, incoherent stream.

For a moment the pure smile on her face flickered with pure killing intent. Then she sat down on the edge of the platform, swung her legs like a carefree student, and continued to watch him.

### Character Notes

**Luo Juexing**  
Takes the bullet meant for Orange without hesitation. The act is both physical salvation and the clearest declaration of his feelings yet. Even as he loses consciousness he prioritizes stopping the final death, handing Shang Xuan the critical envelope.

**Yang Xueqing**  
Plays Russian roulette with cold theatricality, sparing Orange and Tang Gufeng by chance (or design) while ensuring Song Xi faces the final chamber. When her first attempt is thwarted she simply relocates the execution to the place of her strongest power—the tracks of train 5120.

**Song Xi**  
Reveals his true nature in the most cowardly way possible: using Orange as a human shield. His subsequent flight and binding on the rails complete the circle of judgment the ghost has prepared for him.

**Shang Xuan**  
Moves to protect Orange but is outpaced by Juexing. After the shooting he becomes the carrier of the envelope and the one who must interpret its contents—pushing him further into reluctant alliance.

**Qingmu Chengzi (Orange)**  
Survives the bullet only because Juexing intercepts it. The blood on her face and the name she calls in that moment mark an irreversible shift in their relationship.

**Huang Jiaye**  
Caught between official duty and the escalating supernatural stakes. His presence keeps the police response coordinated even as the case spirals beyond ordinary jurisdiction.
