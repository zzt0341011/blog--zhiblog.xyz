Xiaohai sat collapsed on the ground. In his left hand he gripped a stone-carved arm; in his right he held a brush, moving it back and forth with mechanical repetition. His clothes and trousers were soaked, flecked with stone dust.

His vacant eyes fixed on the Medusa statue a short distance away. Soft, fragmented words spilled from his lips without pause.

Juexing propped the battered bicycle against a post and helped Orange to the entrance of the altar grounds. She started forward; he shook his head.

“Stay outside. And do not look directly at the Medusa statue.”

He finished the warning and walked slowly toward Xiaohai.

At first the boy continued scrubbing the stone arm with complete focus. When Juexing came within ten paces, Xiaohai’s head snapped around. The eyes that met Juexing’s were twisted and malevolent. Juexing stopped where he stood.

“Get away! Get away!” Xiaohai clutched the stone arm tightly against his chest, certain Juexing had come to take it.

Juexing raised both hands in a calming gesture. The boy’s agitation lessened slightly, yet he still glared, refusing to let anyone come closer.

Orange hovered fearfully at the gateway, watching every movement, frustrated by her own helplessness.

Juexing crouched low and spoke gently.

“Xiaohai, give the arm back to her. Then come home with us. All right?”

Xiaohai shook his head violently and hugged the stone limb even tighter.

Juexing studied the scene. Red clay had been smeared across the Medusa statue in crude, almost comical shapes. He understood the origin of the curse. Rising, he lifted a nearby bucket, filled it at a faucet, and approached the statue. Just as he prepared to pour, Orange’s voice rang out:

“Behind you!”

Something heavy struck his left calf. His leg buckled; he pitched forward. The bucket slipped from his hands and water flooded the ground.

Xiaohai stood behind him, the stone arm raised, a cold smile on his face.

Orange limped forward a few steps. Juexing’s sharp command stopped her.

“Stay back. Go outside.”

Though she feared for him, she knew better than to become another burden. Reluctantly she retreated.

The moment Juexing pushed himself up, the sky bled into deep crimson. A howling wind tore across the altar. The Medusa statue began to writhe. Hundreds of stone serpents twisted and reared as though challenging him. When her eyes snapped open, an invisible force hurled Juexing backward.

Xiaohai’s lips curved. He lifted his face toward the living statue the way a lost child returns to its mother.

Juexing’s hand closed on a discarded brush. He swung hard. The wooden handle cracked against the back of Xiaohai’s head. The stone arm clattered to the ground in a puff of dust. Xiaohai’s eyes rolled white and he collapsed at the statue’s feet.

Juexing rose, pressed his left hand over the birthmark on his right arm. A thread of blue light spiraled outward, wrapping the animated Medusa and forcing her movement to slow.

While the mark held her, he snatched up the bucket again, refilled it, and began washing the red clay from the stone. Orange refused to remain idle. She found a second bucket and limped forward to help, scrubbing every crevice with careful urgency.

Little by little the statue quieted. The sky faded from blood-red to ordinary gray. Medusa settled like a great beast sinking back into sleep.

They cleaned every inch. In his heart Juexing asked the guardian to spare the foolish children who had wronged her.

Twenty minutes later a pristine Medusa stood before them once more. Juexing and Orange bowed deeply. He lifted the still-unconscious Xiaohai and carried him out of the altar grounds.

“Is it over?” Orange exhaled.

“Not yet.”

“What else is left?”

“If Medusa had forgiven them, she would have shed tears. She did not. That means we have not finished.”

“Then the final answer is with Du Xiaoxiao?”

They reached the gateway. Juexing’s phone rang—Jiaye.

“I’ll take you both back to the inn first, then find Jiaye. No matter what happens, do not leave the room.” From his pocket he drew the bracelet and showed her the red-string knot he had already tied into it.

“Be careful. I’ll wait for you.”

After leaving Orange and Xiaohai at the inn, Juexing mounted the bicycle and raced toward Du Xiaoxiao’s house. Wind rose in violent gusts, scattering scraps of paper and fruit peels across the road. At the gate of the house Jiaye and Yuxun crouched low, clinging to the nearest solid objects to keep from being blown away.

The family refused to let outsiders near Xiaoxiao. Jiaye and the reporter could only hold position at the gate.

“They’re superstitious too. How long can we keep this up?” Yuxun’s voice sounded thin against the gale.

“Juexing is coming. We don’t give up.” Jiaye’s knuckles were white on the railing.

Juexing jumped off the bicycle and forced himself forward against the mounting wind. On the horizon a mass of black cloud coiled and split open with a crack of thunder. From the rift a colossal stone hand emerged and reached slowly toward the roof of the house.

“What the hell is that?” Jiaye had never imagined a scene from a film could stand before him in waking life.

Yuxun adjusted the angle of her DV camera. She had no intention of missing the shot of a lifetime.

Juexing halted a short distance from the house and watched as Medusa’s iron hand closed around the entire structure and lifted it from its foundations. Inside, Xiaoxiao’s family clung together, eyes squeezed shut.

Jiaye released the railing and sprinted forward. He managed to drag two people free, but Xiaoxiao remained trapped in the giant grip.

The whole village began to shake. Families dropped to their knees in the streets, chanting for mercy, hoping pure devotion might turn the disaster aside.

At the inn the building shuddered violently. Xiaohai threw himself into Orange’s arms, tears streaming. She glanced at the red-knotted bracelet on her wrist, held the boy tightly, and whispered:

“It’s all right. Teacher won’t leave you this time. He’ll stay until everything is finished.”

“Mama!” Xiaoxiao’s cry was thin. The camera slipped from her fingers and rolled to Jiaye’s feet.

Xiaoxiao’s mother tore free of Jiaye’s hold and tried to run back. An unseen force flung her aside; she collapsed unconscious.

Lightning and thunder built toward annihilation. Juexing closed his eyes. A single image flashed through his mind—the camera lying near Jiaye’s shoes. He shouted across the storm:

“The camera! The film inside the camera!”

“What? I can’t hear you!” The wind swallowed the words. Xiaoxiao was already being lifted higher, face turning purple.

Juexing clenched his fists and drove himself through the gale. He reached the camera just as a bolt of lightning struck him and sent him rolling. Even as he tumbled he tore the film from the camera, flicked a lighter, and set the strip ablaze.

Inside the altar, the Medusa statue—unwatched for the moment—quietly shed tears.

The five children’s thoughtless desecration had planted the curse. They had smeared her body with clay and taken mocking photographs. Medusa was the village’s guardian, not an object for tourists to ridicule. The offense had been grave, yet mercy still lived in her. She would not destroy them all.

The giant hand opened. It withdrew into the sky and sealed the rift behind it. Xiaoxiao’s father gathered his wife and daughter and wept without words. When the nightmare finally ended, the village lay still once more.

Juexing watched the last of the black cloud dissolve into ordinary white. He looked at the ruined shell of the house and thought that as long as the family remained, walls could be rebuilt.

Jiaye stood, brushed dust from his clothes, and whispered toward the sky: “Thank you.”

Yuxun’s DV camera had conveniently run out of power. She had captured only fragments, yet enough for a story no one would soon forget.

Orange and Xiaohai stepped out of the inn as the first edge of sunset appeared. Villagers knelt in gratitude, foreheads touching the earth.

“Thank you… thank you…” Xiaoxiao’s father dropped to his knees before Juexing. Because of them his family—and the entire village—had been spared death’s judgment.

Juexing raised him to his feet.

“Live well from now on. Medusa will protect Dumei Village as she always has. If we trust her, she will trust us in return.”

By the time Du Xiaoxiao woke, Juexing and the others had already left the village. She walked to the window and looked toward the altar. A thin thread of red light circled once above the shrine and vanished.

Still in pajamas and slippers, she ran alone to the altar. Before the restored statue she dropped to her knees, hands clasped at her chest, head bowed, and prayed—repenting for a moment of childish mischief.

Medusa, like a gentle mother, reached out and rested a stone hand on the girl’s head.

“A child who knows she was wrong is still a good child.”

Xiaoxiao nodded. Fresh tears of relief slipped down her face. When she opened her eyes she thought she saw the faintest smile at the corner of the statue’s mouth. She promised herself that from this day forward she would guard Medusa the way Medusa had always guarded Dumei Village.

### Character Notes

**Luo Juexing**  
Reads the true nature of the curse from the red clay and the missing tears. Uses the birthmark’s blue light to suppress the awakened Medusa long enough to cleanse her, then risks everything to burn the offending film. His focus remains on ending the cycle rather than claiming credit.

**Qingmu Chengzi (Orange)**  
Despite her injured ankle she refuses to stand idle, helping scrub the statue clean. The red-knotted bracelet becomes her quiet anchor while she waits at the inn with Xiaohai.

**Xiaohai**  
Under the statue’s residual influence he becomes hostile and protective of the stone arm. Once the immediate possession breaks he is left exhausted and terrified, clinging to Orange for safety.

**Du Xiaoxiao**  
The final living link to the original desecration. Her camera holds the photographs that sealed the curse. After the storm she returns alone to the altar and receives Medusa’s forgiveness.

**Medusa (the Guardian)**  
Not a monster but the village’s ancient protector. The children’s mockery and photographs turned her power against them. Mercy remains possible; tears signal true forgiveness.

**Huang Jiaye & Meng Yuxun**  
Witness the climax at Xiaoxiao’s house. Jiaye acts on instinct to save lives; Yuxun records what she can before her camera dies. Both are left shaken by how real the “unbelievable” has become.
