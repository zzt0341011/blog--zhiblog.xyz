Almost overnight the Medusa-curse case became the talk of the entire city. Backed by the powerful images and sharp writing of star reporter Meng Yuxun, the obscure profession of “cursebreaker” spread through every street and alley.

Orange’s three students—Chen Cheng, Yu Xiaohai, and Du Xiaoxiao—returned to class. The ordeal had taught them a hard truth: malicious desecration could bring unimaginable punishment. Determined to share what had truly happened, they took turns guarding the Medusa statue in Dumei Village so that she might continue protecting the place in peace.

Because of her injured ankle Orange requested a week of leave and stayed home. Juexing visited her from time to time, though the “heat” around him forced him to disguise himself carefully each time he stepped outside, lest the paparazzi catch him.

Jiaye, for his part, received glowing praise for the two supernatural cases. His superiors took notice. Even if the stories sounded improbable, a major newspaper had reported them; that alone carried weight. Plans were drawn up to form a special unit dedicated to such “intangible” incidents, with Jiaye as its natural leader. So far the unit consisted of exactly one person—no one else was willing to transfer in.

Jiaye could only sigh as he tossed the newspaper aside. “Unreliable is still unreliable, no matter how strange it gets. Better accept it.” He shrugged and set off in search of a quiet café.

Exhausted, Juexing had slept straight through a day and a night. Hunger finally forced him out of bed. From the kitchen came the clatter of pots and pans and the rich scent of potato-and-beef stew. He pinched his thigh hard—not a dream. Who was in his kitchen?

He slipped out of bed, picked up a baseball bat leaning near the window, and crept to the kitchen doorway. A woman with her hair tied back and an apron around her waist was working at the induction cooker. The fragrance rose from the pot beside her.

“You’re awake?”

The moment she turned, the bat slipped from Juexing’s hand and clattered to the floor.

“How did you get here? Your ankle isn’t fully healed.”

It was Orange.

“It’s almost better. You hadn’t left the apartment for days, so I came to check on you.” She carried a plate of stir-fried green beans to the table.

Juexing swallowed, eyes fixed on the steaming dishes. Orange smiled. “There’s still fish-head soup. We can eat in a minute.”

She returned to the pot, stirred, tasted a spoonful, and nodded in satisfaction. As she reached to turn off the heat she burned her finger, jerked her hand back, and tugged at her own ear in reflex.

Juexing stepped forward. “I’ll do it.” Using a pair of tongs he lifted the hot pot safely onto the table. Orange ladled out the beef and potatoes.

She hung the apron on its hook and sat across from him. “What? Doesn’t it taste good?”

“Do you remember what you promised me?” Juexing asked quietly.

The smile vanished from her face, replaced by helpless resignation.

“You promised that once the case was over you would leave China and go back to Japan.”

Orange lowered her head and twisted her fingers together. Tears welled almost at once. Juexing knew the timing was cruel, yet he could not bear to see her pulled into these matters again.

“After I finish this meal I’ll leave,” she said. “I’ll disappear from your sight…”

Juexing picked up his bowl and chopsticks, placed a piece of beef in her bowl, and ate only rice himself. Orange stared at the rice. The tears would no longer stay. She set the bowl down.

“I know you think I’m a burden—someone who only causes you trouble. Do you think I want to be like this? I don’t even have any real friends here…”

She stood, snatched her bag from the sofa, yanked the door open, and ran out. Juexing sat motionless, staring at the colorful dishes she had cooked, and exhaled a long, heavy breath toward the open doorway.

“I’m sorry…”

Orange raced all the way to the apartment entrance, looked up at the window of his unit, and shouted:

“I hate you, you hypocrite!”

Meng Yuxun happened to be walking past at that exact moment. Orange, head down and moving fast, never noticed her. Yuxun took in the girl’s dejected figure and the silhouette of Juexing at the upstairs window, pieced together enough of the scene, and chose not to call out. She walked straight into the lobby, entered the elevator, and pressed the button for Juexing’s floor.

Just as the doors began to close, a wrinkled hand shot between them. Yuxun jumped. The doors reopened and an old woman pushed a cart inside.

“Give me a hand, will you?”

Yuxun shot her an irritated look, reluctantly pulled the cart the rest of the way in, then wiped her fingers with a tissue and dropped it onto the cart. The old woman eyed her sideways and shook her head.

“Not even close. Not even close.”

Yuxun ignored her, took out a compact, and checked her makeup.

The moment the elevator opened she hurried out, unwilling to share another second with a scavenger.

Inside the apartment Juexing stared at the meal Orange had prepared. A familiar ache of reluctance rose in him. Once, long ago, someone else had cooked an entire table of dishes and waited for him to wake. The good days had not lasted…

He forced the memory away, but it slipped through every crack like a ghost. He shoved the rice bowl aside; the crash of porcelain had barely faded when the doorbell rang.

Yuxun heard the noise and called, “Teacher Luo? It’s Yuxun.”

Juexing opened the door but kept his body blocking the entrance. He had no intention of letting her inside.

“Just eating? Smells wonderful—potato-and-beef stew, right?” She tried to step past him. He did not move.

“Is this how Teacher Luo treats guests?”

“What do you want? We’re not exactly acquaintances.”

Yuxun’s eyes flicked. She leaned in close. Cornered, Juexing was forced to take a step back; she slipped through the gap at once.

He closed the door and watched her walk into the dining area and survey the table.

“Wow. Who knew you were not only a skilled cursebreaker but also a decent cook? Marrying you would be a feast for both the eyes and the stomach.”

She reached for a pair of chopsticks. Juexing snatched them away.

“Say what you came to say and leave.”

“Fine, don’t eat then. I just saw someone running off in tears. I wonder who broke her heart.” She settled onto the sofa.

“Stop changing the subject.”

“It’s nothing major. My editor-in-chief wants to meet you. I’ve already arranged the time and place. Here’s the formal invitation.” She drew a red envelope from her bag and set it on the table.

“Tell your boss I’m not going.”

“Don’t be like that. I really think you should show up. Otherwise even going to the school might become difficult later.” She posed prettily, then walked to the door and turned back with a smile.

“Almost forgot—thank you for letting me witness that magnificent scene. It made me believe this world is full of ‘wonderful’ things.”

She left.

Sea wind carried the sharp scent of salt. Under the night sky Orange sat alone on the edge of the beach, watching the dark blue water shimmer with reflected light. It had been some time since she last came to the shore; returning to the sea calmed her more than she expected.

Shang Xuan approached slowly, a paper bag in one hand, and stopped behind her.

Five minutes passed before Orange sensed the gaze. She turned and found him standing there. Without a word she faced the water again. Shang Xuan sat down on her right.

“The night’s not bad. Your mood, though… less so.”

“How did you know I was here?”

Her stomach answered for her with a loud growl. Shang Xuan handed her a hamburger.

“Eat something first.”

Orange looked at the man beside her. She could not explain the situation, yet she knew he had always been kind to her.

“There’s milk tea too.” He simply passed her the entire bag and turned his own gaze to the sea.

She held the bag and the burger, watching him with an expression she herself could not name. The slight upward curve of his mouth carried a quiet, unreadable charm.

“Isn’t it contradictory?” he asked.

“Hm?”

“You and Luo Juexing.”

Orange stared at the sand, took a bite of the hamburger, and chose not to break the fragile peace of the moment.

“Do you know why I keep saving you?”

She shook her head while chewing.

“Because I like you.”

The words were completely unguarded.

“What?” She swallowed hard and turned to stare at him, afraid she had misheard.

Shang Xuan lowered his head for a moment, then stood, blew her a flying kiss, and smiled.

“Yes. You heard right. I have to go. Rest early. Good night.”

Before leaving he winked. The small electric jolt of that gesture left Orange stunned. She opened her mouth to call after him, but he only raised a hand without turning and gradually faded from sight.

She watched the direction he had disappeared, her feelings now even harder to name. Turning back to the moonlit water, she finished the rest of the hamburger and wondered: Was it really as contradictory as he said?

A short distance away Juexing stood watching the girl on the beach. He had come upon the scene by chance and understood at once that neither he nor Shang Xuan could offer her a good ending. As a friend, he felt obligated to warn her.

“Teacher Luo, what are you peeking at?”

Little Bell appeared from nowhere, leisurely sucking on a lollipop.

Orange heard the voice behind her. When she turned she saw Little Bell linking arms with Juexing and strolling toward the street. She rose and watched them go, an unexpected ache blooming in her chest.

Why, she asked herself, did it hurt at all?

### Character Notes

**Luo Juexing**  
Keeps his distance for Orange’s safety, even when it wounds them both. The meal she cooks awakens old, painful memories he tries—and fails—to suppress. He remains determined to push her out of the cursed world before it consumes her.

**Qingmu Chengzi (Orange)**  
Her ankle is healing, but the emotional injury cuts deeper. The promise to leave China collides with her growing attachment. Shang Xuan’s confession and the sight of Little Bell with Juexing leave her more confused than ever.

**Shang Xuan**  
Continues to appear at pivotal emotional moments. His open confession—“Because I like you”—is delivered with the same casual flying kiss that has become his signature. Whether the feelings are genuine or part of a larger design remains unclear.

**Meng Yuxun**  
Uses the sensational report to elevate both her own career and the public profile of cursebreakers. She delivers her editor’s invitation with characteristic boldness and a pointed reminder that refusing may carry consequences.

**Huang Jiaye**  
Reaps professional rewards from the two cases, yet finds himself alone in the newly proposed special unit. His pragmatic resignation—“unreliable is still unreliable”—captures his position between the ordinary world and the paranormal.

**Little Bell (Xiaolingdang)**  
A new and enigmatic presence. She appears without warning, addresses Juexing with familiar ease, and leads him away in full view of Orange. Her identity and intentions are not yet revealed.
