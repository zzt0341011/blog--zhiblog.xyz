Every beautiful thing must pass through repeated trials, or it cannot last. She had been an ordinary girl—gentle inside and out, the kind anyone would like at first glance. Because she was introverted, the bullying stayed locked inside her; she never told anyone. That silence became the bullies’ license to go further. In plain terms they were jealous of her looks. Behind her back they called her a “fox spirit.” In many corners of the school they beat her. She still said nothing. She had no friends at all.

A few people tried to help. None of them escaped the “punishment” that followed. She was isolated completely. She knew she was unlucky, so she asked for nothing. She even knelt and begged them to stop. What she received in return was mockery after mockery, and bucket after bucket of filthy water. Finally, one night during the summer holiday when the teachers had all left, they trapped her inside a teacher’s empty dormitory.

She was terrified, cold, and hungry. They played ghost tricks to frighten her further. A mirror hanging on the wall fell and shattered. Something even more terrible happened then—someone made a suggestion that turned the night into a nightmare the school has never been able to erase.

When the new term began, the teacher who found her was so horrified by her appearance that he wet himself and suffered a complete mental collapse. He is still in the hospital.

She became the past, a private nightmare shared by many at the school. But the people who had wielded the knives did not stop…

Juexing rubbed his dry eyes. When he looked up someone was standing in the bedroom doorway. He assumed it was Zilan. He set the files aside, gently lifted Orange’s head onto a cushion, then walked to the doorway. Zilan sat on a chair, gazing at the pale light of approaching dawn beyond the window.

“Why did you stop me?” she demanded.

“You’re not Zilan. You’re Qiaohe.”

“Who I am doesn’t matter. They liked bullying people—so they have to pay the price.”

Juexing took a few steps forward. She stopped him at once.

“Don’t come closer… I don’t know how wide the definition of ‘good person’ is in this world.”

“Orange and I both want to help you. We mean no harm.”

“Is that so? If you really want to help, then let me leave. You can save her for a moment, but you can’t save her for a lifetime.”

Her tone was hard, offering no compromise. Juexing shifted sideways, trying to approach from another angle. Without warning she spun around, a pair of scissors in her hand, the point pressed against the pulse of her left wrist.

“Get out, or I’ll die right here in front of you.”

Juexing stepped back through the doorway.

“Rest. Don’t hurt Zilan.” He had no choice but to close the door. Alone, Zilan sat on the edge of the bed and noticed the diary Orange had left on the nightstand. She reached for it and began to read.

Orange slept deeply. The recent days of running back and forth had left her exhausted and thin. Juexing draped his own coat over her, then sat on the single armchair opposite.

Through the night he wrote her a note. At first light he left without even taking the coat, walking straight out of her apartment. He had thought the whole day through. There was still no perfect plan—only the necessity of taking the gamble.

All night he had reread and dissected the tragedy from two years earlier. At last he found the opening: two mirrors were involved from beginning to end.

One was the cracked mirror discovered in Zilan’s dormitory—the very weapon that had carved Qiaohe’s face and driven her to suicide. The other hung in the teacher’s dormitory where Orange now stayed; a lingering resentment still clung to that room because it was the place where Qiaohe’s body had been found.

Qiaohe’s spirit had been imprisoned inside the fractured glass. Forced every day to watch Zilan being bullied in the same way, her resentment grew heavier until she finally broke free of the mirror and possessed the living girl. It was easy to imagine the sensation Zilan must have felt when she stood behind Wang Mei.

Juexing quickened his pace. He intended to finish the exchange ritual during the daylight hours while Zilan was still at Orange’s apartment—transferring the rage-filled Qiaohe from the broken mirror into the intact one. He hoped that inside a whole, undamaged reflection she might see a complete and kind version of herself, and in that recognition lay down the will to kill.

When Orange woke, sunlight already filled the living room. She blinked; Juexing was gone. As she sat up, the note he had left slipped to the floor. She picked it up, read it carefully, then hurried to the bedroom. Zilan was still asleep on the bed.

Orange stood in the doorway and smiled with quiet relief. How long had it been since the girl had slept past sunrise, curled up like a lazy cat unwilling to rise?

Sensing someone watching, Zilan lifted her head. Orange smiled.

“Sleep a little longer. I’ll make you breakfast.”

Zilan thought she must still be dreaming. She rubbed her eyes, yet Orange’s smile remained solid and real.

“Mm.” She nodded and lay back down. Beside the pillow lay Orange’s diary. She remembered reading it for a long time the night before before finally falling asleep. Every line had moved her. For the first time she began to feel that the world might not be entirely hopeless.

Still shaken by the previous night and by the news of Wang Mei’s death that had already spread, Shuangfei and Xiao Qing quickly met to discuss what to do. Every movement they made was under Jiaye’s surveillance. Juexing had told him to stay glued to them; under no circumstances were they to be allowed any “dangerous” ideas.

Jiaye did not fully understand what “dangerous” meant in this context, but professional instinct warned him that the inner world of these young people was difficult to read. For safety he kept a continuous twenty-four-hour watch.

A sharp knock on the car window. Yu Xun had brought breakfast. Jiaye unlocked the doors; she slid in and handed him a sandwich.

“Thanks, dear.” He took a bite and kept the binoculars trained on the figures in the distance.

Yu Xun pinched him hard enough to make him yelp.

“I’ve told you many times—don’t be so familiar. I haven’t even agreed to anything yet.”

“Just practicing. Don’t be mad.” He rubbed his thigh and continued eating.

“Have you been staking them out all night?”

He nodded. “No choice. Last time they only escaped the ghost’s claws by luck. Their safety is critical, so I have to do it this way.”

“You’re the boss. Why do you have to do everything yourself?”

“That’s just my fate. Besides, if a superior can’t take the lead, how can he set an example for his team? More importantly, I’m not doing this only for the job—I’m doing it for my brother.” He swallowed the rest of the sandwich in one go, thumped his chest, and asked, “Any soy milk?”

“About to choke to death?” She passed him the cup.

“I can’t afford to choke. It would be too hard on you.” His shameless grin was sometimes genuinely funny. Yu Xun began to detect a different flavor in him.

In the distance the two girls talked calmly at first, then the conversation turned into an argument. Xiao Qing seemed to disagree with Shuangfei; Chen Dong appeared to side with Shuangfei. Eventually Xiao Qing yielded. After another short exchange Chen Dong nodded and left. Jiaye immediately radioed the nearby team to begin tailing him and to report every ten minutes.

Shuangfei and Xiao Qing remained seated, silent now—probably waiting for Chen Dong to return.

“They look like they’re planning something,” Yu Xun said.

“Very possible. Don’t worry. They won’t slip through the net I’ve set.”

Juexing went straight to Zilan’s room at the school, took down the mirror from the wall, and carried it to Orange’s teacher dormitory. The timing was perfect.

He hung the cracked mirror opposite the intact one already on the wall, creating a direct confrontation. He drew the curtains so the room was as dark as possible, then placed a wooden box between the two mirrors. In the center of the box he set a string of symbols he had written. At each of the four corners he stood a white candle of uneven height. Red threads bound the candles; the two diagonal threads were fixed to the centers of the opposing mirrors, while the remaining two he held in his own hands.

He stood between the mirrors, twenty centimeters from the box, closed his eyes, and silently recited:

*Soul exchange—begin now…*

He gave a light flick to the threads in his hands. The white candles ignited one after another, from lowest to highest. The moment all four flames were lit, a figure slowly appeared inside the cracked mirror on the left. It was Lin Qiaohe.

“I didn’t expect you to be this persistent,” she said with a cold smile.

Juexing kept his eyes closed, continuing the chant under his breath. Qiaohe reached out to strike him; the instant she crossed the first barrier, a voice from the opposite mirror forced her back.

“You—” She sounded angry.

Juexing stopped the chant and opened his eyes to look at the left-hand mirror.

“Don’t think being a cursebreaker makes you special. If you have the ability, face me one-on-one!”

“You were originally a kind-hearted girl, top of your class. Because you were introverted you were bullied again and again. Because you never spoke up, they grew bolder—pouring filthy water on you, beating you, even drawing on your face with marker pens.” As he spoke, something tightened in his chest. In that moment he saw clearly how terrifying the human heart could be.

“Yes. And then what? That’s all in the past. I’ve been dead a long time…” Qiaohe still smiled coldly, as though she had already tasted every bitterness the living world could offer.

“I am deeply sorry for what happened to you. Their crimes are grave; the law will punish them. You cannot replace the law of the living with your own.”

“So you’re saying what I did was wrong?”

“Your mottled face gradually eroded the goodness in your heart. Evil thoughts slowly took possession of your soul. You should have crossed the cycle of rebirth and returned to the world as a new person. Instead, one single wrong thought left you wandering here, unable to leave.”

The words he spoke were formal, almost like those of a Taoist priest or a ritual master—each syllable deliberate and heavy.

### Character Notes

**Lin Qiaohe**  
The full backstory is finally spoken aloud: isolation, repeated humiliation, the final night of terror in the empty teacher’s dorm, the broken mirror, and the irreversible choice that followed. Her spirit, long trapped in the cracked glass, has grown powerful enough to possess the living and to argue with a cursebreaker face to face.

**Luo Juexing**  
Chooses ritual over confrontation. By placing the two mirrors in opposition—the weapon and the place of death—he attempts to give the resentful spirit a chance to see an unbroken reflection of herself and, through that vision, release the killing intent. The formal language of the rite shows him stepping fully into the role fate assigned him.

**Mo Zilan**  
Even while possessed, the living girl’s consciousness is not entirely extinguished. Reading Orange’s diary softens something inside her; the peaceful sleep that follows is the first genuine rest she has known in a long time.

**Qingmu Chengzi (Orange)**  
Her diary becomes an unexpected instrument of healing. The simple morning kindness she shows Zilan continues to counteract the despair that has fed the curse.

**Huang Jiaye & Yu Xun**  
Maintain the external watch on the remaining two bullies. Their banter softens the tension of the stakeout while the net around Shuangfei and Xiao Qing tightens.

**The Two Mirrors**  
One fractured and stained with old violence; one whole and still carrying the memory of a body discovered. The ritual places them in dialogue, forcing the past to confront the possibility of release.
