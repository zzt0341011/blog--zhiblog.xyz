“Little Bell? What are you doing here?” Juexing asked.

“This is for you. I hope you’ll take it seriously after you see it.” Little Bell’s expression was dark as she held out an envelope. Orange closed the door behind her and said, “Since you’re already here, stay for dinner.”

“Yeah, one more person is just one more pair of chopsticks,” Xiaoqing added with a smile.

“Is it all right?” Little Bell asked Juexing politely.

“Of course. Eat with us. Let me see what this is.” Juexing reached to open the envelope, but Little Bell stopped him.

“Look at it in the bedroom. I’d like you to keep this private.”

Xiaoqing and Zilan tactfully moved to the living room—one opening a laptop, the other turning on a drama. Orange was already busy in the kitchen. Only the two of them remained.

Once the bedroom door was shut, Juexing opened the envelope. Inside was another photograph—unimaginative in format, yet the content made his expression change completely.

“I knew you’d look like that. When I first saw it I felt even worse than you do now.” Little Bell’s eyes had already reddened.

“Where did this come from?” Juexing could tell the photo itself was not edited. The problem lay with the woman in it.

“Don’t ask me about the source. I only want you to help me—and help Shang Xuan.”

“You care about him a lot…”

“Of course. I like him. Is that not allowed?” Little Bell sat on the edge of the bed, watching Juexing still holding the photograph.

Orange carried a dish to the dining table and noticed the bedroom door was closed. They must be discussing something. Every time she met Little Bell she felt the girl was unfathomable—almost like a messenger whose appearance always signaled new trouble.

“Teacher?” Zilan called to the dazed Orange.

“Oh—almost ready. Just a minute.” Orange set the plate down and returned to the kitchen.

Juexing put the photo away, thought for a moment, then looked at Little Bell.

“Well? Are you going to help or not?” She seemed short on patience.

“If this is a curse, I’ll handle it whether you ask or not. If it isn’t… then I’m sorry.” He could not give her the answer she wanted yet.

“Fine. I’ll figure it out myself.” She stood, put on a casual face, opened the door, and joined the girls at the table, chatting as if nothing had happened.

Juexing remained by the bedroom doorway, watching her. For a moment he could not decide. He did not want the newly settled life to be thrown into turmoil again.

Orange stood at the kitchen doorway watching him. From the look between his brows she could tell another problem had found him. Yet she told herself that from now on she would face every challenge at his side.

“Is dinner ready?” Juexing had somehow appeared beside her. Orange snapped back to the present, leaned back to check the pot, and said, “Five more minutes.”

“You’ve worked hard.”

“Not at all. Being able to cook for you makes me happy.”

“Being able to eat what you cook makes me even happier.”

“Smooth talker. All you men are the same.”

“Whether you like it or not is no longer up to you.” He reached out to tickle her. Orange blocked with both hands. Their playful struggle was completely different from the easy laughter of the three girls in the living room.

That Sunday it was Wan Ting’s turn to work the early shift. She planned to return Shang Xuan’s jacket before heading to the supermarket, but no one answered the doorbell no matter how long she rang, and her calls went unanswered. Disappointed, she hung the jacket back up at home and left for work.

The supermarket was the largest in the suburbs—five floors. She worked as a cashier on the second level.

Shang Xuan woke a little past noon. Checking his phone he found five missed calls from Wan Ting. He had set the phone to vibrate and slept too deeply to notice.

He called back; she did not answer. He guessed she was already at the supermarket.

After a quick wash he changed clothes and reached for his jacket—only to discover yesterday’s was missing. He pulled a grey one from the wardrobe instead, thinking it must still be at her place.

He decided to stop by the supermarket first, buy a few things for lunch, and, of course, see her.

“Fifty yuan total. Thank you.” Wan Ting operated the register with practiced ease and handed over the receipt.

The customer did not take it. She looked up—and saw him.

“It’s you…” she said with a smile.

“How much was it again?” Shang Xuan took the receipt and glanced at it.

“I called you earlier. You know that, right?”

“Yes. I’ll come by for the jacket when I have time. Thanks.” He paid, smiled, and walked away.

Wan Ting said a quick word to a colleague, then hurried after him.

“What is it?” he asked.

“Your change.” She held out a crisp ten-yuan note.

“What time do you finish?”

“Very late tonight…” she answered, looking down.

“Call me when you’re done.” He took the note with a smile, picked up his bag, and left. Wan Ting stood watching his retreating figure for a long moment. Perhaps her life was about to change because of him.

“Wan Ting! What are you doing standing around?” a heavyset woman called.

“Nothing. I’m going back now.” She hurried to her station. The woman muttered a few complaints and strode into the office.

In the free time that followed, Shang Xuan began looking into Wan Ting’s background—unaware that every move he made was already being watched.

Because Orange had to work the next day, the group returned from the seaside park, talked a little longer, and then Orange left with Zilan and the others on the express train back to the city. Once they were gone, Juexing took the photograph to Jiaye.

During this period Jiaye and Yu Xun had grown noticeably closer. Days were busy; evenings they spent talking. Sometimes luck in love arrived whether one wanted it or not—Jiaye had reported for work that morning with a pair of dark circles and kept dozing at his desk.

“Boss, a Mr. Luo is here to see you,” a colleague said, knocking.

“Who’s disturbing my meditation this early?” Jiaye squinted at the person who entered.

“It’s almost evening. Still not awake, Old Huang?” Juexing teased.

The familiar voice snapped Jiaye fully alert. He invited Juexing to sit.

“What wind blew you here?”

“I want to borrow five million from you—or from your girlfriend.”

“Borrow money? You’re joking…” The number alone made Jiaye’s eyes go wide.

“Maybe you don’t have it, but Yu Xun’s net worth is probably more than five million.” Juexing spoke with complete seriousness.

“Haha… you’ve never been short of money. This has to be a joke.” Jiaye’s smile looked forced.

“Do I look like I’m joking?” Juexing fixed him with a direct stare until Jiaye shivered and sank back into his chair.

“Will you lend it or not?”

“Of course I’ll lend it. Can’t afford to cut off a source of wealth.” Jiaye reached for the phone to call Yu Xun. Watching him tremble, Juexing pressed his hand down.

“I know Yu Xun has made a lot from covering paranormal cases. I was just worried you might end up as a live-in son-in-law—you’re too proud for that.”

“You scared me. But that is something worth thinking about. All right—what do you actually need?”

“I need you to look into someone.”

“Background checks are my specialty. What do you have?”

Juexing handed over the photograph. “I only know her name is Wan Ting. The rest is up to you.”

“Wan Ting… why does that name sound so familiar…” Jiaye sifted through his memory, then slapped his forehead. “Right—daughter of the rich merchant Lu Zhengyao. Lu Wanting.”

“Lu Zhengyao died of an incurable illness three years ago, didn’t he?” Juexing had seen the city-wide news coverage.

“Yes. But his daughter supposedly disappeared over an inheritance dispute…” Jiaye was not entirely certain.

“Keep this quiet. Contact me directly only.” Juexing took the photo back and left.

Jiaye thought: this might be another strange case. Better to tell Yu Xun first.

When Yu Xun received the call she was organizing files. The moment Jiaye mentioned Lu Zhengyao and his daughter Lu Wanting, she froze.

“Hello? Hello?” Jiaye’s voice came through the line.

“I’m fine. Why are you suddenly talking about this?”

“It’s Juexing. He showed me a photo of Lu Wanting. Something feels off, so I’m telling you now—if it turns into a big story you’ll have the first lead.” Jiaye finished in one breath and drained a glass of water.

“I love you for this. I’ll check the archives. See you tonight.” She hung up quickly.

Although she had not covered the original reports on the merchant’s death, she clearly remembered that one week after Lu Zhengyao died of late-stage liver cancer, his daughter was found dead from asphyxiation inside an office in her father’s building. Police and forensics confirmed the cause, but no killer was ever found. The case was eventually sealed as one of the city’s top unsolved mysteries.

“So what he saw can’t be the real Lu Wanting… then who is it?” Yu Xun murmured while flipping through the files.

When she reached the entry on Lu Wanting’s death she froze in shock. The densely written A4 page was now completely blank—as if it had been replaced with a fresh sheet. She flipped through several more pages. Every report concerning Lu Wanting had vanished; everything else remained intact.

“How is this possible?”  

She closed the folder at once. A single thought circled in her mind:

This is definitely a curse.

### Character Notes

**Luo Juexing**  
Presented with a new photograph that immediately raises alarm. He remains cautious about involving himself unless a curse is confirmed, yet the image is disturbing enough that he seeks Jiaye’s help.

**Qingmu Chengzi (Orange)**  
Senses that Little Bell’s arrival signals fresh trouble. Continues to choose standing beside Juexing no matter what comes next.

**Little Bell**  
Openly admits her feelings for Shang Xuan and seeks Juexing’s help on his behalf. Acts as a reluctant messenger carrying dangerous information.

**Shang Xuan**  
Begins investigating Wan Ting while remaining unaware that he himself is under observation. Their interactions hint at a deepening personal connection.

**Lu Wanting (Wan Ting)**  
Appears in the present as a supermarket cashier, yet historical records of her death have begun to disappear. Her true identity and current state are now in question.

**Huang Jiaye**  
Quickly identifies the woman in the photo and agrees to dig deeper under strict secrecy.

**Yu Xun**  
Discovers that every official record of Lu Wanting’s death has been erased from the archives—an unmistakable signature of residual force at work.
