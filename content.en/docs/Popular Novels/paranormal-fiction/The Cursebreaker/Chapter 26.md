The weekend finally arrived. Orange turned the entire dormitory upside down searching for the photographs, but they were nowhere to be found. She could wait no longer. She put on a coat and, under cover of night, boarded the last bus bound for the suburbs.

The late bus carried only a handful of passengers, most of them in pairs. When Orange had first seen those photographs her heart had leapt—he was awake. Almost at once the joy collapsed. The second person in the pictures was someone she had never expected. The woman who had once promised to call the moment he woke had, in Orange’s absence, done something so cold-blooded she could scarcely believe it.

Every time the thought returned, her chest tightened. She closed her eyes and tried not to imagine what she might find. She was terrified her worst guess would prove true.

By the time the bus reached its final stop only the driver and Orange remained.

“We’re almost there, miss,” the driver said, glancing at her in the rear-view mirror.

She nodded, gathered her things, and when the bus halted she thanked him before stepping down.

It had been nearly a month since she last returned to the suburbs. Only a month—yet everything felt changed.

She did not go home. She went first to Juexing’s building, rehearsing every possible scenario and the words she might need. In the lobby she met Yu Xun coming out.

The moment Yu Xun saw her, her footsteps stopped. Orange’s appearance was completely unexpected.

Orange still managed a small smile. Yu Xun raised a hand in return. The distance between them narrowed, yet they kept an arm’s length apart.

“I want to know what this is really about,” Orange said.

“What is about what?” Yu Xun pretended not to understand.

“You promised you would tell me the moment Juexing woke up. You broke that promise.”

“I did tell him about you. He has no memory of you at all. I asked a doctor; he could only speculate that the bullet damaged certain nerves and caused—”

Orange cut her off.

“I don’t want to hear medical explanations. Tell me what you did to him.”

“It’s simple. I’m his girlfriend now.” Yu Xun smiled. She had no idea how terrifying that smile looked to Orange.

Orange stepped back. Her eyes filled with pure, wounded disbelief.

Yu Xun’s thoughts raced. After a moment she said, “I should have told you myself. That was wrong of me. But Juexing and I are together now, and that isn’t necessarily a bad thing. You want him to be happy too, don’t you?”

Orange said nothing. Her expression was crushed. Tears gathered at the rims of her reddened eyes. She had never imagined that someone she trusted—someone she had even considered a friend—would so openly cut in and take what she loved.

“Orange…” Yu Xun began again, but Orange stopped her.

“Enough. You’re right. As long as you’re sincere with him, I’m willing to step aside.”

Yu Xun extended her right hand, intending a handshake. Orange had no heart for it. To take that hand would feel like total surrender.

*This isn’t what Juexing wants.*  

She told herself that. She needed to hear the truth from his own mouth. Even if the answer was the same, at least she could walk away cleanly.

“There’s no point calling,” Yu Xun said the instant Orange reached for her phone. “He changed his number.”

Orange’s hand froze in mid-air. She had not expected the woman in front of her to go this far. Behind the soft exterior lay a mind of deep calculation. She even began to wonder how Juexing had ever befriended someone like this—and what Yu Xun’s real purpose was. Deep down she knew Yu Xun did not love him. What she loved was probably only his identity: the cursebreaker who could speak with gods and ghosts.

Orange put the phone away, glanced up at the dark windows of Juexing’s apartment, then turned and walked toward her own home. In that moment she felt like a child with nowhere to go, longing for any shelter that might carry her through the cold night until morning.

She forced back the tears and dragged her heavy heart and heavier feet forward. Without realizing it she arrived at the riverside park. Though the hour was late, a few people still strolled and talked. She had often imagined bringing her family and the person she loved here one day—cracking sunflower seeds, talking about nothing and everything. That picture now seemed almost impossible.

She sat on the stone bench by the railing. The moment she settled, rain began to fall without warning—first scattered drops, then a steady, increasing downpour. The remaining people scattered in haste. Only Orange remained on the bench in the rain. At such an hour no one should appear in the park, and certainly no one should stand behind her holding an umbrella.

Perhaps someone would.

The figure who appeared in moments of crisis—half-blond hair, the strange right eye, the habitual flying kiss.

Orange smiled. The wetness on her face could no longer be distinguished as rain or tears. Her quiet sobs drew footsteps. The person who approached was someone she had not dared to hope for—the kind of probability that lottery players dream of when they buy a single ticket and win five million.

An umbrella opened above her head. She continued to cry with her head lowered until he crouched in front of her. Through tear-blurred eyes she looked up.

“Juexing…”

She could not believe what she saw.

“Yes. It’s me. Where have you been all this time?”

It was his voice—the voice she had longed for day and night. There was no mistake.

Orange threw herself into his arms. The heavy rain beat against the umbrella while one of them held it and the other wept. In the rainy night the scene carried an extra edge of desolation.

“Drink some hot milk first.”

Juexing watched as Orange dried her hair with a towel.

She changed clothes in the bedroom, then jumped onto the sofa, cupped the steaming milk, and looked at him.

“What is it?” he asked.

“Thank you.”

“I should be the one thanking you.”

“Thank me for what? I haven’t even decided to forgive you yet.” She took a sip; a thick white mustache of milk clung playfully to the corner of her mouth.

“Thank you for the more than ten days of meticulous care.” He took out the small notebook and held it out to her.

Orange accepted it, glanced at the pages, then pouted and scooted closer.

“You read my private diary. What should I do about that?”

“In front of me you don’t have any privacy left.” He smiled.

“Oh, is that so? Well, it’s true—when I was bathing you, you never tried to hide anything either.” She did not blush in the slightest.

“Then that means nothing was left covered…” He slapped his thigh in mock horror.

“Can’t be helped. You’re the one who was as stiff as a wooden post.” She laughed.

Their eyes met. An unspoken current moved between them. Orange rested her head against his chest and took his hand.

“I don’t want to keep running away. Will you be with me?”

Juexing gently circled his arms around her.

“Let me take care of you properly from now on.”

“You said it. I didn’t force you.”

“Mm. I don’t know what the future will look like, but holding on to the present is the most practical thing I can do. I won’t fail the person who can change my fate.”

Orange smiled with pure happiness and closed her eyes. Juexing sat on the sofa with her lying in his arms. They remained like that, motionless, until daylight returned.

---

When Juexing woke he was in bed. Orange was already in the kitchen making breakfast. His phone rang again. He rubbed his eyes, pulled it out, and saw Yu Xun’s number. The call log showed more than twenty missed calls from her.

“Why didn’t you answer?” Orange asked from the kitchen.

He pressed accept. Yu Xun’s voice came through at once, sharp with worry.

“You didn’t come home all night. It was pouring. I was worried about you.”

“Mm. Let’s have lunch together. I have something to talk about.” He hung up, then dialed Jiaye.

“That was Yuxun, wasn’t it?” Orange asked again.

“Yes. Don’t cook lunch. We’ll eat out. My treat—something proper.”

He walked to the table and embraced her from behind.

“Changing tactics already? Attacking through my stomach?”

“Is that so bad? We’ve never properly eaten out together. I’m making an exception and treating you first.”

He bit into the piece of bread she held out.

“I’m warning you—I still haven’t completely forgiven you. Be careful.”

She stuffed the entire remaining piece into his mouth. Juexing immediately pulled an exaggerated gorilla face and circled the table, drawing bright laughter from her.

She had never felt this happy. She was beginning to believe a new life was about to begin.

“I’ve been temporarily transferred to the vocational high school as a counselor,” she said.

The moment the words “vocational high school” left her lips, Juexing’s expression darkened slightly.

“Are you adjusting to life in the city? I’ve heard there’s a rumor about that school.”

“I’ve been there a month already. I’ve definitely noticed some problems.”

“Shang Xuan has been there too, hasn’t he?”

Orange looked up at him and smiled.

“Don’t worry. I’ve already told Shang Xuan that we will only ever be friends. The person I like is you. He knows.”

For a reason she could not see, the words made Juexing think of Li Mei. Years ago Li Mei had said almost exactly the same thing: *Both of you are important to me, but Shang Xuan can only be a friend, because the one I love is Juexing…*

“Are you all right?” Orange asked, noticing how much darker his face had become.

“I’m fine. No matter what happens, I won’t let go of your hand. That’s the promise I’m giving you.”

He closed his fingers around hers, and the smile returned to his face.

### Character Notes

**Qingmu Chengzi (Orange)**  
Chooses confrontation over silent suffering. Her willingness to step aside if Yu Xun is sincere shows both dignity and deep hurt; her decision to hear the truth directly from Juexing shows quiet strength. The rainy reunion and the simple request—“Will you be with me?”—mark the emotional climax of the chapter.

**Luo Juexing**  
The amnesia is cracking. The diary, the rain, and Orange’s presence pull him back toward the person who waited through his unconsciousness. His final promise—to never let go of her hand—echoes and deliberately contrasts the tragedy with Li Mei.

**Yu Xun**  
Maintains her position with calculated calm when face-to-face with Orange, yet the flood of missed calls the next morning reveals underlying panic. The lunch meeting Juexing schedules signals that her carefully constructed arrangement is about to be tested.

**Shang Xuan**  
Appears only as the first umbrella in the rain—the familiar rescuer—before Juexing himself arrives. The near-miss underscores how consistently he has been present at Orange’s lowest moments, even as she has chosen another.

**The Vocational High School**  
Mentioned only briefly, yet the shift in Juexing’s expression when he hears the name makes clear that the residual resentment surrounding Lin Qiaohe and Mo Zilan has already entered his awareness.
