The car tore through empty streets. Five checkpoints blocked the road into the city center; the last one even had tanks. To Huang Jiaye none of that mattered. With his practiced driving, a policeman’s instincts, and every high-speed chase scene he had ever admired, he threaded the black Mercedes through the “army” without being stopped.

Inside the car the group finalized the plan: drop Juexing and Orange at the exhibition hall, take Meixue to the evacuation zone, then find a fuel tanker.

Jiaye spun the wheel and smashed through the first barrier. Guards fired warning shots into the air; the runaway Mercedes ignored them.

“Report—black Mercedes has breached the line. Request Level-One alert.”

Headquarters locked onto the vehicle by satellite within seconds.

“We’re done,” Jiaye said. “The car’s being tracked. We’ll be lucky to reach the third checkpoint.”

“Stick to the plan. Take care of Meixue.” Juexing and Orange climbed out. Before they left, Jiaye pressed a pistol into Juexing’s hand.

“Only ten rounds, but better than nothing. Be careful.”

Juexing took it, jaw tight. “You too. We’ll meet at the exhibition hall.” He seized Orange’s hand and the two of them vanished into a side alley. Jiaye swung the car around and headed for the nearest gas station.

The fourth checkpoint was far stronger—an iron wall in every sense. Yet the men guarding it seemed unaware that the city center they “protected” was already the nest of a gigantic demonic plant. Did none of them smell the death and blood in the air?

Juexing and Orange crouched in the shadows while he studied the layout.

“I’ll draw them off. You get through the service door and open it from the inside.” He pointed to a narrow passage guarded by only two men, then snapped a small stick in his hand. He fired once at a distant warning light. The sharp crack pulled every eye. He shoved the pistol into Orange’s hands.

“If you’re in danger, you know what to do. Use both hands if you have to. Hitting the target doesn’t matter—just shoot.”

Orange clutched the gun, reluctant to let him go. Juexing ruffled her hair and smiled.

“See you on the other side.”

He slipped into the open. Several guards spotted the moving figure at once and shouted warnings. The two nearest the passage raised their submachine guns and gave chase. Orange sprinted to the locked door, raised the pistol, and fired. Sparks flew; the lock shattered. She shoved the door open and raced down the corridor to the second door—the rendezvous point—where two more guards stood.

It was the first time in her life she had held a firearm. Sweat dripped from her palms; her heartbeat thundered.

Juexing moved fast, but the pursuers finally opened fire. Bullets cracked around him. He stumbled and went down. Four armed men closed in, puzzled by the lack of blood. In that single second of hesitation Juexing rolled, seized one gun, and ripped it free. An elbow to a throat, a kick that sent another man flying, and he had the last guard as a human shield.

“Nobody move. I don’t want to hurt you,” he said in rough Japanese.

The remaining three kept their weapons trained on him but dared not shoot. Juexing dragged his hostage to the door, emptied the magazine into the lock, and shoved the man aside. The moment the door swung open Orange stepped out and slammed the pistol butts into the two guards behind it. Both crumpled.

Juexing stripped the magazines, slammed the door, and grabbed Orange’s hand.

“Move!”

He fired once into an overhead water pipe. Water erupted; the entire checkpoint dissolved into chaos. In the confusion they sprinted toward the fifth and final line.

Shang Xuan, a master of disguise, had already settled Little Bell and now waited atop the fifth defensive tower. Two figures appeared in his sights. Guards raised their rifles; he stopped them with a word.

“They’re friends.”

The gates opened.

“Just as expected,” Juexing said, glancing up at the tower.

Once they were through, Shang Xuan climbed into a waiting military truck and drove them toward the exhibition hall. The city center was empty of people—replaced by an ocean of green vines and black six-petaled flowers.

“We go into the hall,” Juexing said. “I’m certain the root is under it.”

“I checked the old records,” Shang Xuan replied while driving. “This same green man-eating vine appeared in France once. It swallowed an entire city overnight. No one knows when or how it disappeared.”

“We can’t just sit and wait to die. I already know how to kill the thing.”

“Fire. These plants fear fire more than anything. Burn them and nothing remains.” Shang Xuan understood at once.

Orange had not expected to see Shang Xuan again—much less see him working side by side with Juexing to save the city.

“Stay in the truck,” Juexing told her. “The vines can’t hurt you here. Shang Xuan and I will go in. Wait for Jiaye and the tanker. That tanker is the only weapon that can end this.”

The look he left her with made the mission clear. She stayed.

The two men chopped a path through the vines with axes and stepped into the exhibition hall. Inside, the rooms were almost intact—corridors still corridors, studios still studios—except for the green stains, the blood, and the missing painting of the Soul-Binding Tower.

“The painting is probably in the tunnel,” Juexing said.

Shang Xuan handed him a high-powered flashlight. They exchanged a glance, then Juexing led the way into the tunnel. Even the intense beam was swallowed by the darkness until it became a weak glow.

The entire passage was dark green, as if woven from a million living vines. Walking felt like stepping on layer after layer of plant flesh. After ten minutes the darkness behind them was absolute. Even two men this bold felt the cold crawl of fear.

“What is it?” Shang Xuan asked when Juexing stopped.

The flashlight rested on a corpse. Shang Xuan did not recognize the man, but Juexing did: DouDou, married to Orange’s classmate Meixue for less than a week. In his arms he still clutched an oil painting—the Soul-Binding Tower.

“You knew him?”

Juexing nodded. If they ever got out, he had no idea how he would tell Meixue.

“We have to keep moving or more people will die.” Shang Xuan pulled him forward.

Farther on they found more bodies—men, women, old, young—all innocent, all drained so the plant could feed. The tunnel felt exactly like a road into hell; the deeper and darker it became, the closer death waited.

“Wait.” Shang Xuan froze. He had heard a cry for help.

Both beams swung upward. Bodies hung from the ceiling—some still twitching with residual life. Above each corpse a hole opened into a network of tunnels that connected the underground to every house in the city.

“No… it can’t be.” Shang Xuan stared. Had the roots already spread beneath the entire city, ready to swallow everyone above?

“Save them first!” Juexing fired three rounds into the vines binding a little girl. She dropped; Shang Xuan caught her. Outwardly she looked unharmed, yet a vine was already growing inside her body, ready to burst free the way it had with the detective earlier.

“I want Mama! I want Mama!” The child thrashed, eyes locked on a woman’s body hanging nearby.

Juexing lowered his head, nearly broken by the sight. Shang Xuan gripped his shoulder.

“We don’t give up.”

The words were barely spoken when the entire tunnel shuddered. Vines woke. Both men lost their footing. Juexing scrambled up, pulled Shang Xuan to his feet, and then a deafening buzz rose from every direction.

They ran. Thrashing vines slowed them. Shang Xuan’s leg was seized and yanked backward; he and the girl crashed to the floor. Juexing spun, axe flashing, and cut him free—but the little girl was already beyond saving. Vines erupted from her body and wrapped her into a living cocoon.

“Go!” Shang Xuan shoved Juexing forward. They sprinted on. The tunnel suddenly widened. Juexing stopped just in time; one more step and he would have plunged over the edge into the abyss.

### Character Notes

**Luo Juexing**  
Leads the infiltration with cold precision, yet the sight of DouDou’s body and the hanging children nearly shatters him. He still chooses to keep moving.

**Shang Xuan**  
Finally working openly with Juexing. His knowledge of the vine’s history and his presence at the fifth checkpoint prove decisive. The moment the tunnel awakens, he refuses to abandon hope.

**Qingmu Chengzi (Orange)**  
Given the most important task of all: wait for the tanker. The weight of that single mission is written in the look Juexing leaves her with.

**Huang Jiaye**  
The driver who turns a police car into a battering ram. His part of the plan—securing the tanker—now becomes the only hope left for the city.

**DouDou**  
Newly married, already dead, still clutching the painting that started everything. His body is the first personal cost the group cannot ignore.

**The Demonic Vines**  
No longer random growth. They form a living network beneath the entire city, feeding on the living and the dead alike, and the tunnel is only the throat of something far larger.
