Orange walked home alone. Cold wind gusted along the street; the streetlamps cast only a thin, uncertain path of light.

*Tap. Tap. Tap.*  

The sound of leather shoes on pavement cut sharply through the quiet night. She felt someone following her. Fear kept her from looking back; she only quickened her pace. The footsteps behind her matched the change at once, staying close like a shadow.

Suddenly a handcart rolled out from beside a trash bin. Orange jumped.

“What a pity… what a pity!” an old woman’s voice said. She was using tongs to pick up glass bottles. One of them had been crushed under Orange’s shoe.

“I’m so sorry,” Orange apologized quickly.

“Heh. Did someone throw you out?” the old woman asked without looking up, still collecting bottles.

“Of course not. I left because I was angry.” Orange pouted.

“He’s always like that. You need patience if you want to stay around him.” The old woman chuckled.

Orange stared. How did this stranger know who she was talking about?

“So similar… so similar…” the old woman murmured, glancing sideways at her.

“Similar to whom?”

“Never mind. Better find someone to walk you home. A girl shouldn’t be out alone at night.” She finished gathering the bottles, pushed her cart, and disappeared down the street with a cheerful hum.

“Still here?”  

Juexing’s voice came from behind. He had been watching her stand beside the trash bins.

“I was just leaving. I won’t get in your way.” Orange refused to look at him and walked on.

“Let me walk you home.”

“No need. Thank you.” The coldness in her tone made it clear she was still angry.

Juexing stepped quickly in front of her and stopped.

“This matter is far more serious than you think. I don’t want you—”

“If you don’t want me in danger, then help me fix it.” She cut him off.

Juexing rubbed the bridge of his nose and fell silent again.

Orange looked at his conflicted expression, gave a short *hmph*, and swung her bag at him.

“Move. I won’t trouble you anymore. I’ll handle it myself.”

“All right. I’ll help you. But you have to agree to one condition.”

Orange stopped at once.

“As long as you help me, I’ll agree to ten conditions.” A smile appeared on her face—different from the pure, childish ones she used to wear.

“When this is over, you go back to Japan.”

“You’re trying to send me away? Do I disgust you that much?”

Juexing sighed. “I’m doing this for your own good. I don’t want to see you dragged into this kind of chaos.”

“You’ll stay by my side and protect me the whole time, right? So I don’t have to worry.”

“You really don’t listen. Fine. I’ll take that as a yes.” He gave her a light push, and the two of them walked toward her apartment on the outskirts of the city.

At her door Orange turned. “It’s late. Be careful on your way back. Message me when you get home.”

Juexing nodded and waited until she went inside. The moment the door closed, Orange leaned against it, holding onto something she could not name. Outside, the street was silent. She heard no footsteps of departure. Juexing had not left. Hands in his pockets, he stood quietly watching her door. The two of them remained like that—standing, waiting—on opposite sides of the wood.

---

The next afternoon, the moment class ended, Orange hurriedly cleared her desk and ran downstairs. Juexing was already waiting at the school gate.

“Sorry to keep you waiting.” Her cheeks were faintly flushed; she had clearly run the entire way from the third floor.

Juexing did not move. Orange nudged him. “Why aren’t we going?”

“Let you catch your breath first. Otherwise you’ll collapse halfway.”

She glared. “I didn’t know you could be so mean.”

“That’s enough. I want to stop by the hospital first and check on Cheng Shaoxi’s condition.” He raised a hand and flagged a taxi. They headed toward the hospital.

A ragged cloud drifted across the sky and settled over the hospital roof. Wind howled around the building, yet the pedestrians on the street noticed nothing. An ambulance raced past them, red lights flashing. In the back, a figure struggled. Juexing glanced out the window—and froze. A terrible shadow clung to the roof of the ambulance: a head covered in writhing snakes.

“What’s wrong?” Orange asked.

“Driver, faster, please.”

The driver nodded and accelerated, but traffic lights still cost them five minutes.

Before they even entered the building, Juexing looked up at the cloud-shrouded hospital and felt a sharp sense of danger. He sprinted inside. A shadow slipped from the cloud and followed through the main doors. Juexing ran as if searching for something, then stopped abruptly in front of an emergency-room door.

Orange almost collided with him.

Inside, a girl thrashed wildly. Three doctors tried to restrain her. Orange leaned in for a better look—and her heart sank. The patient was Xiao Xue, one of her own students.

“How is this possible?” she whispered.

“Sedative!” the doctor on the left ordered.

The doctor on the right grabbed a syringe and plunged it toward the girl’s arm. The moment the needle touched her skin, it bent.

Strange phenomena followed at once. The lights in the room began to flicker irregularly. Wind forced its way through a closed window, whipping the curtains with a sound like a beast’s howl. Scalpels on the tray jumped and rattled as if ready to fly free.

On the bed Xiao Xue struggled like a frenzied monster. Her eyes and expression were pure malice; she clawed and bit at the doctors holding her down.

“Help her!” Orange pleaded.

But Juexing could do nothing. He still did not know the source.

Snakes began crawling over the windowsill, tongues glowing faintly red. Without hesitation Juexing rushed to the window and tried to close it. The moment his hand touched the curtain, an invisible force flung him back.

He pressed his left hand over the birthmark on his right arm. Cyan light gathered at his fingertips. In a single sweep, a massive cyan hand slammed the window shut.

The lights could not withstand the strain. With a sharp *pop* they all went out. In the sudden darkness Xiao Xue curled into a ball against the wall, eyes full of hatred, staring at a photograph clutched in her hand.

“Xiao Xue…!”

Her mother’s cry brought everyone back to themselves. The lights returned. The window was closed. Everything looked peaceful, as if nothing impossible had ever happened.

Orange covered her mouth, watching the mother dissolve into tears. She had already seen this scene once before.

The three doctors stood aside, defeated. None of them had ever encountered a case like this. Xiao Xue remained curled in the corner, her body radiating the cold, solid aura of stone. She looked less like a living girl and more like a statue that had somehow learned to breathe.

Orange closed her eyes and leaned against Juexing’s back. He could feel her grief, her fear, and her helplessness. One of the doctors turned, saw Juexing, and pointed with a trembling finger.

“You… aren’t you… Teacher Luo…?”

---

Another body was added to the morgue: a girl named Xiao Xue.

Juexing stood between the two corpses and watched them in silence. He knew the curse had only just begun.

After helping Xiao Xue’s mother through the worst of the shock and waiting until she finally fell asleep, Orange slipped quietly into the morgue.

“Any clues?” she asked in a shaky whisper, glancing around as if the same malignant presence might appear again.

Juexing remained silent, still staring at the bodies.

After several minutes he spoke. “It has something to do with the place they visited for the cultural festival.” In his hand was a blurred photograph—identical to the Medusa image he had at home.

“The cultural festival?” Orange thought quickly. On the final day Cheng Shaoxi had been absent. The next day Xiao Xue had collapsed. Both students had been in the same group.

“You’re right. Who else was in their group?”

“You mean… they’ll die one after another?” The horror of the idea was almost too large to hold.

“Until the curse is broken, yes.” Juexing’s voice left no room for doubt.

“The others were… were…” Orange’s mind scattered under the weight of fear and fragmented information. She could not recall the names.

“They should have turned in their project reports. We need to go back to the school immediately and find the records.”

They left the morgue at once.

By the time they reached the school it was already past five. Skipping dinner, they went straight to the teachers’ office. Orange opened the cabinet and pulled out the stack of cultural-festival projects, searching rapidly.

“Here!” She handed four notebooks to Juexing. He opened one. Inside was nothing but pages of meaningless garbled text.

“How is this possible?”

Juexing stared at the chaos of characters. Suddenly a woman’s soft laughter rose from the notebook—clear enough for both of them to hear.

At that exact moment Huang Jiaye knocked, cleared his throat, and stepped inside.

“Given the unusual nature of these two cases, the superintendent has listed them as priority investigations. That’s why I’m here.” His expression shifted from serious to his usual half-grin.

“What do we do next? There are still three students left. We can’t let anything happen to them.” Orange’s voice was urgent.

Juexing wrote down the remaining names: Chen Cheng, Yu Xiaohai, and Du Xiaoxiao.

“Should we go find them?”

“First priority is to gather them in one place and find out exactly where they went. Then we go there ourselves and look for the root of the curse.”

“So you don’t have a solid plan either?” Jiaye asked.

“No one can give guarantees with this kind of thing. Since you’re here, their protection is your responsibility.” Juexing patted Jiaye’s shoulder, then turned to Orange. “Get their addresses. We leave immediately.”

Orange took the list and ran to the student-affairs office. From the records she copied the three addresses. When they finally stepped out of the school gates, night had already fallen.

### Character Notes

**Qingmu Chengzi (Orange)**  
Her anger at Juexing softens into reluctant reliance. The second petrification in front of her eyes deepens both her fear and her determination. She is no longer merely curious; she is invested.

**Luo Juexing**  
Uses the cyan power sealed in his birthmark for the first time in front of Orange. The appearance of the Medusa shadow on the ambulance confirms the curse is actively hunting. He accepts the case under one condition: Orange must leave China when it ends.

**Huang Jiaye**  
Officially drawn into the investigation as the cases are elevated. His role shifts from occasional ally to formal protector of the remaining students.

**Xiao Xue**  
Second confirmed victim. Her death in the emergency room, witnessed by medical staff, makes the curse impossible to dismiss as private tragedy.

**The Medusa Curse**  
Spreads through a shared cultural-festival outing. Photographs serve as both trigger and medium. The curse now claims victims in rapid succession; three names remain on the list.

**The Old Woman**  
A brief, enigmatic encounter. She appears to know both Juexing’s nature and Orange’s resemblance to someone from the past. Her warning about walking alone at night lingers.
