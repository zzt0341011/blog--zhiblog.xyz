It was the tenth floor of an apartment building, facing south, filled with clear sunlight. At the far right of the balcony a beach umbrella leaned at an angle. Sitting there with a cup of rich coffee, watching the night street below, might count as one of life’s genuine pleasures.

If the camera turned from the balcony into the rooms, it would reveal a luxurious two-bedroom, two-living-room residence. Anyone who lived in such a place was unlikely to be ordinary.

He was neither royalty nor the scion of a wealthy clan—only a man a little less ordinary than most. He still kept half his hair dyed blond and still performed that signature flying-kiss gesture. By now the reader will know who he is.

Three large suitcases stood in the living room. Books and papers were stacked in disorder. The bed in the bedroom was covered with scattered clothes and trousers. The scene looked less like a robbery and more like someone preparing to move or to leave on a long journey.

Did he truly believe leaving this city would be better? Was it because of everything he had seen and lived through recently, or because deep inside he had never escaped the single moment from five years ago?

Neither Granny Luo Shen’s words, nor Little Bell’s slightly childish smile, nor the slowly deepening feelings between Orange and Juexing seemed to have left any lasting mark on him. He simply wanted to go somewhere else—somewhere, perhaps, where only he could find happiness.

On the table by the door sat a glass tank. Five goldfish of different colors and shapes swam inside, blinking their golden eyes and blowing bubbles as they waited for their owner to return. They looked so peaceful it was as if they already knew they would not be leaving. Were they attached to something, or had fate already decided that this sudden journey would be delayed?

Orange finished work and was about to leave the school gates when Xiao Qing and Zilan ran after her.

“Tomorrow there’s no class,” Xiao Qing said. “Teacher, why don’t you take us to Teacher Luo’s place to play?”

Orange’s eyes widened. Ever since Juexing had appeared in their sight, the two girls seemed especially fond of him. He had not visited the vocational high school for a full week—partly because the university laboratory was under renovation, partly because Yu Xun’s column needed him as a guest. Apart from Sundays, when he could see Orange, the rest of his time was swallowed by work.

“Look, Teacher Orange’s face is red. She’s jealous, Sister Xiao Qing!” Zilan said deliberately. Orange smiled and patted Xiao Qing’s head.

“All right. Sunday. I’ll take you to see him on Sunday, okay?”

“Okay!” Both girls nodded, linked hands, and left. Watching their relationship grow closer made Orange happy, yet mixed with the happiness was a quiet recollection of Xiao Qing’s recent behavior. The girl carried a complicated feeling toward Juexing that Orange herself could not fully read. What exactly was it?

A taxi stopped and the driver tapped the horn. Orange nodded to him and climbed in. Before leaving the city, Shang Xuan had asked to see her once.

It was lunchtime, yet Juexing still found a moment to call Orange, ask after her, and only then hang up and climb into Jiaye’s car. Today Jiaye and Yu Xun had suddenly decided to treat him to a big meal. Looking at their expressions, Juexing suspected the lunch would not be free.

The corridor was empty. In front of the elevator stood a young woman with her hair tied back, wearing a dull-colored jacket, jeans, and blue canvas shoes. From a distance she looked like a village girl—her clothes a little outdated.

Shang Xuan, carrying a bag of vegetables, walked toward the elevator and stopped behind her. He watched her lower her head, reach toward the button, then pull her hand back, undecided.

He waited a while. She continued to hesitate; he could see she was trembling.

He reached past her and pressed the open button. After a moment the elevator chimed and opened its arms.

Shang Xuan stepped past her. A brief red glow flashed across the birthmark on his arm and vanished. They stood opposite each other. He was about to ask whether she wanted to come in when she stepped forward with heavy feet, still looking down, and entered.

He closed the door. The moment it sealed, her unease grew sharper; she did not even dare press a floor button.

“Are you all right?” he asked. Inside the nearly sealed elevator a chill circulated.

“E-eighth… floor.” She clenched both fists as if restraining something; her body trembled in small, continuous shudders. Shang Xuan pressed 8 for her and 10 for himself.

The elevator rose quickly, yet to her it felt as long as several hours. She kept her head lowered; the bangs covered half her face. Shang Xuan could not see her features clearly, but from her figure she seemed a good-looking girl.

The red numbers changed: three, four, five…

Silence filled the car. He could hear her murmuring something, as if comforting herself. This elevator had been repaired once before and had never failed again—until today, when the two of them happened to step inside.

The car shuddered, then stopped with a heavy *clang* between the seventh and eighth floors.

Her heart pounded violently. The sudden stillness made the world spin. She covered her mouth as if about to vomit, then crouched, breathing rapidly. Shang Xuan crouched as well.

“Miss, are you all right…?”

She could not speak—only shook her head again and again, as though she had seen or felt something abnormal.

He pressed the open button several times. Nothing responded. She clutched her chest; her face had turned the color of iron and she looked ready to faint.

Shang Xuan struck the elevator door—*slap, slap*. After a few blows the car began to descend. At the sixth floor the doors opened.

She staggered out, supporting herself on the frame. Before she could steady herself her legs gave way and she collapsed. Shang Xuan caught her at once. A set of house keys fell from her pocket.

She had fainted. Cold sweat beaded on her forehead; her complexion was terrible.

He supported her with one arm and fished for his phone with the other, intending to call an ambulance. Before he could dial she spoke:

“I just left the hospital. Please just help me home. Thank you.”

Shang Xuan checked the number on the key—8-04. He lifted her into his arms and began climbing the stairs. She lay quietly against him, her face full of sorrow and quiet melancholy, as though she had already endured a great deal of pain.

He unlocked the door. The moment he stepped inside he noticed something unusual: apart from the entrance door, every interior door was made of glass. The whole apartment looked as if it contained only that single solid door.

He carefully laid her on the bed, removed her shoes, drew the blanket over her, and then left the bedroom.

He had intended to leave at once, yet when he saw a prescription lying on the dining table he changed his mind. He pulled a chair to the bedroom doorway and sat down. He did not know why; he only felt, deep down, that he could not simply walk away. What if she needed help after he left?

Shang Xuan seemed to have forgotten the appointment he had made with Orange. The airline ticket tucked in his pocket was likewise completely forgotten.

Without realizing it, his heavy eyelids closed. He woke to the loud protests of an empty stomach; he had not even eaten lunch.

When he lifted his head and opened his eyes she was lying on her side, watching him. The sudden gaze startled him.

“You’re awake. I should go.” He stretched his stiff muscles.

“You’ve stayed with me from the elevator until now?” Her color had improved; she looked like someone recovering from a serious illness. She sat up.

“Yes. I was worried that if you needed help and no one was here, it would be dangerous.” He smiled.

She studied the man in front of her carefully. Although he gave off a slightly “bad-boy” impression, an unmistakable gentlemanly quality ran through him. She felt no need to be on guard.

His stomach growled again. She immediately got out of bed, put on her shoes, tidied her hair and clothes, and said, “Why don’t I cook something? You can leave after you’ve eaten.”

“I couldn’t possibly…” He tried to refuse politely, but she had already walked into the kitchen.

“Oh no—I forgot to buy vegetables. I’ll go down and get some.” She turned, ready to hurry out.

“No need. I have some here. I bought them to eat anyway—just use them.” He handed her the bag from the table.

She smiled. “I still don’t know how to thank you, and now you’re spending money as well.”

“It’s fine. Just cook quickly—I’m really hungry.”

She nodded, carried the bag into the kitchen. Shang Xuan walked into the living room and began examining the layout. In one corner a wooden stand that looked like a small shrine caught his attention.

He did not approach it. Instead he closed his eyes and pressed his left hand over the birthmark on his right arm. To his relief the mark did not react. He decided he was overthinking.

Aromas began to drift from the kitchen, making his stomach complain even louder. He simply sat down at the dining table and waited.

“I was in too much of a hurry, so I just made some plain noodles for now. I also started a big-bone soup—you can drink it this afternoon.” She carried out two bowls of yangchun noodles.

Shang Xuan smiled. “When you’re hungry, anything tastes good.” He took the chopsticks without ceremony and began eating with open appetite.

She only watched him and did not pick up her own chopsticks. After he had eaten half the bowl he noticed her gaze, slowed down, and looked up.

“Why aren’t you eating?”

“I’m not hungry. If it isn’t enough for you, take this one too.” She pushed her bowl toward him.

He waved a hand. “I’m only hungry, not a bottomless pit.”

“I still don’t know your name. I’m Lu Wanting.”

“I’m… Shang Xuan.” He paused, yet still gave the name.

“You’re the cursebreaker Shang Xuan?” Her eyes widened.

“Maybe just the same name…” He tried to sound casual, careful not to let any crack show.

“That’s good. That’s good.” Wanting answered thoughtfully.

### Character Notes

**Shang Xuan**  
Packs to leave the city and the unresolved weight of Li Mei’s death, yet a single elevator malfunction and a stranger’s distress are enough to delay the departure. His decision to stay, cook with the vegetables he himself bought, and quietly check his birthmark shows that the protective instinct remains stronger than the desire to escape.

**Lu Wanting**  
A new presence—recently discharged from hospital, intensely claustrophobic in elevators, living in an apartment where every interior door is glass. Her recognition of the name “Shang Xuan” and the subsequent relief suggest she already knows more about cursebreakers (or about him) than she admits.

**Qingmu Chengzi (Orange)**  
Caught between the innocent affection of her two students and the complicated undercurrent in Xiao Qing’s feelings toward Juexing. The promised Sunday visit keeps the domestic thread alive while Shang Xuan’s path begins to diverge.

**Luo Juexing**  
Appears only briefly—calling Orange, then being pulled into a “not-free” lunch with Jiaye and Yu Xun—reminding the reader that his ordinary life continues even as new currents form elsewhere.

**The Goldfish**  
Five silent witnesses in Shang Xuan’s apartment. Their calm certainty that they will not be leaving foreshadows the delay of the journey more clearly than any spoken line.

**The Glass-Door Apartment**  
An unusual living space that immediately draws Shang Xuan’s professional attention. The shrine-like stand in the corner and the lack of reaction from his birthmark leave the nature of the place deliberately unresolved.
