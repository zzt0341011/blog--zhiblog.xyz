This was no accident. Someone had deliberately committed murder.

*Bang.* The door of the room locked from the outside. Besides the two of them, someone else was inside the house.

“Open the door! Let me out!” He rushed to the door and yanked the handle with all his strength, but the person outside had already chained it shut. Even if the lock turned, he could not leave.

He collapsed against the door. Silence returned as if no sound had ever been made.

He looked at the girl lying not far away—disheveled hair, dirty clothes, the bright eyes behind the bangs already drained of color. Through those eyes he seemed to see everything that had happened in this room earlier: fear turning into terror, terror into helplessness. She had been like an abandoned child who could find neither family nor the road home. She struggled, begged death not to linger beside her. Her fragile frame could no longer endure the torment. Her breathing came in broken gasps, her heartbeat raced and slowed without pattern, until she finally slumped to the floor. Her trembling hand reached for some last hope, then fell limp.

He stood quickly, took the descending arm, and a bone-deep cold spread from his palm through his entire body—sharper than any electric shock.

He gathered her into his arms as if holding the person he loved most, not daring to delay a single second.

Tears finally broke the dam, flooding with emotion too large to contain.

“No. I won’t let you leave like this.” He swore it.

He lifted her, opened the opposite window, and looked down into bottomless darkness. Yet this was only the second floor—why had the tombstones below vanished?

He brushed the tangled black hair from her face and quietly studied the features that were already losing their color. The moon, unable to bear the solitude any longer, slipped free of the clouds and spilled its pitying light across the window, across her jade-white skin.

“I’ll take you away from here. Even if the bottom is a ten-thousand-foot abyss, I’ll be with you…”

Two figures fell from the sky in a perfect free-fall. A heavy *thud* as they struck the muddy ground. Tombstones of every size lay crooked on both sides. The only new sound was a single scream…

---

“Ah—!”

Wanting sat bolt upright. The nightmare had not yet faded; cold sweat had already soaked her pajamas. The scene had been so real. She could not remember the man’s face—only that he kept half his hair dyed blond.

She got out of bed, walked to the coat rack, and looked at Shang Xuan’s jacket hanging alone. Thoughtfully she reached out and touched it. At the right sleeve a point of red flashed and vanished. She stepped back instinctively; when she looked again it was only an illusion.

She smiled with quiet relief, took a change of clothes from the wardrobe, and went into the bathroom.

At the same moment the clock passed 3:00 a.m., Shang Xuan also jolted awake from the nightmare. He immediately opened his right palm. Exactly as before, a white, ice-like mark flashed across it and disappeared. Was the woman in the dream Wanting? Had something truly happened to her?

After a dream like that, sleep was impossible. He rubbed his temples, went to the kitchen, and heated a glass of milk. The journey he had planned for today would probably remain only a distant wish.

He sat in the living room with the milk. Outside the floor-to-ceiling window the sky was beginning to lighten—something he had never watched at this hour before. Two floors below, on the same side of the building, Wanting also stood at her window holding a cup of milk tea, watching the pale dawn. Two floors above, another person stood at a window as well.

In an apartment building that still held only a few scattered lights, the two of them formed a single straight line of quiet scenery.

Shang Xuan did not know what the dream meant, yet instinct told him Wanting carried secrets. Why did she take psychiatric medication? Why did elevators terrify her? Why did she refuse to speak of her family? The questions made him deeply interested in her. Whether that purposeful interest would quietly become a bridge for the fire of love between them remained unknown…

The next morning when Orange woke she found herself lying in the bed. She yanked the blanket down to check herself—then heard a voice from the kitchen:

“If you’re awake, come eat breakfast.”

“Oh no, oh no!” She scrambled out and ran to Juexing’s side.

“Ohayō, Teacher Orange!” Xiao Qing sat at the dining table eating a cheese bread.

Zilan emerged from the bathroom, greeted her as well, and sat down beside Xiao Qing.

“This…” Orange thought she must still be dreaming.

“They took the early express train. I thought you had given them the address. Turns out they went to find Jiaye first…” Juexing gestured for her to wash up and join them.

“Teacher, are you all right?” Zilan looked at Orange’s blank expression.

Orange recovered. “I’m fine, fine—just surprised.” She forced a smile and disappeared into the bathroom.

“How do you even know Jiaye?” Juexing handed them two glasses of milk.

“Of course! Detective Huang was the one who carried me into the ambulance. His arms are really strong and his chest is super hard. Does he have abs?” Xiao Qing teased.

“You little brat—who taught you to talk about things like that?” Juexing laughed.

“Please. I already knew this stuff in my first year of high school. As for who taught me… I’m not too clear myself.” She played along.

Orange had been in the bathroom for some time. Juexing felt something was off and knocked. She pulled him inside, closed the door, and whispered, “Is this a hallucination?”

“What’s wrong with you? They’re only kids. No need to be so nervous.”

She exhaled. “Not a hallucination? I was overthinking.” The two of them finally returned to the table.

“Teacher, you’re so disloyal—running over to Teacher Luo’s place by yourself and then staying the night with him…” Xiao Qing left the sentence unfinished; a little suspense always felt better.

“Oh—nothing happened between Teacher Luo and me. I just slept…”

“‘Just slept’? Why does that sound so emotionally rich?”

Juexing glanced at Orange’s innocent expression and smiled. “All right, don’t take the joke too far. Listen carefully: Orange and I are adults, so certain things are perfectly normal—for example, the two of us sleeping under the same roof.”

Orange looked up at him; he was protecting her.

“Okay, okay, I get it. Actually Teacher Orange has misunderstood me and Teacher Luo all along. I came today to clear that up.” Xiao Qing’s tone turned serious.

“Then go ahead.” Juexing passed Orange a cheese bread. Everyone waited.

“First, I think Teacher Luo is a good man. Good men are rare, so of course there are always bees and butterflies around him. Second, my wish is that someday I’ll also meet a man like Teacher Luo who will take care of me for a lifetime. So Teacher Orange, you don’t need to worry at all—the person Teacher Luo likes is you!”

The words still felt a little improper to Orange, yet they eased her heart considerably.

“They’re only children. Between us there’s nothing more than family affection,” Juexing said.

“Maybe work pressure has been too high lately. Sometimes I overthink things. Sorry, everyone.” Orange smoothed the moment over.

“Fine. All problems solved. But there’s still one thing I hope Teacher Luo will agree to.”

“Let’s hear it. I’ll do my best.”

“I want to recognize you as my older brother—and Zilan too!”

“Yes, yes! At school we’ll still call you teachers, but outside school you’ll be big brother and big sister!” Zilan added.

“Haha—I thought it was something serious.” Juexing laughed.

“By the way, I heard the seaside park is really fun. Shouldn’t we go play there?” Xiao Qing suddenly remembered.

Orange checked the clock; it was still early. “I’ll go to the market and buy things for lunch. After we eat we can go out.”

“All right. You two stay home and behave. Don’t open the door for strangers.” Juexing smiled and followed Orange out.

The two girls high-fived. “The world is ours—let’s play to our hearts’ content!”

---

Orange and Juexing walked side by side. Even though the girls had clarified everything, Orange still seemed not entirely at ease.

“Didn’t sleep well last night?” he asked.

“Am I the kind of person who measures a gentleman’s belly with a petty mind?” she countered.

“It’s only human. At least now I know I still carry a lot of weight in your heart.”

“You were testing me? From now on you’re not allowed to use methods like that. And last night—did you take advantage while I was asleep?”

“Haha. I’m a proper gentleman. If I wanted something I’d take it openly—I wouldn’t sneak around.”

“Hmph. Hard to tell. Anyway, what should we make for lunch?”

“Simple home dishes and a big-bone soup.” The moment he said it Orange’s eyes lit up; she pulled him toward the market.

---

*Ding-dong.* Someone rang the doorbell of Juexing’s apartment.

The room fell silent at once. People who ring the doorbell usually don’t have a key. Xiao Qing and Zilan exchanged glances. Zilan took a baseball bat and stationed herself in the kitchen; Xiao Qing peered through the peephole.

The person standing outside was her.

“It’s you!” Xiao Qing said, opening the door.

“Eh? Why are *you* here?” Little Bell looked equally surprised.

Zilan, who had been waiting in hiding, sensed something wrong, gripped the bat, and crept out. Only when she raised it did she recognize Little Bell.

“Whoa—you’re here too?” Little Bell jumped at the sight of the raised bat.

“We’re classmates. Come in. Teacher Luo and Teacher Orange went out to buy things.” Xiao Qing invited her inside as if it were her own home.

“What are you doing here?” Zilan asked.

“I came to deliver something. I’ll give it to Teacher Luo when he gets back and then leave.”

She had barely finished speaking when Juexing and Orange returned, talking and laughing. The door had not been fully closed. Juexing’s instincts flared; he shoved the door open hard and dropped into a defensive stance. The three girls in the living room stared at him with identical expressions of astonishment.

### Character Notes

**Luo Juexing**  
In the dream he chooses to leap into the abyss with the dead girl rather than abandon her—an act that mirrors the principle he has struggled with since Li Mei. In the waking world he protects Orange’s reputation in front of the girls and accepts the new “big brother” role with easy warmth.

**Qingmu Chengzi (Orange)**  
Wakes to an unexpectedly full apartment and momentary panic that the previous night’s closeness was somehow exposed. Xiao Qing’s clear declaration finally eases the lingering insecurity.

**Zhao Xiaoqing & Mo Zilan**  
Their early-morning arrival and the formal request to become “siblings” outside school mark the completion of the healing process that began after the fire. The bond is now chosen rather than forced by trauma.

**Shang Xuan & Lu Wanting**  
Share the exact same dream at the exact same hour. The white ice-mark on his palm and the red flash on his jacket’s sleeve confirm a supernatural link between them that neither fully understands yet. Both stand at their windows watching the same dawn—two points on one vertical line.

**Little Bell**  
Arrives with something to deliver to Juexing, walking straight into the newly formed “family” gathering and setting up the next small complication or revelation.

**The Shared Dream**  
No longer only Juexing’s. The fact that Wanting experiences the identical sequence—including the half-blond man—raises the possibility that the dream is a residual echo capable of reaching more than one connected soul.
