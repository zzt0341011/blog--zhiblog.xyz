Standing under the eyes of everyone, with friendship on one side and love on the other—why was this kind of dilemma always thrown to him? He understood that the choice between saving and not saving might cost him a portion of friendship, and to Juexing those feelings were hard to let go.

In the end he made his decision. He leapt past Little Bell into the pit. Was it boundless darkness, or the far shore where dawn suddenly appeared? As a cursebreaker he had gone against heaven’s will to rescue a solitary soul…

“Nothing can be forced. Every choice has its value. Once you decide to act, do not regret it.” The words given to him after he completed his training circled again and again through his mind.

The great fire was extinguished. Lu Shengnan and Tian Shubin were caught red-handed. At first they refused to lower their proud heads, yet when Little Bell handed the recording pen from her pocket to Jiaye they finally understood the deeper meaning of the saying “Heaven watches what men do.”

Juexing climbed out of the sewer pit holding only a set of clothes. There was no sign of Wanting. Shang Xuan stared at the garments in a daze, then clutched them tightly to his chest as though reuniting with a lover lost for many years. The depth of that feeling was beyond anyone’s comprehension.

Everything seemed to have reached a perfect full stop. Whether it had been a curse or some other reason, no one would bother to speculate further.

Yu Xun reported the entire case without exaggeration, stirring another wave across the city. Under the pressure of public opinion and the evidence supplied by the police, the court decided to reopen the three-year-old unsolved case. In a few days Lu Shengnan and Tian Shubin would probably enter prison to finish their remaining lives. In a few days the cleared case would be talked about in every street and alley. To Shang Xuan, however, all of it was as insignificant as dust.

He stood before the eighth-floor door that still hung the sign “Away on Business,” staring blankly at the two plane tickets in his hand.

“I could have… I could have flown far away…” He leaned against the door, crumpled the tickets into a ball, and tossed them casually downstairs. A couple who looked like husband and wife stood nearby with suitcases, watching the man collapsed at the doorway.

Shang Xuan turned his face toward them. The man adjusted his glasses and said with a friendly smile, “Sir, are you all right?”

Shang Xuan rose woodenly and stepped aside. The man took out a key and opened the door.

“Sir, are you sure you’re all right?” the man asked again.

“This is your home, isn’t it?”

The man nodded. “We went to Singapore on business. Before leaving we hired a Miss Lu to look after the place. She just left.”

“What? You saw Wanting?” Shang Xuan’s spirit returned at once.

“Yes, just downstairs. She said she was going to some cemetery. Sounded rather gloomy.” As the man spoke, his wife came out carrying a small shrine. “What is this thing? Don’t put useless objects in the house.”

“If you don’t want it, give it to me.” Shang Xuan took the shrine from her hands and shot into the elevator like the wind.

Today was the day of Wanting’s cremation. Many relatives and friends had come. Most of them knew that the father-and-daughter case had finally been cleared and that Lu Shengnan and the others had received the legal punishment they deserved. They were filled with excitement and emotion.

In the large hall of the cemetery a white crystal coffin stood. According to the instructions left by Mr. Lu Zhengyao, the cemetery director, Mr. Xu, was to cremate the body of his daughter Lu Wanting today and continue to bury her in the original plot.

The hall was black and white. At the front of the right-hand row one seat remained empty, as though reserved for a particular person. On that seat lay a letter written in Wanting’s hand: *To the one I deeply loved.*

Everyone was immersed in sorrow. The condensed air froze the entire scene. From a distance a figure approached—half-blond hair, black suit, steps steady yet faintly melancholy. Dust-covered leather shoes entered the cemetery hall, walked straight through the crowd, and sat in the empty seat on the right.

The coffin was opened. Wanting lay peacefully inside. Mr. Xu looked at her and could not help murmuring, “Strange… strange!”

The crowd held its breath and stared.

“What is strange?” someone asked.

“Do you see the smile at the corner of her mouth? I remember when she was placed in the coffin her expression was full of helplessness…”

Shang Xuan stood beside her and slowly opened the letter. The ink was not yet fully dry; it must have been written only a short while earlier. Dense lines covered the page, as though telling a story—from the moment they met until the moment they parted, all in the space of a single night. One passage in particular touched a raw nerve; tears of long-held weariness fell lightly onto Wanting’s clothes.

*I’m sorry. I always wanted to tell you face to face, but I truly could not look that fact in the eye. Without any doubt I had already fallen deeply for you. Every moment spent with you was the most brilliant time of my existence. I knew it would be brief. I knew it was only a dream about to end. Yet I still imagined many “what ifs”—what if time could stop, what if everything were real… I did something I considered absurd yet could not help doing: I stole your heart. When you read this letter we will have returned to separate worlds. In truth we never should have intersected. Why, then, were we made to meet? Perhaps you are in pain now. How could I not be? Can I ask you to treat it all as a dream? After waking, return to the world that belongs to you and begin again.*

As he read further, the remaining lines had already been blurred by tears. He crumpled the paper in his palm and whispered, “Begin again… how easy is that to say?”

Everyone present was moved to tears by his every action. They understood why the man kneeling before the coffin was so heartbroken. Perhaps the two of them had shared a brief and beautiful past. Those who know how to cherish look especially pitiable when they weep—whether man or woman.

The staff carried Wanting’s body into the cremation chamber. As the door slowly closed, Shang Xuan wiped away the last of his tears, stood to one side, and closed his eyes in silent mourning. He sincerely hoped Wanting could live well in another world. He hoped she would remember that someone in this world would always be praying for her…

Before Shang Xuan left, Mr. Xu handed him a document—the transfer of Lu Shengnan’s property. Apart from the portion reserved for his wife, the remainder was to be returned to Wanting. Since Wanting could no longer receive it, it was to be given to Shang Xuan.

Shang Xuan opened the transfer paper and saw the sum: thirty million. He gave a cold laugh and looked up at the sky.

“Thirty million? It cannot buy back a stolen heart…”

He stuffed the document back into Mr. Xu’s arms and left without looking back. What he needed was not money.

At the intersection Little Bell sat on a stone waiting for Shang Xuan to appear. Although Wanting was gone, the pain and experiences the woman had brought her still left a bitter taste. She finally realized how narrow her own heart had been. If feelings cannot withstand a test, they will inevitably fall apart in the end.

“Were you waiting for me?” Shang Xuan asked.

“Yes. I was waiting for you. I have something I want to say.”

“I plan to leave this place for a while. Do you want to come with me?”

“All right—but only after I finish the college entrance exams. When summer vacation begins I’ll go with you, to the ends of the earth.” Little Bell smiled.

Looking at her happy face, Shang Xuan could not help smiling as well. Perhaps he no longer remembered everything that had once happened between them, yet the persistence he saw in her was so similar to his own.

Little Bell waved a hand; only then did Shang Xuan return to himself. “All right. I’ll wait for you.”

Hearing the three words “I’ll wait for you,” she understood that their relationship had taken another step forward. Perhaps once they went somewhere else the spark of love would appear. She linked her arm through his. “Let’s go get something to eat.”

Watching their retreating figures, Juexing let out a long breath and accepted the soy milk Orange handed him.

“Man proposes, heaven disposes. I hope they end up together.”

“Why do you suddenly sound like a charlatan?” Juexing teased.

“What is a charlatan?”

“Oh—right, you don’t have that word in Japan. Sorry.” He started walking forward.

“Hey, Luo! Are you insulting me? Tell me what it means right now!” Orange chased after him and pinched his waist hard.

“Ow—that hurts!” The two of them played along the path outside the cemetery, completely out of place with the solemn atmosphere.

While chasing each other they reached the intersection; Juexing accidentally bumped into a handcart. Both of them looked—and saw Granny Luo Shen.

“Long time no see. How are the two of you?” the old woman smiled.

Juexing quickly righted the overturned cart. “I’m really sorry.”

“Looking at the two of you, are you planning to get married?”

Juexing and Orange exchanged a glance. Orange hurried to explain, “We have no plans to marry for the time being.”

Granny Luo Shen chuckled. “Come on. Looking at how happy you are, I can hardly stand it. Remember to save me a seat at the wedding banquet—preferably near the door. I’m slow on my feet and don’t want to miss the food.”

“Of course. If we ever do get married, you’ll be the first to receive an invitation.” Juexing smiled.

“Don’t worry—I’ll prepare a big, fat red envelope for you.” She laughed again, then pushed her cart away.

Suddenly a gust of wind blew a poster from the cart; it slapped against Juexing’s calf with a soft *pa*.

“What is that?” Orange asked.

Juexing picked it up. It was a promotional poster for an oil-painting exhibition to be held at an art gallery in Shibuya, Japan.

“Oh—it’s an exhibition in Shibuya. Let me see.” Orange took the poster and checked the dates; they fell exactly during the summer vacation.

Juexing gave her a light push. “Is it that fascinating? You’re staring so hard.”

“By the way, I plan to go back to Japan for a while during the summer break. You’re coming with me.”

“There’s something I’d like to discuss with you.” Juexing rubbed the bridge of his nose.

“What is it?” She folded the poster and turned to look at him.

“Just now Granny Luo Shen mentioned marriage. I wanted to ask your opinion.” He deliberately slowed his words and watched her expression, expecting her to blush. Instead her calm face left him a little disappointed.

“Hmph. When you decide to tell me what ‘charlatan’ means, I’ll consider answering that question.” With that she climbed into a taxi. Juexing moved to follow, but she blocked him.

“Hey, I haven’t gotten in yet!” He stared wide-eyed as she stuck out her tongue; with a *whoosh* the car sped away. Juexing stood at the intersection, mouth half open, the poster still in his hand.

### Character Notes

**Luo Juexing**  
Chooses to leap into the pit and save the residual soul, knowingly violating the precepts of a cursebreaker. After the resolution he returns to a lighter, teasing dynamic with Orange and begins looking toward Japan.

**Shang Xuan**  
Clings to Wanting’s empty clothes, reads her final letter at the cremation, rejects the returned fortune of thirty million, and accepts Little Bell’s company for a temporary departure from the city.

**Lu Wanting**  
Her residual form vanishes after the pit. The physical body is cremated with a peaceful smile that was not present when she first entered the coffin. Her farewell letter asks Shang Xuan to treat their time together as a dream and begin again.

**Little Bell**  
Hands over the recording that seals the criminals’ fate. After the cremation she waits for Shang Xuan and receives his invitation to leave with him once her exams are finished.

**Qingmu Chengzi (Orange)**  
Stands with Juexing through the final moments, then shares a playful, future-oriented conversation about Japan and the meaning of “charlatan.”

**Granny Luo Shen**  
Appears at the end with a cart, teases the couple about marriage, and leaves behind a poster that points them toward Shibuya.
