Orange rose early, washed, prepared breakfast, and after eating took her bag to the office. In the short time she had been at the vocational high school she was always the first to arrive and the last to leave. Her colleagues regarded her with quiet respect; it was obvious she was a teacher who took her duties seriously.

A courier found his way to the office through the security desk and handed her a package. She wondered who would send her something by express. The courier offered no explanation, collected her signature, and left.

Several colleagues gathered around as though something unusual had appeared. Orange smiled.

“I have no idea who sent it…”

“Maybe your boyfriend? Weekend concert or movie tickets?” a male teacher suggested.

“That’s right. Someone as pretty as Teacher Qingmu must have plenty of admirers,” a female teacher added with open envy.

Orange only kept her head down and smiled as she tore open the strip on the courier bag. Inside lay several photographs. Her expression changed at once. She still drew them out. The moment she looked, she froze.

“Huh—these look like candid shots. Who are that man and woman?” someone sharp-eyed remarked.

“Don’t talk nonsense…” Another teacher had already noticed the shift in Orange’s face. The atmosphere around the desk turned cold.

Orange clenched the photographs, brows drawn tight. Without even picking up her bag she pushed back her chair, parted the crowd, and ran out of the office.

The remaining teachers exchanged glances and reached the same conclusion: “A third party has appeared…”

Clutching the photos, Orange ran the whole way. More than a month had passed. Juexing had woken and not a single call or message had come. Now these intimate photographs with Meng Yuxun. She refused to believe he would do such a thing, yet she needed to confront him face to face.

Unfortunately, as she cut across the sports field, one person forced her to stop.

“Stop it! You’re not allowed to bully Zilan any more.”

She changed direction and ran toward them. In her haste the photographs slipped from her fingers and scattered on the ground.

Mo Zilan was crouched on the ground, arms wrapped around her head, body rocking, muttering “Go away… go away…”

“Teacher Qingmu, we really didn’t do anything to her,” the tall girl said. “We were just passing by and saw her talking to the swing. Then she suddenly turned and glared at us and said—”

She stopped. When Mo Zilan had spoken those words her eyes had been terrifying.

“Wang Mei, what exactly happened just now?” Orange asked.

“I came out of the restroom and ran into Xiao Qing and Shuangfei. The three of us were walking back to the classroom. When we passed the swings we saw Zilan standing alone beside one, talking as if someone were there, but the seat was empty. We ignored her. Then all of a sudden she turned and stared at us and said—”

Wang Mei also stalled, as though the words themselves were too frightening to repeat.

Mo Zilan leapt up as if possessed. Orange told the three girls, “Go to class,” then chased after her.

After running for some time Zilan finally collapsed beside a trash bin, curling into a ball. Orange crouched beside her.

“Whatever it is, you can tell me. I’ll help you.”

Through the tangled bangs Zilan looked up. Orange gently brushed the hair aside. The moment she saw the terrified, still-childish face, she started. The resemblance to Lin Qiaohe was far too strong.

Mo Zilan gazed at her timidly. Suddenly something behind Orange made Zilan’s expression twist with successive spasms. Orange herself felt a pair of eyes fixed on her back. She turned slowly. Nothing stood there except sunlight.

When she looked back, Zilan had fainted from fright. Orange had no choice but to carry her to her own dormitory.

---

Shang Xuan was crossing the sports field when he noticed a photograph lying on the ground. The moment he picked it up, surprise was followed by a thin smile. He slipped it into his pocket, left the school, and boarded a bus bound for the suburbs.

Because of Zilan, Orange seemed to have forgotten the photographs entirely. She washed the girl, changed her into clean pajamas, and laid her on her own bed. The previously unkempt appearance became far more comfortable under Orange’s hands.

When she checked the clock it was already noon. She decided to cook a proper meal so they could eat together; perhaps she could learn more from the girl and make her work easier.

She drew the blanket a little higher, smiled, and left the dormitory. The instant the door closed, a figure stood in the center of the room—a nearly white dress, faint scarlet seeping through the fabric, swaying like bloodstains…

---

Shang Xuan rang Juexing’s doorbell with the photograph still in his hand. The person who opened the door surprised him completely.

“Oh, it’s you. Juexing hasn’t come back from class yet. Want to come in and sit?”

Yu Xun had not expected him, but she recovered her composure at once. His arrival might even be an opportunity.

Shang Xuan remained in the doorway, photograph still visible. Yu Xun had already seen the pictures; they held no novelty for her.

“Why are you in his apartment?”

“Come in and have some tea. I’ll explain slowly.” She invited him inside.

A cup of fragrant black tea was set before him. Shang Xuan handed her the photograph.

“What exactly are you playing at?”

“Boeing 747—something big. You understand?” she quipped.

“Speak normally. I know you reporters love talking in circles.”

“I’m with Juexing now, and we’re very happy.” She smiled.

“Juexing woke up a long time ago. It’s because of you that he hasn’t contacted Orange, isn’t it?”

“Looking at it another way, you and I want the same thing. You like Orange, don’t you? We each get what we need. Isn’t that convenient?”

Yu Xun laid the scheme bare as though she were a venomous woman flourishing a poisoned blade.

Shang Xuan shook his head.

“You can’t do this. You don’t even like him. You’re only harming him.”

“Worry about yourself. I’m giving you a perfect chance and you’re still trying to look noble. While Orange and Juexing haven’t reunited yet, go tie her down. Maybe in a little while you’ll even come back to thank me.”

The words struck Shang Xuan’s weakest point. On reflection it truly was an absolute opportunity—he could win Orange’s heart with almost no effort. And yet…

Yu Xun watched his conflicted expression.

“Don’t worry. My feelings for Juexing are genuine. I’ll stay by his side from now on.”

Shang Xuan stood, walked straight to the door. Yu Xun watched him turn the handle from the living room. With this exchange she had taken a major step forward. The slight upward curve of her lips announced a quiet victory.

Shang Xuan walked through the crowded streets as though he had lost both direction and motive. Yu Xun’s words kept echoing. Although the chance was heaven-sent, he could not feel glad. From Juexing’s position, taking advantage of the situation was simply wrong.

“Ah!” He punched a utility pole. Little Bell, on her way home from school, happened to pass at that exact moment.

“Eh? What are you doing here?”

Shang Xuan came back to himself and smiled at her without speaking.

“You haven’t had lunch yet, have you? I know a new steak place nearby that’s running a promotion. Let’s eat together.” She linked her arm through his. The brightness of her smile made refusal almost impossible.

---

When Orange returned from the supermarket, Zilan was still asleep on the bed. She carried the bags into the kitchen and began cooking. The sounds and spreading aroma eventually woke the girl; her stomach growled loudly. She sat up and looked around the dormitory. Suddenly she pulled the blanket over her head in terror. A soft *ge-ge* creaking drifted down from the ceiling. Orange heard it clearly from the kitchen as well.

“You’re awake?” Orange emerged wearing an apron. The sound stopped at once. She wiped her hands, poured a glass of water, and handed it to Zilan. The girl accepted it with both hands, still trembling.

“It’s all right. I’ll finish cooking. After we eat you can sleep properly. Don’t skip afternoon classes.” Orange smiled, stroked her hair, and returned to the kitchen.

Zilan sipped the water. When she lowered her head she noticed she was wearing pajamas. She climbed out of bed, walked to the mirror, and the moment she saw her reflection she dropped the glass. It shattered on the floor.

Orange rushed out and found her standing before the mirror, touching her own face and speaking in broken fragments:

“This… this isn’t my face…”

Orange placed her hands on the girl’s shoulders.

“It might just be too much pressure. I don’t think this face is bad at all. It’s exactly as pretty as they say.”

She perhaps did not see the pure terror and deep unease in Zilan’s eyes. This was a face that did not belong to her—the face of someone who had already become part of the school’s history. When that presence had climbed onto her body, even Zilan herself did not know.

Orange guided her to the table, then brought out the fragrant dishes from the kitchen.

She filled a bowl of rice, handed Zilan a pair of chopsticks, and said, “Eat first. Afterward sleep well. Everything will be all right.”

“Is that really true, Teacher?”

“Of course. As long as you face things with a smile. And I promise Xiangfei and the others won’t bully you again.” Orange placed a slice of meat in Zilan’s bowl.

Zilan nodded and began to eat. The person beside her felt like a savior. That bright smile gradually infected her. Was this the thing her mother had once called “hope”?

### Character Notes

**Qingmu Chengzi (Orange)**  
The anonymous photographs strike at the exact point of her greatest vulnerability—Juexing’s silence since waking. Yet her instinct to protect Mo Zilan overrides personal shock, reinforcing her role as the one who stands between the living and the unresolved dead.

**Mo Zilan**  
The physical resemblance to Lin Qiaohe is no longer subtle. Her panic before the mirror (“This isn’t my face”) and the creaking from the ceiling strongly suggest progressive possession or spiritual overlay. The fragile hope she begins to feel in Orange’s presence may be the only remaining anchor keeping her identity intact.

**Shang Xuan**  
Refuses Yu Xun’s cynical bargain even though it would clear the path to Orange. The internal conflict is genuine: opportunity versus principle. His decision not to exploit the situation marks a quiet moral stand.

**Yu Xun**  
Openly frames the arrangement as mutually beneficial and claims genuine feeling for Juexing. The smile she wears after Shang Xuan leaves shows she believes she has secured her position.

**Lin Qiaohe’s Residual Presence**  
Appears more boldly—descending hair, creaking ceiling, the white dress stained with scarlet, and the near-transparent feet of earlier chapters. The school’s unresolved bullying death is no longer content to remain in the past.

**The Photographs**  
An external catalyst. Whoever sent them (and Shang Xuan’s private smile upon finding one) introduces a new layer of manipulation beyond Yu Xun’s own schemes.
