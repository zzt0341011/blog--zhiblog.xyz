“Juexing!”

Orange picked up the slip of paper. On it was written a single sentence:

**Thank you, Teacher Qingmu!**

She smiled and turned—only to see Juexing standing motionless in front of the mirror. His behavior was strange enough to frighten her. She started forward to pull him away, but he waved her back without looking, signaling her not to approach.

The mirror reflected countless faces of Juexing, each wearing a slightly different expression—conflicted, sorrowful, joyful, melancholy…

He slowly raised a hand and brushed it across the surface. A careless movement—his ring finger was sliced by a sharp edge of glass. Blood soaked into the mirror and began to flow along the cracks, growing more vivid with every second.

Juexing put the finger in his mouth, still staring. The mirror seemed to go mad. A sound like shattering glass erupted from it. He staggered backward, shielding Orange with his body. Suddenly a figure burst out of the glass and slammed into him with crushing force, hurling him across the room.

Orange ran to his side and helped him up. When she looked back at the mirror it stood perfectly still again—except that one new face now stared out from the glass. Whether it belonged to Qiaohe or to Zilan, she could no longer tell.

“Are you all right?”

“I’m fine. We have to find Zilan right away. Her next target is probably Shuangfei.” He rubbed his chest. He now had a clear picture of what had happened in this dormitory two years earlier.

“What did you see inside the mirror?” Orange asked urgently.

Juexing glanced at the darkening sky. Night had already fallen, yet the curse still had to be broken.

“We need to split up. Can you manage?” He held her arms, reluctance clear in his voice.

“Yes. What do you need me to do?”

“First find the teacher from Academic Affairs. Give him the files on both Qiaohe and Zilan. Make him write down everything that happened to Qiaohe two years ago. Then take those records and wait for me outside Shuangfei’s house.”

“Are you sure he’ll tell the truth?”

“This is serious. He’ll understand the weight of it. I’m going after Shuangfei now. Remember—get the Academic Affairs teacher first, then the files.” With that he left the school in a hurry.

Orange stood alone on the empty sports field for a moment, then dialed Teacher Li from Academic Affairs. He arrived by car shortly afterward.

---

Through an informant Jiaye located the residence of Shuangfei’s boyfriend, Chen Dong. Chen was a second-generation rich kid; his family had bought him a mansion in the city. A building that conspicuous was easy to find.

Jiaye guessed Shuangfei would be staying there, though what they were doing was anyone’s guess. He and Yu Xun had just arrived when Juexing caught up with them.

The entire mansion blazed with light like a small palace, yet not a single person was visible. Only faint laughter drifted down from one of the second-floor rooms.

“Did you hear that?” Jiaye whispered from beneath a palm tree, glancing upward.

Yu Xun fixed him with an icy stare that made him distinctly uncomfortable.

“What do you think? You’re a police officer. Do we really have to sneak around like thieves?”

“Keep your voice down. Juexing said not to tip them off…” He lowered his tone even further.

Yu Xun’s temper flared. She had just begun to snap “You—” when a sharp *pop* sounded. The circuit breaker tripped. Every light in the mansion died, plunging the grounds into darkness.

“It’s starting!” Jiaye pressed Yu Xun down. He could feel her trembling.

He could not see Juexing, but he was certain the cursebreaker had already gone inside.

“What do we do now?” Yu Xun whispered.

“We go in and look. My colleagues are standing by nearby. Don’t worry.”

They crouched low, circled to the main entrance, and found the door slightly ajar—someone had entered only moments earlier.

Jiaye carefully pushed it open with his left foot and drew Yu Xun inside.

The interior was not completely dark; several candles still burned. Jiaye covered his mouth to keep from laughing. In his experience, lighting candles at a moment like this meant only two possibilities: either the person was not right in the head, or…

They both froze. Footsteps were descending the stairs—one slow step after another, unnervingly loud in the silence.

“Must be on the second floor,” Yu Xun said.

“That doesn’t make sense. If the lights went out they should have made some noise.”

“Unless…” An ominous premonition crawled across both their foreheads. They swallowed at the same time. Beside the candles on a side table a new shadow had appeared—long hair, unmistakably a girl’s.

Upstairs the scene was completely different. Chen Dong was still showering, unaware of anything outside because the bathroom light remained on. Shuangfei sat with earphones in, a facial mask on, eyes closed, equally oblivious.

The bedroom door opened. Darkness flooded the room. Shuangfei felt someone pinning her feet. She opened her eyes and found a long-haired girl sitting astride her. She tried to scream; a hand clamped over her mouth.

She struggled, yet all strength seemed to have drained away. Only rapid breathing and a pair of terror-filled eyes remained.

“Do you still remember me?” The voice was soft, yet it sent a chill from Shuangfei’s ears straight to the soles of her feet.

She shook her head frantically—then, when she clearly saw the face above her, she nodded just as frantically.

“Good. I thought you might have forgotten. After all this time, remembering is a good thing.”

Chen Dong heard voices outside and assumed Shuangfei was muttering to herself; he paid little attention.

The girl gave a cold laugh and traced her right hand back and forth across Shuangfei’s face.

“What expression do you think your boyfriend will wear when he sees you like this?”

A mirror appeared in front of her. In the glass Shuangfei saw a face already carved into chaos—the same terrified eyes staring back at her.

She shook her head desperately and squeezed her eyes shut. The girl laughed again.

“Bullying me was one thing. Why did you have to disfigure my face? Did my appearance offend you? I’ll tell you this much—Wang Mei is already gone. You’re next. Xiao Qing will be soon after.”

She raised the mirror and smashed it down on Shuangfei’s head. At that exact moment Chen Dong, wrapped only in a towel, stepped out of the bathroom. The instant the door opened, every remaining light died.

“Help me—” The three words finally escaped Shuangfei’s lips. Chen Dong panicked, slamming the light switches, but nothing responded.

“It’s useless. No one can save you.” The girl held a jagged shard of mirror and slowly brought it toward Shuangfei’s face.

“No—!”

A flash of cyan light struck the shard from her hand. Juexing lunged forward and shoved Shuangfei to the floor. The girl snatched up another piece of glass and drove it toward his throat.

Instinctively he caught both of her wrists. Her strength far exceeded what he could withstand. The sharp edge was about to pierce his neck—

*Click.*

Someone somewhere restored the circuit breaker. The girl’s strength vanished. She collapsed onto Juexing’s chest.

Jiaye and Yu Xun reached the second floor to find Chen Dong, still in his towel, shielding the curled-up Shuangfei in a corner, and Mo Zilan lying motionless across Juexing.

Juexing removed the remaining glass from her hand, wiped the blood from his palm with a tissue, and carried her downstairs.

After a brief explanation to Chen Dong, Jiaye left the mansion with the others.

---

Orange had been standing outside Shuangfei’s family home with the files for forty minutes. The moment she saw Juexing step out of the car she ran to him.

“How is Zilan?”

“A little later and it would have been too late.” He managed a small smile.

“What do we do next?”

“She’ll have to stay with you for now. She can’t go back to the dormitory.”

“All right. Let’s take her to my place. Here are the records you asked for—everything’s here.”

“I may have to spend the night at your place again.”

“Of course. You can’t leave me alone with her.” She smiled.

---

Back at Orange’s apartment she cleaned and bandaged Zilan’s wounds, settled her in her own bed, then sat down beside Juexing so as not to disturb his concentration on the files.

She leaned against him. Her heavy eyelids were already drooping. Juexing said quietly, “Go to sleep. I’ll take the sofa. It’s fine.”

“No. I want to stay with you. Wherever you sleep, I sleep.” Her eyes were half-closed.

He smiled, stroked her hair, and let her rest her head on his lap. Still drowsy, she murmured, “Summer vacation is almost here. I want to go back to Japan for a while. Come with me?”

“Ah?”

“What? Taking your boyfriend home is perfectly normal. Are you shy?” She pinched his nose lightly. The happy look on her face eased something tight in his chest.

“When this is over, I’ll go with you. All right?”

Orange nodded and closed her eyes, slipping into sleep. Juexing continued turning the pages. Each line felt like watching a tragic film. He sank deeper into the turbulent story—blink after blink…

Everyone possesses jealousy, because everyone is selfish. Behind that selfishness no one is ever allowed to surpass them. Whoever touches that boundary, whoever crosses that gulf, will fall headlong with no way back. Whether the landing is hell or heaven, who can say? When she reached the end of every road and every hope, she could only make the worst possible choice…

### Character Notes

**Luo Juexing**  
The mirror attack confirms the residual spirit can now physically emerge from reflective surfaces. His cyan light intervention saves Shuangfei at the last possible moment, yet the near-overpowering strength of the possessed Zilan shows how dangerous the entity has become.

**Qingmu Chengzi (Orange)**  
Completes her assigned task with the Academic Affairs records and immediately prioritizes Zilan’s safety. The soft domestic scene that follows—her refusal to sleep apart, the casual invitation to Japan—reveals how completely she has chosen to stand beside him.

**Mo Zilan / Lin Qiaohe**  
The boundary between the living girl and the residual spirit continues to blur. The attack on Shuangfei is carried out through Zilan’s body, yet the voice and the knowledge belong to Qiaohe. The thank-you note left for Orange remains one of the few clear signs that Zilan’s own consciousness is still present.

**Lu Shuangfei**  
The second near-victim. The forced confrontation with her own disfigured reflection and the explicit threat against Xiao Qing establish the order of the remaining targets.

**Huang Jiaye & Yu Xun**  
Provide the external perimeter and the power restoration that breaks the spirit’s hold at the critical second. Their presence keeps the official world overlapping with the supernatural one.

**The Cracked Mirror**  
No longer a passive object. It multiplies faces, draws blood, and expels a physical entity. It has become both weapon and gateway.
