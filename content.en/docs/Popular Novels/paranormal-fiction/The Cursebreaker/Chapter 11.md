By the time Orange reached home it was already past ten. She tossed her bag onto the sofa, went into the kitchen, and took out two packets of instant noodles. The hamburger from earlier had long since vanished into hunger.

Watching the water begin to bubble, she remembered cooking in Juexing’s kitchen. That moment had felt like happiness—the smile on her face had been proof enough. Happiness, however, arrived quickly and left even faster. She wondered what he was truly afraid of, or what he was running from. If he felt nothing for her, he should have refused everything from the very beginning.

The water boiled over, lively as a spirit pushing at the lid. She lifted it, a cloud of steam rising, dropped in the noodles, and covered the pot again. Five minutes later she carried a fragrant bowl to the television, switched it on, and stared at whatever program happened to be playing. The first image was a news segment about a newly opened express train.

After seeing Little Bell off, Juexing was about to close his door when Jiaye appeared carrying a full case of beer. One foot shot out and blocked the doorway.

“You don’t look so great yourself,” Juexing remarked dryly.

“Do you think people in power just have ‘luck’ stuffed inside their heads?” Jiaye dropped onto the sofa, cracked a can open with a sharp *pop*, and tossed one to Juexing. Juexing caught it cleanly, took a long swallow, then stood by the window watching the night deepen.

“Why so late? It’s already eleven.”

“Could I have come earlier? You were already booked. I had to sneak in.” Jiaye’s laugh was cold.

Juexing managed a thin smile and fell silent. Jiaye kept drinking as though every bottle in the world had personally offended him.

“Slow down or you won’t make it home.”

Jiaye exhaled a cloud of alcohol, tipped sideways onto the cushions, and muttered, “Eight or nine things out of ten in life never go the way you want…” Then he began to snore.

Juexing gave a quiet snort, gently removed the half-empty can from Jiaye’s hand, set it on the table, fetched a blanket from the bedroom, and covered him. He went into the bathroom to shower.

Warm steam slowly fogged the large mirror. Juexing stood under the spray, letting his body relax, then wiped a clear patch on the glass. Without warning a train streaked across the mirror’s surface. He stumbled backward several steps. When he looked again, only his own startled reflection remained.

He rubbed his temples, shut off the water, wrapped a towel around himself, and changed into pajamas in the bedroom. As he turned toward the desk, a slip of paper that looked like a ticket lay pinned under the lamp. He picked it up.

It was a trial-ride ticket for the newly opened express train, valid from the 10th to the 12th of the current month.

“That’s tomorrow through the day after…”

On the reverse side a brand-new train was printed; the service number stood out clearly: 5120.

Juexing slipped the ticket into a drawer, closed the bedroom door, switched off the light, and lay down.

He had no clear memory of when Jiaye left. He did remember the formal invitation that had arrived earlier. With no laboratory class scheduled, a proper meal would do no harm. After washing up he dug through the wardrobe, selected a casual outfit, put on a pair of non-prescription glasses, struck a few poses in the mirror, and decided it was good enough.

He rode the elevator to the lobby, stepped outside, flagged a taxi, and headed for the address on the invitation. He still had half an hour to spare.

After class Orange deliberately wandered near the laboratory building, but Juexing was nowhere in sight. She was about to leave when Director Wang emerged from his office.

“Well, if it isn’t Teacher Qingmu. Looking for someone?” He adjusted his glasses and gave her a longer look. Flawless beauty, as always.

“I was just passing by. Good afternoon, Director Wang.”

“Care to come in for tea?”

“No need. I’m finished for the day—time to go home and cook.” She left the corridor quickly and only exhaled once she reached the school gate. Less than a minute later her phone rang. The number belonged to an old classmate.

“Orange? It’s Song Xi. Remember me?”

“Yes, it’s me. What’s up?”

“Today’s my birthday. We haven’t seen each other in a while and I miss you. Come to the party.”

“Oh—congratulations.”

“You’re free this afternoon, right? I’ll pick you up downstairs around five. Don’t be late.” He hung up before she could answer, leaving only the dial tone.

Orange slipped the phone into her bag and started toward her apartment. Perhaps it would be a chance to meet new people. She smiled and decided to buy a nice outfit first.

Nearly an hour later in the department store she glanced at the large display screen in the atrium. The red digits read 12:30. Only then did she realize she had forgotten lunch entirely—shopping had been too enjoyable. Carrying her bags with cheerful satisfaction, she left the mall. Tonight she would not cook; she would find a quiet, comfortable place and eat a proper meal.

The street was crowded. Floating scents of every kind of food tempted her empty stomach. While she was still deciding where to go, her eyes happened to pass through a floor-to-ceiling window and landed on Juexing, Meng Yuxun, and a middle-aged man with glasses and a thinning hairline sharing a table. The sign above the entrance made it clear: only the wealthy or well-connected dined here.

Orange’s arms dropped to her sides. A heavy feeling settled over her. *Does he like Reporter Meng? No wonder. She can eat in a place like this, while all I can offer him is home-style cooking.*

Suddenly Juexing turned his head in her direction. She quickly raised the shopping bags to shield her face and hurried past, weaving left and right so he would not see her.

“Something wrong with the food?” Yuxun asked.

“Nothing…” Juexing shook his head.

“Teacher Luo, I heard about the city-wide sensation you caused five years ago. That’s why I’m asking—no, begging—you this time.” The middle-aged man set down his knife and fork and clasped Juexing’s hand with sincere intensity.

“I’ll find a time to take a look.” Juexing nodded and gently withdrew his hand.

“My card. The address is on it.” The man offered the card with both hands. Juexing accepted it politely. The three continued their conversation over excellent seafood and steak.

Orange walked on with a pout. The small improvement in her mood had been ruined by the scene that looked far too intimate.

“Why do you still care so much?” she muttered to herself.

A high-school girl trailed behind her, a lollipop stick between her teeth. Orange glanced left and right, then entered a noodle shop on the right. The girl followed and sat down directly across from her.

Orange did not notice at first; she was staring at the vinegar bottle on the table.

“What’s wrong? You look upset.”

Orange looked up and jerked backward in surprise.

“What? Do I look like a monster? You’re that scared?” The girl laughed.

“No… why are you here too?”

“Don’t tell me you forgot you invited me for noodles, Teacher Orange.” She flicked the plastic stick into the trash bin and rested her chin in both hands, watching her.

“I still don’t know your name.”

“Right. Same as Teacher Luo—just call me Little Bell. It’s friendlier.”

Two steaming bowls arrived. Little Bell seized chopsticks and spoon and attacked the noodles with both hands, clearly starving.

“Slow down. If it’s not enough we can order another bowl.”

Little Bell, cheeks full of noodles, looked up. The simple offer warmed something inside her.

“Heh. What is it?” Orange asked, noticing the odd expression.

Little Bell only shook her head and kept eating. Orange had almost no appetite, but pure hunger forced her to manage a few bites and some broth.

“Actually, Teacher Luo is a really good person. You should give him a little time.” Little Bell wiped her mouth with a napkin.

Where had she heard those words before? Orange set down her chopsticks and sighed.

“Maybe. Maybe if I just give up, I won’t have to work so hard.”

“Have you laid your cards on the table with him?”

“Laid my cards…?”

“So you haven’t. Once you make your position clear, you’ll know what to do. Anyway—thanks for the noodles. I’m heading out.” Little Bell shouldered her schoolbag, nodded politely, and left. Orange paid at the counter and walked home.

She dropped the two new outfits at the dry cleaner’s; they were ready by half past four. In front of the mirror she tried both sets repeatedly. A birthday party probably called for an evening dress, but she lacked the taste for one. In the end she chose the new purple dress and a pair of heels.

Song Xi arrived downstairs exactly on time, stepped out of the car, and rang her phone.

The moment Orange appeared, the pure, almost otherworldly quality of her presence sent Song Xi’s imagination racing. He collected himself, opened the rear door, and made a polite “after you” gesture.

Under a pine tree a short distance away, Shang Xuan stood chewing gum. In his left hand he held a trial ticket for the express train. He flicked the ticket lightly with one finger, turned, and walked away.

### Character Notes

**Luo Juexing**  
The train that flashes across the bathroom mirror and the trial ticket that appears under his lamp mark the start of a new, still-unexplained thread. He keeps the appointment with Yuxun’s editor, remaining polite but non-committal.

**Qingmu Chengzi (Orange)**  
Caught between lingering feelings for Juexing and the practical need to move forward. The glimpse of him at the luxury restaurant wounds her pride; Little Bell’s advice and Song Xi’s invitation offer temporary distraction.

**Shang Xuan**  
Appears once more at a pivotal moment, this time holding an identical trial ticket for train 5120. His silent observation and casual departure suggest he is several steps ahead of everyone else.

**Little Bell**  
Continues to surface at emotionally charged moments. She offers Orange surprisingly mature counsel about Juexing, then vanishes as quickly as she arrived. Her true connection to the unfolding events remains hidden.

**Meng Yuxun**  
Uses the high-end lunch to broker a meeting between Juexing and her editor-in-chief. Her presence at the table deepens Orange’s sense of inferiority.

**Song Xi**  
An old classmate whose birthday invitation pulls Orange into a social evening she did not plan. His reaction to her appearance hints at lingering interest.

**Huang Jiaye**  
Drowns professional frustration in beer and passes out on Juexing’s sofa, a brief comic counterpoint to the growing tension.
