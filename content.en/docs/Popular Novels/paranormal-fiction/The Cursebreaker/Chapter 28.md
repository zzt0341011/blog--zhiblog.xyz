Around two in the afternoon Jiaye received an emergency call. A death had occurred in an apartment in the city center. The forensic team was already on site. He set aside the files in front of him, gathered a few colleagues, and headed straight for the scene.

The room was decorated in a soft, childish style: Winnie-the-Pooh curtains, two small cacti leaning against each other on the windowsill, and walls covered with posters of celebrities—Horikita Maki, Kuraki Mai, Yamashita Tomohisa, Takeshi Kaneshiro…

It was a warm, carefully kept space. In life the girl had been an excellent student. Somehow she had been drawn into a conspiracy no one knew about. That unknown entanglement might well be connected to the way she died.

Her parents had already cried through the entire morning and were no longer able to speak coherently with the police. The moment Jiaye arrived, the forensic doctor approached and delivered the findings in a professional tone:

“She is deceased. Time of death approximately eleven o’clock last night. Preliminary conclusion: arterial severance by glass shards leading to massive blood loss…”

“Suicide?” Jiaye asked, doubtful.

The doctor nodded, then added, “But the manner of death is unusual.”

Jiaye walked to the body and lifted a corner of the white sheet. The girl’s face had been sliced into unrecognizable ruin by broken mirror glass—completely disfigured. Scattered fragments on the floor still reflected the shifting figures of the living.

He replaced the sheet. As he turned away, a cold hand suddenly closed around his wrist. A wave of chill shot straight to his brain. When he looked down there was nothing—yet a small slip of paper now rested in his palm. He turned his back to the others and unfolded it. Two characters were written there:

**Coincidence.**

He understood at once. This was no ordinary suicide.

“Wrap it up,” he ordered. He directed his colleagues to remove the body, then walked over to the two parents.

“Rest assured. I will get justice for your daughter.”

The elderly couple stared at him with empty eyes. Everyone was already gone. What use was justice now?

If Jiaye did not pursue the truth, the next victims would simply arrive one after another.

He dialed Juexing. At that moment Juexing and Orange were already on their way to the hospital.

When Orange saw Wang Mei lying in the morgue, an unprecedented wave of unease surged from her chest into her mind. Fragment after fragment flashed past: the office, the files, Zilan, the swings, the toilet…

Like a player stuck on repeat, the images kept looping. Dizziness overtook her. Juexing quickly steadied her.

“Are you feeling unwell?”

“How could this happen? She was fine… why would she…” Orange could not imagine what would drive Wang Mei to suicide—or to destroy her own face so thoroughly.

“What did the forensic report say?” Juexing asked.

“Time of death around eleven last night. Clear signs of violent struggle before death. Suspected self-inflicted arterial cuts with mirror glass leading to fatal blood loss.” Jiaye handed over the report.

Juexing flipped through several pages. Nothing seemed out of place—except the phrase “violent struggle,” which held his attention.

“It may not be that simple.”

“There’s also this.” Jiaye remembered the note that had appeared in his hand just before he left the scene and passed it over.

“Coincidence?” Juexing murmured. The soft sound was still loud enough for Orange to hear.

“Qiaohe?” she said suddenly.

“Is something wrong?”

“Qiaohe was also a student at the vocational high school. She committed suicide there two years ago. Everyone at the school seems to avoid the subject. I only found out by accident when I saw her file—right before I met Mo Zilan.”

“I’ve heard a little about the deaths at that school,” Jiaye said. “At the time the forensic examination found nothing unusual about the cause of death. But the condition of the body…” He stopped short, eyes widening. “The facial injuries were exactly the same. The doctor concluded those wounds had been inflicted by someone else.”

“In other words, before Qiaohe died, someone disfigured her face,” Juexing said.

“Yes. That’s how it looks.”

“Then isn’t this another curse?” Orange asked.

“From the current clues, roughly a fifty-percent chance.”

“What do we do? I remember Xiao Qing and Shuangfei were always with Wang Mei. If this really is a curse, they’re in danger.”

“Why are you so sure it’s them and not someone else?”

“Because I noticed that Zilan’s face looks very much like the dead girl Qiaohe—and those three were always bullying her…” Orange’s breathing grew short. She stepped back and braced herself against the morgue door.

Juexing considered for a moment.

“We should go back and talk to the student named Zilan first. Xiao Qing and Shuangfei can be tracked by Jiaye.”

“No problem. I’ll leave right away.” Jiaye clapped Juexing on the shoulder and walked out.

Juexing guided Orange to a chair in the corridor so she could steady herself.

“I’ll get you a carton of milk from the vending machine.”

She nodded.

After he left, the corridor fell completely silent—not even a whisper of wind. The unease in Orange’s chest had just begun to ease when a long, stretched shadow caught her attention.

She turned slightly toward a glass door a short distance away. At first the glass was only fogged. Then the fog was slowly cleared from within, revealing a figure—small in stature, clearly a girl.

The facial outline sharpened from blur to clarity. Orange stared. It was a pretty face. The owner of that face was named Zhao Xiaoqing.

She smiled at Orange, then slowly raised a hand. Long nails sank deep into her own skin and began to drag downward until blood splattered across the glass…

A cold laugh rang out. Orange’s legs gave way and she collapsed to the floor. Juexing returned with the milk, saw her sitting there, and followed her gaze. Apart from the still-fogged glass door, nothing was visible.

“It’s all right. Only a hallucination.” He helped her up carefully and handed her the milk.

“It wasn’t a hallucination. Just now, outside that door, I saw Xiao Qing. She was the same as Wang Mei…” Orange could not finish the sentence. She was terrified that speaking the words would make them real.

“I’ll call Jiaye and ask about their situation.” He dialed at once. Jiaye reported that Zhao Xiaoqing was at home online and showed no abnormality for the moment; he was already on his way to Lu Shuangfei’s house.

“They should be safe for now. Let’s go back to the school and talk to Zilan.”

Orange finished the milk, steadied her breathing, and followed Juexing out of the hospital.

Jiaye located Lu Shuangfei’s address. Her mother told him the girl had gone out with her boyfriend and would return late; she did not say where. Standing in the doorway, Jiaye had just found the phone number when Yu Xun called.

“I heard something strange happened. I’m downstairs at your place right now. Come pick me up.” She hung up without waiting for a reply. Jiaye pocketed the phone and turned toward the stairs. At the far end of the corridor a figure stood watching his retreating back with open contempt.

---

When Orange and Juexing returned to the school, Zilan was no longer in the office. According to the teacher on duty she had woken, then left—presumably for the dormitory.

“Let’s check the dorm,” Orange said.

Juexing nodded and followed. The girls’ dormitory had only two floors; most students lived in the city. Zilan appeared to be an orphan, so the school had given her a relatively comfortable single room. Orange still knew very little about her. Although she had tried hard to help, the girl’s defenses were deep and hard to breach. Just when they had begun to draw closer, this new crisis had erupted.

Walking behind Orange, Juexing could read from her posture how seriously she took the matter—or rather, how seriously she took this student named Zilan. Perhaps she had simply been born with that kind of kindness. No matter what obstacles appeared, she always stepped forward first.

He reached out and took her hand. Warmth flowed from his palm straight into her heart.

“What’s wrong?” she asked with a small smile.

“Nothing. Just wanted to tell you you’re not fighting alone. You have me, and Jiaye, and the others.”

Orange tightened her fingers around his. “We’re here. The front room is hers.”

She knocked three times. No response came from inside. The elderly floor supervisor shuffled over, smiling.

“Teacher Qingmu, Zilan hasn’t come back since last night.”

“Could you open the door for us? We’d like to take a look.” Juexing felt certain something was hidden in the room; leaving without checking might mean missing a vital clue.

Orange understood and added, “Yes, please open it for us.”

The supervisor returned to her own room, fetched a ring of keys, found the correct one, and unlocked the door.

Juexing entered first. The room was neatly kept; Zilan was clearly a clean and orderly girl. Orange walked to the bed and saw the pajamas folded carefully on top of the covers. Beneath them lay a slip of paper.

Juexing scanned the room and found nothing obviously wrong—until a poster on the wall caught his attention. A breeze slipped through the open window without permission and lifted one corner of the poster. Light leaked from behind it. He reached out, tore the poster down, and revealed a mirror hanging on the wall—more precisely, a mirror that had been broken and painstakingly glued back together, its surface covered in cracks.

The mirror seemed to possess a magnetic power of its own. It drew Juexing’s gaze deeper and deeper, pulling him step by step closer.

### Character Notes

**Wang Mei**  
The second confirmed victim. Her death by self-inflicted mirror cuts, the disfigurement, and the note reading “Coincidence” mirror the earlier fate of Lin Qiaohe and establish a clear pattern of revenge focused on facial destruction.

**Lin Qiaohe**  
The original victim from two years earlier. The identical method of facial injury (then inflicted by others, now self-inflicted under supernatural compulsion) confirms that the residual resentment has evolved and is actively targeting the former bullies.

**Mo Zilan**  
Still missing from both office and dormitory. The neatly folded pajamas and the hidden cracked mirror in her room strongly suggest the residual presence is using her living space—and possibly her body—as an anchor.

**Luo Juexing**  
Immediately recognizes the supernatural signature in the “violent struggle” and the note. His decision to prioritize speaking with Zilan shows he understands that the living girl who resembles the dead one is the current focal point of the curse.

**Qingmu Chengzi (Orange)**  
The vision of Zhao Xiaoqing clawing her own face in the hospital corridor marks her increasing sensitivity to the residual presence. Her physical reaction and the fear that speaking the vision will make it real reveal how deeply the school’s unfinished business has already affected her.

**Huang Jiaye**  
Receives the impossible note in the presence of the body and correctly interprets it as a message from the other side. His promise to the grieving parents and his rapid deployment to track the remaining two girls keep the official investigation aligned with the supernatural one.

**The Cracked Mirror**  
The central object of the new arc. Hidden behind a poster in Mo Zilan’s room, already repaired yet still fractured, it exerts a visible pull on Juexing—signaling that the next confrontation will take place through reflection.
