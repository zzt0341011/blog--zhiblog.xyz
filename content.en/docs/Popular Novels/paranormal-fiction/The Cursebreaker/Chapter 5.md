The rich scent of milk filled the living room. Orange sat in front of her computer and carefully typed the URL from the slip of paper. A password dialog appeared. She hesitated, the warning echoing in her mind: *Getting mixed up in these strange things won’t do you any good. Weigh it carefully. Don’t throw your life away.*

“What kind of person is he really?” she murmured. “An ordinary man… or someone like the special people in movies, with abilities no one else has?”

She pressed Enter.

The screen went black for a moment, then returned. A search bar waited. She typed three characters: Luo Juexing. What appeared was everything the library had hidden—detailed information about the secretive profession known as cursebreaker.

---

The alarm rang twice before Orange jolted awake. She grabbed the clock on the nightstand.

“Not good—I’m late!”

She scrambled out of bed and into the bathroom. At the breakfast cart she bought soy milk and a cake, then sprinted toward the school. Today was the final day of the cultural festival; every advisor would be collecting projects.

Rushing with her head down, she failed to see the high-school girl standing at the corner. They collided with a sharp *smack*. Soy milk splattered across the ground, filling the air with the smell of peanuts.

“Clumsy! Didn’t you see me standing right here?” the girl said haughtily. As she lifted her face, Orange froze. The resemblance was uncanny.

“Sister Li Mei…?”

She could not believe her eyes. Two people so similar should not exist.

“Sorry—did I hurt you?” Orange brushed soy milk from her skirt. The palm of her left hand was scraped.

“I’m fine. But your skirt and your breakfast…”

“It’s nothing. I’ll deal with it at home. I have to go.” Orange gave a quick smile and continued running.

The girl looked down at the spilled soy milk. A reflection appeared in the puddle.

“You’re here again?” she asked.

“This is for you. Bring the jacket back next time. Stop wasting the family’s money.” A young man handed her an envelope, mounted a folding bicycle, and rode off humming.

Inside the envelope was a single photograph. The background looked like a rural village. In the center stood a stone statue of a monster covered in scales, with hair like coiling snakes, wild-boar tusks, iron hands, and a pair of wings.

She studied the image for a long time, found nothing useful, returned it to the envelope, and continued toward school.

---

Orange arrived, swallowed the cake in a few bites, drank a cup of warm water, and entered the classroom. Most of the students were already there. As usual she began with roll call.

“Cheng Shaoxi…”

No answer. She raised her voice and repeated the name. Still silence.

Probably delayed by something, she told herself, and moved on. Of the thirty students, only Cheng Shaoxi was missing.

“All right. Please hand in your cultural-festival projects in order, then we’ll begin.”

Halfway through the lesson her phone rang inside her bag. She told the class to discuss among themselves and stepped into the corridor. The number belonged to a landline. After a brief hesitation she answered.

“Is this Teacher Qingmu? I’m Cheng Shaoxi’s mother. He’s not feeling well today, so I’d like to request leave for him. Is that all right?”

The woman’s voice trembled.

“Is it serious?”

A sharp crash of breaking glass came through the line.

“Just a small cold. I’ll take him to the doctor.”

A sudden *zzz-zzz* static rose, growing louder until Orange had to pull the phone away. When she listened again, only a busy tone remained.

She stared at the screen—and her heart lurched. The wallpaper had changed to an image she had never seen. The phone slipped from her fingers and hit the floor. By the time she calmed enough to pick it up, everything had returned to normal.

The dismissal bell rang. She walked back into the classroom, dismissed the students, and sat staring at the phone. Then a terrible thought struck her. Fear flooded her eyes.

---

From the students she obtained Cheng Shaoxi’s home address and took a taxi at once. The house was a two-story villa standing among mango trees, giving the courtyard a quiet, pastoral feeling.

“Who are you looking for?” an elderly woman asked from the gate.

“I’m Qingmu Chengzi, Cheng Shaoxi’s tourism teacher. I heard he’s ill, so I brought some fruit.”

The old woman lowered her head and said nothing. After a minute a slightly breathless woman—clearly Shaoxi’s mother—emerged.

“Teacher Qingmu, please come in.” Her hand shook violently as she opened the gate.

“Thank you. Is Shaoxi resting inside?”

“Yes. I’m sorry you had to come all this way.” She led Orange into the living room and quietly signaled the old woman upstairs.

The house was simple and bookish. A goldfish tank and a pot of evergreen added warmth. Orange found herself envying such a peaceful home.

“Please sit. What would you like to drink?”

“Anything is fine.” Orange set the fruit down. Almost at once a crash and muffled shouting came from the second floor:

“All of you… turn to stone…!”

Followed by strange, broken laughter.

Orange looked toward the stairs. A black shadow flashed past and vanished. Shaoxi’s mother stood abruptly, panic plain on her face.

“Is something wrong?”

Without answering, the mother ran upstairs. Orange followed. Shaoxi’s door stood open. The room faced away from the light; heavy curtains made it almost dark. In the corner the boy crouched, clutching something, trembling violently.

“Xixi, it’s Mom. Don’t be afraid!”

He pointed at her as if she were a stranger.

“Stay back or I’ll turn you to stone too!”

She froze. Orange stood in the doorway. The entire room seemed wrapped in invisible wind and sand; faint sounds of grit shifting filled the air.

The door behind Orange slammed shut with a heavy *bang*. Shaoxi’s mother collapsed to the floor. In the corner a blurred shadow stretched behind the boy, swaying like forked snake tongues.

Orange barely dared to breathe. She stepped backward, mind blank.

The room began to shake violently. Shaoxi let out a piercing scream. His face twisted, eyes rolled white, and he curled tightly before falling still. In his clenched hand was a crumpled photograph—its image already blurred beyond recognition. Moments later the room grew quiet again.

“Xixi…!”

His mother’s heart-rending cry filled the house. The instant her hand touched her son’s body, she felt something hard as stone and fainted.

Sirens of ambulance and police cars soon wailed outside the mango villa. Medical staff and officers arrived after Orange’s call. Shaoxi’s mother remained unconscious from shock; his father was racing back from another province.

“How could this happen?” Orange finished giving her statement and stepped out of the police station, still unaware that a curse had already claimed its next victim.

“Hey, beautiful teacher—what are you doing here?”

Huang Jiaye had spotted her.

“My student… something happened.” Her thoughts were too chaotic to explain.

“The mother and son who just left for the hospital?”

She nodded, face ashen. Jiaye looked concerned.

“Are you all right? Want me to take you home?”

She shook her head. He could only watch as she started to walk away.

Suddenly she turned.

“Do you know where Juexing lives?”

“I’ll write the address for you.” He fetched paper and pen from the duty room and handed her the slip.

“Thank you.” She accepted it with both hands and bowed deeply.

“You’re sure you don’t need a ride?”

She shook her head again, flagged a passing taxi, waved once, and left.

---

In the elevator Orange stared at the address, anxiety tightening her chest. Ever since she had witnessed the curse-breaking, strange events had followed one after another. Sometimes she even questioned everything she had learned as a psychologist.

The elevator chimed. She stepped out, found the correct door, and raised her hand to ring the bell.

The door opened before she could touch it. Two people stared at her in surprise.

“Sister Li Mei…” a young girl said.

Juexing gave the girl a gentle push. “She isn’t Li Mei. Go home.”

“Oh—right. He asked me to give you this.” The girl almost forgot. Juexing urged her to leave. As she passed Orange she smiled and whispered:

“Getting more interesting.”

Orange remained frozen in the doorway. Juexing glanced at the clock—already half past ten.

“How did you find this place? It’s cold outside. Come in.”

He stepped aside and closed the door behind her.

“Something to drink?”

She shook her head, still dazed.

“Are you all right?”

“I saw something I still can’t believe…”

A sudden draft swept the letter from the table. The photograph inside slid out onto the floor. Orange stared at the half-visible image and went rigid. The blurred background looked painfully familiar.

Juexing bent, picked up the photo, and the moment he saw it his expression hardened.

“Has he been cursed?” Orange asked in a terrified whisper.

The photograph felt heavy in Juexing’s hand, as though it carried the weight of several lives.

“Snake Woman… Medusa,” he said quietly.

Orange stood, seized his wrist. “I watched him struggle. It was horrible. He was talking nonsense. What is happening?”

Juexing returned the photo to the envelope, gently freed his wrist, and sat down. He had no words to offer. Orange felt the distance between them open like a chasm. Only despair remained.

After a long silence she steadied herself, stood, and bowed.

“I understand…”

She turned to leave.

“Wait.”

She wiped the corner of her eye and looked back.

“He’s my student. Please help him.”

Juexing’s brow furrowed. He already knew this case would involve many people. The legend of Medusa was something even he had never faced. Finding a way to break it would be extremely difficult.

“It’s not that I refuse to help. It’s just…”

He swallowed the rest.

“If you say that, you’re admitting it’s a curse. You’re a cursebreaker—how can you have no solution?” Orange’s voice rose.

Juexing did not know how she had learned of his former identity, but someone had clearly begun leaking information. A larger scheme was already in motion.

“Forget it. I won’t force you. I’ll find a way myself.” Anger and disappointment colored her words.

“Where are you going?”

“Where I go is none of your business.”

She opened the door, stepped out, and slammed it shut behind her.

Juexing remained seated, staring at the closed door. Outside, Orange stood motionless in the hallway, suddenly without direction.

What was she supposed to do now?

### Character Notes

**Qingmu Chengzi (Orange)**  
The events of the night shatter the last of her rational defenses. Witnessing a living student turn to stone forces her to confront a reality her psychology training cannot explain. Her demand for help from Juexing marks a turning point—she is no longer a distant observer.

**Luo Juexing**  
Faced with a curse even he has never encountered, he hesitates. The appearance of the Medusa photograph and the sudden knowledge of his past identity confirm that someone is orchestrating events from the shadows. His silence is not indifference; it is the weight of responsibility.

**Shang Xuan**  
Continues to appear at critical moments, supplying both information and warnings. The photograph he delivers becomes the key link between the boy’s petrification and the larger threat. His true allegiance remains unclear.

**Cheng Shaoxi**  
The first confirmed victim of the new curse. His final words—“turn to stone”—and the matching photograph mark the arrival of the Medusa legend in the modern city.

**Xiao Lingdang (Little Bell)**  
A young girl who appears at Juexing’s door and addresses Orange as “Sister Li Mei.” Her final whispered line—“Getting more interesting”—echoes Shang Xuan’s earlier remark, hinting at deeper connections.

**The Medusa Curse**  
An ancient force previously unknown even to experienced cursebreakers. It manifests through photographs, auditory hallucinations, and rapid petrification. Its full scope and origin remain unknown.
