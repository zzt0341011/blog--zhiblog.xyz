Night settled quietly over the city. Even the busiest people eventually had to stop and rest.

Juexing stood by the window holding a glass of juice when Jiaye’s car rolled into a parking space below. He had known the detective would come; he simply had not expected him so late.

Jiaye tossed a set of photographs of the straw doll onto the table and dropped onto the sofa with an expression of pure accusation.

“Give me an explanation.”

His voice was low enough to raise the hair on the back of the neck.

“What kind of explanation do you want? That a curse killed him?” Juexing asked.

“This is murder, man. I don’t want any part of it.”

“Unfortunately I was there.”

“Then why didn’t you stop it?”

“I couldn’t.”

The words had barely left his mouth before Jiaye lunged up, seized him by the collar, and snarled, “You bastard!”

Juexing did not meet his eyes. He remained silent. No matter what happened, he would not betray Shang Xuan. He simply could not.

“Tell me who did it. Using a ghost to kill someone is still murder. It still has to be punished.” Jiaye released him.

“Have you watched the elevator footage? What did you think?”

“The method was too cruel. Even if that man deserved ten thousand deaths, no one has the right to take the law into their own hands.”

“The law is only a tool for the rich and powerful to exploit. Ordinary people are the ones who suffer. Tell me—who exactly am I supposed to protect?”

Jiaye stepped back several paces. The man in front of him suddenly felt like a stranger. The tone, the expression—both had become frightening.

“Don’t look at me like that. I haven’t changed. I’ve only started seeing things more clearly.” Juexing drained the glass in one swallow.

Jiaye sank onto the sofa as if his soul had left his body. The image from the elevator replayed in his mind: the middle-aged man slumped in the corner, eyes wide with terror, staring at the lower left of the car. Reflected in those dilated pupils was a long-haired girl. Then hands that no longer belonged to him drove themselves into his own eyes…

Jiaye let out a sharp shout and kicked the table. Fortunately little sat on it, or everything would have shattered.

At that exact moment both of their phones rang. The same word reached both ears. They spoke it together:

“Train?”

They raced downstairs, climbed into Jiaye’s car, and sped toward the station. A crowd had already gathered to watch the most dramatic scene train 5120 had yet produced.

The train sat at the midpoint station. A few scattered passengers waited on the platform. In one shadowed corner a dark-skinned man burned joss paper, murmuring under his breath.

A slanted shadow stood in the center of the seventh carriage. People on the platform first thought it was an illusion—until they looked closer. The long black hair hanging down belonged unmistakably to a girl.

Someone screamed. Passengers still trying to board stumbled backward. The train doors slammed shut with a heavy thud. Those left outside either fled or stayed rooted, staring.

Inside the carriage more and more people sensed her presence. Suddenly a small child stumbled forward, tripped over the girl standing in the aisle, and burst into loud sobs. The lights died the moment the crying began.

“What’s going on? The doors won’t open!” A man near the exit yanked at the handle with all his strength. The doors did not move.

Panic swept the car. A wave of powerful resentment flooded the space. An attendant from the sixth carriage forced open the connecting glass door—only for the long-haired high-school girl to spin around and point at him. An invisible force lifted the man off his feet and hurled him to the floor. Passengers tried to escape into the sixth carriage, but the glass door sealed itself at once. Everyone backed against the walls, trembling, staring at the girl.

By the time Juexing and Jiaye arrived the train was still at the midpoint station, yet the darkened seventh carriage revealed nothing of what was happening inside.

The platform had emptied. Fear of the ghost—or fear of being marked by it—had driven everyone away.

“The doors are locked.” Jiaye strained at the emergency handle. A bloodless girl’s face suddenly appeared on the glass. He jerked his hands back and nearly fell.

Juexing clapped him on the shoulder. “We go in through the eighth carriage.”

Only the seventh carriage had been dark. The moment Juexing prepared to enter the eighth, every light on the entire train went out. The conductor received an emergency call, realized something was wrong, and tried to take control—only for the train to begin moving on its own. Every system had failed.

“Damn it. We have to get on now—the train is leaving.” Seeing that the eighth carriage was also locked, Juexing pulled Jiaye toward the side of the train and the two of them began climbing onto the roof.

Over the public-address system the conductor tried to calm the passengers. Instead of his voice, a wet gurgling sound filled the cars, followed by the clear, unmistakable laugh of a high-school girl.

Every passenger heard it. Outside the windows the world blurred into streaks of speed.

While the conductor stood helpless, a figure slipped into the driver’s cab. In the darkness only a small red light moved across the console. A pair of gloved hands worked the controls with a skill that left the conductor staring. The train’s speed stopped climbing. Then, carriage by carriage, the lights returned. The returning glow calmed the passengers at least a little.

Juexing and Jiaye crawled forward on the roof until they reached the skylight of the seventh carriage. Jiaye pried it open. Wind roared in. When the lights came back on, a bald middle-aged man stood in the center of the aisle, eyes wide with terror, both hands locked around his own throat. His face had turned a deep, suffocating blue.

From the skylight Juexing could not see the full scene below—only the rising screams. The passengers, however, could see clearly: behind the choking man stood the disheveled high-school girl, her pale, slender hands clamped over his, forcing him to strangle himself. The hatred on her face needed no translation.

“What do we do? The skylight is too small to climb through!” Jiaye shouted over the wind.

Juexing glanced along the side of the train, considering the connecting passage between the sixth and seventh carriages. He signaled Jiaye and they crawled onward until they dropped down beside the glass door of the seventh car. Juexing slid a thin card into the gap, then wrenched the handle. The door gave way.

He was still too late. The bald man went limp and collapsed. A long purple bruise circled his neck.

When the train finally stopped it was half past ten. The moment the doors opened, every remaining passenger fled as if pursued by demons. Within half an hour the only people left aboard were the conductor, a few attendants, Juexing, Shang Xuan, and Jiaye.

They stood in silence around the twisted body in the center of the carriage. Jiaye pulled on gloves and removed a wallet from the inner pocket of the man’s suit. The identity card belonged to Fan Shijie—a well-known investor.

Shang Xuan glanced at the card and already understood. This was only the beginning. The real performance had yet to start.

“Why are you here?” Juexing asked the man standing beside him.

“Just returning a favor. Looks like there’s nothing left for me to do. I won’t get in your way. Good night.”

Shang Xuan jumped down from the carriage. The moment he looked up, the night sky filled with drifting sheets of joss paper—large and small, swirling like ash.

Jiaye spoke quietly. “Fan Shijie was one of the major investors in this express-train project.”

“Obviously she’s targeting them,” Juexing said, thoughtful.

“You mean this is only the start? There will be more?”

“She appeared the moment the express line opened. She’s tied to this project somehow.”

“Then we need to notify headquarters immediately and take measures. We can’t let her keep killing at will.” Jiaye sealed the identity card in an evidence bag.

Yu Xun had been watching from the shadows for a long time. When only the two men remained in the carriage she slipped inside, still demanding material for her story. Juexing turned and stepped off the train—directly onto a piece of joss paper. He looked down. It was identical to the one he had found earlier that day. Had some tragedy taken place here?

He scanned the empty platform. Nothing and no one connected to the spirit money remained.

“I’m heading back,” he told Jiaye over the phone. “I’ll handle this case for you. In exchange, stop asking about the elevator.”

He hung up, pulled his coat tighter, and walked toward the last departing train of the night.

Jiaye finally managed to shake Yu Xun off and immediately called the station. Police cars and an ambulance arrived twenty minutes later. As the body was loaded into the ambulance, a shadow slipped in after it. By the time Jiaye noticed, both vehicles had already turned and driven away.

Yu Xun raised her camcorder and filmed the departing ambulance. Through the rear window she saw the long-haired high-school girl press a pale hand against the glass. Both she and Jiaye stumbled backward in shock.

“What was that?”

Yu Xun replayed the footage. The image was nothing but distorted blur, twisted beyond recognition.

“Looks like I have no choice but to make that deal with him,” Jiaye said, voice heavy with meaning.

With a heavy thud the train doors closed. The conductor started the engine to return the train to the depot. Even after an incident this shocking, the schedule still had to be kept.

The two of them watched the train pull away and finally released the breath they had been holding. Jiaye no longer felt any desire to sleep. He wanted a drink. Yu Xun glanced at him.

“Thinking of getting something to drink?”

Jiaye draped an arm over her shoulders with casual boldness.

“Keep reporting. I hope you’ll keep covering these strange cases without giving up. As for me… I may have to change the way I do things.”

Yu Xun looked at him in confusion. The tone no longer belonged to the man she thought she knew.

### Character Notes

**Luo Juexing**  
Openly challenges Jiaye’s rigid view of the law, revealing a harder, more pragmatic edge. He protects Shang Xuan’s identity and offers a clear bargain: he will handle the ghost train if the elevator case is dropped. The decision marks a further step away from simple rule-following.

**Huang Jiaye**  
Confronted with evidence that the law cannot touch certain evils, he begins to fracture. The final line—“I may have to change the way I do things”—signals the beginning of a moral shift that will define his role going forward.

**Shang Xuan**  
Appears at the critical moment to stabilize the runaway train from the driver’s cab, then leaves amid a storm of joss paper. His “favor repaid” claim and quiet satisfaction at Fan Shijie’s death show he is still actively shaping the board.

**The High-School Girl**  
No longer limited to appearances or dreams. She now locks doors, extinguishes lights, seals connecting passages, and forces victims to strangle themselves. Her targets are specifically linked to the express-train project.

**Fan Shijie**  
Wealthy investor in the 5120 project. His death inside the sealed seventh carriage confirms the ghost’s motive is tied to the construction or opening of the line.

**Yu Xun**  
Persists in chasing footage even after witnessing the girl on the ambulance window. Her distorted recording proves the entity can interfere with modern recording devices.

**Joss Paper**  
Appears again—first found by Juexing earlier in the day, then falling from the sky after the train stops. Its presence suggests ritual activity or unresolved funerary resentment connected to the railway.
