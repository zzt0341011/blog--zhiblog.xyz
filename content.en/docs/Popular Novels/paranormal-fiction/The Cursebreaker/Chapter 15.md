Because Orange had morning classes, Juexing went to the train station alone. Jiaye had also seen the newspaper and arrived near the station at almost the same time, though he headed first to the station office in search of any useful leads.

Juexing walked to the midpoint platform of train 5120 and circled it several times. Everything looked perfectly normal. No visible trace of residual resentment clung to the place.

He wondered whether the phenomenon only appeared at night. Perhaps. As he turned down the connecting corridor, a yellow scrap of paper drifted toward him and settled at his feet.

He bent and picked it up. It was a piece of joss paper—spirit money. He looked around in confusion. Why would joss paper appear here?

At that moment Yu Xun approached. Juexing turned and studied her. The dull, frightened look in her eyes told him she had encountered something similar.

“Are you all right?” he asked.

“You saw the report. The situation is far more complicated than I thought. In the darkroom I… met…” She stopped herself and glanced around warily, as if the thing she refused to name might be standing nearby.

Juexing rubbed the light stubble on his chin.

“I’ve started investigating this so-called ghost train as well.”

“Please give me everything you find. Otherwise I won’t be able to rest.” A faint, almost involuntary smile touched her lips. The performance was surprisingly convincing.

“In the end you still want the story. I’ll say this once: the more you chase these things, the less good it does you.”

The terror of the darkroom had not diminished Yu Xun’s determination. If anything, it had sharpened her hunger for an exclusive. Perhaps only a genuine brush with death would make her understand this was no game.

Juexing fell silent. He slipped the joss paper into his pocket and walked toward the city center. Yu Xun did not follow. Instead she headed for the station office—and ran straight into Jiaye. The two struck up an easy conversation almost at once.

News that a high-school girl’s figure had been seen at the station spread quickly through the crowd. Many people came hoping to witness the phenomenon for themselves, yet the more deliberately they looked, the less they saw.

Shang Xuan sat at a breakfast stall eating fried dough sticks and drinking soy milk while reading the newspaper. The front-page story was already losing its freshness. Without a new development the ghost-train incident would soon fade from public attention. He set the paper down and murmured, almost to himself:

“Looks like it’s time to spice up the game.”

He left money for the meal and walked away. At that moment a black Mercedes rolled to a stop at the intersection. The window lowered, revealing a middle-aged man in glasses. Beside him sat Song Xi.

“That’s the one,” the man said. “He ruined my deal.”

Song Xi studied the half-blond young man carefully. He seemed familiar—perhaps from a recent gathering.

“Remember what I told you. Teach him a proper lesson.”

“No problem. I know how to handle it. We’ll talk about the partnership after I’ve dealt with him.” Song Xi signaled the driver and dialed a number on his phone.

The Mercedes swept past Shang Xuan. He gave a soft, cold snort and ignored it completely. Watching the car disappear, he smiled.

“Looks like you’ll be the first one on stage. Bad luck for you.”

When Juexing crossed the sidewalk a short while later he caught a glimpse of Shang Xuan’s back in the distance. The other man was carrying a small box. Juexing almost dismissed it—then a sudden thought struck him. What if the box contained…

Events rarely unfold as expected.

Shang Xuan followed the Mercedes until he located the middle-aged man’s destination: a twenty-story apartment building. Disguising himself as a cleaner, he slipped into the elevator behind his target. Standing at the man’s back, he carefully opened the box, slipped a small straw doll into the coat pocket, and closed his fingers around a small bell.

The elevator doors opened. The middle-aged man stepped out. Shang Xuan pushed the cleaning cart after him—only to find the corridor empty.

*Impossible. No one moves that fast.*

A cold laugh sounded behind him. More than a dozen men holding wooden clubs stood at Song Xi’s back. The middle-aged man emerged from the opposite direction.

“Kid, changing clothes doesn’t make you invisible. You’re too naïve.”

Shang Xuan realized he had walked into a carefully prepared trap.

Song Xi stepped forward.

“Because of you I nearly lost a deal worth over a hundred million. You know how you should compensate me, don’t you?”

“And to think Orange still considers you a friend. You’d sell even her for money. You’re pathetic.”

The words had barely left his mouth before Shang Xuan’s fist crashed into Song Xi’s face, sending him staggering backward with stars in his eyes.

The blow ignited the entire group. At Song Xi’s order, a dozen clubs swung toward Shang Xuan.

A one-sided brawl erupted beside the elevators.

Outnumbered and outmatched, Shang Xuan had no real chance. Song Xi and the middle-aged man stood to the side, watching and cheering with growing excitement.

The elevator doors opened. Shang Xuan dodged the clubs and edged toward the opening. Song Xi shouted:

“Block the elevator! Don’t let him escape!”

In a flash of inspiration Shang Xuan broke for the emergency stairs instead. The pack thundered after him. Gritting his teeth against the pain, he climbed toward the top floors.

“Post men at every elevator on the ground floor,” the middle-aged man ordered. “He is not to leave this building.”

Song Xi smiled obsequiously. “Brilliant. Even if he grows wings he won’t get away.”

On the nineteenth floor Shang Xuan paused to catch his breath. Hearing no pursuit behind him, he turned back toward the elevators. Bloodied fingers pressed the call button. The doors opened—just as two club-wielding men stepped out of the adjacent car. Shang Xuan was already inside. The doors closed.

“Boss, he got into elevator number two. He’s headed down.”

“Perfect. We just wait.”

The group surrounded the second elevator and watched the red numbers descend: fifteen… ten…

Shang Xuan leaned against the wall, cradling his injured arm. He had not expected them to try to kill him outright. Nor did he intend to let the matter end here. He looked at the bell in his hand. Once he was safely away, these people would pay dearly.

The elevator reached the fifth floor. He moved to press the open button—but the car refused to stop. It continued its relentless descent.

“They’re controlling it?” Shang Xuan muttered in shock.

Song Xi had tampered with the control panel. For safety, elevator number two would open only on the ground floor.

The middle-aged man and Song Xi waited with cold smiles, staring at the doors that were about to open.

A sudden metallic clang made Shang Xuan look up. A gap had appeared in the elevator’s ceiling panel. A hand reached down and shoved the cover aside. Standing above him, offering an unexpected hand, was the last person he expected to see.

“What are you staring at? Climb up. I don’t want you finished this early.”

Shang Xuan gave a low, bitter laugh, then reached up and let himself be pulled through the hatch. The two of them climbed the elevator shaft to the fourth floor, exited through a ventilation duct, and dropped into the shaft of elevator number one.

While the hunters congratulated themselves, the doors of elevator number two finally opened. Dozens of eyes stared into an empty car that contained only a few smears of blood.

“What—?”

The middle-aged man rushed inside, searching frantically. There was no sign of Shang Xuan. Then, with sudden violent force, the doors slammed shut. A dark blur shoved the men outside away from the car.

Song Xi stabbed at the call buttons with trembling fingers. Nothing responded. Before everyone’s eyes the elevator began to accelerate upward in a straight, screaming line. The floor indicator dissolved into red static, then flashed a single word:

**DEAD**

“Get to the control room! Stop the elevator!” Song Xi shouted. One of his men scrambled away on hands and knees.

The elevator moved like a possessed thing, racing up and down the shaft. Inside, the middle-aged man’s screams rose and broke. The car spun him like a centrifuge at impossible speed.

Juexing clamped a hand over Shang Xuan’s fingers where they still gripped the bell.

“You can’t do this. It’s no different from murder.”

“Too late…”

Shang Xuan crushed the bell. A fine white powder slipped through his fingers and drifted away.

The two of them stood side by side and watched the doors of elevator number two slide open once more. The middle-aged man lay crumpled in the corner, eyes gouged out by his own hands, fingers locked around his throat, body covered in blood.

Juexing exhaled a long, regretful breath and helped Shang Xuan toward the exit. Shang Xuan shook off his support.

“Men like that deserve to die. If you’d been in my place you would have done the same.”

“What exactly happened?”

“That bastard planned to force himself on Orange. Tell me he didn’t deserve it.” Shang Xuan licked the blood still drying at the corner of his mouth and walked on without looking back.

As Juexing turned, police cars and an ambulance were already arriving.

Jiaye received the report and reached the scene moments later. The instant he stepped out of the car he spotted Juexing standing nearby. His first instinct told him this was a homicide.

He gave Juexing a brief nod, then entered the building. Song Xi and several of his men had been gathered in the lobby for questioning. The middle-aged man’s body was carried out; the expression on the corpse was one of pure, unfiltered terror, as though he had seen something inside the elevator that no human mind could endure.

A uniformed officer found a small straw doll in the dead man’s coat pocket and handed it to Jiaye in an evidence bag. Jiaye examined the doll, then glanced sideways at Juexing standing in the distance.

*Could it be…*

“Retrieve the elevator surveillance footage,” he ordered the nearest officer.

### Character Notes

**Luo Juexing**  
Appears at the critical moment to extract Shang Xuan from a death trap, yet immediately tries to stop the lethal curse. His presence confirms that, despite their enmity, he will not stand by and watch Shang Xuan die. The moral line he draws—“this is no different from murder”—highlights the growing tension between justice and vengeance.

**Shang Xuan**  
Willing to use a straw-doll curse and a death-bell to eliminate a man who threatened Orange. His claim that the victim “deserved it” reveals both protective instinct and a willingness to cross lines Juexing still refuses to cross. The unexpected rescue does not soften his edge.

**Song Xi**  
Reappears as a direct antagonist. His willingness to sell out Orange for a business deal and to orchestrate a near-murder in a public building marks him as a dangerous, opportunistic enemy.

**Yu Xun**  
Still chasing the story despite her darkroom trauma. Her brief meeting with Juexing and subsequent conversation with Jiaye keep her positioned as both potential ally and liability.

**Huang Jiaye**  
Arrives as the official investigator. The discovery of the straw doll and his pointed glance at Juexing signal that the police are now circling the edges of the supernatural conflict.

**The Straw Doll & Bell**  
Classic tools of directed curse work. Once activated, the doll links the victim to the bell’s power, turning an ordinary elevator into an instrument of psychological and physical destruction.
