A sharp *click* broke the pre-dawn quiet. The anti-theft lock on the little van gave way.

The door swung open without a sound. A shadow slipped inside, then pulled the door shut again.

At first light Juexing woke to a strange noise—something that sounded like a muffled scream. He turned toward the cargo area. Jiaye still snored heavily. The rest of the van was empty. Xiaohai was gone.

“Damn it!”  

The shout woke Orange. She rubbed eyes rimmed with faint dark circles. The two of them hurried to the van and rapped on the window until Jiaye stirred.

“From the footprints, he headed toward Dumei Village,” Juexing said, crouching beside the open door and studying the blurred tracks in the dirt.

“We’re finished—the car keys are gone!” Jiaye patted every pocket in growing panic and found nothing.

“We’ll have to walk. Call a tow truck for the van later.”

“Is Xiaohai in danger?” Orange asked.

“Hard to say. We don’t know when he left or how. The worst possibility is…” Juexing did not finish the sentence. Everything remained unknown.

Orange stared at the empty cargo space, face tight with worry. If she had stayed in the van, this might never have happened.

Juexing stepped beside her. “This isn’t your fault, Tangerine.”

She turned and fixed him with a mildly annoyed look. “My name is Orange. Not Tangerine.”

“Close enough. Let’s move. Once we cross that ridge we’ll be there.”

Jiaye looked up. The high ridge vanished into drifting cloud. His expression said he hoped they were joking about climbing it.

“Hey—wait for me!” By the time he reacted, Orange and Juexing were already walking.

As the police officer present, Jiaye took the lead, moving like a wary hound and missing no possible clue. Juexing walked in the middle. Orange brought up the rear.

“Come here… come here…”  

Orange stopped. Someone seemed to be calling her name.

She looked left. Only deep trees and undergrowth. When she turned right, a figure roughly Xiaohai’s size stood half-hidden beside a tree, right hand pointing straight at her.

She raised a hand to call Juexing, but the figure appeared frightened and bolted into the woods. Fear for Xiaohai overrode caution. She ran after him without thinking. Juexing felt the sudden absence behind him and spun around. Orange was gone.

“Jiaye, hold up!”

“Where’s the beauty?”

“She may have seen an illusion of Xiaohai and followed it.”

“Then we split up. Meet back here.” Jiaye dragged a large stone into the path and wrapped his tie around it as a marker.

“That won’t help. Whoever finds her first goes straight into the village. We meet there.”

“Fine. Watch yourself.” Jiaye headed left.

Juexing scanned the surroundings, retraced their steps a short distance, and discovered a three-way fork he had not noticed before. After a brief pause he took the left path.

Orange chased the fleeing figure until the ground simply disappeared beneath her feet. She tumbled down the slope.

The incline was far steeper than it looked. She slid and rolled for what felt like five long minutes before she finally stopped at the bottom of a closed depression. Her right ankle twisted hard; sharp pain pulsed upward. Both palms and the backs of her hands were scraped raw and beading with blood.

She brushed away grass and dirt, then shouted “Help!” toward the ridge. Only echoes answered. Was this a dead end?

An unnatural wind stirred. Fear gathered in slow layers. The hollow grew darker until she could barely see her own hands.

“Juexing… where are you? Come get me…” She curled in on herself. Tears slipped down her face.

A blurred shape appeared above her, grinning. The smile and the low, chilling laugh that followed only tightened the fear around her throat.

“I can’t just sit here and wait to die.”  

Gritting her teeth against the pain, she limped forward, one hand trailing along whatever surface she could reach, forcing herself in a single direction.

Juexing ran through the trees calling her name. The woods remained empty. Jiaye, searching on his side, did find someone—but the figure’s build and clothing were clearly not Orange’s.

The woman held a DV camera and appeared to be filming.

“Who’s there?” Jiaye called, instantly alert.

She jumped, then turned and walked toward him.

“Oh. It’s a person…”

“What exactly did you mean by that?”

“Sorry—I’m lost. Do you know the way to Dumei Village?”

Jiaye studied her. Slender, strikingly beautiful, with a sultry edge that placed her in an entirely different category from Orange.

She took a few wary steps back. His expression had turned a shade too interested. He cleared his throat and produced his badge.

“I’m police. We’re here investigating the case in Dumei Village.”

“The petrification-curse case? That’s why I’m here too. I heard Teacher Luo came as well.”

“Given the nature of the case, I can’t say much.”

“No problem. Lead the way, then.”

“What’s your name, pretty reporter?”

She kept the camera trained on him with professional calm.

“I asked you a question. Why aren’t you answering?”

“Meng Yuxun. And you, Officer?”

“Huang Jiaye. You can call me Ye-ye…”

“Calling you ‘Grandpa’ would be giving you far too much advantage.”

They talked as they walked. Before long the village gate of Dumei appeared ahead of them—and with it, Jiaye completely forgot they were still supposed to be looking for Orange.

Juexing reached the edge of a steep drop and found a bracelet lying in the grass.

“It’s Tangerine’s.” He slipped it into his pocket, took a few careful steps forward, and stared into the dark depth. Shouting produced no answer.

“She fell. There has to be a way down.” He told himself he would not leave until he found her.

Far below, Orange limped toward a faint glow that looked like candlelight.

A soft *crunch-crunch* sounded behind her. She froze, dropped into a crouch, and felt the earlier terror rush back.

Half-blond hair. An odd right eye. But the familiar flying-kiss gesture told her everything.

Shang Xuan had appeared without warning.

“We meet again,” he said with a smile.

“How are you here?”

“You forgot? Whenever you’re in danger, I’m the first one who shows up.” He turned and crouched.

“What are you doing?”

“Your ankle is badly hurt. Obviously I’m carrying you up. You’re trying to reach Dumei Village, right?”

Orange hesitated. Shang Xuan glanced back.

“Or I can just pick you up in my arms.”

“N-no. Carrying is fine.”

She lowered her head, thoughts in chaos. Shang Xuan hoisted her onto his back and started toward the light. Orange rested against him, staring at the dark half of his hair, and thought, *If only this were Juexing…*

Juexing finally found a usable path down the slope. At the bottom he discovered Orange slumped unconscious against a tree.

He ran the last distance and dropped to one knee. Scrapes covered her hands and face; her right ankle was already swelling.

“Tangerine. Tangerine?”

In the dream she heard someone calling.

When Orange opened her eyes, Juexing sat at a small table with his eyes closed.

“Where am I?”

He came to the bedside, helped her sit up, and handed her a cup of water.

“We’re in a small inn in Dumei Village. Your ankle has been treated. The doctor says it will need a full week to heal properly.”

Orange exhaled in relief and leaned against his chest.

“I’m sorry I worried you.”

Juexing was silent a moment. “Rest. Leave the rest to me.”

“Where’s Jiaye? I haven’t seen him.”

“We agreed to meet in the village. He’s probably somewhere nearby.”

“I thought the person was Xiaohai. After I followed, I fell.”

Juexing refilled the cup. “You’re back. That’s the only thing that matters.”

Orange held the cup with both hands. Her eyes were red; faint tear tracks still marked her face.

A heavy pounding shook the door.

“Bad news, Xing-zai! I found Xiaohai!”

Both of them jolted awake. Juexing opened the door and Jiaye half-fell into the room.

“You saw him?”

Jiaye crossed to the table, poured water, drained it in noisy gulps, then belched with satisfaction.

“Someone spotted him near the altar.”

“The altar?”

“A villager who cleans it told me. And—” Jiaye waved toward the doorway. “Let me introduce someone.”

Meng Yuxun stepped inside and offered her hand.

“Teacher Luo. A pleasure.”

Juexing ignored the hand and returned to his seat.

“What’s a reporter doing here? Looking for a sensational story?”

“Teacher Luo thinks the case is unreliable? I personally don’t believe you waste time on nonsense.”

Her tongue was famously sharp; few people in the press corps could out-talk her.

Juexing had no desire to involve outsiders.

“Jiaye, this is your mess. Clean it up. But I’m warning you now—if anything goes wrong, I take no responsibility.”

“Relax. I’ll only do my job. I won’t interfere with your plans,” Yuxun said.

“We’re all friends here. No need to make it so tense. I’ll take responsibility for her safety,” Jiaye offered.

“I’m going to the altar. Don’t wait for me.” Juexing stood to leave.

Orange called after him. “I want to go too.”

He hesitated. Leaving her alone here was also unsafe.

“All right. Come with me.”

“What about us?” Jiaye asked.

“Du Xiaoxiao is your responsibility. Once you locate her, tell me where. We’ll join you. Time is short.”

Juexing found a battered bicycle, settled Orange on it, and pedaled toward the altar.

Watching their figures disappear, Shang Xuan smiled.

“The game is only just beginning…”

“I didn’t expect Teacher Luo to have such a temper,” Yuxun remarked.

“Can’t really blame him. He’s carrying a lot,” Jiaye answered.

“From the way you talk, the two of you go back a long way?”

“It’s a long story. Let’s change the subject.” Jiaye’s expression shifted into something more calculating.

Yuxun glanced at him, clearly reading the intent.

“Fine. As long as you let me turn the petrification case into a proper report, we can talk about anything.”

“Really?” Jiaye shot her a playful look.

“Really. No jokes.”

### Character Notes

**Luo Juexing**  
Focused and protective once Orange is hurt. He refuses to leave the search until she is found, then immediately shifts to the next lead—the altar—while keeping outsiders at arm’s length.

**Qingmu Chengzi (Orange)**  
Impulsive when she believes Xiaohai is in danger. The fall leaves her injured and shaken, yet she still insists on accompanying Juexing to the altar rather than remaining behind.

**Huang Jiaye**  
Easily distracted by a beautiful woman. He forgets the search for Orange the moment Meng Yuxun appears, then tries to negotiate media access in exchange for cooperation.

**Shang Xuan**  
Appears the instant Orange is in real danger, carries her to safety, then watches from the shadows as events unfold. His closing line confirms he is orchestrating or at least observing a larger game.

**Meng Yuxun**  
Glamorous investigative reporter with a DV camera and a sharp tongue. She has already connected the case to the “petrification curse” and to Teacher Luo. Her presence raises the stakes for secrecy.

**Xiaohai**  
Still missing. The only concrete lead is a villager’s sighting near the village altar.
