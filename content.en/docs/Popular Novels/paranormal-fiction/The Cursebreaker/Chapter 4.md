Time passed without warning. Two full days later, Orange finished clearing the documents on her office desk, checked her phone, and saw it was already past one in the afternoon. She pouted, pressed a hand to her empty stomach, picked up her bag, and left the office.

Since Juexing had walked out of that dark, oppressive room, she had heard nothing more from it. Had the curse truly been lifted, exactly as he claimed?

A strange urge to laugh rose in her chest—the kind of laugh that mocked herself. She walked with her head down, watching a few dry leaves on the ground, and failed to notice the broad-shouldered man striding straight toward her.

By the time she reacted, he had already seized her bag.

“Hand over the money. Refuse and I’ll take more than that.”

Orange had only ever seen scenes like this in soap operas. She could not believe someone would attempt a robbery in broad daylight. She refused to let the criminal succeed. Her fingers locked around the strap and she refused to yield.

The man’s gaze dropped to her chest and lingered. Seizing the moment, she drove her knee upward into his stomach. He was tall and solid; the blow barely registered. She began to scream for help.

Despair set in quickly. The alley was almost deserted. She had no idea why her feet had carried her here.

“Cooperate, will you? Don’t make this harder than it needs to be!” the man barked, trying to intimidate her with volume. Orange was not the type to bow to evil. She continued to protect her bag with stubborn strength.

“A grown man with working hands and feet, and this is how you make a living? Pathetic.”

A man with half his hair dyed blond appeared at the other end of the alley. He leaned one shoulder against the wall, looking every bit the street punk, and regarded the robber with open contempt. Orange noticed that his right eye was somehow different from ordinary people’s, though she could not immediately say how.

“You little bastard, acting tough? Who the hell do you think you are? Get lost.”

The blond-haired man held a small stone. He tossed it lightly into the air a few times, caught it, then in one fluid motion pitched it like a baseball. The stone traced a clean arc and struck the robber squarely on the forehead.

The large man released Orange’s bag at once, enraged. He swung a heavy fist. Orange raised her bag to shield her face. The difference in size between the two men was obvious; they were not in the same league.

Yet the opposite of what she expected happened. The blond man slipped aside, drove a sharp hook into the robber’s gut, then spun and kicked him hard enough to send him sprawling. The big man hit the ground and stayed there, dazed.

The stranger brushed dust from his clothes, blew Orange a casual flying kiss, flicked his hair, and vanished from sight.

Orange forced her scattered thoughts back into place. Still trembling, she hurried past the groaning man on the ground and out of the alley.

She had not gone far when she nearly collided with Juexing.

“What’s wrong? You look pale.”

“Ah—what did you say?” She turned and only then realized who stood in front of her.

“Your hand is hurt.” Juexing pointed to a thin scrape on her right hand—probably from the struggle.

He took a bandage from the small pouch at his waist, peeled the wrapper, gently took her wrist, and covered the wound with careful precision.

“Th-thank you.” Orange managed a small smile.

“If you’re all right, I need to get to the school.” He turned to leave.

“Wait…”

“What is it?”

“What time do you finish this afternoon? I have something I want to ask you.”

“Around three. I’ll message you when I’m done. Tell me where to meet.” He held up his phone and gave it a little shake.

“Okay.” Orange nodded and watched him walk away.

Not far off, the half-blond man chewed a piece of gum and murmured, “Getting more interesting.” He flicked the wrapper aside, mounted a folding bicycle parked nearby, and pedaled off, humming as if he had no particular destination.

At roughly three-fifteen, Juexing arrived at the small café they had agreed on. Orange sat at the outermost table by the window. He spotted her the moment he reached the door.

“Sorry. Traffic.”

“It’s fine. I ordered you a black coffee. Is that okay?”

Juexing nodded. “Thanks.”

A waiter soon brought two cups and a small plate of pastries.

Orange stirred her coffee with a spoon, suddenly unsure how to ask the question that had been sitting in her chest. Juexing watched her silence and seemed to understand why she had asked to meet.

“You were in the room that night, weren’t you?” he said quietly.

“Yes. I’m sorry. I didn’t get your permission. Is that… a problem?”

“Since we’re sitting here, I’ll be direct. This is not a game. From now on, stay out of it.”

“You’re really angry?” Orange bit her lower lip.

Juexing fell silent. He did not know how to explain the danger. Once a person touched a true curse, the consequences ranged from madness to death, and none of it could be controlled or predicted.

She saw the heaviness in his expression and understood the seriousness. Still, she needed to know what it all meant.

“I’ll tell you a story,” Juexing said at last. “After you hear it, you will go back to teaching, keep your head down, and never ask about these things again. Agreed?”

Orange could not think of a better answer, so she simply nodded.

The story he told was this:

They had been a deeply loving couple, on the verge of marriage, when an accident intervened. A hidden third party stepped forward, openly challenged her, and declared she would take her place. In truth the third party had already stripped him of everything he owned, forcing him to leave with her.

The woman was abandoned without mercy. She had walked beside him through every hardship, supported him until he had a career and a future, only to discover he had taken a mistress behind her back.

She chose to pretend she did not know. She continued to help him, hoping pure and simple love would bring him back.

Heaven tilted the scales against her. In the end the tragedy she had dreaded arrived. In a moment of rage he killed her. Only after he woke and finally saw the third party’s true face did he realize he had nothing left. He chose to end his own life.

Orange’s eyes stung. She grabbed a tissue from the table and wiped her nose.

“How did you know what happened inside that room?”

Juexing finished the last of his coffee, took out his wallet, and stood to pay. Orange snatched up her bag and caught his wrist.

“You still haven’t answered me—”

He glanced at her hand. She released him at once. After settling the bill he turned back.

“I don’t want you involved because it’s for your own good. Even if you learned everything, it would bring you no benefit.” He gave a faint smile and walked out the door.

By the time Orange recovered and ran after him, he had already disappeared into the crowd.

She sighed. “I didn’t even get to the important part. I’m so stupid.”

She stood blankly at the entrance for a moment, then an idea struck her. The university library. There had to be some record of Luo Juexing there. She nodded to herself.

“I don’t believe your mouth is that tight. I’ll start with your background. Something will surface.”

It was not yet four. The library stayed open until five-thirty. She flagged a taxi and headed straight there.

Walking down the library corridor she felt as though a pair of eyes were fixed on her. She lowered her head, checked her surroundings, decided it was only nerves, and went inside.

In the computer room she found an empty terminal and typed Luo Juexing’s name into the search bar. The results that appeared made her freeze.

Only a few brief, unimportant lines of personal introduction showed. Everything else had been blocked.

The man sitting beside her leaned back in his chair and watched her for a long moment. Orange felt the strange weight of his gaze and turned sharply. It was the half-blond stranger who had saved her earlier.

Up close he was strikingly handsome. The air around him was nothing like a street punk’s. Orange felt an unexpected pull. He smiled and blew her another flying kiss—casual, open, almost provocative.

“Why are you here?” she asked quietly.

He took a student ID from his pocket, waved it in front of her, then dropped it on the desk.

“I knew you’d come. I’ve been waiting.”

The words startled her. Did people with true foresight actually exist? This was not the place to talk. She stood.

“Can we go somewhere else?”

“Whatever.” He rose, stretched with a yawn that somehow still looked cool, and drew every nearby eye.

Orange followed him out, her heartbeat climbing in sharp peaks. He chose the same café where she had met Juexing, even the same table—only the direction of the seats was reversed.

“I should thank you for helping me earlier,” she said.

“It was nothing. Don’t mention it.”

“My name is Qingmu Chengzi. What’s yours?”

“Shang Xuan. The ‘Shang’ from monk, the ‘Xuan’ from mystery.”

The way he explained it was so matter-of-fact that Orange almost laughed.

“I have a question—”

He cut her off, took a folded slip of paper from his pocket, and handed it to her.

“There’s a URL on this. It contains almost everything you want to know. The password is also my phone number.”

Orange accepted the paper as if it were treasure. He paused, then added:

“But he was right. Getting mixed up in these strange things won’t do you any good. Weigh it carefully. Don’t throw your life away.”

He stood, took out his wallet, and walked to the counter. The movement, the tone—both were uncannily similar to Juexing’s. By the time Orange looked up again, Shang Xuan had vanished like a gust of wind—appearing without warning, gone without a trace.

### Character Notes

**Qingmu Chengzi (Orange)**  
Psychologist and confirmed atheist whose carefully ordered worldview continues to crack. Curiosity drives her forward even after clear warnings. The events of the alley and the library push her deeper into territory she does not yet understand.

**Luo Juexing**  
Remains distant and protective. He offers a carefully edited story of the room’s past as a final attempt to keep Orange away from the curse. His concern is genuine; his methods remain opaque.

**Shang Xuan**  
New and enigmatic. Half-blond hair, an unsettling right eye, casual confidence, and knowledge he should not possess. He intervenes at critical moments, provides information, then disappears. His motives are unknown. Whether ally, observer, or something else remains to be seen.

**The Residual Presence (resolved)**  
The story Juexing told matches the bones and the files recovered in Chapter 3. The couple’s tragedy, the betrayal, the murder, and the final suicide form the core of the resentment that had clung to the apartment.
